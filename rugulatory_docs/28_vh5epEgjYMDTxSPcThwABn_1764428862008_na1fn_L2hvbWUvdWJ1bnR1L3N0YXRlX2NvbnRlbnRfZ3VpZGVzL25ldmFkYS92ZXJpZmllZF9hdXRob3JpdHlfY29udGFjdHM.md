## 1. Nevada Medicaid Programs: Eligibility and Services

The **Division of Health Care Financing and Policy (DHCFP)** is the state agency responsible for administering the Nevada Medicaid program, which provides essential health coverage and long-term services and supports (LTSS) to eligible low-income residents.

### Nevada Medicaid (DHCFP)

This contact is the primary resource for general inquiries regarding the state's Medicaid and Nevada Check Up programs. They can provide information on eligibility criteria, application status, covered services, and managed care organizations.

*   **Agency:** Division of Health Care Financing and Policy (DHCFP)
*   **Program:** Nevada Medicaid
*   **Primary Contact Number:** **(800) 992-0900**
*   **Website:** [https://www.medicaid.nv.gov/](https://www.medicaid.nv.gov/)
*   **Key Services:** Information on the **Assisted Living (AL) Waiver**, the **Waiver for the Frail Elderly (FE)**, and other Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) that help seniors and individuals with disabilities receive care outside of a nursing home setting.

### The Importance of the DHCFP

The DHCFP is the gateway to financial assistance for long-term care. Their representatives can clarify the complex financial and functional eligibility requirements, including the income and asset limits for long-term care Medicaid programs. They also oversee the state's transition to a managed care model for many Medicaid recipients, making them the authoritative source for understanding how services are delivered.

## 2. Health Facility Licensing and Quality Oversight

The **Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC)** is the state's regulatory body, ensuring that all licensed health facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living facilities, meet minimum standards of quality and safety.

### Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC)

This general contact number is for inquiries related to facility licensing, regulatory standards, and general compliance questions. It is the best starting point for understanding the legal requirements for a facility to operate in Nevada.

*   **Agency:** Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC)
*   **Parent Agency:** Division of Public and Behavioral Health (DPBH)
*   **Primary Contact Number:** **(775) 684-1030**
*   **Website:** [https://www.dpbh.nv.gov/regulatory/hcqc/health-facility-licensing-and-information/](https://www.dpbh.nv.gov/regulatory/hcqc/health-facility-licensing-and-information/)
*   **Key Services:** Information on facility licensing status, the process for becoming a licensed health facility, and access to the **Health Facility Locator** for viewing inspection reports and compliance history.

### The HCQC's Regulatory Authority

The HCQC enforces the **Nevada Revised Statutes (NRS) Chapter 449** and the **Nevada Administrative Code (NAC) Chapter 449**. This includes setting standards for staffing, resident rights, physical environment, and quality of care. Contacting the HCQC is crucial for verifying a facility's credentials before making a placement decision.

## 3. Ombudsman and Advocacy Services

The **Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** provides confidential advocacy for residents of long-term care facilities, ensuring their rights are respected and their quality of life is maintained.

### Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The LTCOP is an independent advocate for residents in nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and residential care homes. This helpline is the primary contact for residents, family members, and staff to report concerns related to resident rights and quality of life.

*   **Agency:** Aging and Disability Services Division (ADSD)
*   **Program:** Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)
*   **Primary Contact Number (Helpline):** **(888) 282-1155**
*   **Website:** [https://adsd.nv.gov/programs/seniors/ltcombudsman/ltcombudsprog/](https://adsd.nv.gov/programs/seniors/ltcombudsman/ltcombudsprog/)
*   **Key Services:** Confidential complaint investigation and resolution, education on **Residents' Rights**, and mediation between residents and facility staff. The Ombudsman's role is purely advocacy, and they will not reveal the identity of the complainant without consent.

### The Importance of the LTCOP

The LTCOP is often the first and most accessible resource for resolving day-to-day issues within a facility. Unlike regulatory agencies, the Ombudsman focuses on person-centered advocacy and can often resolve issues quickly and informally, preventing them from escalating into formal complaints.

## 4. Complaint and Abuse Reporting Processes

Nevada has two distinct and equally important channels for reporting serious concerns about health facilities and vulnerable adults: the HCQC for regulatory violations and Adult Protective Services (APS) for abuse and neglect.

### HCQC Complaint Hotline (Regulatory Violations)

This dedicated hotline is for reporting allegations of regulatory violations, substandard care, or non-compliance with licensing standards in a licensed health facility.

*   **Agency:** Bureau of Health Care Quality and Compliance (HCQC)
*   **Purpose:** Complaint Intake and Prioritization
*   **Primary Contact Number (Complaint Hotline):** **(702) 668-3250**
*   **Website:** [https://www.dpbh.nv.gov/regulatory/hcqc/health-facility-licensing-and-information/health-facilities-complaints/](https://www.dpbh.nv.gov/regulatory/hcqc/health-facility-licensing-and-information/health-facilities-complaints/)
*   **Key Services:** Initiating an investigation into a facility's compliance with state and federal regulations. Complaints are prioritized based on the severity of the potential harm to residents.

### Adult Protective Services (APS) Statewide Hotline (Abuse and Neglect)

This is the mandatory reporting line for all suspected cases of abuse, neglect, exploitation, isolation, or abandonment of a vulnerable adult (age 18 and older).

*   **Agency:** Aging and Disability Services Division (ADSD)
*   **Program:** Adult Protective Services (APS)
*   **Primary Contact Number (Statewide Hotline):** **(888) 729-0571**
*   **Website:** [https://adsd.nv.gov/Contact/Contact_ReportAbuse/](https://adsd.nv.gov/Contact/Contact_ReportAbuse/)
*   **Key Services:** Investigation of all forms of vulnerable adult maltreatment. This service is critical for situations involving immediate danger or criminal activity.

### Distinguishing Between Complaint Channels

It is vital to use the correct channel:
*   **HCQC:** For facility-wide issues, quality of care, and licensing violations.
*   **APS:** For direct harm to a vulnerable adult (abuse, neglect, exploitation).
*   **LTCOP:** For resident rights issues and quality of life concerns.

## 5. Veterans Benefits and Long-Term Care

The **Nevada Department of Veterans Services (NDVS)** is the state agency dedicated to assisting veterans and their families in accessing the full range of federal and state benefits they have earned.

### Nevada Department of Veterans Services (NDVS)

The NDVS is the central resource for all state-specific veterans' benefits, including long-term care options, financial assistance, and advocacy.

*   **Agency:** Nevada Department of Veterans Services (NDVS)
*   **Primary Contact Number:** **(775) 688-1653**
*   **Website:** [https://veterans.nv.gov/](https://veterans.nv.gov/)
*   **Key Services:**
    *   **Veterans Service Officers (VSOs):** Connecting veterans with accredited VSOs for free assistance in filing claims for federal benefits (e.g., VA Aid and Attendance, disability compensation).
    *   **State Veterans Homes:** Providing information on eligibility and admission to the **Southern Nevada State Veterans Home (Boulder City)** and the **Northern Nevada State Veterans Home (Sparks)**.
    *   **State Benefits:** Information on the **Veterans' Property Tax Exemption** and other state-level financial and educational benefits.

### The Role of the NDVS in Long-Term Care

For veterans, the NDVS is the first step toward securing long-term care. They manage the application process for the State Veterans Homes, which offer high-quality skilled nursing care. Furthermore, their VSOs are essential in helping veterans apply for the federal **Aid and Attendance Pension**, which can provide significant financial support for long-term care costs.

By keeping this list of verified authority contacts readily available, consumers are empowered to quickly access the appropriate resources for financial assistance, quality assurance, advocacy, and support.
