# Honoring Service: A Comprehensive Guide to Veterans Benefits for Long-Term Care in Ohio

Ohio has a profound commitment to its veteran population, offering a range of state and federal benefits designed to support their long-term care needs. The **Ohio Department of Veterans Services (ODVS)** is the primary state agency dedicated to connecting veterans and their families with the resources they have earned through their service. This comprehensive guide details the long-term care options available to Ohio veterans, with a focus on the state's two Veterans Homes and the crucial financial assistance programs that help cover the cost of care.

## The Ohio Veterans Homes (OVH)

Ohio operates two state-of-the-art Veterans Homes, providing high-quality, long-term care to eligible veterans. These facilities offer a continuum of care, from domiciliary to skilled nursing.

### Locations and Services

1.  **Ohio Veterans Home - Sandusky:** Located on the shores of Lake Erie, the Sandusky home is a large facility offering two levels of care:
    *   **Nursing Home Care:** Provides 24-hour skilled nursing care for veterans with complex medical needs.
    *   **Domiciliary Care:** Provides a residential setting for veterans who are capable of self-care but require some assistance and supervision.
2.  **Ohio Veterans Home - Georgetown:** Located in the southern part of the state, the Georgetown home is a smaller facility that also offers:
    *   **Nursing Home Care:** Dedicated to providing intermediate and skilled nursing care.
    *   **Domiciliary Care:** A residential option for veterans requiring a supportive living environment.

### Eligibility and Admission

Admission to the Ohio Veterans Homes is open to qualifying Ohio veterans, and the process is managed by the ODVS. The primary eligibility criteria include [1]:

*   **Veteran Status:** Must be a veteran of the United States Armed Forces.
*   **Discharge Status:** Must have an honorable discharge or a discharge under honorable conditions.
*   **Residency:** Must have been a citizen of Ohio for one year or more at the time of application.
*   **Medical Need:** Must require the level of care provided by the home (nursing or domiciliary).

The application process involves submitting a formal application, which is reviewed by an admissions team to determine eligibility and appropriate placement. While the homes are state-run, the veteran is responsible for the cost of care, which can often be covered by a combination of personal funds, Medicare, Medicaid, and VA benefits.

## Federal VA Financial Assistance for Long-Term Care

Beyond the state homes, Ohio veterans can access significant financial assistance through federal Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) programs to pay for long-term care in a variety of settings, including private nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and in-home care.

### 1. VA Aid and Attendance (A&A) Benefit

The Aid and Attendance (A&A) benefit is a special monthly pension available to wartime veterans and their surviving spouses who require the aid of another person to perform daily activities or are housebound. This benefit is non-service-connected, meaning the need for care does not have to be related to military service.

*   **Purpose:** The A&A benefit is designed to offset the high cost of long-term care. The funds can be used to pay for in-home care, assisted living, or nursing home care.
*   **Eligibility:**
    *   **Service:** Must have served at least 90 days of active duty, with at least one day during a period of war.
    *   **Financial:** Must meet certain income and asset limits, which are adjusted annually. The VA considers the applicant's unreimbursed medical expenses (including the cost of care) when determining eligibility.
    *   **Medical:** Must require assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) or meet other specific medical criteria.

### 2. VA Health Care and Community Living Centers

The VA health care system provides a range of long-term care services, often referred to as **Community Living Centers (CLCs)** (formerly known as VA nursing homes).

*   **Eligibility:** Veterans enrolled in the VA health care system may be eligible for CLC care, which provides 24/7 skilled nursing and medical care.
*   **Other Services:** The VA also offers other long-term care services, including:
    *   **Home-Based Primary Care (HBPC):** A team of health professionals provides comprehensive care in the veteran's home.
    *   **Respite Care:** Short-term care provided to a veteran to give the family caregiver a break.
    *   **Hospice and Palliative Care:** Specialized care for veterans with life-limiting illnesses.

## The Role of the Ohio Department of Veterans Services (ODVS)

The ODVS is the central hub for veterans' advocacy and resource connection in Ohio. While the ODVS does not directly administer federal VA benefits, it plays a crucial role in assisting veterans with the application process and connecting them to local support.

### County Veterans Service Offices (CVSOs)

Ohio is unique in that it has a **County Veterans Service Office (CVSO)** in all 88 counties. These offices are staffed by accredited Veterans Service Officers (VSOs) who provide invaluable, free assistance to veterans and their families.

*   **Services Provided:**
    *   **Benefit Counseling:** VSOs are experts in federal and state benefits and can advise veterans on their eligibility for A&A, VA health care, and other programs.
    *   **Application Assistance:** They assist with the preparation and submission of complex VA claims and appeals.
    *   **Financial Assistance:** Many CVSOs offer temporary financial assistance for veterans facing hardship, which can include help with medical bills or housing.

The ODVS strongly encourages all Ohio veterans to establish a relationship with their local CVSO to ensure they are maximizing all available benefits [2].

## Veterans on Medicaid

For many Ohio veterans, especially those requiring nursing home care, Medicaid is a necessary component of their long-term care plan. The **Veterans on Medicaid** program is an initiative by the Ohio Department of Medicaid (ODM) to ensure that veterans who are Medicaid recipients are also accessing all available VA benefits.

*   **Coordination of Benefits:** The program focuses on coordinating Medicaid and VA benefits, which can be complex. For example, a veteran may be eligible for both Medicaid and VA benefits, and the two programs must work together to cover the cost of care.
*   **Service-Connected Disabilities:** Veterans with service-connected disabilities may be eligible for higher levels of VA compensation, which can impact their Medicaid eligibility. The program helps navigate these interactions to ensure the veteran receives the maximum benefit from both sources [3].

## Conclusion: A Network of Support

Ohio's commitment to its veterans is evident in the comprehensive network of support for long-term care. From the state-run Ohio Veterans Homes to the crucial financial assistance provided by the federal VA A&A benefit, and the local, hands-on support of the County Veterans Service Offices, Ohio veterans have multiple avenues for securing the care they need. The key to accessing these benefits is proactive engagement with the ODVS and the local CVSOs, ensuring that no veteran is left unaware of the benefits they have earned through their service and sacrifice.

## Detailed Examination of the VA Aid and Attendance Benefit

The VA Aid and Attendance (A&A) benefit is often the most significant financial resource for veterans needing long-term care outside of the VA system. A deeper understanding of its financial and medical requirements is crucial for successful application.

### Financial Eligibility for A&A

Unlike Medicaid, the VA does not have a strict income cap, but rather a complex system that considers the veteran's income and unreimbursed medical expenses (UME).

*   **Income Calculation:** The VA calculates the applicant's **Countable Income** by taking their gross income and subtracting all UMEs. UMEs include the cost of assisted living, home health care, and other medical expenses not covered by insurance. If the Countable Income is below the Maximum Annual Pension Rate (MAPR) for the veteran's status (e.g., single veteran, married veteran, surviving spouse), they may be eligible.
*   **Asset Limit:** The VA has an asset limit, which is tied to the Medicaid Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA). In 2025, the net worth limit (assets plus gross annual income) is approximately **\$150,538** [4]. This limit is more generous than the Medicaid asset limit, making it a viable option for veterans who are not yet Medicaid-eligible.

### Medical Eligibility for A&A

To qualify for the "Aid and Attendance" level, the veteran must meet one of the following medical criteria:

1.  Require the aid of another person to perform at least two Activities of Daily Living (ADLs), such as bathing, dressing, or feeding.
2.  Be confined to the home due to a disability.
3.  Be a patient in a nursing home due to mental or physical incapacity.
4.  Have severely impaired eyesight.

The application requires a detailed report from a physician to substantiate the need for aid and attendance.

## The Indispensable Role of County Veterans Service Offices (CVSOs)

The 88 County Veterans Service Offices are the backbone of veterans' support in Ohio. Their services are not just administrative; they are deeply personal and localized.

### Localized Financial Assistance

Beyond federal benefits, many CVSOs administer a local **Veterans Relief Fund** or similar program. This fund, supported by county tax levies, provides emergency financial assistance to indigent veterans and their families. This can include:

*   **Rent and Mortgage Assistance:** Preventing homelessness.
*   **Utility Payments:** Ensuring essential services are maintained.
*   **Food and Clothing Vouchers:** Addressing immediate needs.
*   **Medical and Dental Bills:** Covering expenses not covered by other programs.

This local safety net is a crucial resource for veterans in immediate need, often bridging the gap while federal VA or Medicaid applications are being processed.

### Accreditation and Expertise

VSOs are accredited by the VA, meaning they have the legal authority to represent veterans in their claims. This expertise is vital because:

*   **Claims are Complex:** VA claims, especially for A&A or service-connected disability compensation, are notoriously complex and require specific forms, evidence, and legal arguments.
*   **Appeals Process:** If a claim is denied, the VSO can represent the veteran through the lengthy and detailed VA appeals process, significantly increasing the veteran's chance of a successful outcome.

## Conclusion: A Model of State and Local Support

Ohio's approach to veterans' long-term care is a model of coordinated state, federal, and local support. The Ohio Veterans Homes provide a direct care option, while the ODVS and the County Veterans Service Offices ensure that veterans can access the financial and medical benefits they need to choose care in the setting that best suits them. For any Ohio veteran or their family, the first and most important step is to contact their local CVSO to begin the process of securing the full range of earned benefits.
