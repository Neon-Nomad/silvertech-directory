# Delaware Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Reporting Concerns and Seeking Resolution

Delaware maintains a multi-layered system for addressing complaints related to long-term care, ensuring that residents, family members, and concerned citizens have clear pathways to report issues and seek resolution. The process is primarily managed by two key state agencies: the **Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ)** for licensed facilities and **Adult Protective Services (APS)** for vulnerable adults in community settings [1] [2].

This guide provides a detailed breakdown of the official complaint process in Delaware, including the roles of the DHCQ, APS, the Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP), and the process for reporting Medicaid fraud.

## The Primary Regulatory Authority: Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ)

The DHCQ, a division of the Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS), is the regulatory body responsible for licensing and certifying long-term care facilities in Delaware. As such, it is the primary agency for investigating complaints against licensed facilities, including nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and intermediate care facilities for individuals with intellectual disabilities.

### DHCQ Complaint Hotline and Reporting

The DHCQ operates a dedicated, toll-free line for reporting complaints and incidents in long-term care facilities. This hotline is staffed 24 hours a day, seven days a week, to ensure that serious concerns can be reported immediately.

*   **DHCQ Complaint & Incident Reporting Hotline:** **(877) 453-0012** [3]
*   **Scope of Complaints:** The DHCQ investigates complaints related to:
    *   **Quality of Care:** Issues such as inadequate wound care, medication errors, poor hygiene, and insufficient staffing.
    *   **Resident Rights Violations:** Concerns about privacy, dignity, freedom from restraints, and the right to participate in care planning.
    *   **Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation:** Allegations of physical, verbal, sexual, or mental abuse, as well as neglect (failure to provide necessary care) and financial exploitation.
    *   **Environmental Concerns:** Issues related to the safety, sanitation, and maintenance of the facility.

### The DHCQ Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, the DHCQ initiates a formal investigation process:

1.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are immediately triaged based on the severity of the allegation. Complaints alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are prioritized and require an on-site investigation within 24 hours. Less severe complaints are also investigated, typically within a short timeframe.
2.  **Unannounced Survey:** The investigation is conducted by a DHCQ survey team, which arrives at the facility unannounced. This ensures that the team observes the facility under normal operating conditions.
3.  **Fact-Finding:** The investigation involves:
    *   Interviewing the resident, family members, and staff.
    *   Reviewing the resident's medical records and care plan.
    *   Observing the care provided and the general environment of the facility.
    *   Reviewing facility policies and procedures.
4.  **Findings and Enforcement:** If the investigation substantiates the complaint, the DHCQ issues a Statement of Deficiencies (SOD) to the facility. The facility is then required to submit a Plan of Correction (POC) detailing how it will remedy the violations. Depending on the severity, the DHCQ may impose sanctions, including fines, denial of payment for new admissions, or, in severe cases, license revocation [4].

## The Role of Adult Protective Services (APS)

For vulnerable adults who are not residing in a licensed long-term care facility, the primary reporting and investigative agency is **Adult Protective Services (APS)**, which operates under the Division of Services for Aging and Adults with Physical Disabilities (DSAAPD).

### APS Reporting and Scope

APS is responsible for intervening when an adult (age 18 or older) is unable to protect themselves from abuse, neglect, exploitation, or self-neglect.

*   **APS Reporting Hotline:** **(888) 277-4302** (Toll-Free) [2]
*   **Scope of Complaints:** APS investigates situations where a vulnerable adult is:
    *   **Abused:** Physically, emotionally, or sexually harmed by another person.
    *   **Neglected:** Deprived of necessary care, food, shelter, or medical services by a caregiver.
    *   **Exploited:** Subjected to the illegal or improper use of their funds, property, or resources.
    *   **Self-Neglected:** Unable to provide for their own essential needs due to physical or mental impairment.

### APS Investigation and Intervention

The APS process is focused on intervention and providing protective services:

1.  **Assessment:** APS social workers conduct an in-depth assessment of the adult's situation, determining the nature of the risk and the adult's capacity to make decisions.
2.  **Service Planning:** If the adult is found to be at risk, APS works with the individual, family, and community resources to develop a service plan. This plan may include arranging for home-delivered meals, in-home care, medical appointments, or temporary shelter.
3.  **Legal Action:** In cases where the vulnerable adult lacks the capacity to consent to services and is in imminent danger, APS may petition the court for guardianship or other legal interventions to ensure the adult's safety.

## The Ombudsman's Role in the Complaint Ecosystem

The **Delaware Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** serves as an independent advocate and a vital resource within the complaint ecosystem. While the Ombudsman does not have regulatory authority to issue fines or revoke licenses, their role is crucial for residents.

### Advocacy and Mediation

The Ombudsman's primary function is to advocate for the resident's wishes and mediate disputes. A resident or family member can contact the Ombudsman at **(800) 223-9074** to:

*   **Discuss a Complaint:** The Ombudsman can provide confidential advice on the best way to proceed with a complaint.
*   **Mediate with the Facility:** The Ombudsman can intervene directly with facility staff and administration to resolve issues informally.
*   **Refer to the Regulator:** If a formal regulatory investigation is needed, the Ombudsman can assist the resident in filing a complaint with the DHCQ or APS.

The Ombudsman's independence ensures that residents have a non-regulatory, confidential ally throughout the complaint process.

## Reporting Fraud, Waste, and Abuse

In addition to quality of care and abuse complaints, Delaware also maintains a mechanism for reporting fraud, waste, and abuse within the Medicaid system.

### Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)

The **Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)**, housed within the Delaware Department of Justice, is responsible for investigating and prosecuting providers who defraud the Medicaid program.

*   **MFCU Hotline:** **(302) 577-5000** [5]
*   **Scope of Investigation:** The MFCU focuses on:
    *   **Provider Fraud:** Billing for services not rendered, double-billing, or providing medically unnecessary services.
    *   **Abuse and Neglect:** The MFCU also has jurisdiction to investigate and prosecute criminal cases of abuse or neglect of Medicaid recipients in healthcare facilities.

The following table summarizes the key agencies and hotlines for reporting concerns in Delaware's long-term care system:

## Conclusion

Delaware's long-term care complaint process is designed to be comprehensive, ensuring that every concern, from a minor rights violation to a serious case of abuse, has a designated pathway for investigation and resolution. By utilizing the DHCQ for regulatory enforcement, APS for community-based protection, the LTCOP for confidential advocacy, and the MFCU for criminal fraud, the state provides a robust safety net. Understanding which agency to contact based on the nature and location of the concern is the most critical step for any citizen seeking to protect the health and welfare of a vulnerable loved one.

## Mandatory Reporting Requirements and Legal Protections

Delaware law includes specific provisions designed to encourage the reporting of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults, particularly those residing in long-term care facilities. These laws provide both a legal obligation for certain professionals and protections for all reporters.

### The Duty to Report

Delaware has **mandatory reporting laws** that require certain individuals to report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult to the appropriate state agency (DHCQ or APS) [8]. This duty typically falls upon:

*   **Healthcare Professionals:** Physicians, nurses, dentists, social workers, and other licensed medical personnel.
*   **Facility Administrators and Staff:** Any employee of a long-term care facility, including CNAs, aides, and management.
*   **Law Enforcement:** Police officers and other peace officers.

Failure to report suspected abuse by a mandatory reporter can result in civil penalties and, in some cases, professional disciplinary action. This legal requirement is a critical layer of the state's protective system, ensuring that those most likely to witness abuse are legally bound to act.

### Immunity for Reporters

To ensure that individuals feel safe coming forward, Delaware law grants **immunity from civil or criminal liability** to any person who, in good faith, makes a report of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation [8]. This protection is vital, as it removes the fear of legal reprisal for those who report their concerns.

### Retaliation is Prohibited

Furthermore, state and federal regulations strictly prohibit retaliation against a resident, employee, or family member who files a complaint or cooperates with an investigation. Facilities are subject to severe penalties if they are found to have retaliated against a complainant, which reinforces the resident's right to voice concerns without fear of negative consequences.

## Transparency and Public Information: The Right to Know

A key aspect of the complaint process is the public's right to access information about facility performance and regulatory actions. This transparency empowers consumers to make informed choices about long-term care.

### Accessing DHCQ Survey Reports

The DHCQ is committed to making the results of its investigations public. After a complaint investigation or annual survey is completed, the resulting **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD)** and the facility's **Plan of Correction (POC)** become public records. Consumers can typically access these reports through the DHCQ's website or by contacting the division directly [4].

These reports provide detailed, factual information about:

*   **Specific Violations:** The exact regulation that was violated.
*   **Severity and Scope:** The seriousness of the deficiency and how widespread the problem is within the facility.
*   **Facility Response:** The steps the facility has promised to take to correct the problem and prevent its recurrence.

### The Adult Abuse Registry

As noted in the Licensing Authority section, the DHCQ maintains the **Adult Abuse Registry**, which is a public resource that lists individuals who have been civilly found to have abused, neglected, or exploited a vulnerable adult [9]. This registry is a direct outcome of the investigative process and serves as a critical public safety tool, preventing individuals with a history of misconduct from gaining employment in long-term care settings.

## The Intersection of Complaints and Quality Improvement

The complaint process in Delaware is not solely punitive; it is also a vital mechanism for quality improvement. Every substantiated complaint provides the DHCQ and the facility with data on where care processes are failing.

### Systemic Change

The DHCQ uses complaint data to identify systemic issues across the state. For example, if multiple facilities are receiving complaints about medication errors, the DHCQ may launch a targeted, statewide initiative to review and improve medication administration protocols.

### Facility-Level Quality Assurance

Facilities are required to have a Quality Assurance and Performance Improvement (QAPI) program. The findings from DHCQ investigations are a mandatory input into this program. This means that a complaint, once substantiated, must lead to a formal review of the facility's policies and procedures to ensure the root cause of the problem is identified and corrected, thereby preventing future occurrences.

## Conclusion

The Delaware long-term care complaint process is a robust, multi-agency effort that provides a comprehensive safety net for vulnerable adults. From the 24-hour regulatory hotline of the DHCQ to the protective intervention of APS and the confidential advocacy of the LTCOP, multiple avenues exist for reporting concerns. The state's commitment to mandatory reporting, legal protections for reporters, and public transparency ensures that the system is not only responsive to individual complaints but also drives continuous quality improvement across the entire long-term care sector. By understanding these pathways, Delaware citizens can effectively utilize the system to protect their loved ones and hold facilities accountable for the quality of care they provide.
