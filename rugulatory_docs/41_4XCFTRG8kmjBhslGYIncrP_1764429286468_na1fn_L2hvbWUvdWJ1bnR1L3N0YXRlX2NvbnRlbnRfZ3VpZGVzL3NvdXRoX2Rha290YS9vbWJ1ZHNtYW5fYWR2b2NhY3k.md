# South Dakota Long-Term Care Ombudsman and Advocacy Programs

## A Voice for Vulnerable Residents: Protecting Rights and Ensuring Quality of Care

For residents of long-term care facilities in South Dakota, navigating the complexities of care, resident rights, and facility operations can be challenging. The **South Dakota Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)**, housed within the Department of Human Services (DHS), serves as a critical, independent advocate for these vulnerable individuals [1]. Alongside the LTCOP, organizations like **Disability Rights South Dakota (DRSD)** provide essential legal-based advocacy, ensuring that the rights of all residents are protected.

This comprehensive guide explores the mission, services, and scope of the LTCOP and other key advocacy resources in South Dakota. It is designed to empower residents, their families, and caregivers with the knowledge needed to address concerns, resolve complaints, and ensure a high quality of life and care in long-term care settings.

***

## The South Dakota Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The LTCOP is a federally mandated, state-operated program dedicated to protecting the health, safety, welfare, and rights of residents in long-term care facilities. The program operates with a philosophy of **resident-focused advocacy**, meaning the Ombudsman works to resolve issues based on the wishes of the resident.

### Mission and Core Principles

The core mission of the South Dakota LTCOP is to improve the quality of life and care for all residents of long-term care facilities. The program is guided by several key principles [2]:

*   **Confidentiality:** All reports and inquiries are strictly confidential. The Ombudsman will not disclose the identity of a resident or complainant without their express permission.
*   **Independence:** The program operates independently of the licensing and regulatory agencies, ensuring that its primary focus remains on advocacy rather than enforcement.
*   **Empowerment:** The Ombudsman seeks to empower residents to resolve concerns on their own behalf whenever possible, providing them with the information and support needed to assert their rights.

### Services Provided by the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman acts as a mediator, investigator, and educator. The services provided are multifaceted and include:

| Service Category | Description of Ombudsman Activities |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Complaint Resolution** | Investigating and resolving complaints made by or on behalf of residents regarding quality of care, resident rights, abuse, neglect, or facility conditions. |
| **Information and Referral** | Providing information to residents, families, and the public about long-term care services, resident rights, and how to find quality care. |
| **Education and Training** | Educating residents, facility staff, and the public about resident rights, legislative changes, and best practices in long-term care. |
| **Systems Advocacy** | Working with state agencies and the legislature to recommend changes to regulations and laws that affect the health, safety, and rights of residents. |
| **Facility Access** | Visiting facilities regularly to talk with residents, observe conditions, and monitor the quality of care. |
| **Council Support** | Assisting in the development and maintenance of resident and family councils, which are essential forums for resident self-advocacy. |

### Facilities Covered by the LTCOP

The South Dakota LTCOP provides services to residents in a wide range of long-term care settings, including [3]:

*   **Nursing Facilities (NFs):** Facilities providing 24-hour skilled nursing care.
*   **Assisted Living Centers (ALCs):** Facilities providing supportive services and personal care.
*   **Licensed Adult Foster Care Homes:** Small, residential settings providing care for a limited number of adults.
*   **Community Living Homes:** Residential settings providing services to individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities.

### Common Issues Addressed by the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman handles a broad spectrum of issues, with common complaints often revolving around:

*   **Resident Rights Violations:** Denial of the right to privacy, the right to refuse treatment, or the right to manage one's own finances.
*   **Quality of Care:** Concerns related to inadequate personal hygiene, improper medication administration, or insufficient pain management.
*   **Transfer and Discharge:** Disputes over involuntary transfers or discharges from a facility, including failure to provide proper notice or a safe transition.
*   **Dignity and Respect:** Issues concerning staff attitudes, lack of respect for personal preferences, or failure to address grievances promptly.
*   **Dietary Concerns:** Complaints about the quality, variety, or nutritional adequacy of meals.

## Advocacy for Individuals with Disabilities: Disability Rights South Dakota (DRSD)

**Disability Rights South Dakota (DRSD)** is the federally mandated Protection and Advocacy (P&A) system for the state [4]. The P&A network is the largest provider of legal advocacy services for people with disabilities in the United States. DRSD’s mission is to protect and advocate for the rights of South Dakotans with disabilities.

### The Role of the P&A System

DRSD provides legal-based services to ensure that individuals with disabilities are free from abuse, neglect, exploitation, and discrimination. Their advocacy extends to various settings, including long-term care facilities, where they work to ensure residents with disabilities receive appropriate services and are not subjected to rights violations.

### Key Advocacy Areas for DRSD

DRSD’s work is crucial for long-term care residents who have a qualifying disability. Their advocacy focuses on:

*   **Abuse and Neglect:** Investigating incidents of abuse and neglect in facilities and pursuing legal remedies.
*   **Access to Services:** Ensuring residents have access to necessary medical, social, and rehabilitative services, including those provided through Medicaid waivers.
*   **Community Integration:** Advocating for the right of individuals to live in the most integrated setting appropriate to their needs, often challenging unnecessary institutionalization.
*   **Discrimination:** Addressing discrimination in housing, employment, and access to public services.

While the LTCOP focuses specifically on facility residents, DRSD provides a broader legal and systemic advocacy framework for the disability community, often collaborating with the Ombudsman on cases involving residents with disabilities.

## Resident Rights in South Dakota Long-Term Care

Both the LTCOP and DRSD rely heavily on the established rights afforded to residents under state and federal law. South Dakota has specific administrative rules that outline the **Bill of Rights** for residents in nursing homes and assisted living centers.

### Nursing Facility Resident Rights

Nursing facility residents are protected by extensive federal regulations (42 CFR Part 483) and state rules, which guarantee rights such as:

*   **The Right to Dignity and Respect:** To be treated with consideration, respect, and full recognition of their dignity and individuality.
*   **The Right to Participate in Care:** To participate in the planning of their care and treatment, including the right to refuse treatment.
*   **The Right to Privacy:** To have privacy in their medical treatment, personal care, and communications.
*   **The Right to Voice Grievances:** To voice complaints without fear of reprisal and to have those complaints promptly addressed.
*   **The Right to Self-Determination:** To choose their own schedules, activities, and healthcare providers.

### Assisted Living Center Resident Rights

Residents of Assisted Living Centers are protected by **ARSD 44:70:09**, which outlines their rights, including [5]:

*   The right to be treated with consideration, respect, and full recognition of their dignity and individuality.
*   The right to be free from mental and physical abuse, neglect, and exploitation.
*   The right to receive a written contract or agreement detailing services and charges.
*   The right to manage their own financial affairs.
*   The right to privacy in their room, communications, and visits.

The Ombudsman program provides direct access to the relevant state administrative rules, such as **Chapter 44:73:11** for nursing homes and **Chapter 44:70:09** for assisted living centers, to ensure residents and families can review their specific rights [6].

## How to File a Complaint and Seek Advocacy

Seeking assistance from the Ombudsman or DRSD is a confidential and straightforward process.

### Contacting the Long-Term Care Ombudsman

The LTCOP is the primary resource for resolving facility-level complaints.

**Steps to Contact the Ombudsman:**

1.  **Gather Information:** Collect all relevant details, including the resident's name, the facility name, the date(s) of the incident(s), and a clear description of the concern.
2.  **Contact the Ombudsman:** Call the toll-free number or send an email.
3.  **Provide Consent:** If the complaint is made by a family member or friend, the Ombudsman will require the resident's consent to investigate, due to strict confidentiality rules.
4.  **Investigation and Resolution:** The Ombudsman will investigate the complaint, which may involve visiting the facility, interviewing the resident and staff, and reviewing records. They will then work with all parties to achieve a resolution that is satisfactory to the resident.

## Conclusion

The South Dakota Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program and Disability Rights South Dakota are indispensable resources for ensuring that long-term care residents are treated with dignity, respect, and receive the quality of care they deserve. The LTCOP provides a direct, confidential channel for resolving complaints and advocating for individual residents, while DRSD offers powerful legal advocacy for the rights of the disability community. Families and residents should view these programs not as a last resort, but as essential partners in maintaining a high standard of care and quality of life in South Dakota's long-term care facilities.
