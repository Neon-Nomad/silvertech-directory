# Connecticut Veterans Benefits: Long-Term Care and Support Services

The state of Connecticut is committed to providing comprehensive support and long-term care services to its veterans, their spouses, and eligible dependents. The primary state agency responsible for administering these benefits is the **Connecticut Department of Veterans Affairs (DVA)**. The DVA offers a range of programs, from residential and skilled nursing care at the State Veterans Home to financial assistance and advocacy services.

## The Connecticut State Veterans Home at Rocky Hill

The centerpiece of Connecticut's state-level long-term care for veterans is the **Connecticut State Veterans Home** located in Rocky Hill. This facility is unique in that it offers a continuum of care, including a residential program focused on rehabilitation and a skilled nursing unit for veterans requiring 24-hour medical care.

### 1. The Healthcare Center (Skilled Nursing Care)

The Healthcare Center at the Rocky Hill campus provides comprehensive, long-term skilled nursing care for veterans who require a high level of medical attention.

#### Admission Requirements for the Healthcare Center:

To be considered for admission to the DVA Healthcare Center, a veteran must meet the following criteria [1]:

*   **Service Requirement:** Must have served in the Armed Forces of the United States and received an **Honorable Discharge** or a **General Discharge Under Honorable Conditions**.
*   **Residency:** Must be a resident of the State of Connecticut at the time of application.
*   **Medical Need:** Must require **twenty-four hour long-term skilled nursing care**, as determined by a medical evaluation.
*   **Application:** A formal application, including a report of medical history and current condition, must be submitted to the DVA.

#### Services Provided:

The Healthcare Center is a licensed and certified skilled nursing facility that provides a full range of services, including:

*   24-hour skilled nursing and medical care.
*   Physical, occupational, and speech therapy.
*   Hospice and palliative care.
*   Nutritional services and dietary counseling.
*   Social services and recreational activities.

### 2. The Residential Facility (Temporary Housing and Rehabilitation)

The Residential Facility is designed for veterans who are capable of performing their daily activities of living (ADLs) independently but are in need of temporary housing, rehabilitation, and support services to transition back to independent living.

#### Eligibility for the Residential Facility:

*   **Service Requirement:** Must have received an Honorable Discharge or a General Discharge Under Honorable Conditions.
*   **Functional Ability:** Must be able to perform regular activities of daily living without assistance, such as bathing, eating, and dressing [2].
*   **Focus:** The program is focused on rehabilitation, vocational training, and social reintegration.

#### Services Provided:

The Residential Facility offers a structured environment with programs aimed at self-sufficiency, including:

*   Temporary housing and meals.
*   Substance abuse recovery programs (often a dedicated unit).
*   Vocational and educational counseling.
*   Case management and social work services.
*   Access to the campus's recreational and therapeutic facilities.

## State Financial and Tax Benefits for Veterans

Beyond the State Veterans Home, Connecticut offers several financial benefits that can indirectly support a veteran's long-term care planning and financial stability.

### Property Tax Exemptions

Connecticut law provides significant property tax exemptions for veterans who served during a time of war, their spouses, and Gold Star parents.

*   **Basic Exemption:** Veterans who served during a statutory period of war are eligible for a minimum property tax exemption of **$1,000** on the assessed value of their property [3].
*   **Additional Exemptions:** Veterans with a service-connected disability are eligible for increased exemptions, which can range from $2,000 to $10,000, depending on the percentage of their disability rating from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA).
*   **Income-Based Exemptions:** Veterans who meet certain income limits are eligible for an additional exemption, which can further reduce their property tax burden.

### Financial Assistance Programs

The DVA administers several funds and programs designed to provide direct financial aid to veterans and their families facing hardship.

*   **Aid and Assistance Fund:** This fund provides temporary financial assistance for eligible veterans and their families who are experiencing financial distress. Funds can be used for essential needs, including medical expenses, housing, and utility payments, which can be crucial in a long-term care crisis.
*   **Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF):** While a federal program, it is administered by local agencies in Connecticut and provides case management and temporary financial assistance to very low-income veteran families who are homeless or at risk of becoming homeless [4].

## Federal Benefits for Long-Term Care

Connecticut veterans are also eligible for a range of federal benefits administered by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), which can be utilized for long-term care.

### VA Health Care and Community Care

Veterans enrolled in the VA health care system may be eligible for various long-term care services, which can be provided in VA facilities or through community providers under the VA's Community Care program. These services include:

*   **Home-Based Primary Care (HBPC):** Health care services provided to veterans in their homes.
*   **Skilled Home Health Care:** Short-term, skilled services like nursing or physical therapy.
*   **Adult Day Health Care:** Health care and rehabilitative services in a supervised, non-residential setting.
*   **Community Living Centers (VA Nursing Homes):** Long-term care provided in VA-owned and operated facilities.

### Veterans Pension with Aid and Attendance (A&A)

The **Veterans Pension** program provides monthly payments to low-income wartime veterans. The **Aid and Attendance (A&A)** benefit is an increased monthly payment available to veterans who qualify for the basic pension and require the aid of another person to perform daily activities, or who are housebound.

*   **Purpose:** The A&A benefit is non-service-connected and is specifically designed to help offset the costs of long-term care, including in-home care, assisted living, or nursing home care.
*   **Eligibility:** Requires a wartime service period, specific income and asset limits, and a medical need for assistance with ADLs.
