# Connecticut Long-Term Care Complaint Process: How to Report Concerns and Ensure Quality Care

The state of Connecticut maintains a robust system for receiving, investigating, and resolving complaints regarding the quality of care and services provided in long-term care facilities. The primary authority for investigating regulatory violations is the **Connecticut Department of Public Health (DPH)**, specifically its **Facility Licensing and Investigations Section (FLIS)**. Additionally, the **Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** serves as a confidential advocate for residents, offering an alternative and often preferred route for complaint resolution.

## The Department of Public Health (DPH) Complaint Process

The DPH FLIS is the state's official regulatory body responsible for ensuring that licensed healthcare facilities comply with state and federal regulations. Complaints filed with DPH are treated as allegations of regulatory non-compliance and can lead to formal investigations, citations, and penalties.

### Facilities Covered by DPH FLIS

The DPH FLIS investigates complaints against all licensed healthcare facilities, including:

*   **Nursing Homes (Skilled Nursing Facilities)**
*   **Residential Care Homes (RCHs)**
*   **Assisted Living Services Agencies (ALSAs)**
*   **Home Health Agencies**
*   **Hospitals and other licensed facilities**

### How to File a Complaint with DPH FLIS

Anyone—including residents, family members, facility staff, or the general public—can file a complaint with DPH FLIS.

1.  **Contact the Complaint Hotline:** The most direct way to file a complaint is by calling the DPH Healthcare Quality and Safety Branch.
    *   **DPH FLIS Complaint Hotline:** **(800) 828-9769** (Toll-Free) or **(860) 509-7400** (Local) [1].
2.  **Submit a Written Complaint:** Complaints can also be submitted in writing via mail or online through the DPH website. A written complaint ensures all details are accurately recorded. The complaint should include:
    *   The name and address of the facility.
    *   The name of the resident (if applicable and permission is given).
    *   A detailed description of the incident or concern, including dates and times.
    *   The names of any witnesses.
    *   The name and contact information of the complainant (though complaints can be filed anonymously).
3.  **Anonymity and Confidentiality:** Complainants can choose to remain anonymous. If the complainant provides their contact information, DPH will keep their identity confidential and will not disclose it to the facility without the complainant's express written consent.

### The DPH Investigation Procedure

Once a complaint is received, DPH FLIS follows a structured investigation process:

1.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are assessed for severity and immediacy. Complaints alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are investigated within **two working days** [2]. Less severe complaints are typically investigated within 45 days.
2.  **On-Site Investigation:** A team of DPH surveyors (which may include nurses, social workers, and other specialists) conducts an unannounced visit to the facility. The investigation involves:
    *   Interviewing the resident, family, and staff.
    *   Reviewing medical records, facility policies, and staffing schedules.
    *   Observing the care provided and the facility environment.
3.  **Findings and Citations:** If the investigation confirms that a violation of state or federal regulations occurred, the facility is issued a **Statement of Deficiencies (Form CMS-2567)**, commonly known as a citation.
4.  **Plan of Correction (POC):** The facility is required to submit a written Plan of Correction detailing the steps it will take to remedy the deficiencies and prevent recurrence.
5.  **Follow-Up and Enforcement:** DPH reviews the POC and conducts a follow-up visit to ensure the corrective actions have been implemented. Depending on the severity and scope of the deficiencies, DPH may impose sanctions, including civil monetary penalties, denial of payment for new admissions, or, in severe cases, license revocation.
6.  **Notification:** The complainant is notified of the outcome of the investigation, including a summary of the findings and any enforcement actions taken.

## The Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP) as an Advocate

While DPH focuses on regulatory compliance, the **Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** focuses on advocacy and mediation. The Ombudsman is often the preferred first point of contact for residents and families because the process is confidential, resident-centered, and aimed at resolving issues quickly and informally.

### When to Contact the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman should be contacted for issues related to:

*   **Resident Rights Violations:** Concerns about dignity, privacy, freedom of choice, or the right to refuse treatment.
*   **Quality of Life Issues:** Problems with food, activities, laundry, or the general environment.
*   **Communication Problems:** Disputes with facility staff or management that require mediation.
*   **Financial Concerns:** Issues related to personal funds management or billing.

### How to Contact the LTCOP

*   **LTCOP Toll-Free Hotline:** **(866) 388-1888** [3].
*   **Website:** [https://portal.ct.gov/ltcop](https://portal.ct.gov/ltcop)

The Ombudsman will work directly with the resident and the facility to find a satisfactory resolution. If the issue is a serious regulatory violation, the Ombudsman will advise the complainant to also file a complaint with DPH FLIS and can assist them in doing so.

## Complaint Process for Assisted Living Services Agencies (ALSAs)

Connecticut's unique model for assisted living means that complaints are often directed at the licensed **Assisted Living Services Agency (ALSA)** rather than the residential community itself.

*   **DPH FLIS:** The DPH FLIS has the authority to investigate complaints against the ALSA regarding the quality and delivery of personal care and supportive services.
*   **LTCOP:** The Ombudsman program also advocates for residents receiving services from an ALSA within a Managed Residential Community (MRC).

Residents of assisted living facilities have a **Residents' Bill of Rights** which explicitly grants them the right to register complaints with the Commissioner of Public Health [4].

## Other Key Complaint and Reporting Resources

In addition to DPH and the LTCOP, several other agencies play a role in protecting vulnerable adults in Connecticut:

| Agency/Program | Focus Area | Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adult Protective Services (APS)** | Investigation of abuse, neglect, exploitation, or abandonment of vulnerable adults (ages 18-59) and elders (age 60+) living in the community. | **(888) 447-3339** (Toll-Free) |
| **Office of the Attorney General** | Consumer protection issues, including deceptive practices or fraud by long-term care providers. | **(860) 808-5318** |
| **Connecticut State Police** | Criminal matters, such as physical assault, sexual abuse, or theft. | **911** or local police department |
| **Connecticut Department of Social Services (DSS)** | Complaints related to Medicaid (HUSKY Health) benefits, eligibility, or billing. | **(800) 859-9889** |

## Summary of Complaint Pathways

| Concern Type | Recommended First Contact | Regulatory Authority | Outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Resident Rights, Quality of Life, Mediation** | **Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** | LTCOP (Advocacy) | Confidential resolution, mediation, and advocacy for the resident. |
| **Regulatory Violations, Health & Safety** | **DPH FLIS Complaint Hotline** | DPH FLIS (Licensing/Certification) | Formal investigation, issuance of citations, and enforcement actions. |
| **Abuse, Neglect, Exploitation (Community)** | **Adult Protective Services (APS)** | DSS/Local Police | Investigation of protective service needs and intervention. |
| **Criminal Activity (Assault, Theft)** | **Local Police/State Police** | Law Enforcement | Criminal investigation and prosecution. |
