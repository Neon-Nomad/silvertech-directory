# Washington State Long-Term Care Licensing and Regulation: A Comprehensive Guide

The licensing and regulation of long-term care facilities in Washington State is a complex, two-tiered system involving two primary state agencies: the **Department of Health (DOH)** and the **Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS)**. This dual oversight ensures that facilities providing different levels of care—from skilled nursing to residential assistance—meet stringent state and federal standards for safety, quality of care, and resident rights.

This comprehensive guide details the roles of these agencies, the specific regulations governing each type of long-term care setting, and the critical standards facilities must meet to operate legally in Washington.

## The Dual Regulatory Landscape

In Washington, the type of facility dictates which state agency is responsible for its licensing and oversight.

1.  **Department of Health (DOH):** The DOH, specifically its **Health Systems Quality Assurance Division (HSQA)**, is responsible for licensing and certifying **Nursing Homes** (also known as Skilled Nursing Facilities). This is the agency that ensures compliance with both state and federal (Medicare/Medicaid) regulations [1].
2.  **Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS):** The DSHS, through its **Aging and Long-Term Support Administration (ALTSA)**, is responsible for licensing and regulating **Assisted Living Facilities (ALFs)** and **Adult Family Homes (AFHs)**. These facilities provide residential care and services, but not the 24-hour skilled nursing care found in nursing homes [2].

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nursing Homes (Skilled Nursing Facilities)** | Department of Health (DOH) | Revised Code of Washington (RCW) 18.51; Washington Administrative Code (WAC) Title 388, Chapter 97 |
| **Assisted Living Facilities (ALFs)** | Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS) | RCW 70.129; WAC Title 388, Chapter 78A |
| **Adult Family Homes (AFHs)** | Department of Social and Health Services (DSHS) | RCW 70.128; WAC Title 388, Chapter 76 |

## Nursing Home Licensing and Oversight (DOH)

Nursing homes in Washington provide the highest level of long-term care outside of a hospital, offering 24-hour skilled nursing, rehabilitation, and medical services. The DOH's oversight is rigorous, focusing on compliance with the federal requirements necessary for Medicare and Medicaid certification.

### Governing Regulations and Standards

The primary state regulations for nursing homes are found in the **Revised Code of Washington (RCW) 18.51** and the **Washington Administrative Code (WAC) 388-97** [3]. These codes cover a vast array of operational standards, including:

*   **Licensure Requirements:** Detailed procedures for initial licensing, renewal, and change of ownership [4].
*   **Resident Rights:** Mandating the protection of residents' dignity, self-determination, and freedom from abuse or neglect.
*   **Quality of Care:** Standards for physician services, pharmaceutical services, dietary services, and specialized rehabilitative services.
*   **Infection Control:** Strict protocols for preventing and controlling the spread of infections.

### Staffing Requirements

Staffing is a critical component of nursing home quality. Washington state regulations, in conjunction with federal requirements, mandate minimum staffing levels to ensure adequate care.

*   **Minimum Staffing Standard:** WAC 388-97-1090 requires nursing homes to ensure a sufficient number of qualified nursing personnel are available 24 hours a day, seven days a week [5]. Crucially, the state mandates a minimum of **3.4 hours of direct care per resident day (HPRD)**, which includes the time provided by Registered Nurses (RNs), Licensed Practical Nurses (LPNs), and Certified Nursing Assistants (CNAs) [6].
*   **Enforcement:** A nursing home that fails to meet the minimum 3.4 HPRD requirement for any quarter is subject to fines and other enforcement actions [6].

### Inspection and Survey Process

The DOH conducts regular, unannounced inspections, known as surveys, to monitor compliance.

*   **Frequency:** Federal law requires that every Medicare/Medicaid-certified nursing home receive a standard survey **at least once every 15 months**, with the average interval not exceeding 12 months [7]. Washington's DOH adheres to this schedule.
*   **Scope:** The survey team, composed of nurses, social workers, and other professionals, reviews resident care plans, observes care delivery, interviews residents and staff, and examines facility records.
*   **Complaint Investigations:** The DOH also conducts unannounced complaint investigations in response to allegations of abuse, neglect, or regulatory violations [1].

## Assisted Living Facility (ALF) Oversight (DSHS)

Assisted Living Facilities (ALFs) provide housing, support services, and personal care to residents who need assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) but do not require 24-hour skilled nursing care. DSHS is the licensing authority for ALFs.

### Governing Regulations and Standards

ALFs are regulated under **RCW 70.129** and **WAC 388-78A** [8]. The regulations emphasize resident choice, dignity, and the provision of services tailored to individual needs.

*   **Scope of Care:** ALFs must admit or retain only those individuals whose needs they can safely and appropriately serve. They are required to provide services such as medication management, personal care, and assistance with self-administration of medication [9].
*   **Service Agreements:** Each resident must have a written service agreement that details the services to be provided, the cost, and the resident's rights and responsibilities.
*   **Staffing and Training:** WAC 388-78A-2450 requires ALFs to provide sufficient, trained staff to furnish the services and care needed by each resident [10]. Staff must meet specific training requirements, including specialized training for residents with dementia or mental health needs [11].

### Inspection and Monitoring

DSHS monitors ALFs through a combination of initial licensing inspections, renewal inspections, and complaint investigations.

*   **Inspections:** DSHS conducts unannounced inspections to ensure compliance with WAC 388-78A. These inspections review staffing levels, training records, service plans, and the overall environment of the facility.
*   **Enforcement:** If an ALF is found to be out of compliance, DSHS enforcement actions can range from civil fines and stop-placement orders (prohibiting new admissions) to summary suspension or revocation of the facility's license [12].

## Adult Family Home (AFH) Oversight (DSHS)

Adult Family Homes (AFHs) are residential homes licensed to care for a small number of non-related residents, typically up to six [13]. They offer a more home-like setting than larger institutional facilities while providing room, board, and necessary supervision and personal care. DSHS is also the licensing authority for AFHs.

### Governing Regulations and Standards

AFHs are regulated under **RCW 70.128** and **WAC 388-76** [14]. The regulations are designed to maintain the residential character of the home while ensuring professional care standards.

*   **Capacity and Environment:** The small capacity is a defining feature, intended to foster a more intimate and personalized care environment. The physical environment must be safe, clean, and accessible.
*   **Caregiver Qualifications:** The AFH licensee must have a minimum of 80 hours of training in adult family home care before the license is issued [15]. Caregivers must be certified as Home Care Aides (HCAs) unless they hold an exemption (e.g., RN, LPN, or CNA) [16].
*   **Staffing:** WAC 388-76-10200 requires that at least one qualified caregiver be present in the home whenever one or more residents are present [17].

### Inspection and Monitoring

DSHS's oversight of AFHs is crucial due to their small, decentralized nature.

*   **Inspections:** DSHS conducts inspections to ensure compliance with WAC 388-76. These inspections focus on the quality of care, the safety of the environment, and the qualifications of the staff.
*   **Stop Placement Orders:** A common enforcement tool for AFHs is the **Stop Placement Order**, which prevents the home from accepting new residents until identified deficiencies are corrected [18].

## Enforcement Actions and Public Information

Both DOH and DSHS maintain public records of enforcement actions taken against licensed facilities. This transparency is vital for consumers researching long-term care options.

### DOH Enforcement (Nursing Homes)

The DOH issues civil fines, conditional licenses, and can ultimately suspend or revoke a nursing home's license for serious or repeated violations of state and federal regulations [19]. The DOH Newsroom often publishes summaries of disciplinary actions against health care facilities and providers [20].

### DSHS Enforcement (ALFs and AFHs)

DSHS enforcement actions against ALFs and AFHs include:

*   **Civil Fines:** Monetary penalties for specific violations.
*   **Stop Placement Orders:** Prohibiting the facility from admitting new residents.
*   **Summary Suspension/Revocation:** For severe violations that pose an immediate threat to resident health or safety, DSHS can summarily suspend a license, followed by a process for permanent revocation [21].

Consumers can access detailed inspection reports, complaint investigation findings, and enforcement actions for both nursing homes (via the federal Medicare Care Compare website and DOH) and for ALFs and AFHs (via the DSHS website) [22].

## Conclusion

Washington State's long-term care licensing system is designed to protect vulnerable residents by establishing clear, enforceable standards for all care settings. The DOH's focus on the medical and skilled care standards of nursing homes, combined with the DSHS's oversight of the residential and personal care provided by Assisted Living Facilities and Adult Family Homes, creates a comprehensive regulatory framework. Understanding the specific regulations (WACs) and the roles of these two key agencies is essential for both providers seeking to comply and consumers seeking quality care.
