# The KanCare System: A Comprehensive Guide to Kansas Medicaid Programs

The state of Kansas administers its Medicaid program under the name **KanCare**, a comprehensive, managed care model that delivers healthcare services to eligible residents across the state [1]. KanCare is a critical component of the state's healthcare infrastructure, providing essential medical, behavioral health, and long-term services and supports (LTSS) to a diverse population, including children, pregnant women, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities [2]. This guide provides an in-depth exploration of the KanCare system, focusing particularly on its structure, eligibility requirements, and the vital Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) waivers that support long-term care, such as the Frail Elderly (FE) Waiver.

## Understanding the KanCare Managed Care Model

KanCare operates under a managed care system, meaning the state contracts with private Managed Care Organizations (MCOs) to deliver healthcare services to beneficiaries. This model is designed to improve health outcomes, enhance care coordination, and control costs by shifting the responsibility of service delivery to the MCOs [2].

### The Three Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)

As of the current system, KanCare is managed by three distinct MCOs. Beneficiaries are required to choose one of these plans, and each MCO is responsible for coordinating all of the member's covered services, including physical health, behavioral health, and long-term care [1].

| Managed Care Organization (MCO) | Key Contact Information | Value-Added Services (General Examples) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Healthy Blue** | 1-833-838-2593 | Wellness programs, extra vision and dental benefits, over-the-counter allowances [7] |
| **Sunflower Health Plan** | 1-877-644-4623 | Transportation assistance, health education, incentives for healthy behaviors [7] |
| **United HealthCare** | 1-877-542-9238 | Fitness memberships, healthy food benefits, and care coordination services [7] |

The MCOs compete on the basis of quality and value-added services, offering beneficiaries a choice and encouraging innovation in service delivery. While the core medical benefits are the same across all three plans, the "value-added" services can vary significantly, making the selection process an important decision for new enrollees [7].

### Core Services Covered by KanCare

KanCare provides a robust package of benefits that covers a wide range of necessary medical services. These services are consistent across all three MCOs and are mandated by the state and federal Medicaid guidelines [1].

*   **Primary and Specialty Care:** Doctor’s office visits, check-ups, and access to specialists.
*   **Hospital Services:** Inpatient and outpatient hospital care.
*   **Prescription Drugs:** Coverage for necessary medications through a formulary.
*   **Behavioral Health:** Mental health and substance use disorder services.
*   **Preventive Care:** Vaccines, screenings, and routine check-ups.
*   **Dental and Vision:** Comprehensive dental care for children and limited adult dental benefits, along with eye doctor visits.
*   **Transportation:** Non-emergency medical transportation to and from appointments.
*   **Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS):** Including nursing facility services and HCBS waivers.

## Eligibility for KanCare

Eligibility for KanCare is determined by the Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS) and the Kansas Department of Health and Environment (KDHE), based on federal and state guidelines [2]. Eligibility is generally categorized by population group, with specific income and resource limits for each group.

### Key Eligibility Groups

1.  **Children and Families:** Includes children under 19, pregnant women, and non-disabled/non-elderly parents or caretaker relatives.
2.  **Elderly and Persons with Disabilities:** Individuals aged 65 or older, or those with a disability, who meet specific medical and financial criteria. This group is the primary focus for long-term care services.
3.  **Other Groups:** Includes individuals receiving Supplemental Security Income (SSI) and those who qualify under specific breast and cervical cancer treatment programs.

### Financial and Functional Eligibility

For the elderly and disabled seeking long-term care, two types of eligibility must be met:

*   **Financial Eligibility:** Applicants must meet strict income and asset limits, which are often tied to the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) or specific Medicaid-defined limits. For those whose income exceeds the limit, Kansas has a "Medically Needy" program (also known as a spend-down) that allows them to qualify after incurring medical expenses that reduce their countable income [3].
*   **Functional Eligibility:** This is the medical requirement, which assesses the applicant's need for a nursing facility level of care. An assessment is conducted by the Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) to determine if the individual requires the level of assistance provided in a nursing home setting [3].

## The Frail Elderly (FE) Waiver: An Alternative to Nursing Home Care

The **Frail Elderly (FE) Waiver** is Kansas' primary Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) program designed to provide long-term care to seniors in their own homes or in community settings, such as assisted living facilities, as an alternative to institutional nursing home care [3]. This waiver is crucial for promoting independence and quality of life for Kansas seniors.

### FE Waiver Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible for the FE Waiver, an individual must meet three core criteria [3]:

1.  **Age Requirement:** Must be 65 years of age or older.
2.  **Functional Need:** Must meet the Medicaid nursing facility threshold score, indicating a need for a nursing home level of care.
3.  **Financial Status:** Must be financially eligible for Medicaid (KanCare).

### Services Provided Under the FE Waiver

The FE Waiver covers a comprehensive list of services aimed at supporting daily living and health management in a home or community setting. These services are tailored to the individual's needs as determined by a person-centered service plan [3].

| FE Waiver Service Category | Description of Support |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Care Services** | Assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, grooming, and mobility. |
| **Adult Day Care** | Structured programs in a community setting that provide social, recreational, and health-related services during the day. |
| **Comprehensive Support** | A flexible service that can include various forms of non-medical assistance and support. |
| **Home and Environmental Modification** | Changes to the home (e.g., ramps, grab bars) to ensure the individual's safety and accessibility. |
| **Vehicle Modification Services** | Modifications to a personal vehicle to accommodate a disability. |
| **Personal Emergency Response System (PERS)** | Electronic device that allows the individual to call for help in an emergency. |
| **Specialized Medical Equipment and Supplies** | Items necessary for health and safety that are not covered by regular Medicaid. |
| **Financial Management Services** | Assistance with managing the budget and payments for self-directed care options. |
| **Nursing Evaluation Visit** | Periodic visits by a registered nurse to assess health status and monitor care needs. |
| **Oral Health Services** | Dental care services beyond what is covered by standard KanCare. |
| **Enhanced Care Services** | Additional services designed to meet complex needs. |
| **Wellness Monitoring** | Services to monitor health indicators and provide early intervention. |

### The Application Process for the FE Waiver

The application process for the FE Waiver is managed through the Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) [3].

1.  **Initial Contact:** The applicant or their representative contacts the ADRC at **1-855-200-ADRC (2372)** to request a FE waiver or learn about LTSS options.
2.  **Functional Assessment:** The ADRC conducts a functional eligibility assessment to determine if the applicant meets the nursing facility level of care threshold.
3.  **Financial Application:** The applicant applies for Medicaid (KanCare) financial eligibility through the KanCare Clearinghouse.
4.  **Enrollment:** Once both functional and financial eligibility are confirmed, the individual is enrolled in the FE Waiver program and selects one of the three MCOs. The chosen MCO then works with the member to develop a person-centered plan of care.

## The Role of the Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS)

The Kansas Department for Aging and Disability Services (KDADS) plays a central and critical role in the administration of KanCare and its HCBS waivers [3]. KDADS is the state agency responsible for overseeing the long-term care system, including the licensing of adult care homes and the management of the HCBS programs.

### KDADS and HCBS Oversight

KDADS is the single state agency designated to administer the HCBS programs, including the FE Waiver. This oversight includes:

*   **Policy Development:** Creating and implementing the rules and regulations governing the waivers.
*   **Program Management:** Ensuring the waivers operate within federal guidelines and meet the needs of Kansans.
*   **Quality Assurance:** Monitoring the quality of services provided by the MCOs and HCBS providers.

### The Importance of the KanCare Ombudsman

While the KanCare system is managed by MCOs, beneficiaries have access to the **KanCare Ombudsman** for assistance with complaints, grievances, and appeals related to their MCO or the services they receive [6]. The Ombudsman acts as an impartial advocate, helping members navigate the complex managed care system and ensuring their rights are protected.

## Navigating the KanCare Appeals and Grievance Process

KanCare members have the right to appeal decisions made by their MCO or the state. The process is multi-layered and includes both internal MCO processes and external state-level appeals [6].

| Process Step | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Grievance** | A complaint about the quality of care, services, or the behavior of staff. This is typically handled internally by the MCO. |
| **Appeal (Internal)** | A request to review a decision by the MCO to deny, reduce, or terminate a service. The MCO must respond within a set timeframe. |
| **Fair Hearing (External)** | If the internal appeal is denied, the member can request a Fair Hearing with the state's Office of Administrative Hearings. This is an impartial review of the MCO's decision. |
| **Expedited Appeal** | Available for cases where the standard timeframe could seriously jeopardize the member's life or health. |

The KanCare Appeals & Fair Hearings web page provides detailed information on this process, and the KanCare Ombudsman is a valuable resource for members needing assistance with filing an appeal [6].

## Conclusion: The Future of Long-Term Care in Kansas

KanCare, with its managed care structure and the essential Frail Elderly Waiver, represents Kansas' commitment to providing comprehensive healthcare and long-term services to its most vulnerable residents. The system is complex, involving multiple state agencies and private MCOs, but its core mission remains focused on delivering person-centered care that promotes independence and quality of life. Understanding the roles of KDADS, the MCOs, and the specific benefits of the FE Waiver is essential for any Kansas resident or family member navigating the long-term care landscape.
