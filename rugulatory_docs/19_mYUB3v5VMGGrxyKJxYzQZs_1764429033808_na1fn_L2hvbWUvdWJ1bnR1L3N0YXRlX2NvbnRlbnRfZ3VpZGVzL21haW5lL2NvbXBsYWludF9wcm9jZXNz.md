# Navigating the Complaint Process for Long-Term Care in Maine

Ensuring the safety, dignity, and quality of care for residents in long-term care facilities is a paramount concern in Maine. The state has established a multi-layered system for reporting and investigating complaints related to nursing homes, assisted living facilities, residential care facilities, and home health agencies. Understanding which agency to contact and the specific process involved is the first critical step for residents, family members, and concerned citizens.

The three primary entities involved in the complaint process are the **Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC)**, **Adult Protective Services (APS)**, and the **Maine Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP)**. Each agency has a distinct focus and jurisdiction.

| Complaint Type | Investigating Agency | Primary Focus | 24/7 Hotline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regulatory Non-Compliance** | Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC) | Violations of state licensing rules and federal certification standards (e.g., staffing, sanitation, quality of care). | (800) 383-2441 |
| **Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation** | Adult Protective Services (APS) | Protecting vulnerable adults from harm caused by others or self-neglect. | (800) 624-8404 |
| **Quality of Life & Rights** | Maine Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP) | Resolving complaints related to resident rights, quality of life, and care issues through mediation and advocacy. | (800) 499-0229 |

## 1. Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC) Complaint Process

The **Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC)**, part of the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), is the state's regulatory authority responsible for licensing and certifying long-term care facilities. The DLC investigates complaints alleging violations of state licensing rules or federal certification requirements.

### What the DLC Investigates

The DLC’s jurisdiction covers issues of **regulatory non-compliance** that affect the health, safety, and welfare of residents. This includes, but is not limited to:

*   **Quality of Care:** Concerns about inadequate medical care, improper medication administration, or failure to follow a care plan.
*   **Staffing:** Allegations of insufficient staffing levels, unqualified personnel, or failure to conduct required background checks.
*   **Physical Environment:** Issues related to sanitation, fire safety, maintenance, and the overall condition of the facility, including compliance with building codes.
*   **Resident Rights Violations:** Violations of a resident's statutory rights, such as the right to privacy, freedom from restraint, the right to manage personal finances, or the right to be free from verbal abuse.
*   **Facility-Reported Incidents:** The DLC also reviews and investigates incidents that facilities are mandated to report, such as serious injuries, unexpected deaths, or elopements.

### How to File a Complaint with the DLC

Complaints can be filed anonymously, and the identity of the complainant is kept confidential to the extent permitted by law. The DLC offers several methods for filing a complaint:

1.  **Online Complaint Form:** The most efficient method is to use the online form provided by the DHHS on the DLC's safety and reporting page [1]. This form guides the complainant through providing necessary details.
2.  **Toll-Free Complaint Line:** Individuals can call the DLC's dedicated complaint line at **(800) 383-2441** during business hours. This is the primary method for urgent, non-life-threatening concerns.
3.  **Email or Fax:** Complaints can be submitted via email (using a secure/encrypted format) or fax to the DLC complaint email address or fax number provided on their website [1].
4.  **Mail:** A complaint form can be downloaded, completed, and mailed to the Division of Licensing and Certification office in Augusta.

### The DLC Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, the DLC assesses its severity and urgency to determine the appropriate response timeline.

*   **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are categorized based on the potential for harm to residents. Allegations of **Immediate Jeopardy** (a situation where a resident's health or safety is in imminent danger) are investigated within **two working days**. Other serious complaints are investigated within **ten working days**. Less serious complaints are investigated during the facility's next scheduled survey [2].
*   **Unannounced Survey:** The investigation is typically conducted through an **unannounced on-site survey** by a team of surveyors, which may include nurses, social workers, and other specialists. The surveyors interview residents, family members, and staff, observe care being provided, and review medical records and facility policies.
*   **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD):** If the investigation confirms a violation of state or federal regulations, the DLC issues an SOD to the facility, detailing the findings and the specific regulation violated.
*   **Plan of Correction (POC):** The facility is required to submit a written POC detailing how and when they will correct the cited deficiencies. The DLC reviews and approves the POC.
*   **Enforcement Action:** Depending on the severity and scope of the deficiencies, the DLC may impose sanctions, which can range from civil monetary penalties (fines) to denial of payment for new admissions, or even license revocation for persistent non-compliance.

## 2. Adult Protective Services (APS) Reporting

**Adult Protective Services (APS)**, a program within the DHHS Office of Aging and Disability Services (OADS), is the designated agency in Maine to receive and investigate reports of **abuse, neglect, or exploitation** of vulnerable adults.

### What APS Investigates

APS focuses on protecting vulnerable adults, defined as individuals 18 years of age or older who are unable to protect themselves from abuse, neglect, or exploitation because of a physical or mental impairment or an advanced age.

*   **Abuse:** The willful infliction of injury, unreasonable confinement, intimidation, or cruel punishment that causes or is likely to cause physical harm or pain or mental anguish; sexual abuse or sexual exploitation; financial exploitation; or the intentional, knowing, or reckless deprivation of essential needs [3].
*   **Neglect:** The failure of a caregiver to provide the necessary care, supervision, or services to maintain the physical and mental health of a vulnerable adult, or the failure of the vulnerable adult to provide for their own essential needs (**self-neglect**).
*   **Exploitation:** The illegal or improper use of a vulnerable adult or their resources for another person's profit or advantage.

### How to File a Report with APS

Maine law mandates that certain professionals (e.g., medical personnel, law enforcement, facility staff) must report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation. However, **anyone** can and should report concerns.

*   **24/7 Toll-Free Hotline:** The most direct way to report is by calling the APS hotline at **(800) 624-8404**. This line is staffed 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and is the correct number to call for immediate concerns of abuse or neglect.
*   **Online Reporting:** Reports can also be made through an online form provided by the DHHS [4].

When making a report, be prepared to provide as much detail as possible, including the name and location of the vulnerable adult, the nature of the harm, and the name of the alleged perpetrator, if known.

### The APS Investigation Process

*   **Immediate Response:** APS prioritizes reports based on the severity of the risk. Reports of immediate danger are investigated immediately.
*   **Investigation and Assessment:** An APS worker will conduct a thorough investigation to determine if abuse, neglect, or exploitation has occurred. This involves interviewing the vulnerable adult, the alleged perpetrator, and other witnesses, as well as reviewing relevant records.
*   **Service Planning:** If the investigation confirms the allegations, APS works with the vulnerable adult to develop a service plan to ensure their safety and well-being. Services may include emergency shelter, medical care, home-based services, or legal assistance.
*   **Referral to Law Enforcement:** If the findings suggest criminal activity, APS will refer the case to the appropriate law enforcement agency.

## 3. Maine Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP)

The **Maine Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP)** serves as an independent advocate for residents of long-term care facilities and recipients of home care services. While the DLC focuses on regulatory compliance and APS on protective services, the Ombudsman focuses on **resident rights and quality of life** issues.

### What the MLTCOP Investigates

The Ombudsman is authorized to investigate complaints made by or on behalf of long-term care consumers regarding:

*   **Quality of Life:** Issues such as poor food quality, lack of activities, or disrespectful treatment by staff.
*   **Resident Rights:** Violations of rights guaranteed under the law, such as the right to privacy, the right to visitors, or the right to participate in care planning.
*   **Care Concerns:** Issues that may not rise to the level of regulatory violation but still negatively impact the resident's well-being, such as communication problems or minor care discrepancies.
*   **Grievances:** The Ombudsman can assist residents in filing and pursuing grievances within the facility's internal system.

### How to File a Complaint with the MLTCOP

*   **Toll-Free Number:** The MLTCOP can be reached at **(800) 499-0229** or (207) 621-1079.
*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman's services are free and strictly confidential. The Ombudsman will not take action without the resident's permission, unless the resident is unable to communicate their wishes and a serious threat to their health or safety exists.

### The MLTCOP Resolution Process

The Ombudsman's approach is one of **mediation and advocacy**.

*   **Fact-Finding:** The Ombudsman will visit the facility, speak with the resident privately, and gather information from all parties involved.
*   **Negotiation and Mediation:** The Ombudsman works to resolve the complaint at the lowest possible level, often by mediating a solution between the resident and the facility staff or administration.
*   **Systemic Change:** If a complaint reveals a systemic problem, the Ombudsman may work with the facility or advocate for policy changes at the state level.
*   **Referral:** If the complaint involves abuse, neglect, or a serious regulatory violation, the Ombudsman will refer the case to APS or the DLC, with the resident's consent.

## Legal Protections for Complainants and Residents

Maine law provides important protections for individuals who report concerns about long-term care facilities.

*   **Retaliation Prohibition:** Facilities are strictly prohibited from retaliating against a resident, employee, or any other person who files a complaint, reports a violation, or cooperates with an investigation. Retaliation can include eviction, discharge, or any form of discrimination. This protection is codified in state law to encourage reporting without fear of reprisal [5].
*   **Whistleblower Protection:** Employees of long-term care facilities who report suspected violations of law or rule to the appropriate authority are protected from adverse employment action, such as termination or demotion, under Maine's Whistleblower Protection Act. This is a crucial safeguard for facility staff who are often the first to witness and report wrongdoing.
*   **Confidentiality:** As noted, the DLC and the MLTCOP maintain the confidentiality of the complainant's identity to the extent possible, encouraging individuals to come forward without fear of reprisal.
*   **Right to Appeal:** Residents have the right to appeal certain facility decisions, such as an involuntary transfer or discharge. The Ombudsman can provide assistance with this appeal process.

## The Role of Law Enforcement

In cases where a complaint involves suspected criminal activity, such as assault, theft, or severe neglect, law enforcement must be involved.

*   **Mandatory Reporting:** Facilities are required to report suspected crimes to local law enforcement and the DLC immediately. Failure to do so can result in severe penalties.
*   **Concurrent Investigation:** Law enforcement, APS, and the DLC may conduct concurrent investigations. The DLC and APS focus on regulatory compliance and protective services, while law enforcement focuses on criminal prosecution.

## Conclusion

The Maine complaint process is designed to be accessible and responsive, offering multiple avenues for reporting concerns. For immediate threats of abuse or neglect, **Adult Protective Services (800-624-8404)** is the correct first call. For issues of regulatory non-compliance, the **DLC (800-383-2441)** is the authority. For advocacy and quality of life concerns, the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (800-499-0229)** provides confidential support. By utilizing these resources, individuals can play an active role in maintaining the highest standards of care for Maine's vulnerable population. The state's commitment to a transparent and protected reporting system is a vital component of its long-term care oversight.
