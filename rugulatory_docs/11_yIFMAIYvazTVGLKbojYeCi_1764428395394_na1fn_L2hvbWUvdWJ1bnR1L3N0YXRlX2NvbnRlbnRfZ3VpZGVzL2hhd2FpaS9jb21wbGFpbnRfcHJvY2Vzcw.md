# Hawaii Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Multi-Agency Approach to Resident Protection

For residents and families navigating the long-term care system in Hawaii, understanding the proper channels for filing a complaint is crucial for ensuring accountability and quality of care. The complaint process in Hawaii is managed through a multi-agency network, with different bodies responsible for investigating various types of concerns, from facility licensing violations to issues with Medicaid services and suspected abuse.

The three primary entities involved in the long-term care complaint process are the **Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA)**, the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)**, and **Adult Protective Services (APS)**. Additionally, the **Med-QUEST Division (MQD)** handles grievances and appeals related to Medicaid services.

This guide provides a detailed, step-by-step breakdown of how to file a complaint in Hawaii, identifying the correct agency for each type of concern and outlining the expected investigation process.

## 1. Complaints Against Licensed Facilities: Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA)

The **Office of Health Care Assurance (OHCA)**, a division of the Hawaii Department of Health (DOH), is the state's licensing and certification authority. OHCA is the appropriate agency for complaints concerning the quality of care, safety, sanitation, staffing levels, and compliance with state and federal regulations in licensed facilities.

### What OHCA Investigates

OHCA investigates complaints against all licensed long-term care facilities, including:

*   Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs)
*   Adult Residential Care Homes (ARCHs) and Expanded ARCHs (E-ARCHs)
*   Assisted Living Facilities (ALFs)
*   Home Health Agencies

### How to File a Complaint with OHCA

Filing a complaint with OHCA can be done confidentially, and the agency is mandated to investigate all credible reports.

| Method | Contact Information | Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Complaint Hotline** | **(808) 692-7420** | This is the dedicated line for the Medicare Section of OHCA, which handles complaints against Medicare/Medicaid-certified facilities (SNFs) and other licensed facilities. |
| **Email** | DOH.OHCALicensing@doh.hawaii.gov | For submitting the completed complaint form. |
| **Fax** | (808) 692-7414 or (808) 692-7447 | For faxing the completed complaint form. |
| **Mail** | Department of Health, Office of Health Care Assurance, 601 Kamokila Blvd., Room 337, Kapolei, Hawaii 96707 | For mailing the completed complaint form. |

**The Complaint Process:**

1.  **Obtain the Form:** The DOH provides a **Complaint Intake Form** (or similar document) which can be downloaded from the OHCA website [1]. While a form is preferred, a complaint can often be initiated by phone.
2.  **Provide Details:** The complainant should provide as much detail as possible, including the name and address of the facility, the names of the residents and staff involved, the date and time of the incident, and a clear description of the alleged violation.
3.  **Investigation:** OHCA is required to investigate complaints related to immediate jeopardy (a situation where a resident's health or safety is in danger) within **two working days**. Other non-immediate jeopardy complaints are typically investigated within **ten working days** [2].
4.  **Unannounced Survey:** The investigation is conducted through an **unannounced on-site survey** by OHCA inspectors.
5.  **Findings and Enforcement:** If the complaint is substantiated, OHCA issues a deficiency citation to the facility and requires a Plan of Correction (POC). For serious violations, administrative penalties, fines, or even license revocation may be pursued.

## 2. Advocacy and Resident Rights: Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** is the resident's advocate. The Ombudsman is the ideal contact for issues related to resident rights, quality of life, and disputes that may not involve a direct violation of a licensing regulation but still negatively impact the resident.

### What the LTCOP Investigates

The LTCOP is a confidential and independent resource that investigates and resolves complaints made by or on behalf of residents in all long-term care facilities, including those in ARCHs, SNFs, and ALFs.

*   **Resident Rights:** Violations of the right to privacy, dignity, self-determination, or freedom from restraint.
*   **Quality of Life:** Issues with food, activities, social isolation, or general facility environment.
*   **Communication:** Problems with facility staff, communication with family, or discharge/transfer notices.

### How to Contact the LTCOP

The Ombudsman's services are free and confidential.

| Method | Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Toll-Free Hotline** | **(888) 229-2231** |
| **Local Phone** | (808) 586-7268 |
| **Website** | [https://www.hi-ltc-ombudsman.org/](https://www.hi-ltc-ombudsman.org/) |

**The LTCOP Process:**

1.  **Confidential Intake:** The Ombudsman or a certified volunteer takes the complaint, ensuring the resident's identity is protected.
2.  **Resident Consent:** No action is taken without the resident's (or their legal representative's) consent. The process is entirely resident-directed.
3.  **Mediation and Resolution:** The Ombudsman works to resolve the issue through mediation, negotiation, and advocacy with the facility administration.
4.  **Referral:** If the complaint involves abuse, neglect, or a serious licensing violation, the Ombudsman will refer the case to the appropriate agency (APS or OHCA) while continuing to advocate for the resident's well-being.

## 3. Suspected Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation: Adult Protective Services (APS)

For any suspicion of **abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation** of a vulnerable adult in a long-term care setting or in the community, the report must be made to **Adult Protective Services (APS)**, which is part of the Department of Human Services (DHS) [3].

### How to Report to APS

APS provides crisis intervention and investigation services for vulnerable adults, regardless of their income.

| Method | Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Statewide APS Reporting Line** | **(808) 832-5115** |
| **APS Report Form** | DHS-1640 (Available on the DHS website) |

**The APS Process:**

1.  **Immediate Call:** State law mandates that reports of suspected abuse be made immediately by phone.
2.  **Investigation:** APS investigators will conduct an assessment to determine if the vulnerable adult is in danger and if protective services are needed.
3.  **Coordination:** APS works closely with law enforcement (Honolulu Police Department has specific policies for vulnerable adult abuse) and the Office of Health Care Assurance to ensure the safety of the resident and the appropriate legal action is taken [4].

## 4. Disputes Over Medicaid Services: Med-QUEST Division (MQD) Grievance and Appeals

Complaints related to the denial, reduction, or termination of Medicaid services, or dissatisfaction with the Managed Care Organization (MCO) providing the services, must be addressed through the **Med-QUEST Division (MQD) Grievance and Appeals** process.

### What MQD Grievance and Appeals Handles

*   **Grievances:** Complaints about the quality of care, rudeness of staff, or the MCO's operations.
*   **Appeals:** Formal requests to review a decision by the MCO or MQD to deny, reduce, or terminate a covered service (e.g., a denial of a request for personal assistance services).

### How to File a Grievance or Appeal

Since QUEST Integration is a managed care program, the process begins with the member's MCO (e.g., AlohaCare, HMSA, Kaiser Permanente).

1.  **File with the MCO:** The member must first file a grievance or appeal directly with their MCO. The MCO has specific timeframes for responding.
2.  **Appeal to MQD (Fair Hearing):** If the member is dissatisfied with the MCO's decision on an appeal, they have the right to request a **Fair Hearing** with the Med-QUEST Division. This is a formal, administrative review of the decision.
    *   **MQD Contact:** Members can contact the MQD Grievance and Appeals office at **(808) 692-8094** for assistance with the Fair Hearing process [5].
3.  **Expedited Appeals:** For cases where a delay could seriously jeopardize the member's health, an expedited appeal can be requested, which requires a much faster decision timeframe.

## Conclusion

The Hawaii long-term care complaint system is designed to provide multiple layers of protection. By correctly identifying the nature of the complaint—whether it is a licensing violation (OHCA), a resident rights issue (LTCOP), suspected abuse (APS), or a service denial (MQD)—residents and their advocates can ensure their concerns are directed to the authority best equipped to investigate and resolve the issue. This multi-pronged approach is essential for maintaining high standards of care and protecting the dignity of Hawaii's long-term care population.
