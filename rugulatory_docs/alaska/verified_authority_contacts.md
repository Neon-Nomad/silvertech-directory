# Verified Authority Contacts for Long-Term Care in Alaska: A Comprehensive Directory

Navigating the long-term care system in Alaska requires interaction with multiple state and federal agencies, each with a distinct role in funding, regulating, and advocating for the elderly and disabled population. Having a verified, comprehensive directory of these key authorities is essential for families, caregivers, and residents seeking information, assistance, or resolution for a complaint.

This guide provides a detailed breakdown of the primary governmental and non-profit organizations that govern long-term care in Alaska. For each entry, we outline the agency's mission, the specific services they provide related to long-term care, and all verified contact information, including physical addresses, phone numbers, and official websites.

---

## I. Alaska Department of Health: Medicaid and Public Assistance

The **Alaska Department of Health (DOH)** is the central state agency responsible for the health and well-being of all Alaskans. Within the DOH, two divisions are critical to long-term care: the Division of Public Assistance (DPA) and the Division of Health Care Services (DHCS).

### A. Division of Public Assistance (DPA) - Medicaid Programs

The DPA administers the **Alaska Medical Assistance (Medicaid)** program, also known as **DenaliCare**. This division is the primary point of contact for all matters related to Medicaid eligibility, enrollment, and benefits, including the **Alaskans Living Independently (ALI) Medicaid Waiver** which funds community-based long-term care.

#### Mission and Services
The DPA's mission is to help Alaskans meet their basic needs and achieve self-sufficiency by providing public assistance programs [1]. For long-term care, their services include:

*   **Eligibility Determination:** Processing applications for Medicaid, including the complex financial and functional eligibility for long-term care services.
*   **Benefits Counseling:** Providing information on covered services, such as nursing home care and Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers.
*   **Application Support:** Assisting applicants with the necessary documentation for the 60-month look-back period and asset verification.

## II. Alaska Department of Health: Licensing and Regulation

The **Health Facilities Licensing and Certification Unit (HFLCU)** is the state's regulatory arm, ensuring that all licensed health facilities meet minimum standards of quality and safety.

### B. Health Facilities Licensing and Certification Unit (HFLCU)

The HFLCU, housed within the Division of Health Care Services, is responsible for the oversight of nursing homes, assisted living homes, and other healthcare providers.

#### Mission and Services
The HFLCU's goal is to ensure providers deliver safe, quality services by enforcing state licensing regulations and federal certification requirements [4]. Their key functions include:

*   **Licensing and Certification:** Issuing and renewing licenses for long-term care facilities and certifying them for participation in Medicare and Medicaid.
*   **Inspections (Surveys):** Conducting unannounced, on-site inspections to verify compliance with health, safety, and patient care standards.
*   **Complaint Investigation:** Formally investigating complaints related to quality of care, facility conditions, and regulatory violations.

## III. Advocacy and Protection Services

For issues related to resident rights, abuse, and consumer protection, three distinct agencies provide critical advocacy and investigative services.

### C. The Office of Long Term Care Ombudsman (OLTCO)

The OLTCO is an independent advocate for the rights, dignity, and welfare of Alaskans aged 60 and older residing in assisted living or nursing homes.

#### Mission and Services
The Ombudsman's role is to resolve problems and complaints on behalf of residents, acting as a mediator and advocate [7]. Their services are confidential and include:

*   **Complaint Resolution:** Investigating and resolving complaints related to quality of life, resident rights, and facility practices.
*   **Resident Rights Education:** Informing residents, families, and facility staff about the rights guaranteed under state and federal law.
*   **Facility Visits:** Making regular, unannounced visits to long-term care facilities to monitor conditions and build relationships with residents.

### D. Adult Protective Services (APS)

APS is the state agency responsible for investigating reports of abuse, neglect, self-neglect, and exploitation of vulnerable adults.

#### Mission and Services
APS works to prevent or stop harm to vulnerable adults who are unable to protect themselves due to a physical or mental limitation [9]. Their services are investigative and protective:

*   **Harm Investigation:** Conducting investigations into all reports of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation.
*   **Protective Service Planning:** Developing and implementing protective service plans to ensure the safety and well-being of the vulnerable adult.
*   **Referral to Law Enforcement:** Referring cases of criminal abuse or exploitation to the appropriate law enforcement agency.

### E. Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)

The MFCU, part of the Alaska Department of Law, is dedicated to combating fraud and abuse within the Medicaid program.

#### Mission and Services
The MFCU investigates and prosecutes two main types of cases [11]:

*   **Provider Fraud:** Fraudulent claims made by healthcare providers (e.g., nursing homes, doctors) to the Medicaid program.
*   **Patient Abuse/Neglect:** Criminal investigation and prosecution of abuse or neglect of Medicaid patients in healthcare facilities.

## IV. Veterans Affairs and Benefits

The **Alaska Department of Military and Veterans Affairs (DMVA)** is the state agency dedicated to serving Alaska's veterans and their families.

### F. State Office of Veterans Affairs (SOVA)

The SOVA is the primary resource for state-level veteran benefits, including long-term care options and financial assistance.

#### Mission and Services
The SOVA assists veterans in obtaining all state and federal benefits they are entitled to [13]. Their services include:

*   **Benefits Counseling:** Providing information on the Alaska Veterans and Pioneers Home, the VA Aid and Attendance benefit, and other long-term care resources.
*   **Claims Assistance:** Employing accredited Veterans Service Officers (VSOs) to assist with filing claims for VA disability compensation, pension, and healthcare.
*   **State Benefits:** Providing information and assistance with the Disabled Veteran Property Tax Exemption and other state-specific programs.

## VI. Conclusion

The long-term care landscape in Alaska is supported by a network of dedicated governmental and advocacy organizations. From the financial lifeline provided by the Division of Public Assistance's Medicaid program to the regulatory oversight of the HFLCU and the protective services of the OLTCO and APS, these agencies form a comprehensive safety net. By keeping this verified directory of contacts readily available, Alaskans can ensure they have immediate access to the information and assistance necessary to navigate the system, resolve concerns, and secure the highest quality of care for themselves or their loved ones.
