# The Voice of the Resident: Ohio's Long-Term Care Ombudsman and Advocacy Programs

For residents of nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and those receiving home care, the **Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman** serves as a crucial advocate, protector, and resource. Mandated by the federal Older Americans Act and Ohio law, the Ombudsman Program is dedicated to improving the quality of life and quality of care for all consumers of long-term care services in the state [1]. This comprehensive guide explores the mission, services, and impact of the Ohio Ombudsman Program, detailing how it works to resolve complaints, protect resident rights, and empower individuals to make informed decisions about their care.

## The Mission and Structure of the Ombudsman Program

The Ohio Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program is housed within the **Ohio Department of Aging (ODA)**. Its mission is rooted in the principles of empowerment, education, and advocacy, ensuring that the concerns of long-term care consumers are heard and addressed [2].

### Who the Ombudsman Serves

The program's advocacy extends to all individuals receiving long-term care, regardless of their age or payment source. This includes:

*   **Residents of Nursing Homes:** Individuals receiving skilled nursing care or long-term custodial care.
*   **Residents of Residential Care Facilities (Assisted Living):** Individuals residing in licensed assisted living settings.
*   **Consumers of Home Care:** Individuals receiving services through Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers, such as PASSPORT or the Assisted Living Waiver.

### The Ombudsman Network

The State Long-Term Care Ombudsman oversees a network of regional ombudsman programs, often operated by local non-profit organizations or Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs). This decentralized structure ensures that local ombudsmen are familiar with the specific facilities and community resources in their area, providing a more personalized and effective level of advocacy [3].

## Core Services of the Ombudsman Program

The Ombudsman Program provides a range of services, with the primary focus on complaint investigation and resolution.

### 1. Complaint Investigation and Resolution

The most visible and critical function of the Ombudsman is to investigate and resolve complaints made by or on behalf of long-term care consumers.

*   **Scope of Complaints:** Complaints can cover a wide spectrum of issues, including [4]:
    *   **Quality of Care:** Issues related to medication errors, inadequate wound care, poor hygiene, or insufficient staffing.
    *   **Resident Rights Violations:** Violations of the right to privacy, the right to refuse treatment, or the right to manage one's own finances.
    *   **Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation:** Suspected physical, emotional, or financial mistreatment.
    *   **Facility Practices:** Concerns about food quality, room temperature, transfer/discharge notices, or restrictive policies.
*   **Process:** The ombudsman's approach is confidential and resident-directed. They visit the facility, interview the resident, family, and staff, and review relevant documents. The ombudsman does not act as a regulator (like the Ohio Department of Health) but as a mediator and advocate, working with the resident and the facility to achieve a satisfactory resolution.

### 2. Resident Rights Education and Protection

A fundamental aspect of the ombudsman's role is to educate residents, families, and facility staff about the rights guaranteed to long-term care residents under both state and federal law.

#### The Ohio Nursing Home Patient Bill of Rights

Ohio law, specifically the **Ohio Revised Code (ORC) Section 3721.13**, establishes a comprehensive set of rights for residents of nursing homes and residential care facilities. The ombudsman is instrumental in ensuring these rights are upheld [5]. Key rights include:

*   **Right to a Safe and Clean Environment:** The right to a living environment that is safe, clean, and comfortable.
*   **Right to Self-Determination:** The right to manage one's own financial affairs, choose one's own physician, and participate in the planning of one's care.
*   **Right to Privacy and Confidentiality:** The right to private and unrestricted communication, including visits and phone calls, and confidentiality of personal and medical records.
*   **Right to Refuse Treatment:** The right to refuse medical treatment or participation in experimental research.
*   **Right to be Free from Abuse and Restraints:** The right to be free from physical, mental, and emotional abuse, and from physical or chemical restraints imposed for discipline or convenience.

The ombudsman provides training and materials to help residents understand and assert these rights, acting as a powerful check against institutional practices that may infringe upon resident autonomy.

### 3. Community Education and Systemic Advocacy

The ombudsman program also engages in broader systemic advocacy and community education.

*   **Facility Selection Counseling:** Ombudsmen provide advice and information to prospective residents and their families on how to choose a long-term care facility, including information on facility quality, inspection reports, and complaint history.
*   **Policy Recommendations:** Based on the patterns of complaints and issues they observe, the ombudsman program provides recommendations to state agencies and the legislature to improve laws, regulations, and policies affecting long-term care consumers.
*   **Volunteer Program:** The program relies heavily on trained volunteers who regularly visit facilities, build relationships with residents, and serve as the eyes and ears of the program, helping to identify and report issues before they escalate into formal complaints.

## The Distinction Between Ombudsman and Regulator

It is essential for consumers to understand the difference between the Ombudsman Program and the regulatory agency, the Ohio Department of Health (ODH).

| Feature | Long-Term Care Ombudsman (ODA) | Regulatory Agency (ODH) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Role** | Advocate, Mediator, Educator | Licensor, Certifier, Enforcer |
| **Focus** | Resident Rights, Quality of Life, Complaint Resolution | Compliance with Laws and Regulations |
| **Authority** | Persuasion, Negotiation, Advocacy | Inspection, Citation, Fines, License Action |
| **Goal** | Improve individual resident's situation and quality of care | Ensure minimum standards of health and safety are met |
| **Confidentiality** | High degree of confidentiality; resident-directed | Findings are public record |

While the Ombudsman can refer serious issues, such as suspected abuse or neglect, to the ODH for investigation, their primary strength lies in their ability to resolve issues informally and confidentially, focusing on the resident's wishes.

## Detailed Exploration of Resident Rights and Advocacy

The foundation of the Ombudsman's work is the protection of resident rights. A deeper understanding of these rights, as codified in the Ohio Revised Code and federal law, highlights the scope of the Ombudsman's advocacy.

### Financial and Personal Rights

Beyond the right to be free from abuse, Ohio law grants residents significant control over their personal and financial lives:

*   **Right to Manage Financial Affairs:** Residents have the right to manage their own personal financial affairs. If a resident chooses to delegate this responsibility to the facility, the facility must provide a quarterly written accounting of all transactions [5]. The Ombudsman often intervenes when there are disputes over a resident's funds or concerns about financial exploitation.
*   **Right to Privacy and Communication:** This includes the right to private, unmonitored visits from family, friends, and the Ombudsman. It also guarantees the right to send and receive unopened mail and to have private telephone conversations. The Ombudsman ensures that facilities do not restrict these vital connections to the outside world.
*   **Right to Participate in Care Planning:** Residents have the right to be fully informed of their medical condition and to participate in the planning of their medical treatment. This includes the right to refuse any treatment or medication. The Ombudsman ensures that informed consent is obtained and that the resident's wishes are respected, even if they conflict with the facility's recommendations.

### Advocacy for Quality of Life

The Ombudsman's advocacy extends beyond clinical care to the resident's overall quality of life, which is a key focus of federal nursing home regulations.

*   **Dignity and Respect:** Residents have the right to be treated with consideration, respect, and full recognition of their dignity and individuality. This includes the right to voice grievances without fear of reprisal.
*   **Social and Activity Needs:** The facility must provide an ongoing program of activities designed to meet the interests and the physical, mental, and psychosocial well-being of each resident. The Ombudsman monitors whether facilities are providing meaningful activities that prevent boredom and isolation.
*   **Transfer and Discharge Rights:** One of the most common areas of Ombudsman intervention is involuntary transfer or discharge. Residents have the right to remain in the facility unless specific, legally defined criteria are met (e.g., non-payment, medical needs cannot be met, or the resident's safety is endangered). The Ombudsman ensures that proper notice is given and that the resident has the opportunity to appeal the decision.

## Systemic Advocacy and Policy Influence

The Ombudsman Program's impact is not limited to individual case resolution; it also drives systemic change through policy advocacy.

*   **Data Collection and Reporting:** The program collects data on the types and frequency of complaints, which are analyzed to identify systemic problems in the long-term care system. This data is used to inform the Ohio Department of Aging, the Ohio Department of Health, and the state legislature.
*   **Collaboration with Regulators:** While independent, the Ombudsman works closely with the ODH and other state agencies. For instance, if the Ombudsman identifies a pattern of regulatory non-compliance at a facility, they can refer the matter to the ODH for a formal investigation and potential enforcement action. This collaboration ensures that both advocacy and regulatory power are brought to bear on persistent problems.
*   **Involvement in Task Forces:** The State Ombudsman often serves on key state task forces and committees, such as the Governor's Nursing Home Quality and Accountability Task Force, ensuring that the resident's perspective is represented in high-level policy discussions.

## Empowering the Community

The Ombudsman Program actively seeks to empower the community to be better advocates for their loved ones.

*   **Facility Selection Counseling:** The Ombudsman provides a valuable service by helping families interpret complex quality data, such as the CMS Five-Star Quality Rating and ODH inspection reports. They can explain what specific deficiencies mean for a resident's daily life and guide families in asking the right questions during facility tours.
*   **Volunteer Opportunities:** The program recruits and trains community members to become certified volunteer Ombudsmen. These volunteers are essential for maintaining a regular presence in facilities, providing a friendly face to residents, and identifying issues that may not be reported through formal channels. This volunteer corps is a testament to the program's commitment to community-based advocacy.

In conclusion, the Ohio Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program is more than a complaint-resolution service; it is a comprehensive advocacy system that upholds the rights and dignity of long-term care consumers. By combining confidential, resident-directed advocacy with systemic policy influence, the Ombudsman ensures that the voice of the resident remains central to the quality of care in Ohio.
