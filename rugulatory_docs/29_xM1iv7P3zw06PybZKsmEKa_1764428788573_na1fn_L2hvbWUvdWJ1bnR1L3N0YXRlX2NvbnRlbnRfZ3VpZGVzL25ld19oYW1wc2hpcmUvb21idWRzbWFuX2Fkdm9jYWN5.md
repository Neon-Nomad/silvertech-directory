# New Hampshire State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program: Advocating for Resident Rights and Quality of Care

The **New Hampshire State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** serves as a critical advocate for residents in nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and other long-term care settings across the state. Mandated by the federal Older Americans Act, the program's core mission is to protect the health, safety, welfare, and rights of residents by investigating and resolving complaints, providing information, and promoting systemic change [1].

This comprehensive guide explores the structure, authority, and vital functions of the New Hampshire LTCOP, detailing its role in complaint resolution, resident rights education, and broader advocacy efforts, all aimed at ensuring a high quality of life and care for the state's most vulnerable population.

## I. Program Foundation and Structure

The New Hampshire LTCOP is a state-level program with a federal mandate, ensuring its independence and authority to act on behalf of residents. It is strategically placed within the state's health and human services infrastructure to maximize its reach and effectiveness.

### A. Administrative Oversight

The LTCOP is housed within the **Bureau of Elderly and Adult Services (BEAS)**, which is a division of the **New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS)**. This placement allows the program to work closely with other state agencies responsible for licensing, certification, and adult protective services, while maintaining its distinct, independent advocacy role [2].

### B. The Federal Mandate

The LTCOP is a direct result of the **Older Americans Act (OAA)**, which requires every state to establish a program to protect the rights of long-term care residents. This mandate ensures that the program has the legal authority to:

1.  **Access** residents, facilities, and resident records, including confidential medical and social records, with appropriate consent.
2.  **Investigate** and resolve complaints made by or on behalf of residents.
3.  **Represent** the interests of residents before governmental agencies and seek administrative, legal, and other remedies to protect residents [3].

The independence of the Ombudsman is paramount, allowing them to advocate without fear of reprisal or conflict of interest from the regulatory or service-providing arms of the state.

## II. Scope of Advocacy and Facilities Served

The New Hampshire LTCOP serves as an advocate for residents in a wide range of long-term care settings. The program's reach extends to any facility that provides continuous care to a resident who is not related to the owner or operator.

### A. Covered Facilities

The Ombudsman's services are available to residents in:

*   **Nursing Facilities (Nursing Homes):** Facilities that provide 24-hour skilled nursing care and rehabilitation services.
*   **Assisted Living Residences (ALRs):** Both Residential Care Facilities and Supported Residential Health Care Facilities.
*   **Residential Care Facilities (RCFs):** Other licensed facilities that provide personal care and supervision.
*   **Board and Care Homes:** Smaller, non-medical residential settings that provide housing and meals.

Essentially, any resident receiving long-term care services in a licensed facility is entitled to the services and protection of the LTCOP [4].

### B. The Role of the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman is not a regulator, inspector, or law enforcement agent. Instead, the role is one of an impartial advocate and mediator. The Ombudsman's primary focus is on the **rights and quality of life** of the resident. This includes issues related to:

*   **Resident Rights:** Ensuring residents are aware of and can exercise their rights, such as the right to privacy, the right to self-determination, and the right to voice grievances without fear of retaliation.
*   **Quality of Care:** Addressing concerns about inadequate medical care, poor hygiene, or insufficient attention to personal needs.
*   **Quality of Life:** Investigating issues related to food quality, activities, social isolation, and the overall living environment.
*   **Financial Matters:** Resolving concerns about facility charges, misuse of personal funds, or Medicaid eligibility issues.
*   **Transfer and Discharge:** Advocating for residents facing involuntary transfer or discharge from a facility [5].

## III. The Complaint Investigation and Resolution Process

The LTCOP is the designated entity for receiving, investigating, and resolving complaints concerning long-term care residents. The process is designed to be confidential, accessible, and resident-centered.

### A. How to File a Complaint

Complaints can be filed by the resident themselves, a family member, a friend, a facility staff member, or any concerned citizen. The Ombudsman accepts complaints through multiple channels:

1.  **Toll-Free Hotline:** The most direct method is calling the statewide toll-free number, **(800) 442-5640**, which connects callers directly to the Ombudsman's office.
2.  **Local Phone:** Callers can also use the local number, (603) 271-4375.
3.  **Mail or In-Person:** Complaints can be submitted in writing or delivered in person to the Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman at the DHHS main building in Concord [6].
4.  **Email:** While not the primary method for urgent complaints, the office can be contacted via email for general inquiries.

### B. The Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, the Ombudsman follows a structured process:

1.  **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman is legally required to maintain the confidentiality of the resident and the complainant. **No action can be taken without the express permission of the resident or their legal guardian** [7]. This is a cornerstone of the program, ensuring residents feel safe to report issues.
2.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are assessed for severity and urgency. Issues posing an immediate threat to a resident's health or safety are prioritized.
3.  **Investigation:** The Ombudsman or a trained volunteer representative will visit the facility, interview the resident, family, staff, and review relevant records (with consent).
4.  **Resolution:** The Ombudsman attempts to resolve the complaint at the lowest possible level, often through mediation and negotiation with the facility administration. The goal is to achieve a resolution that is satisfactory to the resident.
5.  **Referral:** If the complaint involves a violation of licensing standards, abuse, neglect, or exploitation, the Ombudsman will refer the case to the appropriate state agency, such as the Health Facility Licensing Unit or Adult Protective Services (APS), while continuing to advocate for the resident [8].

## IV. Broader Advocacy and Education

Beyond individual complaint resolution, the LTCOP engages in systemic advocacy and public education to improve the long-term care system as a whole.

### A. Policy and Legislative Advocacy

The Ombudsman is mandated to identify problems and patterns in the long-term care system and recommend changes to state laws, regulations, and policies. This includes:

*   **Reporting:** Providing regular reports to the Governor, the Legislature, and the public on the types of complaints received and the systemic issues identified.
*   **Testifying:** Offering expert testimony on proposed legislation that affects long-term care residents.
*   **Collaboration:** Working with provider associations, consumer groups, and state agencies to develop and implement best practices.

### B. Resident and Community Education

A significant part of the Ombudsman's work is empowering residents and the public through education:

*   **Resident Rights Education:** Conducting presentations and distributing materials within facilities to ensure residents know their rights and how to exercise them.
*   **Volunteer Training:** Recruiting and training a network of certified volunteer Ombudsmen who regularly visit facilities, build trust with residents, and assist in complaint resolution.
*   **Public Awareness:** Raising public awareness about long-term care issues and the existence of the LTCOP.

## V. Connection to Adult Protective Services (APS)

While the LTCOP focuses on rights and quality of care, the **Bureau of Elderly and Adult Services (BEAS)**, which houses the LTCOP, also administers the state's **Adult Protective Services (APS)** program.

*   **APS Role:** APS is the state's system for investigating reports of abuse, neglect, and exploitation of vulnerable adults, including those in long-term care facilities.
*   **Distinction:** The Ombudsman is an advocate; APS is an investigative and protective service. If a complaint to the Ombudsman suggests abuse or neglect, the Ombudsman will immediately refer the case to APS for a formal investigation [9].
*   **BEAS Mission:** The broader mission of BEAS is to provide a variety of social and long-term supports to adults aged 60 and older and to adults between the ages of 18 and 60 with a chronic illness or disability, making it the central hub for adult and aging care services in New Hampshire [2].

## VI. Conclusion

The New Hampshire State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program is an indispensable resource for long-term care residents and their families. By providing confidential, independent advocacy, the LTCOP ensures that the voices of residents are heard and that their rights are protected. Whether resolving an individual complaint about care or advocating for systemic policy changes, the Ombudsman is a steadfast champion for quality of life and dignity in New Hampshire's long-term care facilities. Residents and their loved ones are strongly encouraged to utilize the program's toll-free hotline to address any concerns regarding the care or treatment received.
