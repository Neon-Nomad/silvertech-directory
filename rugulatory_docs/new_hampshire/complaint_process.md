# New Hampshire Complaint Process: A Comprehensive Guide to Reporting Concerns in Healthcare and Long-Term Care

The State of New Hampshire is committed to ensuring the safety, quality of care, and rights of its residents, particularly those in long-term care facilities and those receiving public assistance. To uphold this commitment, the state has established a multi-layered system for reporting concerns, filing grievances, and appealing administrative decisions. This system is managed primarily by the **New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS)**, which oversees regulatory enforcement, adult protection, and benefit administration.

This comprehensive guide details the various channels available to New Hampshire residents, their families, and advocates for filing a complaint, reporting abuse, or appealing a decision, ensuring that every individual can navigate the process effectively and seek resolution.

## I. Overview of the New Hampshire Complaint System

New Hampshire's complaint infrastructure is designed to address different types of concerns through specialized agencies. It is crucial to identify the nature of the complaint to direct it to the correct authority for the most effective resolution.

| Type of Concern | Primary Agency | Focus of Investigation | Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regulatory Violations** (Facility Standards, Licensing) | Health Facility Licensing Unit (DHHS) | Compliance with state licensing laws (RSA 151) and administrative rules (He-P 800) for licensed facilities. | (603) 271-9039 |
| **Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation** | Adult Protective Services (APS) | Investigation of reports concerning the mistreatment of vulnerable adults. | **1-800-852-3345** (Toll-Free Hotline) |
| **Resident Rights & Quality of Life** | Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP) | Advocacy, mediation, and resolution of complaints regarding resident rights and quality of care in long-term care facilities. | **1-800-442-5640** (Toll-Free) |
| **Medicaid Eligibility/Benefits** (Administrative Decisions) | Administrative Appeals Unit (AAU) | Impartial hearings for appeals of DHHS decisions regarding eligibility, service denials, or benefit amounts. | (603) 271-4292 |

## II. Reporting Regulatory Violations to the Licensing Authority

Complaints concerning the physical environment, staffing levels, sanitation, or general failure to meet state licensing standards for a health facility should be directed to the DHHS Health Facility Licensing Unit.

### A. The Health Facility Licensing Unit

The **Health Facility Licensing Unit** is responsible for licensing and inspecting a wide range of facilities, including nursing homes, assisted living residences, and hospitals [1].

*   **Scope:** The Unit investigates complaints alleging violations of the New Hampshire Revised Statutes Annotated (RSA) Chapter 151 and the detailed administrative rules (He-P 800 series) that govern facility operation.
*   **Filing a Complaint:** Complaints can be filed via phone, email, or mail.
    *   **Phone:** (603) 271-9039
    *   **Email:** `HFA-LIcensing@dhhs.nh.gov`
    *   **Online:** The DHHS website provides a dedicated page for filing a complaint against a health facility [1].

### B. Required Information for a Complaint

To ensure a thorough and timely investigation, complainants should provide as much detail as possible, including:

1.  **Facility Identification:** The full name and address of the facility.
2.  **Nature of the Complaint:** A clear, detailed description of the incident or concern, including dates and times.
3.  **Individuals Involved:** The names of any residents, staff members, or others involved.
4.  **Complainant Contact:** Contact information for the person filing the complaint (though anonymity can be requested, it may limit the investigation).

### C. The Investigation Process

Upon receiving a complaint, the Licensing Unit will:

1.  **Triage:** Assess the severity of the complaint. Complaints alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are prioritized for immediate, unannounced on-site investigation.
2.  **Investigation:** A surveyor will conduct an unannounced visit to the facility, interview residents and staff, observe care, and review relevant records.
3.  **Findings and Enforcement:** If violations are substantiated, the facility is issued a Statement of Deficiencies and must submit a Plan of Correction. Enforcement actions can range from civil penalties to license suspension or revocation, depending on the severity and scope of the violation [2].

## III. Reporting Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation: Adult Protective Services (APS)

For concerns involving the mistreatment of a vulnerable adult, the appropriate channel is the state's **Adult Protective Services (APS)** program, which is also administered by the DHHS.

### A. The APS Mandate

APS is mandated to investigate reports of abuse, neglect (including self-neglect), and exploitation of adults who are unable to protect themselves due to physical or mental impairment [3].

*   **Vulnerable Adult Definition:** This includes elderly individuals and adults with disabilities who reside in their own homes, in long-term care facilities, or other community settings.
*   **Mandatory Reporting:** New Hampshire law requires certain professionals (e.g., healthcare providers, law enforcement) to report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation. However, any person who has reason to believe a vulnerable adult is being mistreated is encouraged to report.

### B. How to Make an APS Report

Reports can be made 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, through the DHHS Adult Protection Reporting Line:

*   **Toll-Free Hotline:** **1-800-852-3345** (This is the DHHS Customer Service Center, which directs calls to the appropriate intake unit).
*   **Email:** `APSCentralIntake@dhhs.nh.gov`
*   **Emergency:** For immediate danger, always call 911 first.

### C. The APS Investigation

1.  **Intake:** All reports are received and screened by the DHHS intake unit.
2.  **Investigation:** If the report meets the criteria for APS intervention, a social worker is assigned to investigate. The investigation is confidential and focuses on determining the validity of the report and the level of risk to the adult.
3.  **Intervention:** If the report is substantiated, APS works with the adult to develop a service plan to reduce risk and ensure safety. This may involve connecting the adult with community resources, legal services, or other protective measures.

## IV. Advocacy and Mediation: The Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** provides an independent, confidential avenue for residents to resolve complaints related to their rights, dignity, and quality of life within a long-term care facility.

### A. The Ombudsman's Role

The Ombudsman is a resident advocate, not a regulator. Their primary goal is to mediate and resolve conflicts to the satisfaction of the resident.

*   **Scope:** Complaints related to resident rights, quality of care, dignity, food, social activities, and involuntary discharge.
*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman will not disclose the identity of the resident or the complainant without their express permission. Crucially, **the Ombudsman will not investigate a complaint without the resident's consent** [4].

### B. Contacting the Ombudsman

*   **Toll-Free Phone:** **1-800-442-5640**
*   **Local Phone:** (603) 271-4375
*   **Website:** [https://www.dhhs.nh.gov/programs-services/long-term-care-ombudsman-program](https://www.dhhs.nh.gov/programs-services/long-term-care-ombudsman-program)

The Ombudsman is often the best first point of contact for residents who feel their concerns are not being addressed by the facility administration.

## V. Appealing Administrative Decisions: The Administrative Appeals Unit (AAU)

When a resident or applicant disagrees with a formal decision made by the DHHS regarding their eligibility for benefits, the amount of benefits, or the reduction/termination of services, they have the right to request an impartial hearing. This process is managed by the **Administrative Appeals Unit (AAU)**.

### A. Scope of Administrative Appeals

The AAU conducts fair hearings for decisions related to various DHHS programs, most notably:

*   **Medicaid Eligibility:** Denial of application, termination of coverage, or determination of financial liability.
*   **Choices for Independence (CFI) Waiver:** Denial of services, reduction in service hours, or denial of a specific service.
*   **SNAP (Food Stamps) and Cash Assistance:** Decisions regarding eligibility or benefit levels.

### B. The Fair Hearing Process

1.  **Requesting an Appeal:** The request for an Administrative Appeal must be submitted in writing to the AAU. The DHHS provides an official **Appeal Request Form** that should be used [5].
    *   **Deadline:** Appeals must typically be requested within a specific timeframe (e.g., 90 or 120 days) from the date on the notice of decision.
    *   **Continuation of Benefits:** In some cases, if the appeal is filed quickly enough, the recipient may be able to continue receiving benefits at the current level until a decision is made after the hearing.
2.  **The Hearing:** The hearing is conducted by an impartial Hearing Officer from the AAU, who is independent of the DHHS program offices. The hearing is an opportunity for the appellant (the person appealing) to present evidence, testimony, and arguments to support their case.
3.  **Decision:** The Hearing Officer issues a written decision based on the evidence presented and the applicable state and federal laws and regulations. This decision is binding on the DHHS.

### C. Contacting the Administrative Appeals Unit

*   **Phone:** (603) 271-4292
*   **Mailing Address:** Administrative Appeals Unit, NH Department of Health and Human Services, 129 Pleasant Street, Concord, NH 03301

## VI. Grievances and Appeals for Medicaid Managed Care Members

Most New Hampshire Medicaid recipients are enrolled in a Managed Care Organization (MCO). If a complaint is about a service provided by the MCO, the member must first use the MCO's internal grievance and appeals process.

### A. The MCO Grievance Process

A **grievance** is a complaint about the quality of care, rudeness of staff, or a problem with the MCO's operations.

1.  **Filing:** Members can file a grievance by calling the MCO's Member Services line or submitting it in writing. For example, NH Healthy Families provides a dedicated Member Services line for filing complaints [6].
2.  **Resolution:** The MCO is required to investigate the grievance and provide a resolution within a set timeframe.

### B. The MCO Appeal Process

An **appeal** is a request to review a decision by the MCO to deny, reduce, or terminate a service that the member believes is medically necessary.

1.  **Internal Appeal:** The member must first appeal the decision internally with the MCO.
2.  **External Fair Hearing:** If the MCO upholds its decision, the member has the right to request a **State Fair Hearing** through the DHHS Administrative Appeals Unit (AAU). This is the final step in the process and allows for an independent review of the MCO's decision [7].

## VII. Conclusion

The New Hampshire complaint process is a comprehensive network of resources designed to protect consumers and ensure accountability across the healthcare and social services spectrum. Whether the concern is a regulatory violation in a nursing home, a case of suspected abuse, a violation of resident rights, or a disagreement over Medicaid benefits, a clear pathway for resolution exists. Residents and their advocates are strongly encouraged to use the toll-free hotlines and official DHHS resources to report concerns promptly. By utilizing these established channels, individuals contribute to the continuous improvement of care quality and the protection of vulnerable adults throughout New Hampshire.
