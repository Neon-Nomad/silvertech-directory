# Verified Authority Contacts: Essential Resources for Long-Term Care and Veterans in Michigan

Navigating the complex landscape of long-term care, licensing, advocacy, and veterans' benefits in Michigan requires access to accurate and verified contact information for the state's key governmental and non-profit agencies. This document serves as a comprehensive resource guide, consolidating the essential contact details for the primary authorities responsible for these critical services.

Each entry provides verified contact information, a detailed overview of the agency's role, and a summary of the specific programs they administer, ensuring that residents, family members, and advocates can quickly connect with the correct resource.

## 1. Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS)

The **Michigan Department of Health and Human Services (MDHHS)** is the largest state agency in Michigan, responsible for administering a wide range of health, welfare, and assistance programs, including the state's Medicaid system. It is the central authority for determining eligibility and overseeing the delivery of long-term care services funded by Medicaid.

### Role and Programs Administered

The MDHHS is the single state agency responsible for the administration of the Medicaid program under the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). Its primary role in long-term care is to manage the **MI Choice Waiver Program** and determine financial and functional eligibility for all long-term care Medicaid applicants.

#### The MI Choice Waiver Program

The MI Choice Waiver is Michigan's most significant Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) program, allowing eligible individuals to receive nursing-home-level care in their own homes or other residential settings.

*   **Function:** Provides a comprehensive package of services, including adult day health, chore services, community living supports, home modifications, and personal emergency response systems [3].
*   **Eligibility:** Requires applicants to meet the medical necessity for nursing facility level of care and adhere to strict financial limits (e.g., $2,901 monthly income and $9,660 in countable assets for a single applicant in 2025) [4].
*   **Application:** Applications are processed through local MDHHS offices, followed by a functional assessment conducted by a waiver agency.

#### Financial Oversight and Planning

MDHHS enforces the federal **60-month Medicaid Look-Back Period** for all long-term care applications. This process reviews all financial transactions to ensure no uncompensated transfers of assets occurred, which could result in a penalty period of ineligibility [5]. The agency also administers the **Spousal Impoverishment Rules**, which protect a portion of a couple's assets and income for the spouse remaining in the community.

#### Medicare Savings Programs (MSPs)

MDHHS also manages the Medicare Savings Programs (QMB, SLMB, QI), which help low-income Medicare beneficiaries pay for premiums, deductibles, and co-insurance, providing a crucial financial bridge for many seniors [6].

## 2. Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA)

The **Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA)**, specifically its **Bureau of Community and Health Systems (BCHS)**, is the state's regulatory body for long-term care facilities. This agency is responsible for licensing, inspecting, and investigating complaints against nursing homes, Homes for the Aged, and Adult Foster Care homes.

| **Mailing Address (BCHS)** | Bureau of Community and Health Systems, PO Box 30664, Lansing, MI 48909 [8] |

### Role and Regulatory Functions

LARA/BCHS is the primary enforcement authority for quality of care and safety standards in licensed facilities.

#### Licensing and Inspection

*   **Facilities Covered:** Licenses and regulates Nursing Homes, Homes for the Aged (HFA), and Adult Foster Care (AFC) homes. It is important to note that Michigan does not license facilities explicitly as "assisted living" [9].
*   **Inspections:** Conducts regular, unannounced inspections of all licensed facilities to ensure compliance with state licensing rules and federal certification requirements.
*   **Public Records:** Maintains the **Verify a License (VAL)** system, which allows the public to search for a facility's license status, view recent annual inspection reports, and review substantiated complaint findings [10].

#### Complaint Investigation

*   **Process:** BCHS receives complaints via its online form, hotline, or mail. Complaints are triaged based on urgency, with those alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety investigated within 24 to 48 hours [11].
*   **Enforcement:** If a complaint is substantiated, the facility is issued a citation and must submit a Plan of Correction. Penalties can range from fines and directed plans of correction to admission bans and, in severe cases, license revocation.

## 3. Michigan Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP)

The **Michigan Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP)** is an independent, federally mandated advocacy program dedicated to protecting the rights and well-being of residents in long-term care facilities. It is administered by the **Michigan Elder Justice Initiative (MEJI)**.

### Key Contact Information

| Contact Type | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Program Name** | Michigan Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (MLTCOP) [12] |
| **Toll-Free Hotline** | **(866) 485-9393** |
| **Official Website** | [https://mltcop.org/](https://mltcop.org/) |
| **Administering Agency** | Michigan Elder Justice Initiative (MEJI) [13] |

### Role and Advocacy Services

The MLTCOP's role is distinct from that of the regulatory authority (LARA/BCHS). The ombudsman is an advocate, not an enforcer, and all services are free and confidential.

#### Resident-Directed Advocacy

*   **Confidentiality:** The ombudsman only takes action with the resident's consent, and all communication is strictly confidential.
*   **Scope:** Advocates for residents in Nursing Homes, Homes for the Aged, and Adult Foster Care homes.
*   **Services:** Investigates and resolves complaints related to quality of care, resident rights violations, facility environment, and financial issues. They act as a mediator between the resident and the facility [14].

#### Rights Education and Systemic Change

*   **Education:** Provides information and training to residents, families, and staff on the **Michigan Residents' Bill of Rights** and federal rights, empowering residents to advocate for themselves.
*   **Systemic Advocacy:** Identifies patterns of problems within the long-term care system and advocates for changes in state laws, regulations, and policies to improve the overall quality of life for all residents.

## 4. Michigan Veterans Affairs Agency (MVAA)

The **Michigan Veterans Affairs Agency (MVAA)** is the central coordinating agency for all state-level benefits, support, and advocacy for Michigan's veterans and their families.

| **Toll-Free Phone** | **(800) 642-4838** (800-MICH-VET) |
| **Official Website** | [https://www.michigan.gov/mvaa](https://www.michigan.gov/mvaa) |
| **Mailing Address** | 3423 N. Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd. Bldg 32, Lansing, MI 48906 [16] |

### Role and Veterans' Programs

The MVAA connects veterans to the benefits they have earned, serving as a navigator for both federal and state programs.

#### Financial and Emergency Assistance

*   **Michigan Veterans Trust Fund (MVTF):** The MVAA oversees the MVTF, which provides emergency grants to veterans and their dependents facing temporary financial hardship due to unforeseen circumstances (e.g., medical bills, housing crises) [17].
*   **Veteran Service Officers (VSOs):** The MVAA connects veterans with accredited VSOs who provide free assistance with filing claims for federal VA benefits, including disability compensation and pension.

#### Long-Term Care and Housing

*   **Michigan Veteran Homes (MVH):** The MVAA oversees the three state-of-the-art Michigan Veteran Homes (located in Grand Rapids, Chesterfield Township, and Marquette), which provide skilled nursing care and a high quality of life for eligible veterans [18].
*   **Property Tax Exemption:** The MVAA provides information and assistance regarding the **Disabled Veterans Property Tax Exemption**, which grants a complete exemption from property taxes for the homestead of a 100% service-connected disabled veteran or their un-remarried surviving spouse [19].

#### Education and Employment

*   **Education:** Assists veterans in utilizing federal GI Bill benefits and provides information on state-specific educational assistance programs, such as the Michigan National Guard State Tuition Assistance Program (MINGSTAP).
*   **Employment:** Advocates for veterans' preference in public employment and assists with translating military skills to civilian careers.

## Conclusion

The State of Michigan has established a robust network of agencies to support its citizens, particularly those requiring long-term care and its honored veterans. The MDHHS manages the critical financial and service aspects of Medicaid, while LARA/BCHS ensures regulatory compliance and safety through licensing and investigation. The MLTCOP provides independent, confidential advocacy, and the MVAA serves as the central hub for all veterans' benefits. By utilizing the verified contact information and understanding the specific roles of these authorities, Michigan residents can effectively navigate the system and access the high-quality care, support, and benefits they deserve.
