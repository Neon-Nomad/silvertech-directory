# Mississippi Medicaid Programs: A Comprehensive Guide to Eligibility, Benefits, and Long-Term Care

The Mississippi Division of Medicaid (DOM) is the state agency responsible for administering the Mississippi Medicaid program, which provides essential health coverage to eligible low-income adults, children, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities [1]. Known officially as **Mississippi Medicaid**, the program is a critical lifeline for over 600,000 residents, ensuring access to a wide range of medical services and vital long-term care supports [2].

This comprehensive guide delves into the structure of Mississippi Medicaid, outlining the various eligibility pathways, the scope of covered services, and the crucial Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers that allow many Mississippians to receive care in their homes and communities rather than in institutions.

## I. Overview of Mississippi Medicaid

The Mississippi Medicaid program is jointly funded by the state and federal governments, operating under the oversight of the **Mississippi Division of Medicaid** [1]. The agency’s mission is to responsibly provide access to quality health coverage for vulnerable Mississippians [3].

### General Eligibility Categories

Mississippi Medicaid offers coverage through various categories, each with its own set of financial and non-financial requirements. The primary groups covered include:

*   **Children:** Health benefits are provided for children from birth to age 19. Infants born to a Medicaid-eligible mother automatically receive benefits for one subsequent year [4].
*   **Pregnant Women:** Coverage is provided, and women receive benefits for twelve months postpartum [4].
*   **Low-Income Families:** Parents and caretaker relatives with very low incomes may qualify. In Mississippi, the income limit for parents is notably low, set at approximately 28% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) [5].
*   **Aged, Blind, or Disabled (ABD):** Individuals who are 65 or older, blind, or have a qualifying disability are eligible, often through pathways tied to Supplemental Security Income (SSI) [4].

Covered services for those with full Medicaid benefits, provided they are medically necessary, include: office visits, inpatient and outpatient hospital care, prescription drugs, laboratory and X-ray services, family planning services, and long-term care services [6].

## II. Long-Term Care (LTC) Programs

For many elderly and disabled Mississippians, the most critical aspect of the Medicaid program is its coverage for long-term care. This includes both institutional care, such as nursing facilities, and the increasingly popular Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers.

### The Pre-Admission Screening (PAS) Process

Before an eligible beneficiary can enter most Long-Term Care programs, including nursing homes and HCBS waivers, they must undergo a **Pre-Admission Screening (PAS)** [3]. This process is completed by a health care professional and is designed to determine the applicant's medical necessity for long-term care.

*   **Required Threshold:** A score of 50 or above on the PAS application is the required threshold to be considered for entry into the Long Term Care programs [3].
*   **Exemptions:** Hospice and Intermediate Care Facilities for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID) are exempt from this specific PAS requirement [3].

### Institutional Care

Mississippi Medicaid covers several types of institutional long-term care:

1.  **Nursing Facilities (NF):** Medicaid covers the cost of care in certified nursing facilities for beneficiaries who meet the medical necessity criteria established by the PAS process [3]. All Medicaid-certified nursing facilities are surveyed and receive a case mix review annually [3].
2.  **Intermediate Care Facilities for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID):** These facilities provide comprehensive and individualized health care and rehabilitation services to people with intellectual disabilities. The Office of Mental Health is responsible for the policy, monitoring, and enforcement of standards for these facilities [3].

### Financial Eligibility for Long-Term Care (2025)

Medicaid’s financial eligibility rules for long-term care are distinct from those for general coverage. These rules are particularly strict for Nursing Home Medicaid and HCBS waivers.

*   **Income Limit:** For a single applicant in 2025, the monthly income limit is set at **$2,901** (300% of the Federal Benefit Rate for SSI) [7]. If an applicant's income exceeds this limit, they may still qualify by depositing the excess income into a Qualified Income Trust (QIT), also known as a Miller Trust [7].
*   **Asset Limit:** The asset limit for a single applicant is very low, set at **$4,000** [7]. Certain assets are considered "non-countable" or exempt, such as the applicant's primary residence (up to a certain equity value), one vehicle, and personal belongings [7].
*   **Spousal Impoverishment Rules:** For married couples where only one spouse is applying for long-term care Medicaid, the non-applicant spouse (Community Spouse) is allowed to keep a significant portion of the couple's joint assets and income to prevent "spousal impoverishment" [7].

## III. Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) Waivers

The Mississippi Division of Medicaid administers five key HCBS waivers, which are designed to provide services in a person's home or community, offering a crucial alternative to institutionalization. These programs are vital for maintaining the independence and quality of life for many Mississippians [3].

### 1. Assisted Living (AL) Waiver

The AL Waiver is designed to provide services in a Medicaid-approved assisted living facility, serving as an alternative to nursing facility care [8].

*   **Target Population:** Individuals aged 21 or older who are disabled, or those aged 65 or older [9].
*   **Eligibility:** Applicants must meet the financial eligibility criteria for Medicaid and require a level of care found in a nursing facility, as determined by the PAS [3].
*   **Services Provided:** The waiver covers the cost of assisted living services, which typically include personal care assistance, medication management, and 24-hour supervision, but it does not cover the cost of room and board [9].

### 2. Elderly and Disabled (E&D) Waiver

The E&D Waiver is the most widely utilized HCBS program for seniors and individuals with physical disabilities, allowing them to remain in their homes [10].

*   **Target Population:** Individuals aged 21 or older who are elderly or physically disabled [10].
*   **Eligibility:** Must meet Medicaid financial eligibility and require a nursing facility level of care [3].
*   **Services Provided:** This waiver offers a comprehensive suite of services, including:
    *   **Case Management:** Coordination of all services.
    *   **Personal Care Services:** Assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs) like bathing, dressing, and eating.
    *   **In-Home Respite Care:** Temporary relief for primary caregivers.
    *   **Adult Day Care:** Supervised care in a community setting.
    *   **Extended Home Health Services:** Skilled nursing and therapy services beyond standard Medicaid limits [11].

### 3. Independent Living (IL) Waiver

The IL Waiver is specifically tailored for individuals with severe physical impairments, focusing on services that promote maximum independence [12].

*   **Target Population:** Individuals aged 16 or older with severe orthopedic and/or neurological impairments [12].
*   **Eligibility:** Must be Medicaid eligible and require a nursing facility level of care [3].
*   **Services Provided:** Services are geared toward self-direction and independence, and may include:
    *   **Personal Care Services:** Assistance with ADLs.
    *   **Environmental Accessibility Adaptations:** Home modifications such as ramps and widened doorways.
    *   **Specialized Medical Equipment and Supplies:** Items necessary for daily function.
    *   **Case Management:** To coordinate the complex needs of the participants [13].

### 4. Intellectual Disability/Developmentally Disabled (ID/DD) Waiver

This waiver is crucial for individuals with intellectual or developmental disabilities, providing the necessary supports to live outside of an institutional setting [14].

*   **Target Population:** Individuals of all ages who have an intellectual or developmental disability [14].
*   **Eligibility:** Applicants must require a level of care found in an Intermediate Care Facility for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID) [15].
*   **Services Provided:** The ID/DD Waiver offers a wide range of services, including:
    *   **Residential Habilitation:** Support for daily living in a community residence.
    *   **Day Services:** Vocational and non-vocational activities.
    *   **Supported Employment:** Assistance with finding and maintaining a job.
    *   **Therapies:** Physical, occupational, and speech therapy [14].

### 5. Traumatic Brain/Spinal Cord Injury (TBI/SCI) Waiver

The TBI/SCI Waiver addresses the unique and often intensive needs of individuals who have sustained a traumatic brain injury or spinal cord injury [16].

*   **Target Population:** Individuals who have a traumatic brain injury or a spinal cord injury and are medically stable [16].
*   **Eligibility:** Must be a Mississippi resident, medically stable, and meet the financial criteria for full Medicaid benefits [17].
*   **Services Provided:** Services are focused on rehabilitation and long-term support, such as:
    *   **Cognitive Rehabilitation Therapy:** To improve memory, attention, and problem-solving skills.
    *   **Behavioral Support Services:** To manage challenging behaviors.
    *   **Specialized Medical Equipment and Supplies:** Including wheelchairs and communication devices.
    *   **Transitional Living Services:** To help individuals move from an institution back into the community [16].

## IV. The Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)

Mississippi, like all states, is required by federal law to operate a Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP) [18]. This program seeks to recover the costs of certain Medicaid services paid on behalf of a beneficiary after their death.

*   **Recoverable Costs:** The state can seek recovery for all Medicaid payments made for long-term care services, including nursing facility services and HCBS waiver services, for beneficiaries who were 55 years of age or older when they received the services [19].
*   **The Estate:** In Mississippi, the "estate" subject to recovery includes all real and personal property and other assets passing from the deceased beneficiary by will or intestacy [19].
*   **Waivers and Exemptions:** The claim for recovery must be waived if there is a surviving spouse or a surviving dependent who is under the age of 21 or is blind or permanently and totally disabled [18]. Furthermore, recovery may be postponed if the deceased's home is the primary residence of a sibling who has an equity interest in the home and was residing there for at least one year immediately before the beneficiary’s death [18].

## V. Conclusion

Mississippi Medicaid, administered by the Division of Medicaid, is a complex but essential system that provides a safety net for the state's most vulnerable populations. From covering basic health needs for children and families to offering comprehensive long-term care through its five HCBS waivers, the program is designed to promote health, independence, and quality of life. Understanding the specific eligibility requirements, the PAS process, and the services offered by each waiver is the first step for Mississippians seeking to navigate this vital resource.
