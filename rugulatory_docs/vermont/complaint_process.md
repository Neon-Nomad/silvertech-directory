# Vermont Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Reporting Concerns and Ensuring Accountability

The state of Vermont is committed to ensuring that all residents receiving long-term care services are treated with dignity, respect, and receive high-quality care. To uphold this commitment, the state has established clear and accessible channels for reporting concerns, complaints, and suspected instances of abuse, neglect, or exploitation. The primary authority for investigating complaints against licensed long-term care facilities is the **Division of Licensing and Protection (DLP)**, operating under the Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL) [1].

This guide provides a detailed overview of the official complaint process in Vermont, outlining the roles of key agencies, the types of complaints they handle, and the steps individuals should take to report a concern effectively.

## Key Agencies in the Vermont Complaint Process

Multiple state agencies play a role in the long-term care complaint system, depending on the nature and setting of the complaint.

### 1. Division of Licensing and Protection (DLP)

The DLP is the **State Survey Agency** responsible for licensing and certifying nursing homes, residential care homes (RCHs), and assisted living residences (ALRs).

*   **Primary Role:** The DLP investigates complaints related to violations of state licensing regulations and federal certification requirements (Conditions of Participation) for Medicare and Medicaid facilities. This includes issues concerning quality of care, resident rights, staffing levels, and the physical environment of the facility [2].
*   **Complaint Hotline:** The DLP maintains a dedicated, toll-free complaint hotline for reporting concerns about licensed facilities: **(888) 700-5330** [3].

### 2. Vermont Long-Term Care Ombudsman Project (VOP)

The VOP, administered by Vermont Legal Aid, is an independent advocacy program that works to resolve complaints made by or on behalf of residents.

*   **Primary Role:** The VOP focuses on advocating for the rights of residents and mediating disputes between residents and facilities. While they do not have regulatory authority to issue citations, they are often the first point of contact for residents and families and can refer regulatory complaints to the DLP [4].
*   **Contact:** **(800) 889-2047**

### 3. Adult Protective Services (APS)

APS, also housed within the Department of Disabilities, Aging and Independent Living (DAIL), is responsible for investigating allegations of abuse, neglect, and exploitation of vulnerable adults.

*   **Primary Role:** APS intervenes when a vulnerable adult is suspected of being harmed by a specific person or persons, whether in a facility or in the community. This is distinct from the DLP's focus on facility-wide regulatory compliance [5].
*   **Contact:** **(800) 564-1612** (Vermont Adult Abuse Hotline)

## The Official Complaint Process with the DLP

The process for filing a complaint with the Division of Licensing and Protection is designed to be straightforward and confidential.

### Step 1: Filing the Complaint

A complaint can be filed with the DLP via phone, mail, or through their online portal.

*   **Phone:** Call the toll-free complaint hotline at **(888) 700-5330**. This is often the quickest way to report an urgent concern.
*   **Online:** The DLP website provides a form or instructions for submitting a complaint electronically [6].
*   **Information to Provide:** To ensure a thorough investigation, the complainant should provide as much detail as possible, including:
    *   The name and address of the facility.
    *   The name of the resident involved (if applicable).
    *   A detailed description of the incident or concern, including dates, times, and names of staff involved.
    *   The names and contact information of any witnesses.
    *   The complainant's name and contact information (though anonymous complaints are accepted).

### Step 2: Complaint Investigation

Upon receiving a complaint, the DLP assesses its severity and jurisdiction.

*   **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are triaged based on the potential for immediate harm to a resident. Allegations of immediate jeopardy are investigated within two working days, while less severe complaints are investigated within a longer timeframe [7].
*   **Unannounced Survey:** The DLP investigation is typically conducted through an unannounced on-site survey at the facility. Surveyors will:
    *   Interview the resident, family members, and staff.
    *   Review the resident's medical records and the facility's policies and procedures.
    *   Observe the care provided and the physical environment.
*   **Confidentiality:** The DLP is required to protect the identity of the complainant and the resident involved, unless the complainant gives explicit permission to use their name or a court order requires disclosure.

### Step 3: Findings and Enforcement

If the investigation confirms a violation of state or federal regulations, the DLP issues a **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD)** to the facility.

*   **Plan of Correction (POC):** The facility must submit a written Plan of Correction, detailing the steps they will take to correct the deficiency and prevent recurrence.
*   **Enforcement Actions:** The severity of the deficiency determines the enforcement action. Penalties can range from:
    *   Required staff training.
    *   Civil monetary penalties (fines).
    *   Denial of payment for new admissions.
    *   In extreme cases of persistent non-compliance or severe harm, the DLP may recommend termination of the facility's Medicare/Medicaid certification or revocation of its state license [8].
*   **Notification:** The complainant is notified of the findings of the investigation, though specific details may be limited due to privacy laws.

## Complaints for Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS)

The complaint process also extends to services provided in the community, such as those under the Choices for Care (CFC) Medicaid waiver.

*   **Agency to Contact:** Concerns about the quality or delivery of HCBS services (e.g., personal care, case management) should be directed to the **Department of Vermont Health Access (DVHA)**, which administers the Medicaid program, or the local **Area Agency on Aging (AAA)**, which often coordinates the services [9].
*   **Ombudsman Role:** The Long-Term Care Ombudsman also has a role in advocating for individuals receiving HCBS, particularly concerning their rights and the implementation of their Person-Centered Service Plan.

## Distinguishing Between Complaint Types

It is important for the public to understand which agency is best suited to handle a specific type of complaint:

| Complaint Type | Primary Agency | Contact Information |

## The Importance of Documentation and Follow-Up

For any complaint, thorough documentation is essential to support the investigation.

*   **Keep a Log:** Maintain a detailed log of all incidents, including dates, times, locations, names of staff involved, and the resident's condition before and after the incident.
*   **Communicate with the Facility:** While not required, attempting to resolve the issue directly with the facility's administration or social worker is often a good first step. Document these attempts.
*   **Follow-Up with the Agency:** After filing a complaint, follow up with the DLP or the VOP to confirm receipt and inquire about the status of the investigation.

## Conclusion: Accountability Through Action

The complaint process in Vermont is a vital mechanism for ensuring accountability and maintaining high standards of care in long-term care settings. By providing multiple, accessible avenues for reporting concerns—from the regulatory authority of the DLP to the independent advocacy of the Ombudsman and the protective services of APS—the state empowers residents and their families to act as partners in oversight. Understanding and utilizing the **(888) 700-5330** DLP hotline is a critical step for any Vermonter seeking to address serious concerns about the quality of care in a licensed facility.
