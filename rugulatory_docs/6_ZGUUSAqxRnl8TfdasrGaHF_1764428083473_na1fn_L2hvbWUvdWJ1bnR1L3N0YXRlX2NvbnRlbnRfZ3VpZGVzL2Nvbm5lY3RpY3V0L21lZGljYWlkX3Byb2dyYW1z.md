# Connecticut Medicaid Programs: Your Comprehensive Guide to HUSKY Health and Long-Term Care

The state of Connecticut provides essential healthcare and long-term services and supports (LTSS) through its Medicaid program, known as **HUSKY Health**. For older adults and individuals with disabilities requiring long-term care, the primary pathways are through HUSKY C (Medicaid for the Aged, Blind, and Disabled) and the critical **Connecticut Home Care Program for Elders (CHCPE)**, which operates as a Medicaid waiver. Understanding these programs is the first step toward securing financial assistance for nursing home care, assisted living services, or in-home support.

## HUSKY Health: The Foundation of Connecticut's Medicaid System

HUSKY Health is the umbrella term for Connecticut’s Medicaid and Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP). While HUSKY A, B, and D cover children, low-income adults, and parents, **HUSKY C** is the category most relevant for long-term care planning. It provides coverage for Connecticut residents who are 65 years of age or older, or who are blind or disabled, and who meet specific financial and functional eligibility criteria.

### Financial Eligibility Requirements for HUSKY C (Long-Term Care)

Medicaid is a means-tested program, meaning applicants must demonstrate financial need by meeting strict limits on both income and countable assets. These limits are subject to change annually and vary depending on whether the applicant is seeking institutional care (like a nursing home) or home and community-based services (like the CHCPE waiver).

| Financial Category | Applicant Asset Limit (2025 Estimate) | Community Spouse Asset Allowance (CSRA) (2025 Estimate) | Income Limit (Nursing Home/Institutional) (2025 Estimate) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Individual Applicant** | **$1,600** | N/A | $2,829 per month (300% of SSI) |
| **Married Couple (Both Applying)** | **$3,200** | N/A | $5,658 per month (300% of SSI for two) |
| **Married Couple (One Spouse Applying)** | **$1,600** (Applicant) | Up to **$157,920** (Community Spouse) | $2,829 per month (Applicant) |

**Key Financial Rules to Note:**

*   **Asset Limit:** The non-exempt asset limit for an individual is exceptionally low at **$1,600** [1]. Exempt assets typically include the primary residence (up to a certain equity limit), one vehicle, household goods, and certain burial funds.
*   **Income Limit:** For institutional Medicaid (nursing home care), the income limit is set at 300% of the Federal Supplemental Security Income (SSI) benefit, which is approximately **$2,829 per month** in 2025 [2]. Applicants whose income exceeds this limit may still qualify through a "Medically Needy" or "Spend-Down" program, where they spend the excess income on medical expenses until they reach the income threshold.
*   **Spousal Impoverishment Rules:** When one spouse requires long-term care and the other remains in the community (the "Community Spouse"), federal and state rules protect the Community Spouse from becoming impoverished. The Community Spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined assets, known as the Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA), which can be up to **$157,920** in 2025 [3]. They may also be entitled to a Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA) from the applicant's income.
*   **The 5-Year Look-Back Period:** Connecticut, like all states, enforces a 60-month (5-year) look-back period for all asset transfers made for less than fair market value. Any uncompensated transfers during this period will result in a penalty period of Medicaid ineligibility.

### The Connecticut Home Care Program for Elders (CHCPE)

The CHCPE is Connecticut's most vital program for seniors seeking to remain in their homes or a community setting, such as an assisted living facility, rather than entering a nursing home. It is a **Medicaid Waiver** program, meaning it waives the requirement for institutional care to provide services in a less restrictive setting.

**Eligibility for CHCPE:**

1.  **Age:** Must be 65 years of age or older.
2.  **Residency:** Must be a resident of Connecticut.
3.  **Functional Need:** Must be at risk of nursing home placement, as determined by a comprehensive assessment of functional and medical needs.
4.  **Financial Eligibility:**
    *   **Medicaid Waiver (Category 3):** Applicants must meet the same income and asset limits as institutional Medicaid (300% of SSI and $1,600 in assets). This is the most common pathway for long-term care.
    *   **State-Funded (Category 1 & 2):** For those who are not Medicaid-eligible, the state offers limited funding for the program with higher financial limits, though this pathway often has a waiting list.

**Services Covered by CHCPE:**

The CHCPE is designed to provide a comprehensive suite of services that support independent living. The services are determined by a case manager based on the individual's assessed needs and may include [4]:

| Service Category | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Care & Health** | Home Health Aides, Visiting Nurse Services, Personal Care Assistance, Adult Day Health Services. |
| **Homemaker & Chore Services** | Homemaker services, Chore assistance, Companion services, Minor home modifications. |
| **Nutrition & Support** | Home Delivered Meals, Nutritional Counseling. |
| **Emergency & Technology** | Personal Emergency Response Systems (PERS), Telehealth monitoring. |
| **Case Management** | Comprehensive assessment, service coordination, and ongoing monitoring by a dedicated case manager. |
| **Assisted Living Services** | Services provided in a managed residential community (Assisted Living Facility) that contracts with the program. |

The program aims to create a personalized care plan that is cost-effective and prevents or delays the need for institutionalization.

## Applying for Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS)

The application process for HUSKY C and the CHCPE waiver is managed by the **Connecticut Department of Social Services (DSS)**.

### Step-by-Step Application Process:

1.  **Determine the Need:** The applicant must first be assessed as needing a nursing home level of care. This is typically done by the DSS or a designated Area Agency on Aging (AAA).
2.  **Complete the Application:** The primary application form is the **W-1LTSS (Application for Long-Term Services and Supports)** [5]. This comprehensive packet requires detailed information on the applicant's income, assets, medical history, and household composition.
3.  **Gather Documentation:** Applicants must provide extensive documentation, including bank statements, proof of income, insurance policies, deeds, and evidence of all financial transactions for the 60-month look-back period.
4.  **Submission:** The completed W-1LTSS application and all supporting documents must be submitted to the appropriate **Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS) Application Center**. There are regional centers that handle applications for nursing home care (SNFs and ICFs) and a centralized center for home- and community-based services (CHCPE waiver).
5.  **Review and Determination:** DSS reviews the application for both functional (medical) and financial eligibility. This process can take several months, and applicants are strongly advised to seek assistance from an elder law attorney or a certified application counselor.

### Connecticut Long-Term Care Partnership Program

A crucial component of long-term care planning in Connecticut is the **Long-Term Care Partnership Program**. This program is a collaboration between the state and private insurance companies designed to encourage residents to purchase private long-term care insurance. The primary benefit of participating in this program is **asset protection** under Medicaid.

#### How the Partnership Program Works:

1.  **Purchase a Qualified Policy:** An individual purchases a long-term care insurance policy that meets specific state and federal requirements (a "Partnership-qualified" policy).
2.  **Dollar-for-Dollar Asset Protection:** For every dollar the policy pays out in benefits, a dollar of the individual's assets is disregarded for the purpose of determining Medicaid eligibility. This is known as "asset disregard."
3.  **Protection from Estate Recovery:** Assets protected under the Partnership Program are also protected from the state's Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP).

For example, if a Partnership policy pays out $300,000 in benefits, the individual can keep $300,000 in assets and still qualify for Medicaid, even though the standard asset limit is only $1,600. This program is a powerful tool for middle-income residents who wish to protect their savings while ensuring they have access to long-term care services when needed.

## Medicaid Estate Recovery in Connecticut

Connecticut is required by federal law to seek recovery of funds spent on long-term care services from the estates of deceased Medicaid recipients. This process is known as the **Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)**.

### What is Subject to Recovery?

The state seeks to recover the cost of all Medicaid-paid services received after the recipient turned 55 years old, including:

*   Nursing facility services.
*   Home and community-based services (CHCPE).
*   Hospital and prescription drug services received while an inpatient in a nursing facility.

The "estate" for recovery purposes is defined broadly in Connecticut and may include assets that pass outside of probate, such as property held in joint tenancy or life estates, depending on state law and the date the services were received.

### Exemptions and Hardship Waivers

Federal law mandates certain exemptions to the recovery process, which Connecticut adheres to [6]:

*   **Surviving Spouse:** Recovery is deferred if the Medicaid recipient is survived by a spouse. The state may seek recovery from the spouse's estate after their death.
*   **Minor or Disabled Child:** Recovery is prohibited if the recipient is survived by a child who is under age 21 or who is blind or permanently disabled.
*   **Hardship Waiver:** The state may waive recovery if it would cause an undue hardship, such as when the estate is the sole income-producing asset of the heirs (e.g., a family farm or business) or when the property has been the primary residence of the heir for a significant period.

Understanding the MERP is crucial for long-term care planning, as it impacts the inheritance of the recipient's family.

## The Medically Needy (Spend-Down) Program

For individuals whose income exceeds the institutional Medicaid limit (300% of SSI), Connecticut offers the **Medically Needy Program**, often referred to as the "Spend-Down" program. This program allows applicants to qualify for Medicaid by incurring medical expenses that "spend down" their excess income to the eligibility level.

### How the Spend-Down Works:

1.  **Determine Excess Income:** The applicant's monthly income is compared to the Medically Needy Income Limit (MNIL). The difference is the "excess income" or "spend-down liability."
2.  **Incur Medical Expenses:** The applicant must incur medical expenses (e.g., doctor bills, prescription costs, nursing home charges) equal to their excess income.
3.  **Medicaid Coverage:** Once the applicant has met their spend-down liability for the month, Medicaid will cover the remaining eligible medical expenses for that month.

This program is particularly important for individuals entering a nursing home whose income is too high for regular Medicaid but not high enough to cover the full cost of care.

## The Role of Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs) and My Place CT

Connecticut's long-term care system is supported by a network of **Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs)** and the **My Place CT** initiative, which serves as the state's Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC).

### Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs)

AAAs are non-profit organizations that serve as local planning and service delivery points for older adults and their caregivers. In the context of long-term care, AAAs play a crucial role in:

*   **Pre-Screening and Assessment:** AAAs often conduct the initial pre-screening for the CHCPE and other home- and community-based services to determine if an individual meets the functional eligibility criteria (i.e., at risk of nursing home placement).
*   **Information and Referral:** They provide unbiased information about all available long-term care options, including Medicaid, state-funded programs, and private resources.
*   **Case Management:** For some state-funded programs, AAAs may provide case management services, helping to coordinate the delivery of services like home health aides, adult day care, and transportation.

### My Place CT

My Place CT is a partnership between the DSS, DPH, and the AAAs, designed to simplify access to long-term services and supports. It functions as a single point of entry for information and assistance, helping individuals and families understand their options and apply for programs like the CHCPE.
