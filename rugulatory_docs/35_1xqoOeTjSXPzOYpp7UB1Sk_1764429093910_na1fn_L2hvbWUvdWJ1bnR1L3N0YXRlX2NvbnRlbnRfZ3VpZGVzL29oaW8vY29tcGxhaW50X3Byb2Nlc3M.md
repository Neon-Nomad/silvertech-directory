# Navigating the System: The Complaint Process for Long-Term Care Facilities in Ohio

Ensuring the safety and quality of care for residents in Ohio's long-term care facilities is a shared responsibility between state regulatory bodies, advocacy groups, and the public. The formal complaint process, managed primarily by the **Ohio Department of Health (ODH)**, is a critical mechanism for identifying and correcting deficiencies in nursing homes and other healthcare facilities. This comprehensive guide provides a detailed, step-by-step overview of how to file a complaint in Ohio, what to expect during the investigation, and the distinct roles of the regulatory authority and the Long-Term Care Ombudsman in the process.

## The Ohio Department of Health: The Regulatory Complaint Authority

The ODH, through its **Bureau of Survey and Certification**, is the official state agency responsible for receiving, processing, and investigating formal complaints against licensed healthcare facilities, including:

*   **Nursing Homes/Skilled Nursing Facilities**
*   **Residential Care Facilities (Assisted Living)**
*   **Hospices**
*   **Hospitals**

The ODH's role is regulatory; their investigations are focused on determining whether a facility is in compliance with state licensing laws and federal certification requirements [1].

### How to File a Complaint with the ODH

The ODH encourages the public to report concerns about the quality of care, resident rights, or safety issues. The process is designed to be accessible and confidential.

#### 1. Contact the ODH Complaint Unit

The most direct way to file a complaint is through the ODH Complaint Unit.

*   **Toll-Free Complaint Hotline:** **(800) 342-0553**
    *   This hotline is available 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with voicemail coverage during off-hours and holidays. Messages are checked regularly, and urgent complaints are prioritized.
*   **Online Complaint Form:** The ODH maintains a dedicated webpage for filing complaints electronically. This method allows the complainant to provide detailed information and attach supporting documents [1].
*   **Written Complaint:** Complaints can also be submitted in writing via mail to the ODH's central office.

#### 2. Information to Include in the Complaint

To ensure a thorough and timely investigation, the complainant should provide as much detail as possible. Key information includes:

*   **Facility Information:** Full name and address of the facility.
*   **Resident Information:** The name of the resident (if the complaint is on their behalf).
*   **Details of the Incident:** A clear, chronological description of the incident(s), including dates, times, and locations.
*   **Individuals Involved:** Names and titles of any staff members, residents, or witnesses involved.
*   **Harm or Potential Harm:** A description of the injury or negative outcome the resident experienced or was at risk of experiencing.
*   **Complainant's Identity:** The complainant may choose to remain anonymous. However, providing contact information allows the ODH to follow up for clarification and to inform the complainant of the investigation's outcome.

#### 3. Confidentiality and Retaliation

The ODH is legally required to protect the identity of the complainant and the resident from the facility, unless the complainant provides written permission to release their name. Furthermore, Ohio law strictly prohibits a facility from retaliating against a resident, employee, or other person who files a complaint or cooperates with an investigation [2].

## The Complaint Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, the ODH follows a structured process to determine if a violation of law or regulation has occurred.

### 1. Triage and Prioritization

Complaints are immediately reviewed and categorized based on the severity of the alleged violation:

*   **Immediate Jeopardy (IJ):** Allegations that place a resident's health or safety in immediate, serious danger. These complaints are investigated **within two working days** [3].
*   **High Priority:** Allegations of actual harm that is not immediate jeopardy. These are typically investigated within ten working days.
*   **Low Priority:** Allegations of potential for minimal harm or non-care-related issues. These may be investigated during the facility's next scheduled annual survey.

### 2. On-Site Investigation (Survey)

For most high-priority complaints, the ODH Bureau of Survey and Certification dispatches a team of surveyors to the facility for an unannounced visit.

*   **Evidence Gathering:** Surveyors interview the resident, family members, staff, and witnesses. They review medical records, care plans, facility policies, and observation logs. They also conduct direct observation of the care provided and the facility environment.
*   **Focus on Substantial Compliance:** The investigation's goal is to determine if the facility is in "substantial compliance" with state and federal regulations.
*   **Substantiation:** A complaint is "substantiated" if the investigation finds that the facility violated a regulation that directly relates to the complaint's allegations.

### 3. Findings and Enforcement

If the complaint is substantiated, the ODH issues a **Statement of Deficiencies** (Form CMS-2567) to the facility, citing the specific regulations that were violated.

*   **Plan of Correction (POC):** The facility is required to submit a written Plan of Correction detailing the steps they will take to correct the deficiencies and prevent recurrence.
*   **Enforcement Action:** Depending on the scope and severity of the deficiencies, the ODH and the federal Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) may impose sanctions, such as Civil Monetary Penalties (fines), denial of payment for new admissions, or, in severe cases, termination of the facility's Medicare/Medicaid provider agreement [4].
*   **Follow-up:** The ODH conducts a follow-up visit to ensure the facility has successfully implemented the Plan of Correction.

## The Role of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman in Complaints

While the ODH handles the formal, regulatory complaint process, the **Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman** provides a vital, complementary service focused on advocacy and informal resolution.

| Feature | ODH Complaint Process | Ombudsman Advocacy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Goal** | Regulatory compliance and enforcement | Resident-directed resolution and advocacy |
| **Authority** | State and federal law, licensing/certification | Persuasion, mediation, and resident rights |
| **Confidentiality** | Complainant's identity protected from facility | All information is confidential and resident-directed |
| **Focus** | Violation of a specific regulation | Any issue affecting quality of life or rights |
| **Contact** | **(800) 342-0553** (Hotline) | **(800) 282-1206** (Toll-Free) |

The Ombudsman is often the best first point of contact for a resident or family member. They can:

1.  **Provide Information:** Explain the resident's rights and the facility's obligations.
2.  **Attempt Informal Resolution:** Often, the Ombudsman can resolve the issue quickly and confidentially by mediating between the resident and the facility staff.
3.  **Refer to ODH:** If the issue is serious (e.g., suspected abuse or neglect) or cannot be resolved informally, the Ombudsman will assist the complainant in filing a formal complaint with the ODH.

## Accessing Complaint and Inspection Records

Transparency is a key component of the complaint process. The public has the right to access the results of ODH investigations.

*   **Nursing Home Compare:** The federal CMS website, **Nursing Home Compare**, provides detailed reports of all substantiated complaints and the resulting deficiencies for Medicare/Medicaid certified nursing homes [5].
*   **ODH Health Care Provider and Services Webpage:** This ODH resource allows the public to search for specific facilities and view their licensing and certification status, as well as access certain regulatory records [1].

By utilizing both the formal regulatory process of the ODH and the advocacy services of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, Ohio residents and their families have a robust system in place to ensure accountability and high-quality care in long-term care settings.

## In-Depth Look at the Investigation and Enforcement Cycle

The effectiveness of the complaint process hinges on the thoroughness of the investigation and the subsequent enforcement actions. The ODH's process is a continuous cycle of identification, correction, and verification.

### The Statement of Deficiencies (CMS-2567)

When a complaint is substantiated, the ODH issues a formal document, the CMS-2567, which is a public record. This document is critical because it:

*   **Cites Specific Regulations:** It precisely identifies the state or federal regulation the facility violated.
*   **Describes the Findings:** It provides a narrative description of the surveyor's findings, including observations, interviews, and record reviews that led to the citation.
*   **Determines Scope and Severity:** Each deficiency is assigned a letter code (A through L) that indicates the scope (isolated, pattern, or widespread) and the severity (potential for harm, actual harm, or immediate jeopardy). This code directly influences the type and amount of penalty imposed.

### The Plan of Correction (POC)

The facility's response to the CMS-2567 is the Plan of Correction (POC). This is a legally binding document that must detail:

1.  **How the Deficiency was Corrected:** The immediate actions taken to fix the problem for the residents affected by the violation.
2.  **How Recurrence will be Prevented:** The systemic changes to policies, procedures, and staff training that will ensure the violation does not happen again.
3.  **Date of Completion:** A specific date by which the systemic changes will be fully implemented.

The ODH must review and approve the POC. If the plan is deemed insufficient, the facility must revise it.

### Enforcement and Civil Monetary Penalties (CMPs)

The most common and impactful enforcement tool is the Civil Monetary Penalty (CMP). CMPs are fines levied against the facility, and they can be substantial, often ranging from hundreds to thousands of dollars per day of non-compliance.

*   **Per-Day CMPs:** Applied for deficiencies that continue over a period of time.
*   **Per-Instance CMPs:** Applied for a single, severe violation, such as an instance of abuse.

The funds collected from CMPs are often used to benefit residents through the Civil Money Penalty Reinvestment Program (CMPRP), which funds projects that protect and improve the quality of life for nursing home residents.

## The Role of the Ombudsman in Systemic Change

The Ombudsman's work provides a unique perspective that complements the ODH's regulatory function.

### Case Examples of Ombudsman Intervention

*   **Informal Resolution:** A family complains that a resident's favorite blanket was lost and the facility is refusing to replace it. The Ombudsman intervenes, not to cite a regulation, but to mediate a solution, often resulting in the facility replacing the item and improving its personal property tracking policy.
*   **Systemic Advocacy:** The Ombudsman notices a pattern of complaints across multiple facilities regarding inadequate response times to call lights. They use this data to advocate for legislative or regulatory changes to minimum staffing requirements or to push the ODH to focus its surveys on call light response times.

The Ombudsman's ability to focus on the resident's perspective—the quality of life, not just the letter of the law—makes them an invaluable resource for issues that may not rise to the level of a regulatory violation but significantly impact a resident's well-being.

## Final Steps for the Complainant

After filing a complaint with the ODH, the complainant should:

1.  **Document Everything:** Keep a detailed log of all communication with the facility, the ODH, and the Ombudsman.
2.  **Follow Up:** If the complainant provided contact information, the ODH will notify them of the investigation's outcome. If they do not hear back within a reasonable timeframe (which varies based on the complaint's priority), they should call the hotline for an update.
3.  **Utilize Public Records:** Review the facility's inspection history on Nursing Home Compare to see the official findings and the facility's Plan of Correction.

The complaint process in Ohio is a powerful tool for accountability. By understanding the roles of the ODH and the Ombudsman, and by providing clear, detailed information, citizens can effectively advocate for the safety and dignity of long-term care residents.
