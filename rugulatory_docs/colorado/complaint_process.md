# How to File a Complaint Against a Nursing Home or Assisted Living Facility in Colorado

Ensuring the safety and quality of care for residents in long-term care facilities is a top priority for the state of Colorado. The primary regulatory body for investigating complaints against nursing homes and assisted living residences is the **Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment (CDPHE)**, specifically the **Health Facilities and Emergency Medical Services Division** [1].

This comprehensive guide outlines the official complaint process, including how to file a complaint, what information is needed, and what to expect during the investigation. Understanding this process is crucial for residents, family members, and concerned citizens who wish to report substandard care or violations of resident rights.

## The Official Complaint Authority: CDPHE

The CDPHE is the state's designated agency for receiving and investigating complaints regarding licensed health facilities. The Department's goal is to ensure that all facilities comply with state licensing regulations and federal certification requirements (for Medicare/Medicaid certified facilities).

## Step-by-Step Guide to Filing a Complaint

The CDPHE offers multiple methods for submitting a complaint, allowing complainants to choose the most convenient and comfortable option.

### 1. Online Complaint Form

The most efficient way to file a complaint is often through the CDPHE's official online complaint intake form [2]. This form guides the user through the necessary information and ensures all required details are captured.

### 2. Filing by Phone

Complaints can be reported directly to the CDPHE by calling the dedicated complaint hotline. Reporting by phone is particularly useful for urgent matters or for individuals who prefer to speak directly with an intake specialist.

### 3. Filing by Email or Mail

A complaint can also be submitted in writing via email or traditional mail. When submitting a written complaint, it is essential to include all the information detailed below to expedite the investigation process.

*   **Email:** Send the detailed complaint to **cdphe.hfdintake@state.co.us**.
*   **Mail:** Send the complaint to:
    CDPHE, Health Facilities and Emergency Medical Services Division (HFEMSD-C1)
    4300 Cherry Creek Drive South
    Denver, CO 80246-1530

## Essential Information to Include in a Complaint

To ensure a thorough and timely investigation, the complaint should be as detailed as possible. While the CDPHE accepts anonymous complaints, providing contact information allows the investigator to follow up for clarification.

| Required Information | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Facility Identification** | The full name and address of the nursing home or assisted living residence. |
| **Resident Information** | The name of the resident involved (if applicable and if permission is given to share). |
| **Details of the Incident** | A clear, chronological description of what happened, including the date, time, and location of the incident(s). |
| **Individuals Involved** | The names and titles of any staff members, residents, or witnesses involved. |
| **Nature of the Complaint** | Specific allegations, such as neglect, abuse, medication errors, poor food quality, or violations of resident rights. |
| **Complainant Contact** | Your name, phone number, and email address (optional, but recommended for follow-up). |

> **Anonymity and Confidentiality:** The CDPHE states, "We accept anonymous complaints. If you file a complaint, our investigators don't share your name with the health care entity unless you instruct us otherwise." [3] This protection encourages individuals to report concerns without fear of retaliation.

## The Complaint Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, the CDPHE follows a structured process to investigate the allegations:

1.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are assessed for severity. Those alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are prioritized for an immediate, unannounced on-site investigation.
2.  **Unannounced Survey:** An investigator (or a team of surveyors) will arrive at the facility unannounced to conduct the investigation. This is done to observe the facility's normal operations and prevent the facility from preparing for the visit.
3.  **Evidence Gathering:** The investigation involves:
    *   Interviewing the resident, family, and staff.
    *   Reviewing medical records, staffing schedules, and facility policies.
    *   Observing the care provided and the physical environment.
4.  **Findings and Enforcement:**
    *   If the allegations are **substantiated** (found to be true), the facility is cited for a deficiency (violation of state or federal regulations).
    *   The facility must submit a **Plan of Correction (POC)** to the CDPHE, outlining how they will fix the problem and prevent it from recurring.
    *   Depending on the severity of the deficiency, the CDPHE may impose sanctions, including fines, denial of payment for new admissions, or, in extreme cases, license revocation.
5.  **Notification:** The complainant will be notified of the outcome of the investigation, though specific details of the facility's Plan of Correction may be confidential.

## Alternative Avenues for Complaint Resolution

While the CDPHE handles regulatory enforcement, other organizations can assist with complaint resolution, particularly the **Colorado State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)**.

### The Role of the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman is an advocate who works to resolve complaints from the resident's perspective.

*   **Mediation:** The Ombudsman often acts as a mediator between the resident and the facility staff to resolve issues informally and quickly.
*   **Advocacy:** They ensure the resident's rights are protected and can assist the resident in filing a formal complaint with the CDPHE if necessary.
*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman maintains strict confidentiality and will only proceed with a complaint with the resident's permission.

Residents and families are encouraged to contact the Ombudsman at **(303) 862-3524** for confidential advice and assistance with concerns that may not require a formal regulatory investigation.

## Preventing Future Issues

The complaint process is a reactive measure. Proactive steps can help prevent issues from arising:

*   **Regular Visits:** Frequent, unannounced visits to the facility allow family members to observe the quality of care firsthand.
*   **Open Communication:** Maintaining an open and respectful line of communication with facility staff and administration can resolve minor issues before they escalate.
*   **Know Your Rights:** Residents and families should be familiar with the **Resident Bill of Rights** to recognize when a violation has occurred.

By utilizing the official channels provided by the CDPHE and the advocacy services of the Ombudsman, Coloradans can play an active role in maintaining high standards of care in the state's long-term care facilities.
