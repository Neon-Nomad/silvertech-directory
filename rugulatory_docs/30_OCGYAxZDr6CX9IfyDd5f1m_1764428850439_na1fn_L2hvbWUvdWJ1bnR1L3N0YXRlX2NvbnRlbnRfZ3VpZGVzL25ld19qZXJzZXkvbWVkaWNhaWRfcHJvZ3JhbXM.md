# New Jersey Medicaid Programs: Comprehensive Guide to NJ FamilyCare and MLTSS

The state of New Jersey provides essential healthcare coverage and long-term services and supports through **NJ FamilyCare**, the state's publicly funded health insurance program. For elderly residents and individuals with disabilities requiring long-term care, the most critical component of this system is the **Managed Long Term Services and Supports (MLTSS)** program. This comprehensive guide details the structure, eligibility, covered services, and application process for Medicaid long-term care in the Garden State.

## Understanding NJ FamilyCare and the Role of MLTSS

NJ FamilyCare is New Jersey's version of Medicaid, which is a joint federal and state program. It provides free or low-cost health coverage to qualified low-income residents of all ages [1]. For those needing assistance with daily living activities, whether at home, in an assisted living facility, or in a nursing home, the program transitions into the MLTSS model.

The **Managed Long Term Services and Supports (MLTSS)** program was implemented to expand access to home and community-based services (HCBS), promote community inclusion, and ensure the quality and efficiency of long-term care delivery [2]. Before the implementation of MLTSS, New Jersey utilized several separate Medicaid waiver programs, such as Global Options for Long Term Care (GO), which were consolidated into the single, streamlined MLTSS program [3].

Under MLTSS, all long-term services and supports are coordinated through a single Medicaid (NJ FamilyCare) health plan, known as a Managed Care Organization (MCO). This integrated approach means that the MCO is responsible for coordinating all of a member's services, including acute medical care, behavioral health, and long-term care services [2].

### The Administering Authority

The program is overseen by the **New Jersey Department of Human Services, Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services (DMAHS)**.

## Eligibility Requirements for Long-Term Care Medicaid (MLTSS)

To qualify for MLTSS, an applicant must meet both **financial** and **clinical** eligibility criteria. The requirements are generally more stringent for long-term care services than for standard NJ FamilyCare coverage.

### Financial Eligibility Criteria

Financial eligibility for long-term care Medicaid is determined based on the applicant's income and countable assets. These limits are subject to change annually, typically on January 1st. The following figures are based on the most recent available data for 2025 [3]:

#### 1. Income Limits

For an individual applicant, the income limit is set at **300% of the Federal Benefit Rate (FBR)**.

*   **Individual Applicant Income Cap (2025):** \$2,901 per month [3].
*   **Treatment of Income:** All of an applicant's income must be used toward the cost of care, except for a small Personal Needs Allowance (PNA) and any spousal allowances.
*   **Qualified Income Trusts (QIT):** New Jersey is an "Income Cap" state. If an applicant's income exceeds the cap, they may still qualify by establishing a Qualified Income Trust (also known as a Miller Trust) to hold the excess income, making them financially eligible [3].

#### 2. Asset Limits

The asset limit for long-term care Medicaid is significantly lower than the income cap.

*   **Individual Applicant Asset Limit (2025):** \$2,000 [3].
*   **Married Couple (Both Applicants) Asset Limit (2025):** \$3,000 [3].

**Exempt Assets:** Certain assets are considered "non-countable" and do not affect eligibility. These typically include:
*   The applicant's primary residence, provided the applicant has an "Intent to Return" home, or a spouse or dependent relative lives there. The home equity interest must not exceed \$1,097,000 (2025 limit) [3].
*   One vehicle.
*   Household furnishings, personal effects, and clothing.
*   Irrevocable burial trusts.

#### 3. Spousal Impoverishment Rules

When only one spouse is applying for long-term care Medicaid, federal spousal impoverishment rules protect the non-applicant spouse from becoming impoverished.

*   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The non-applicant spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined countable assets. In 2025, the CSRA allows the non-applicant spouse to keep 50% of the couple’s assets, up to a maximum of **\$157,920** [3]. If the non-applicant's share is below a minimum of **\$31,584**, they are allowed to keep the minimum amount [3].
*   **Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMNA):** The non-applicant spouse is also entitled to a portion of the applicant's income to ensure they have a minimum monthly income. In 2025, the minimum MMNA is **\$2,643.75** per month, and the maximum is **\$3,948** per month [3].

### Clinical Eligibility Criteria

In addition to financial requirements, an applicant must meet the clinical standard for the program, which is the **Nursing Facility Level of Care (NFLOC)**.

*   **Adults (Age 21 and Older):** An individual must require hands-on assistance with three or more Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, toileting, locomotion, transfers, eating, and bed mobility. Alternatively, they may qualify if they have cognitive deficits and require supervision and cueing with three or more ADLs [2].
*   **Assessment Process:** The clinical assessment is conducted by the MCOs and a final determination of clinical eligibility is made through the Division of Aging Services (DoAS) Office of Community Options (OCCO) [2].

## Covered Services Under MLTSS

The MLTSS program is designed to provide a comprehensive array of services that allow beneficiaries to remain in their homes and communities for as long as possible. The services are delivered through the chosen MCO and are tailored to the individual's specific needs and circumstances [2].

| Category | Covered Services Under MLTSS |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Core Health Benefits** | Access to all NJ FamilyCare Plan A Benefits (doctor visits, prescriptions, hospitalization, etc.) [2]. |
| **Long-Term Care Settings** | Nursing Home Care, Assisted Living, Community Residential Services [2]. |
| **Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS)** | Care Management, Home and Vehicle Modifications, Home Delivered Meals, Respite, Personal Emergency Response Systems (PERS), Mental Health and Addiction Services [2]. |
| **Assisted Living Program (ALP)** | MLTSS covers the cost of services provided in an Assisted Living Residence (ALR) or a Comprehensive Personal Care Home (CPCH). **Note:** Individuals who opt for ALP services do not receive certain other HCBS like Personal Care Assistant (PCA) services or Chore Services, as these are typically bundled into the assisted living service package [4]. |

The goal of the MLTSS program is to provide a person-centered approach, ensuring that the long-term care benefits received are specific to the member's needs. For example, a person living alone might receive home-delivered meals and a PERS, while an individual with an informal caregiver might be eligible for adult day health care and respite care to support the caregiver [3].

## The Application Process

Applying for long-term care Medicaid in New Jersey involves a multi-step process that addresses both financial and clinical eligibility.

1.  **Initial Contact and Screening:** Individuals aged 21 and older should contact their local County Area Agency on Aging (AAA) - Aging and Disability Resource Connection (ADRC) to receive information on services and to be clinically screened for MLTSS [2].
2.  **Financial Application:** The application for NJ FamilyCare can be submitted online at the NJ FamilyCare website, or through the state's social services portal. This application determines financial eligibility for the program [5].
3.  **Clinical Assessment:** If the financial criteria are met, a clinical assessment will be conducted to determine if the applicant meets the NFLOC. This assessment is crucial for enrollment into the MLTSS program [2].
4.  **MCO Enrollment:** Once approved both financially and clinically, the individual will be enrolled in MLTSS and must choose one of the participating Managed Care Organizations (MCOs).

### Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)

MLTSS services are delivered through a network of MCOs that contract with the state. As of the most recent data, the participating MCOs include [6]:

*   Aetna Better Health
*   Amerigroup New Jersey, Inc.
*   Horizon NJ Health
*   UnitedHealthcare Community Plan
*   WellCare Health Plans of New Jersey

Beneficiaries have the right to choose their MCO, and the MCO will then assign a care manager to coordinate all of the member's long-term care services.

## Medicaid Estate Recovery and Transfer of Assets

New Jersey, like all states, is required by federal law to operate a **Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)**. This program seeks to recover the costs of long-term care services paid by Medicaid from the estates of deceased beneficiaries [7].

*   **Recovery Target:** Recovery is generally sought against the estate of a Medicaid recipient who was age 55 or older when they received long-term care services, or who was permanently institutionalized [7].
*   **Exemptions:** Recovery is typically waived if the deceased recipient is survived by a spouse, a child under age 21, or a blind or permanently disabled child of any age [7].
*   **Transfer of Assets:** Medicaid imposes a **60-month Look-Back Period** for all asset transfers made prior to applying for long-term care [3]. Any uncompensated transfer of assets during this period can result in a penalty period of ineligibility for Medicaid benefits [8]. This rule is a critical consideration for anyone planning for long-term care.

## Conclusion

New Jersey's MLTSS program, delivered through NJ FamilyCare, represents a robust system for providing long-term care services in the community, including in assisted living settings. By understanding the specific financial and clinical eligibility requirements, the comprehensive nature of the covered services, and the implications of estate recovery, New Jersey residents can effectively navigate the system to secure the care they need.
