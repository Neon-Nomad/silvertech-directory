# Delaware Veterans Benefits: A Comprehensive Guide to State Resources and Support

Delaware, the "First State," is committed to honoring its military community by providing a comprehensive suite of state-level benefits and services. These programs, administered primarily through the **Office of Veterans Services (OVS)**, are designed to support veterans and their families in areas ranging from financial relief and employment to healthcare and long-term care [1].

This guide offers an in-depth look at the key benefits available to Delaware veterans, including tax exemptions, educational opportunities, employment preferences, and the critical services provided by the Delaware Veterans Home.

## Financial and Tax Benefits for Delaware Veterans

One of the most immediate and impactful ways Delaware supports its veterans is through significant financial and tax advantages. These benefits are designed to ease the financial burden on veterans and their families.

### State Income Tax Exemptions

Delaware provides a substantial tax exclusion for military retirement income:

*   **Military Retirement and SBP Exemption:** Veterans can exclude up to **$12,500** of their military retirement pay and Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) payments from their Delaware state income tax [2]. This benefit is crucial for retired service members, helping to increase their disposable income.
*   **Active-Duty Income Tax:** Delaware follows federal tax rules regarding the taxation of active-duty military income.

### Property Tax Relief

Delaware offers a property tax exemption to recognize the service of disabled veterans:

*   **Disabled Veterans Property Tax Exemption:** The state provides a property tax exemption on the assessed value of the veteran's primary residence. This exemption is available to veterans who have been honorably discharged and have a service-connected disability rating of 100% from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) [3]. The exemption is also available to the surviving spouse of an eligible veteran.

### Business and Employment Incentives

Delaware encourages the hiring of veterans through tax incentives for employers:

*   **Veterans Opportunity Credit:** This tax credit is available to businesses that hire unemployed veterans, providing a financial incentive to help veterans transition back into the civilian workforce [1].

## Employment and Professional Licensing

Delaware recognizes the valuable skills and discipline veterans bring to the workforce and offers several programs to enhance their employment prospects within the state.

### State Employment Preference

Veterans are granted a **hiring preference** for state employment positions. This preference is applied to veterans who meet the minimum qualifications for the position and is a significant advantage in the competitive state job market. The preference typically applies to veterans who served during a period of war or who have a service-connected disability [4].

### Professional Licensing Assistance

The state has streamlined the process for veterans and their spouses to obtain professional licenses:

*   **Fee Waivers and Reductions:** The Office of Veterans Services works with state licensing boards to waive or reduce fees for professional licenses for military service members and their spouses.
*   **Credit for Military Training:** Delaware law requires state licensing boards to consider relevant military training, education, and experience when evaluating a veteran's application for a professional license, potentially accelerating the process for qualified individuals.

## Education and Training Opportunities

Delaware provides various educational benefits to help veterans and their families pursue higher education and vocational training.

### Delaware Veterans Education Benefits

The state offers financial assistance programs specifically for veterans:

*   **Delaware Veterans Education Benefits:** These programs provide financial aid to eligible veterans for tuition and fees at state-supported institutions of higher learning.
*   **High School Diplomas for Veterans:** The state may award a high school diploma to veterans who left school early to serve in World War II, the Korean War, or the Vietnam War, providing formal recognition of their service and sacrifice [1].

### Educational Benefits for Dependents

Delaware also extends educational support to the children of certain veterans:

*   **Scholarships for Children of Deceased Veterans:** The state offers scholarships to the children of veterans who died while on active duty or as a result of a service-connected disability.

## The Delaware Veterans Home: Long-Term Care and Dignity

For veterans requiring long-term care, the **Delaware Veterans Home (DVH)** in Milford provides a high-quality, state-of-the-art facility offering skilled nursing care [5]. The DVH is a single-location facility dedicated exclusively to serving the state's veterans.

### Eligibility and Admission

Admission to the Delaware Veterans Home is based on a set of criteria designed to ensure the facility serves those who have served the nation:

1.  **Service Requirement:** The applicant must have been honorably discharged from active military service (peacetime or wartime) or have qualifying National Guard Service, with at least 180 days of active duty service.
2.  **Residency Requirement:** The applicant must have resided in the State of Delaware for at least **one year** prior to the submission of the application.
3.  **Medical Need:** The applicant must have a medically determined need for a long-term level of care, specifically skilled nursing care, as determined by a physician.

### Cost of Care

While the DVH is a state-run facility, residents are required to contribute to the cost of their care based on their income and assets. However, the cost is significantly lower than private-pay nursing homes, and the facility is certified to accept Medicare, Medicaid, and VA per diem payments, making it an accessible option for eligible veterans.

## The Office of Veterans Services (OVS): Your Central Resource

The **Office of Veterans Services (OVS)**, located in Dover, is the central hub for all state veterans' affairs. The OVS is dedicated to ensuring that every veteran and their family is aware of and can access the benefits they have earned.

### Service Officers: Expert Assistance

The OVS employs **Veterans Service Officers (VSOs)** who are accredited by the VA and trained to provide expert, free assistance with:

*   **Federal Benefits:** Assisting with claims for VA disability compensation, pension, healthcare enrollment, and burial benefits.
*   **State Benefits:** Guiding veterans through the application process for all Delaware state benefits, including tax exemptions and the Delaware Veterans Home.
*   **Navigating Healthcare:** Helping veterans understand and enroll in VA healthcare and other state-level health programs.

The VSOs are the most critical resource for any veteran seeking to understand and apply for their benefits, acting as a bridge between the veteran and the complex bureaucracy of state and federal programs.

## Conclusion

Delaware's commitment to its veterans is evident in the breadth and depth of the benefits offered, from financial relief through tax exemptions to comprehensive long-term care at the Delaware Veterans Home. The Office of Veterans Services serves as the indispensable guide, ensuring that the state's heroes and their families receive the support they need and deserve. By leveraging the expertise of the Veterans Service Officers and understanding the eligibility requirements for state programs, Delaware veterans can maximize their benefits and enjoy a high quality of life in the First State.

## The Delaware Commission of Veterans Affairs (DCVA) and Advocacy

While the Office of Veterans Services (OVS) is the operational arm, the **Delaware Commission of Veterans Affairs (DCVA)** serves as the advisory and policy-making body, ensuring that the needs of the state's veteran population are continually addressed at the highest levels of state government [8].

### Role and Function of the DCVA

The DCVA is composed of appointed members who are themselves veterans, representing various branches of the military and different veteran organizations. Their primary functions include:

1.  **Advising the Governor:** The Commission advises the Governor, the General Assembly, and the OVS on all matters relating to veterans' affairs, including proposed legislation and policy changes.
2.  **Advocacy and Outreach:** The DCVA works to promote public awareness of veterans' issues and coordinates outreach efforts to ensure that veterans, particularly those in rural or underserved areas, are aware of the benefits and services available to them.
3.  **Coordination of Services:** The Commission plays a key role in coordinating the efforts of various state agencies, federal VA facilities, and private organizations to create a seamless network of support for veterans.

The DCVA's work is critical for maintaining the relevance and effectiveness of Delaware's veteran programs, ensuring they evolve to meet the changing needs of the veteran community.

## Detailed Look at Property Tax Relief and Exemptions

The property tax relief offered to disabled veterans is one of the most valuable state benefits, providing significant long-term financial stability.

### Eligibility for Property Tax Exemption

The exemption is specifically targeted at veterans who have suffered a severe service-connected disability. To qualify, the veteran must meet the following criteria:

*   **Service-Connected Disability:** The veteran must have a 100% service-connected disability rating from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). This includes veterans who are compensated at the 100% rate due to individual unemployability (IU).
*   **Residency:** The property must be the veteran's primary residence in Delaware.
*   **Ownership:** The veteran must own the property.

The exemption is applied to the assessed value of the property, which can result in a substantial reduction or complete elimination of the property tax burden, depending on the county and the assessed value.

### Surviving Spouse Provision

In a crucial provision, the exemption is also extended to the **unremarried surviving spouse** of a veteran who was eligible for the exemption at the time of death, or the unremarried surviving spouse of a service member who died on active duty. This ensures that the financial security provided by the exemption continues for the veteran's family after their passing.

## Delaware Joining Forces (DJF): Community-Based Support

Beyond the formal state agencies, Delaware has fostered a strong community-based support network through the **Delaware Joining Forces (DJF)** initiative [9]. DJF is a collaborative effort that brings together state agencies, non-profit organizations, businesses, and community groups to support service members, veterans, and their families.

### Key Focus Areas of DJF

DJF operates by identifying gaps in services and mobilizing community resources to fill those needs. Its focus areas include:

1.  **Employment:** Connecting veterans with job training, career counseling, and employment opportunities with veteran-friendly employers.
2.  **Education:** Ensuring veterans and their families can easily access educational benefits and resources.
3.  **Wellness:** Promoting mental health services, suicide prevention, and access to quality healthcare.
4.  **Housing:** Assisting veterans and their families with housing stability, including homelessness prevention.

DJF serves as a model for how state government can partner with the private and non-profit sectors to create a comprehensive, holistic support system that addresses the diverse challenges faced by the military community.

## The Delaware Veterans Home: A Closer Look at Care

The **Delaware Veterans Home (DVH)**, located in Milford, is a source of immense pride for the state, offering a level of care and environment specifically tailored to the needs of veterans.

### Services Provided

The DVH is a skilled nursing facility, meaning it provides the highest level of long-term care. Services include:

*   **24-Hour Skilled Nursing Care:** Provided by licensed nurses and certified nursing assistants.
*   **Rehabilitation Services:** Physical, occupational, and speech therapy to help residents regain and maintain function.
*   **Medical Services:** On-site physician services, pharmacy, and specialized medical care.
*   **Hospice and Palliative Care:** Compassionate end-of-life care.
*   **Recreational and Social Activities:** Programs designed to meet the unique social and emotional needs of veterans, fostering camaraderie and a sense of purpose.

### The Application Process

The application for admission to the DVH is a multi-step process that ensures all eligibility requirements are met:

1.  **Application Submission:** The applicant or their representative submits a formal application, including proof of military service (DD-214) and Delaware residency.
2.  **Medical Review:** The DVH staff reviews the applicant's medical records to confirm the need for skilled nursing care.
3.  **Financial Review:** A financial assessment is conducted to determine the resident's contribution to the cost of care.
4.  **Admission:** Once approved, the applicant is placed on a waiting list, if necessary, and admitted when a bed becomes available.

The DVH is a testament to Delaware's commitment to providing its veterans with a dignified and supportive environment for their later years.

## Conclusion

Delaware's support for its veterans is comprehensive, extending well beyond federal benefits to include significant state-level tax exemptions, employment preferences, and dedicated long-term care. The synergy between the Office of Veterans Services, the Delaware Commission of Veterans Affairs, and community initiatives like Delaware Joining Forces creates a robust safety net. By understanding and utilizing these resources, Delaware veterans and their families can access the full range of benefits they have earned through their service to the nation.
