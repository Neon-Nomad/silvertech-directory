# Wisconsin Licensing Authority: Oversight of Long-Term Care and Assisted Living Facilities

The regulation and oversight of long-term care facilities in Wisconsin are primarily the responsibility of the **Wisconsin Department of Health Services (DHS)**. Within the DHS, the **Division of Quality Assurance (DQA)** serves as the central licensing and enforcement authority, dedicated to protecting and promoting the health, safety, and welfare of residents in various regulated health care and community-based settings [1].

This comprehensive guide details the structure of the licensing authority, the different types of facilities regulated, the licensing and inspection processes, and the specific regulations that govern the quality of care in Wisconsin.

---

## The Regulatory Framework: Division of Quality Assurance (DQA)

The **Division of Quality Assurance (DQA)** is the key entity within the Wisconsin DHS that oversees the licensing, certification, and regulation of more than 40 different types of health care providers, facilities, and caregivers [2]. The DQA's mission is to ensure that all regulated entities meet state and federal standards for quality of care and resident safety.

### Key Bureaus within DQA

The DQA is structured into several bureaus, each with specific responsibilities for different facility types. The primary bureau responsible for the oversight of nursing homes and assisted living facilities is the **Bureau of Nursing Home Resident Care** and the **Bureau of Assisted Living** [1].

*   **Bureau of Nursing Home Resident Care:** This bureau is specifically tasked with the licensing and certification of nursing homes, ensuring compliance with both state regulations (Chapter DHS 132) and federal regulations (Code of Federal Regulations, Title 42, Part 483) [3].
*   **Bureau of Assisted Living (BAL):** The BAL oversees the licensing and regulation of assisted living facilities, which in Wisconsin are primarily categorized as Community-Based Residential Facilities (CBRFs) and Residential Care Apartment Complexes (RCACs) [4].

## Regulated Long-Term Care Facility Types

Wisconsin employs specific terminology to categorize its long-term care and assisted living facilities, each with distinct licensing requirements and service limitations.

### 1. Nursing Homes (NH)

Nursing homes, also known as skilled nursing facilities, provide the highest level of medical care outside of a hospital. They are licensed by the state and certified by the federal government to participate in Medicare and Medicaid.

*   **Regulation:** Governed by state administrative code **DHS 132** and federal regulations for skilled nursing facilities.
*   **Scope of Care:** Provide 24-hour skilled nursing care, rehabilitation services, and long-term custodial care for residents who require continuous medical supervision [3].
*   **Oversight:** The Bureau of Nursing Home Resident Care conducts annual, unannounced surveys (inspections) to verify compliance with hundreds of regulatory standards.

### 2. Community-Based Residential Facilities (CBRF)

CBRFs are the most common type of assisted living facility in Wisconsin. They are designed for individuals who need assistance with daily living activities but do not require 24-hour skilled nursing care.

*   **Regulation:** Governed by state administrative code **DHS 83** [6].
*   **Definition:** A CBRF is a place where five or more unrelated adults reside and receive care, treatment, or services that are above the level of room and board [6].
*   **Scope of Care:** Services include assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs), medication management, and supportive services. The level of care provided in a CBRF is generally more intensive than in an RCAC.
*   **Staffing:** Regulations require specific staffing levels and training for staff, particularly in areas like fire safety, first aid, and medication administration [6].

### 3. Residential Care Apartment Complexes (RCAC)

RCACs are designed for a more independent population, offering apartment-style living with access to supportive services. They are often viewed as a bridge between independent living and a CBRF or nursing home.

*   **Regulation:** Governed by state administrative code **DHS 89** [7].
*   **Definition:** An RCAC consists of independent apartments where five or more adults reside, and where supportive services, up to 28 hours per week, are provided [7].
*   **Scope of Care:** Services are generally limited to supportive services, personal care, and medication management. Residents must be able to direct their own care and not require more than 28 hours of services per week.
*   **Independence:** The emphasis is on maintaining the resident's independence and privacy, with each resident having a lockable apartment with a kitchen and bathroom [7].

## The Licensing and Inspection Process

The DQA utilizes a rigorous process of initial licensing, periodic inspections, and enforcement actions to ensure facilities maintain compliance with state and federal standards.

### Initial Licensing

Before a facility can open, it must undergo a comprehensive initial licensing process:

1.  **Application:** The prospective operator submits a detailed application, including facility blueprints, operational plans, and proof of financial stability [8].
2.  **Plan Review:** For new construction or major renovations, the DQA's Plan Review Section must approve the building plans to ensure compliance with fire safety and physical environment standards [8].
3.  **Initial Survey:** A DQA surveyor conducts an on-site inspection to verify that the facility is built and equipped according to the approved plans and that all operational policies and procedures are in place [8].
4.  **License Issuance:** Upon successful completion of the initial survey, the DQA issues a license to operate.

### Periodic Inspections (Surveys)

All licensed facilities are subject to regular, unannounced inspections, known as surveys, to monitor ongoing compliance.

*   **Nursing Homes:** Federal and state law mandates that nursing homes receive a standard survey at least once every 15 months, with the average interval not exceeding 12 months [9]. These surveys are comprehensive and involve reviewing resident medical records, observing care delivery, interviewing residents and staff, and inspecting the physical environment.
*   **Assisted Living (CBRFs and RCACs):** The frequency of inspections for assisted living facilities is determined by state law and may be based on a risk-assessment model, but all facilities are subject to periodic, unannounced visits [10].

### Enforcement and Sanctions

When a facility is found to be non-compliant with regulations, the DQA has a range of enforcement actions it can take, which vary in severity based on the nature and scope of the deficiency.

*   **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD):** Following a survey, the facility receives an SOD detailing all violations. The facility must then submit a **Plan of Correction (POC)** outlining how and when they will correct the deficiencies [9].
*   **Monetary Penalties:** Fines can be levied for serious violations that pose a risk to resident health and safety.
*   **Directed Plan of Correction:** The DQA may require the facility to follow a specific, prescribed plan to correct deficiencies, often involving mandatory training or external consultation [9].
*   **Suspension or Revocation of License:** For severe, repeated, or uncorrected violations that place residents in immediate jeopardy, the DQA can suspend or revoke the facility's license, forcing it to close and relocate residents [1].

## Key Regulatory Focus Areas

The DQA's oversight is concentrated on several critical areas that directly impact the quality of life and safety of residents.

### Resident Rights

A cornerstone of Wisconsin's long-term care regulation is the protection of resident rights. Regulations ensure that residents have the right to:

*   **Dignity and Respect:** Be treated with consideration, respect, and full recognition of their dignity and individuality [11].
*   **Self-Determination:** Participate in the planning of their care and make choices about their daily life, including activities, schedules, and health care [11].
*   **Freedom from Abuse:** Be free from physical, sexual, mental, and verbal abuse, as well as corporal punishment and involuntary seclusion [11].
*   **Privacy:** Privacy in their accommodations, medical treatment, written and telephonic communications, and in visits with others [11].

### Quality of Care and Staffing

Regulations mandate that facilities provide care that is necessary to attain or maintain the highest practicable physical, mental, and psychosocial well-being of each resident.

*   **Care Planning:** Every resident must have a comprehensive, individualized care plan developed by an interdisciplinary team [12].
*   **Medication Management:** Strict rules govern the storage, administration, and documentation of medications, particularly in assisted living settings where staff may not be licensed nurses [6].
*   **Staff Training:** All staff, especially those in direct care roles, must receive specific training relevant to the population they serve, including training on dementia care, resident rights, and emergency procedures [6].

### Physical Environment and Safety

The DQA also ensures that the physical environment of the facility is safe, clean, and homelike.

*   **Fire Safety:** Facilities must comply with the National Fire Protection Association's Life Safety Code, including requirements for sprinklers, smoke detectors, and emergency evacuation plans [8].
*   **Infection Control:** Facilities must have a robust infection prevention and control program to prevent the spread of communicable diseases [1].
*   **Maintenance:** The building and grounds must be maintained in good repair and be free of hazards [1].

## Public Access to Information

Transparency is a key component of the DQA's function. The public has access to facility information, including inspection results and complaint history.

*   **Facility Search:** The DHS website provides a public search tool that allows consumers to look up licensed facilities and view their compliance history, including deficiencies cited during surveys and any enforcement actions taken [13].
*   **Complaint Process:** The DQA maintains a dedicated process for receiving and investigating complaints against all regulated facilities. This process is a vital mechanism for identifying and correcting problems between scheduled inspections [14].

By maintaining a clear structure of regulation, enforcement, and public transparency, the Wisconsin Department of Health Services, through its Division of Quality Assurance, works to safeguard the well-being of all residents receiving long-term care services in the state.
