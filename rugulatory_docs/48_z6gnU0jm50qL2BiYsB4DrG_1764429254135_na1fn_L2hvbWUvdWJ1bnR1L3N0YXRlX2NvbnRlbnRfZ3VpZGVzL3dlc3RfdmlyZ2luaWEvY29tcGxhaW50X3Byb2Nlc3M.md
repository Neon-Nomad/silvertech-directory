# West Virginia Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Reporting Concerns and Ensuring Accountability

Ensuring the safety and quality of care for residents in West Virginia's long-term care facilities is a shared responsibility. The state has established clear channels for residents, family members, and concerned citizens to report complaints regarding nursing homes, assisted living residences, and other licensed healthcare facilities. The primary agency responsible for investigating these complaints is the **Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification (OHFLAC)**, a division of the West Virginia Department of Health and Human Resources (DHHR) [1].

This guide provides a detailed, step-by-step overview of the complaint process in West Virginia, outlining how to file a complaint, what information is needed, the investigation process, and the role of other key agencies like the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program.

## The Primary Complaint Authority: OHFLAC

The **Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification (OHFLAC)** is the state's regulatory body tasked with enforcing state licensure rules and federal certification requirements for long-term care facilities.

### What OHFLAC Investigates

OHFLAC investigates complaints related to violations of state and federal regulations, which can include:

*   **Quality of Care:** Inadequate medical care, medication errors, poor wound care, or failure to follow a care plan.
*   **Resident Rights:** Violations of a resident's right to privacy, dignity, or freedom from abuse and neglect.
*   **Facility Environment:** Unsanitary conditions, fire safety hazards, or maintenance issues that affect resident health and safety.
*   **Staffing:** Insufficient staffing levels or lack of qualified personnel to meet resident needs [4].

## Step-by-Step Guide to Filing a Complaint

Filing a complaint should be done as soon as possible after the incident or concern is observed. The process is designed to be accessible and confidential.

### Step 1: Document the Concern

Before contacting OHFLAC, gather as much detail as possible. Accurate and specific information is crucial for a successful investigation. Key details to record include:

*   **Facility Information:** Full name and address of the nursing home or assisted living residence.
*   **Resident Information:** Name of the affected resident (if applicable and with permission).
*   **Incident Details:** Date, time, and location of the incident or observation.
*   **Nature of the Complaint:** A clear, concise description of what happened or what the concern is.
*   **Witnesses:** Names and contact information of any staff, residents, or visitors who witnessed the event.
*   **Previous Actions:** Any steps already taken to resolve the issue with the facility's administration [5].

### Step 2: Choose a Filing Method

OHFLAC offers three primary methods for submitting a complaint:

1.  **Phone Hotline:** Call the dedicated complaint hotline at **(304) 558-0050**. This is often the quickest way to report an urgent concern, as staff can immediately triage the severity of the issue.
2.  **Online Form:** Use the secure **Online Complaint Form** available on the OHFLAC website. This method ensures all necessary information is captured in a structured format.
3.  **Mail or Fax:** Complaints can be submitted in writing via mail or fax to the OHFLAC office.

**Confidentiality:** Complainants can choose to remain anonymous. OHFLAC is required to protect the identity of any complainant who requests confidentiality, ensuring that facilities cannot retaliate against residents or staff who report concerns [6].

### Step 3: The Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, OHFLAC staff will triage it based on the severity and potential for immediate harm to residents.

1.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints alleging immediate jeopardy (a situation where a resident is at risk of serious injury, harm, impairment, or death) are investigated within two working days. Other, less severe complaints are investigated within a longer, but still prompt, timeframe [7].
2.  **Unannounced Survey:** An OHFLAC surveyor will conduct an unannounced visit to the facility. The investigation involves:
    *   Interviewing the complainant (if their identity is known and they consent).
    *   Interviewing the resident and other residents.
    *   Interviewing staff members.
    *   Reviewing the resident's medical records and the facility's policies and procedures.
    *   Observing the care provided and the facility environment.
3.  **Findings and Citations:** If the investigation confirms that a violation of state or federal regulations occurred, the facility will be issued a **Statement of Deficiencies (Form CMS-2567)**, also known as a citation.
4.  **Plan of Correction (POC):** The facility is required to submit a written **Plan of Correction** detailing the steps they will take to correct the deficiency and prevent its recurrence.
5.  **Follow-up:** OHFLAC conducts a follow-up visit to ensure the facility has successfully implemented the Plan of Correction [8].

### Step 4: Notification of Results

The complainant (if they provided contact information and did not request anonymity) will be notified of the outcome of the investigation, including whether the complaint was substantiated and the actions taken against the facility.

## The Role of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman

While OHFLAC handles regulatory and licensing complaints, the **West Virginia Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** (LTCOP) provides a crucial, non-regulatory avenue for resolving concerns, particularly those related to resident rights and quality of life.

| Ombudsman Contact Information | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agency Name** | Legal Aid of West Virginia, Inc. (LTCOP) |
| **Toll-Free Phone** | (800) 834-0598 [9] |
| **Website** | [https://legalaidwv.org/our-programs/long-term-care/](https://legalaidwv.org/our-programs/long-term-care/) |

**When to Contact the Ombudsman:**

*   **Resident Rights Issues:** Concerns about dignity, privacy, freedom of choice, or retaliation.
*   **Quality of Life:** Issues with food, activities, or social interaction.
*   **Mediation:** When a resident or family member is having difficulty communicating or resolving an issue directly with the facility staff.
*   **Confidentiality:** When the complainant wishes to remain completely anonymous and seeks a resolution through mediation rather than regulatory enforcement [9].

The Ombudsman can often resolve issues quickly through mediation and advocacy, without the need for a formal regulatory investigation. The Ombudsman and OHFLAC often work collaboratively, with the Ombudsman referring serious regulatory violations to OHFLAC.

## Other Agencies Involved in Reporting

Depending on the nature of the complaint, other state agencies may also be involved:

*   **Adult Protective Services (APS):** If the complaint involves suspected **abuse, neglect, or exploitation** of an elderly or disabled adult, a report should be made to the DHHR's APS hotline. APS investigates the welfare of the individual, while OHFLAC investigates the facility's compliance [10].
*   **Law Enforcement:** Any complaint involving criminal activity, such as assault, theft, or severe physical abuse, should be immediately reported to the local police department or the West Virginia State Police.

## Conclusion: Ensuring Accountability

The complaint process in West Virginia is a vital mechanism for holding long-term care facilities accountable and ensuring that residents receive the high-quality, dignified care they deserve. By understanding the roles of the **Office of Health Facility Licensure and Certification (OHFLAC)** and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program**, residents and their advocates can effectively navigate the system to report concerns. The prompt reporting of issues, whether through the OHFLAC hotline at **(304) 558-0050** or the Ombudsman's line at **(800) 834-0598**, is the first and most crucial step in protecting the well-being of West Virginia's long-term care population.
