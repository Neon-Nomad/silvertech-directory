## Introduction to Idaho Medicaid

Idaho Medicaid, administered by the **Idaho Department of Health and Welfare (DHW)** [1], is a vital state and federal program that provides health coverage to eligible low-income adults, children, pregnant women, elderly adults, and people with disabilities. For Idaho's senior population, Medicaid is particularly crucial as it is the primary payer for long-term care services, both in nursing facilities and in home and community-based settings.

The state's approach to long-term care is centered on the principle of helping frail seniors remain in their homes and communities for as long as possible. This is primarily achieved through the **Aged and Disabled (A&D) Waiver**, which is the key program for assisted living and in-home care support.

## Understanding Long-Term Care Medicaid in Idaho

Long-Term Care (LTC) Medicaid in Idaho is distinct from Regular Medicaid, which covers acute medical needs. LTC Medicaid is specifically designed to cover the costs associated with chronic care needs, such as assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) like bathing, dressing, and eating.

Idaho offers three main categories of Medicaid relevant to long-term care for seniors [3]:

1.  **Institutional / Nursing Home Medicaid:** An entitlement program that provides benefits in Medicaid-certified nursing home facilities.
2.  **Medicaid Waivers / Home and Community Based Services (HCBS):** Non-entitlement programs, such as the A&D Waiver, that provide services to delay nursing home admission. Benefits are provided in the home, adult foster care, adult day care, or assisted living.
3.  **Regular Medicaid / Aid to the Aged, Blind, and Disabled (AABD):** While not LTC Medicaid, this program may offer some long-term care benefits, such as personal care assistance.

### The Aged and Disabled (A&D) Waiver

The **Aged and Disabled (A&D) Waiver** is Idaho's primary Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) program for seniors and persons with disabilities who are at risk of institutionalization (nursing home admission). It is a 1915(c) Home and Community Based Services (HCBS) Medicaid Waiver [4].

**Key Features of the A&D Waiver:**

*   **Nursing Home Diversion:** The program's core purpose is to provide the necessary support for individuals to continue living in their homes and communities.
*   **Residential Settings:** Services can be received in a private home, a loved one's home, a certified family home (adult foster care home), or a residential assisted living facility.
*   **Self-Direction:** The program offers a participant-directed component, allowing individuals to hire and manage their own care providers, which can include certain family members (excluding a spouse) [4].

### Comprehensive Services Provided by the A&D Waiver

The A&D Waiver provides a wide array of services and supports, which are tailored to the individual's needs as determined by a service plan. The benefits are extensive and designed to cover nearly all aspects of long-term care outside of a nursing home setting [4]:

| Service Category | Description of Services |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Care & Health** | Attendant Care (personal care and medical tasks), Skilled Nursing, Consultation. |
| **Residential Support** | Adult Residential Care Services (medication assistance, housekeeping, meal preparation in certified settings), Habilitation (day and residential). |
| **Home & Community Support** | Adult Day Health, Companion Services (supervision, socialization, assistance with ADLs), Respite Care (in-home and out-of-home care for caregivers). |
| **Household & Errands** | Homemaker Services (laundry, preparing meals, essential errands), Chore Services (yard/sidewalk maintenance, heavy housework, minor home repairs). |
| **Specialized Needs** | Home Meal Delivery, Home Modifications (ramps, grab bars, widening of doorways), Specialized Medical Equipment/Supplies, Personal Emergency Response Systems. |
| **Employment & Transition** | Supported Employment, Transition Services (to assist institutionalized persons in moving back to the community). |
| **Transportation** | Non-Medical Transportation. |

## Financial Eligibility Requirements (2026)

To qualify for Idaho Long-Term Care Medicaid, applicants must meet strict financial and functional (medical) eligibility criteria. The financial limits are subject to change annually, and the figures below are based on the most recent available data for 2026 [3].

### Income Limits

Idaho is an **Income Cap** state for Nursing Home and HCBS Waiver Medicaid. This means an applicant's gross monthly income must not exceed a certain limit.

| Applicant Status | Institutional / Nursing Home Medicaid Income Limit (2026) | Medicaid Waivers / HCBS Income Limit (2026) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Applicant** | $3,002 per month | $3,002 per month |
| **Married (Both Spouses Applying)** | $3,002 per month (per spouse) | $3,002 per month (per spouse) |
| **Married (One Spouse Applying)** | $3,002 per month (applicant) | $3,002 per month (applicant) |

**Important Income Considerations:**

*   **Qualified Income Trust (QIT):** For applicants whose income exceeds the limit, Idaho allows the use of a Qualified Income Trust (also known as a Miller Trust) to legally reduce countable income to the eligible level [3].
*   **Personal Needs Allowance (PNA):** Institutionalized Medicaid recipients are allowed to keep a small portion of their income, typically $40 per month, as a Personal Needs Allowance. The rest of their income must be contributed toward the cost of their care, known as **Patient Liability** [3].

### Asset Limits

Assets, or resources, are countable items that can be liquidated to pay for long-term care.

| Applicant Status | Asset Limit (2026) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Single Applicant** | $2,000 |
| **Married (Both Spouses Applying)** | $3,000 (combined) |
| **Married (One Spouse Applying)** | $2,000 (applicant) and $162,660 (non-applicant spouse) |

**Exempt Assets:** Certain assets are considered exempt (non-countable) and do not count toward the limit. The most common exempt assets include:

*   **Primary Residence:** The applicant's home is generally exempt if the equity interest is under $713,000, or if a spouse, minor child, or disabled child lives there [3].
*   **Household Goods and Personal Effects**
*   **One Automobile**
*   **Irrevocable Burial Trusts**

### Spousal Impoverishment Rules

When only one spouse applies for Nursing Home or Waiver Medicaid, the **Spousal Impoverishment Provision** protects the non-applicant spouse from becoming impoverished.

*   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The non-applicant spouse (Community Spouse) is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined assets, up to a maximum of **$162,660** in 2026 [3].
*   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The non-applicant spouse is also entitled to a minimum monthly income. As of July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026, the MMMNA in Idaho is **$2,643.75** [3]. If the non-applicant spouse's income is below this amount, income can be transferred from the applicant spouse to bring the non-applicant spouse up to the minimum. The maximum amount of income that can be transferred is **$4,066.50** per month.

## Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)

Idaho, like all states, is required by federal law to operate a Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP) to recover the costs of certain Medicaid benefits paid on behalf of a recipient.

### Recoverable Services

The state seeks to recover payments made for [5]:

*   Nursing facility services.
*   Home and community-based services (including the A&D Waiver).
*   Hospital and prescription drug services related to the long-term care stay.

### The Estate Definition

In Idaho, the state can recover from the recipient's **probate estate**, which includes assets that pass through the probate process (e.g., assets held solely in the deceased's name). The state may also place a lien on the home of a Medicaid recipient while they are alive, which is dissolved if the recipient returns home [5].

### Undue Hardship Waiver

The state will waive recovery if it would cause an **undue hardship** for the heirs of the estate. An applicant for an undue hardship waiver must have a beneficial interest in the estate and must apply for the waiver within ninety (90) days of the death of the Medicaid participant [6]. The Idaho Department of Health and Welfare reviews these applications on a case-by-case basis, considering all facts and circumstances [7].

## How to Apply for Idaho Medicaid

Applying for Medicaid in Idaho can be done through several methods [2]:

1.  **Online:** Through the state's self-service portal, **idalink**.
2.  **By Phone:** Call the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare at **877-456-1233**.
3.  **In-Person:** Visit a local Department of Health and Welfare office.

It is highly recommended to seek assistance from a Certified Medicaid Planner or an elder law attorney, especially when dealing with complex financial situations or when applying for the A&D Waiver.
