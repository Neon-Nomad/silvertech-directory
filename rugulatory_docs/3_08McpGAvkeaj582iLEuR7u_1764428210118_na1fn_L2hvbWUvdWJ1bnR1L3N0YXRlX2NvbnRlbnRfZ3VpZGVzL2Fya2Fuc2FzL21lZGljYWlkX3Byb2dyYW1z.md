# Arkansas Medicaid Programs: A Comprehensive Guide to Eligibility, Benefits, and Enrollment

The Arkansas Medicaid program, a vital component of the state's healthcare system, provides essential medical coverage to low-income adults, children, pregnant women, the elderly, and people with disabilities. Unlike traditional Medicaid models in many states, Arkansas has implemented a unique approach known as **ARHOME (Arkansas Health and Opportunity for Me)**, which utilizes Medicaid funds to purchase private health insurance for a significant portion of its beneficiary population. This comprehensive guide details the structure, eligibility requirements, covered services, and application process for Medicaid in Arkansas, with a special focus on the ARHOME program and the **Living Choices Assisted Living Waiver**.

Administered by the **Arkansas Department of Human Services (DHS), Division of Medical Services**, the program is designed to ensure that eligible residents have access to a wide range of medical services, promoting better health outcomes across the state [1].

| Program Name | Administering Agency | Primary Focus | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arkansas Medicaid (ARHOME)** | Arkansas Department of Human Services, Division of Medical Services | Comprehensive health coverage for low-income and vulnerable populations | Uses Medicaid funds to purchase private health insurance plans |
| **Living Choices Waiver** | Arkansas Department of Human Services, Division of Aging, Adult, & Behavioral Health Services | Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) in assisted living facilities | Alternative to institutional nursing home care |

## The Arkansas Health and Opportunity for Me (ARHOME) Program

The ARHOME program, which replaced the Arkansas Works program on January 1, 2022, is the cornerstone of the state's Medicaid expansion. It is a unique 1115 Waiver demonstration that aims to improve health outcomes by integrating the flexibility of private insurance with the robust coverage of Medicaid [2].

### How ARHOME Works

Under ARHOME, eligible beneficiaries receive their coverage through one of two private health insurance carriers: **Blue Cross Blue Shield** or **Ambetter**. The state uses Medicaid funds to pay the premiums for these private plans. This structure is intended to:

1.  **Provide Comprehensive Coverage:** Beneficiaries receive the full scope of Medicaid benefits, often delivered through a managed care model.
2.  **Promote Health and Well-being:** ARHOME encourages beneficiaries to be active partners in their health care, working with their plans to achieve personal health goals.
3.  **Integrate Care:** The program focuses on coordinating care and addressing social determinants of health.

### Life360 HOMEs: A Focus on Targeted Care

A key feature of the ARHOME program is the inclusion of **Life360 HOMEs**, which are specialized initiatives designed to focus on the health of specific, high-need populations. These initiatives aim to provide more targeted and intensive support to improve health equity and outcomes for these groups [1].

*   **Maternal Life360:** Dedicated to high-risk pregnant and new mothers, as well as their babies. This initiative provides enhanced services and support to reduce maternal and infant mortality and morbidity.
*   **Rural Life360:** Targets Arkansans living in rural communities who often face barriers to accessing healthcare. It focuses on improving access to services and addressing unique rural health challenges.
*   **Success Life360:** Concentrates on young adults who are at the greatest risk for poor health outcomes, helping them transition to stable adult life with consistent healthcare access.

## Detailed Medicaid Eligibility Requirements in Arkansas

Eligibility for Arkansas Medicaid is determined by a combination of factors, including income, household size, age, disability status, and residency. The state divides eligibility into several key categories, primarily utilizing the Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) methodology for most children, pregnant women, and parents, and non-MAGI rules for the aged, blind, and disabled.

### MAGI-Based Eligibility Categories

The MAGI rules are based on a percentage of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) and are used for populations covered under the Affordable Care Act (ACA) expansion and traditional family coverage.

| Eligibility Group | Income Limit (as % of FPL) | Key Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Children (Ages 0-18)** | Up to 211% FPL | Includes the ARKids First program, which covers children from birth through age 18. |
| **Pregnant Women** | Up to 209% FPL | Coverage extends through the pregnancy and for a period postpartum. |
| **Parents and Caretaker Relatives** | Up to 138% FPL | Must have a dependent child in the home. |
| **ARHOME (Expansion Adults)** | Up to 138% FPL | Non-Medicare eligible adults aged 19-64. This is the primary group covered by the ARHOME program. |

### Non-MAGI-Based Eligibility Categories (Aged, Blind, and Disabled)

For individuals who are aged (65 or older), blind, or disabled, eligibility is determined using different financial criteria, often tied to Supplemental Security Income (SSI) limits and resource tests.

*   **Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ABD):** Individuals who qualify for SSI are automatically eligible for full Medicaid benefits. For those who do not receive SSI, the state applies its own income and resource limits.
*   **Medically Needy:** This program is for individuals whose income is too high to qualify for regular Medicaid but who have significant medical expenses. They can "spend down" their income on medical bills to meet a lower income threshold.
*   **Medicare Savings Programs (MSPs):** Medicaid can help pay for Medicare premiums, deductibles, and co-payments for low-income Medicare beneficiaries through programs like the Qualified Medicare Beneficiary (QMB) and Specified Low-Income Medicare Beneficiary (SLMB) programs.

### Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS) Eligibility

Eligibility for long-term care, such as nursing home care or Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers, involves a separate set of financial and functional criteria.

1.  **Functional Need:** An individual must meet the state's criteria for a nursing home level of care, meaning they require assistance with a certain number of Activities of Daily Living (ADLs).
2.  **Financial Need:** The income limit is typically higher than for standard ABD Medicaid, often tied to a percentage of the FPL or a special institutional income cap. Resource limits are also applied, typically $2,000 for an individual. Spousal impoverishment rules protect the income and assets of the non-applicant spouse.

## Key Medicaid Waivers: The Living Choices Program

Arkansas offers several Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers to provide long-term care in a home or community setting, rather than in an institution. The **Living Choices Assisted Living Waiver** is a critical program for those who need assistance but wish to remain in an assisted living environment.

### Living Choices Assisted Living Waiver

The Living Choices program allows beneficiaries to live in apartment-style units within assisted living facilities while receiving necessary personal care and support services. This is a crucial alternative for individuals who require more care than can be provided at home but do not yet require the intensive medical services of a nursing home [3].

#### Eligibility for Living Choices

To be eligible for the Living Choices Waiver, an applicant must meet all of the following criteria [3]:

*   **Age and Disability:** Be aged 65 or older, **OR** be between the ages of 21 and 64 with a physical disability.
*   **Residency:** Be a resident of Arkansas.
*   **Functional Need:** Meet the state's nursing home admission criteria at the intermediate level of care.
*   **Service Need:** Have a need for at least one of the available waiver services.
*   **Financial Criteria:** Meet the established financial criteria for Medicaid LTSS.

#### Services Provided by Living Choices

The waiver covers the cost of the services provided within the assisted living facility, though it generally **does not** cover the cost of room and board. Covered services typically include:

*   **Personal Care:** Assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, and eating.
*   **Medication Assistance:** Help with managing and taking prescribed medications.
*   **24-Hour Supervision:** Access to staff for emergency and non-emergency needs.
*   **Social Activities:** Organized activities to promote social engagement and well-being.
*   **Nursing Services:** Limited skilled nursing services as needed.

### Other HCBS Waivers

While Living Choices is a key program, Arkansas also offers other waivers, such as **ARChoices in Homecare**, which provides services to individuals aged 65 and older and adults with physical disabilities who need an intermediate level of care. ARChoices provides services like attendant care, home-delivered meals, and adult day care, allowing individuals to remain in their private homes or the homes of family members.

## Comprehensive Covered Services

Arkansas Medicaid, including the coverage provided through ARHOME, must cover all federally mandated services and may also cover a number of optional services chosen by the state. The goal is to provide a robust package of benefits to ensure comprehensive care [4].

### Mandatory Services

Federal law requires all state Medicaid programs to cover the following services:

*   **Inpatient and Outpatient Hospital Services:** Coverage for stays and services in a hospital setting.
*   **Physician Services:** Visits and treatments provided by a licensed medical doctor.
*   **Laboratory and X-ray Services:** Diagnostic testing.
*   **Nursing Facility Services:** For individuals aged 21 and older.
*   **Home Health Services:** Skilled nursing and therapy services provided in the home.
*   **Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic, and Treatment (EPSDT) Services:** Comprehensive and preventive health care services for children under age 21 (known as **Child Health Services** in Arkansas).
*   **Family Planning Services and Supplies.**
*   **Rural Health Clinic and Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC) Services.**
*   **Certified Nurse-Midwife Services.**

### Optional Services Covered by Arkansas Medicaid

Arkansas has opted to cover several optional services to enhance the quality of care for its beneficiaries. These often include:

*   **Prescription Drugs:** A comprehensive formulary of medications.
*   **Dental Services:** Coverage for necessary dental care, especially for children.
*   **Vision Services:** Coverage for eye exams and eyeglasses.
*   **Prosthetic Devices, Orthotics, and Durable Medical Equipment (DME).**
*   **Rehabilitative Services:** Including physical, occupational, and speech therapy.
*   **Mental Health and Substance Use Disorder Services:** A wide range of behavioral health treatments.

## The Application and Enrollment Process

Applying for Medicaid in Arkansas is a straightforward process, with multiple avenues available to ensure accessibility for all residents. The application process covers all major programs, including ARHOME and ARKids First.

### How to Apply

Applicants can submit a single application for their entire family through one of the following methods [5]:

1.  **Online (Preferred Method):** The most efficient way to apply is through the **Access Arkansas** portal at [https://access.arkansas.gov](). This platform allows applicants to apply for Medicaid, ARKids First, and other assistance programs simultaneously.
2.  **In-Person:** Applications can be submitted at any local **DHS County Office** statewide. Staff are available to assist with the application and answer questions.
3.  **By Mail or Phone:** Applicants can request a paper application by calling the DHS and then mail the completed form to their local county office.

### Key Steps in the Application Process

1.  **Gather Documentation:** Before starting, applicants should gather necessary documents, including proof of identity, citizenship or immigration status, income (pay stubs, tax returns), and information about any existing health insurance.
2.  **Submit Application:** Complete and submit the application through the chosen method.
3.  **Eligibility Determination:** The DHS will review the application and verify the information. This process includes checking income against the FPL limits and confirming non-financial eligibility factors.
4.  **Enrollment in ARHOME:** If an adult is determined eligible for ARHOME, they will be enrolled in one of the two private health plans (Blue Cross Blue Shield or Ambetter).
5.  **Annual Renewal:** Beneficiaries must complete an annual renewal to confirm their continued eligibility. This is a critical step, and failure to complete the renewal on time can result in a loss of coverage.

## In-Depth: The Private Health Plans of ARHOME

The use of private health insurance carriers is the most distinctive feature of the ARHOME program. This model, often referred to as a "premium assistance" or "private option" model, is designed to integrate Medicaid beneficiaries into the commercial insurance market, which can offer a broader network of providers and a more seamless experience for beneficiaries.

### The Role of Private Carriers

The two primary carriers, Blue Cross Blue Shield and Ambetter, operate as Qualified Health Plans (QHPs) on the state's health insurance marketplace. When an eligible individual is enrolled in ARHOME, the state pays the full premium for their QHP. This means that while the coverage is delivered through a private plan, the benefits package must meet or exceed the federal requirements for Medicaid, ensuring that beneficiaries receive all necessary medical services.

**Benefits of the Private Plan Model:**

*   **Expanded Provider Network:** Beneficiaries often gain access to the same network of doctors and specialists available to commercial insurance customers, potentially increasing their choice of providers.
*   **Familiarity:** The structure of the plan (e.g., ID cards, co-pays, deductibles) is similar to commercial insurance, which can ease the transition for individuals moving off or onto the program.
*   **Continuity of Care:** The model aims to provide a consistent experience, regardless of whether a person is covered by Medicaid or a subsidized private plan.

### Cost-Sharing and Premiums

While the state pays the premium, ARHOME does include some cost-sharing requirements for certain beneficiaries, which are designed to encourage personal responsibility and appropriate use of services.

*   **Premiums:** For beneficiaries with incomes above 100% of the FPL, a small monthly premium may be required. However, these premiums are capped at a very low percentage of the household income and are often waived for those who meet certain criteria, such as participating in healthy behaviors or completing a health risk assessment.
*   **Co-payments:** Small co-payments may be required for non-preventive services, such as doctor visits or non-emergency use of the emergency room. These co-payments are also subject to federal limits and are not applied to services for children (EPSDT), pregnant women, or certain preventive services.

## The Importance of EPSDT (Child Health Services)

The **Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic, and Treatment (EPSDT)** benefit is a mandatory, comprehensive benefit for all Medicaid-eligible children under the age of 21. In Arkansas, this is often referred to as **Child Health Services**. The purpose of EPSDT is to discover and treat health problems early, ensuring that children receive the necessary care to achieve optimal physical and mental health.

### Components of EPSDT

The EPSDT benefit is far-reaching and includes:

1.  **Screening Services:** Comprehensive health and developmental history, a complete physical exam, appropriate immunizations, laboratory tests, and health education.
2.  **Periodic Screenings:** Regular check-ups at specific intervals, from infancy through age 20, to monitor growth and development.
3.  **Vision Services:** Diagnosis and treatment for vision defects, including eyeglasses.
4.  **Dental Services:** Relief of pain and infections, restoration of teeth, and maintenance of dental health.
5.  **Hearing Services:** Diagnosis and treatment for hearing defects, including hearing aids.
6.  **Diagnostic Services:** Follow-up on any conditions discovered during a screening.
7.  **Treatment Services:** Any necessary medical, remedial, or other services to correct or ameliorate physical or mental illnesses or conditions discovered by the screening services, even if the service is not typically covered under the state's standard Medicaid plan for adults. This "treatment mandate" is what makes EPSDT so comprehensive.

## Financial Eligibility for Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ABD)

For the ABD population, the financial eligibility rules are more complex than the MAGI rules, as they involve both income and resource (asset) limits.

### Income Limits

For non-SSI-eligible individuals, Arkansas sets a Special Income Limit (SIL) for institutional and Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) care. This limit is typically 300% of the SSI Federal Benefit Rate (FBR).

*   **Medically Needy Program:** If an individual's income exceeds the standard Medicaid limit, they may still qualify under the Medically Needy program. This program allows them to deduct (or "spend down") their medical expenses from their income until the remaining amount meets the Medically Needy income level.

### Resource Limits (Asset Test)

Unlike MAGI-based Medicaid, the ABD and LTSS programs require an asset test.

*   **Individual Limit:** The resource limit is typically **$2,000** for a single individual.
*   **Countable Resources:** These include bank accounts, stocks, bonds, and second properties.
*   **Exempt Resources:** Certain assets are not counted, including the primary residence (up to a certain equity limit), one vehicle, household goods, and burial funds.

### Spousal Impoverishment Rules

When one spouse requires long-term care (in a nursing home or via an HCBS waiver) and the other spouse remains in the community (the "community spouse"), federal and state rules protect the community spouse from becoming impoverished.

*   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The community spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined countable assets, up to a maximum limit set annually by the federal government.
*   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The community spouse is also allowed to keep a minimum amount of the couple's combined income each month. If the community spouse's own income is below this minimum, a portion of the institutionalized spouse's income can be allocated to the community spouse to bring them up to the MMMNA.

## The Role of the Division of County Operations

The **Division of County Operations (DCO)** within the Arkansas Department of Human Services plays a crucial role in the Medicaid system. The DCO is responsible for the direct interaction with applicants and beneficiaries, handling the initial application intake, eligibility determination, and case management for all public assistance programs, including Medicaid and ARKids First.

*   **Local Access:** Every county in Arkansas has a DHS County Office, providing local access for in-person applications, document submission, and case inquiries.
*   **Case Renewal:** DCO staff manage the annual renewal process, which is essential for beneficiaries to maintain their coverage.

## Conclusion

Arkansas Medicaid, through its innovative ARHOME structure and comprehensive waiver programs like Living Choices, provides a robust safety net for the state's most vulnerable residents. By leveraging private insurance for its expansion population and maintaining strong traditional Medicaid benefits for children, the elderly, and the disabled, Arkansas ensures that its citizens have access to the care they need. Understanding the nuances of ARHOME, the specific eligibility pathways, and the critical role of the Living Choices waiver is essential for any resident seeking to navigate the state's healthcare landscape.
