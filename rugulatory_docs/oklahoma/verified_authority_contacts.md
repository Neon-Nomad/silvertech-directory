# Verified Authority Contacts for Long-Term Care and Senior Services in Oklahoma

## Introduction: Navigating Oklahoma's Senior Services Network

For seniors, their families, and caregivers in Oklahoma, accessing the right information and support often requires navigating a complex network of state and federal agencies. This comprehensive guide provides a verified directory of the most critical governmental and advocacy authorities responsible for Medicaid, licensing, consumer protection, and veterans' benefits in the state. Each entry includes essential contact information, a brief description of the agency's role, and links to official resources, serving as a vital starting point for securing services and resolving concerns.

The agencies listed below are the primary points of contact for:
*   **Financial Assistance and Healthcare:** Medicaid eligibility and services.
*   **Quality and Safety Oversight:** Licensing, inspection, and complaint investigation of long-term care facilities.
*   **Advocacy and Rights Protection:** Ombudsman services for residents of long-term care.
*   **Veterans' Support:** State-specific benefits and services for Oklahoma veterans.

## 1. Oklahoma Health Care Authority (OHCA) – SoonerCare (Medicaid)

The **Oklahoma Health Care Authority (OHCA)** is the state agency that administers the **SoonerCare** program, Oklahoma's version of Medicaid [1]. The OHCA is responsible for determining eligibility, managing the state's Medicaid managed care program (SoonerSelect), and overseeing the **ADvantage Waiver Program**, which provides home and community-based services (HCBS) as an alternative to nursing home care.

### Key Services and Programs Managed by OHCA
*   **SoonerCare Eligibility:** Provides information and assistance with applying for Medicaid coverage for children, pregnant women, adults, and the aged, blind, and disabled [2].
*   **ADvantage Waiver:** Manages the HCBS waiver that allows eligible individuals to receive long-term care services in their home or community setting [3].
*   **SoonerSelect:** Oversees the managed care organizations (Aetna Better Health, Humana Healthy Horizons, Oklahoma Complete Health) that provide healthcare services to most SoonerCare members [4].
*   **Provider Enrollment:** Manages the enrollment and compliance of healthcare providers participating in the SoonerCare network.

## 2. Oklahoma State Department of Health (OSDH) – Long Term Care Service

The **Oklahoma State Department of Health (OSDH)**, through its **Long Term Care Service (LTCS)** division, is the state's primary regulatory and licensing authority for long-term care facilities [5]. This agency is responsible for ensuring that nursing homes, assisted living centers, and other adult care facilities comply with state licensing standards and federal certification requirements.

### Key Regulatory Functions
*   **Licensing and Certification:** Issues and renews state licenses for long-term care facilities and certifies them for participation in Medicare and Medicaid [6].
*   **Inspection and Survey:** Conducts unannounced, periodic surveys (inspections) to monitor facility compliance with quality of care, resident rights, and safety standards.
*   **Complaint Investigation:** Investigates all complaints related to regulatory violations in licensed facilities (see Section 4 for the dedicated hotline) [7].
*   **Enforcement:** Imposes sanctions, including civil money penalties and license revocation, against facilities found to be non-compliant.

## 3. Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

The **Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman** is an independent advocacy program dedicated to protecting the rights, health, safety, and welfare of residents in nursing homes, assisted living, and residential care homes [8]. The ombudsman investigates and resolves complaints on behalf of residents, focusing on quality of life and resident rights issues.

| **Local Contact** | Contact your local Area Agency on Aging (AAA) for the regional ombudsman supervisor [9]. |

### Key Advocacy Services
*   **Complaint Resolution:** Works to resolve issues related to resident care, facility practices, and resident rights, acting as a mediator between the resident and the facility.
*   **Resident Rights Education:** Educates residents, families, and facility staff on the rights guaranteed to long-term care residents under state and federal law.
*   **Systemic Advocacy:** Monitors laws and regulations and advocates for policy changes to improve the quality of long-term care across the state [10].
*   **Confidentiality:** All complaints are handled confidentially, and the ombudsman will not disclose the complainant's identity without consent.

## 4. Complaint and Abuse Reporting Hotlines

Oklahoma maintains distinct hotlines for reporting different types of concerns, ensuring that each issue is directed to the appropriate investigative authority. It is crucial to use the correct hotline based on the nature of the complaint.

### A. OSDH Long Term Care Complaint Hotline (Regulatory Violations)

This hotline is for reporting concerns about the quality of care, safety, and regulatory compliance in licensed long-term care facilities.

| Contact Type | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agency** | OSDH, Long Term Care Service |
| **Complaint Hotline** | **1-800-747-8419** (Toll-Free) |
| **Purpose** | To report violations of state licensing and federal certification standards (e.g., poor care, sanitation issues, inadequate staffing) [11]. |
| **Hours** | Staffed during regular business hours (8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday-Friday). |

### B. Adult Protective Services (APS) Hotline (Abuse, Neglect, Exploitation)

This 24/7 hotline is managed by the Oklahoma Department of Human Services (OKDHS) and is the mandated reporting channel for suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults, including those in long-term care settings [12].

| Contact Type | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agency** | Oklahoma Department of Human Services (OKDHS) |
| **APS Hotline** | **1-800-522-3511** (Toll-Free, 24/7) |
| **Purpose** | To report suspected physical abuse, sexual abuse, emotional abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation of a vulnerable adult [13]. |

## 5. Oklahoma Department of Veterans Affairs (ODVA)

The **Oklahoma Department of Veterans Affairs (ODVA)** is the state agency dedicated to serving Oklahoma's veterans and their families. The ODVA provides direct services, manages the State Veterans Homes, and assists veterans in obtaining their earned state and federal benefits [14].

### ODVA Contact Information

| Contact Type | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agency Name** | Oklahoma Department of Veterans Affairs (ODVA) |
| **General Phone** | **855-701-6382** (Toll-Free) |
| **Official Website** | [https://oklahoma.gov/veterans.html](https://oklahoma.gov/veterans.html) |
| **Mailing Address** | ODVA, 2311 N. Central Ave., Oklahoma City, OK 73105 |
| **Veterans Centers** | Information on the seven State Veterans Homes [15]. |

### Key Services and Resources
*   **Claims Assistance:** Provides free, professional assistance from accredited Veteran Service Representatives (VSRs) to file claims for federal VA benefits, including disability compensation, pension, and healthcare enrollment [16].
*   **State Veterans Homes:** Manages the seven State Veterans Centers that provide long-term skilled nursing and domiciliary care to eligible veterans [17].
*   **Financial Benefits:** Provides information and assistance regarding state-specific financial benefits, such as property tax exemptions and military retirement pay exemptions.
*   **Outreach:** Conducts outreach to connect veterans with employment, education, and mental health resources.
