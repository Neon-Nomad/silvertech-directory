# Comprehensive Guide to DC Medicaid Programs: Eligibility, Benefits, and Long-Term Care in the District of Columbia

The District of Columbia Medicaid program, known as **DC Medicaid**, is a critical component of the District's healthcare infrastructure, providing essential medical coverage to a significant portion of its residents. As a joint federal and state program, DC Medicaid is administered by the **Department of Health Care Finance (DHCF)** [1], and it is designed to ensure that low-income families, children, pregnant women, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities have access to comprehensive healthcare services. Currently, the program serves approximately one in three District residents, underscoring its vital role in public health [2].

This comprehensive guide delves into the specifics of DC Medicaid, including its eligibility framework, the range of covered services, the critical **Elderly and Persons with Physical Disabilities (EPD) Waiver** for long-term care, and the role of Managed Care Organizations (MCOs).

## The Foundation of DC Medicaid: Eligibility and Enrollment

Eligibility for DC Medicaid is determined by a combination of non-financial and financial requirements, including residency in the District of Columbia. The program utilizes two primary methodologies for determining financial eligibility: **Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI)** and **Non-MAGI**.

### MAGI vs. Non-MAGI Eligibility

The Affordable Care Act (ACA) introduced the MAGI methodology, which is used for most eligibility groups, including children, pregnant women, parents/caretaker relatives, and childless adults. MAGI simplifies the process by basing eligibility on tax rules for adjusted gross income, with no asset test.

**MAGI-Based Groups:**
*   Adults (age 21-64) without dependent children.
*   Pregnant women.
*   Parents/caretaker relatives.
*   Children under the age of 21.

**Non-MAGI-Based Groups:**
The Non-MAGI methodology is reserved for populations where income and asset tests are still applicable, primarily those seeking long-term care or who are eligible based on age or disability.

*   Individuals aged 65 or older.
*   Individuals who are blind or have a disability.
*   Participants in Home and Community-Based Waivers (like the EPD Waiver).
*   Individuals seeking Long-Term Care services.
*   Recipients of Supplemental Security Income (SSI).

### Financial Eligibility Criteria (2025 Estimates)

While MAGI eligibility is based on a percentage of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) and has no asset limit, Non-MAGI eligibility for the elderly and disabled has specific income and asset limits that must be met.

| Eligibility Group | Income Limit (as % of FPL) | Asset Limit (Individual) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Children (0-18)** | 324% FPL | None (MAGI) | Highest FPL limit for children in the District [3]. |
| **Pregnant Women** | 324% FPL | None (MAGI) | Coverage extends for 12 months postpartum. |
| **Parents/Caretaker Relatives** | 221% FPL | None (MAGI) | Subject to change based on federal and local policy. |
| **Childless Adults** | 215% FPL (Current) | None (MAGI) | This limit is subject to a planned decrease to 138% FPL [4]. |
| **Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ABD)** | Varies (Non-MAGI) | $4,000 (Individual) | Income limits are complex and often tied to SSI standards [5]. |
| **Nursing Home Medicaid** | $2,901 per month (2025) | $4,000 (Individual) | This is the maximum income for institutional care, with a spouse allowance [6]. |

*Note: The FPL percentages are subject to annual change. Applicants should consult the DHCF website or the DC Health Link for the most current figures.*

## Comprehensive Services Covered by DC Medicaid

DC Medicaid provides a broad spectrum of medical and behavioral health services. These services are designed to be comprehensive, covering everything from routine preventative care to specialized long-term services.

**Key Covered Services Include [7]:**
*   **Inpatient and Outpatient Hospital Services:** Including emergency care and medically necessary surgeries.
*   **Physician and Clinic Services:** Routine doctor visits, specialist consultations, and preventative care.
*   **Prescription Drugs:** Coverage for a wide range of medications.
*   **Mental Health and Substance Use Disorder Services:** Comprehensive behavioral health treatment.
*   **Dental Services:** Coverage for necessary dental treatment.
*   **Vision and Eye Care:** Including eye exams and necessary corrective lenses.
*   **Home Health Services:** Skilled nursing and therapy services provided in the home.
*   **Durable Medical Equipment (DME):** Equipment like wheelchairs, oxygen, and hospital beds.
*   **Transportation:** Medically necessary transportation to and from appointments.

## The Elderly and Persons with Physical Disabilities (EPD) Waiver

For individuals who require a nursing home level of care but wish to remain in their homes or a community setting, the District offers the **Elderly and Persons with Physical Disabilities (EPD) Waiver**. This is a critical Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver that provides an array of supports to promote independence and quality of life [8].

### EPD Waiver Eligibility and Purpose

The EPD Waiver is designed for DC residents who are:
1.  **Elderly:** Aged 65 or older.
2.  **Physically Disabled:** Aged 18 to 64.
3.  **Medically Eligible:** Assessed as requiring a nursing home level of care.
4.  **Financially Eligible:** Meet the Non-MAGI financial criteria for long-term care Medicaid.

The core purpose of the EPD Waiver is to prevent unnecessary institutionalization by funding services that support the individual in their community setting.

### Detailed EPD Waiver Services

The EPD Waiver covers a robust list of services, all coordinated through a person-centered service plan developed by a Case Manager [9].

| Service Category | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adult Day Health Program (ADHP)** | Provides therapeutic, rehabilitative, and support services, including nursing and social work, in a supervised, non-residential setting during the day. |
| **Assisted Living Facilities (ALF)** | Covers the cost of services (not room and board) for participants living in licensed assisted living facilities, promoting independence within a supportive environment. |
| **Case Management Services** | The core service, involving assessment, planning, coordination, and advocacy to ensure all necessary services are delivered effectively. |
| **Personal Care Aide (PCA) Services** | Hands-on assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, grooming, walking, and medication reminders. |
| **Respite Services** | Temporary relief for the primary caregiver, providing PCA-like services in the participant's environment. |
| **Homemaker Services** | General household activities like meal preparation, light housekeeping, and running care-related errands (does not include personal care). |
| **Chore Aide Services** | Heavy-duty, time-limited housecleaning to make the participant's environment clean and safe. |
| **Environmental Accessibility Adaptation (EAA)** | Physical adaptations to the home, such as ramps, stair-lifts, and grab bars, necessary to ensure the participant's health and safety. |
| **Community Transition Services** | Non-recurring expenses for individuals transitioning from an institution (like a nursing home) back into a community setting, such as security deposits and essential household items. |
| **Participant-Directed Services (Services My Way)** | Allows participants to have greater control over their care, including the ability to hire, train, and manage their own personal care workers. |
| **Therapies** | Includes Occupational Therapy (OT) and Physical Therapy (PT) to maintain or improve functional abilities and prevent further disability. |
| **Personal Emergency Response Services (PERS)** | An electronic communication system that allows the participant to signal for help in an emergency. |

### Applying for the EPD Waiver

The application process for the EPD Waiver is coordinated through the **Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC)**, a part of the Department of Aging and Community Living (DACL). Interested individuals should contact the ADRC to begin the screening and application process [10].

## DC Medicaid Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)

The majority of DC Medicaid beneficiaries receive their care through a Managed Care Organization (MCO). MCOs are private health plans contracted by the DHCF to provide all covered Medicaid services to their members. This system is designed to coordinate care, improve health outcomes, and manage costs.

As of the current period, the three primary MCOs operating in the District are [11]:
1.  **Wellpoint DC**
2.  **AmeriHealth Caritas DC**
3.  **MedStar Family Choice DC**

Beneficiaries are typically required to choose one of these MCOs upon enrollment. The MCO then manages the member's care, including access to a network of doctors, specialists, and hospitals.

## The Application Process for DC Medicaid

The method of application depends on the eligibility category:

*   **MAGI-Based Applicants:** Individuals who qualify under MAGI (e.g., children, childless adults) should apply through **DC Health Link** or the **District Direct** website/mobile app [12].
*   **Non-MAGI-Based Applicants:** Individuals who are aged, blind, or disabled, or who are applying for long-term care services, must submit the **Integrated Application for Medical Assistance** through the DHCF or a designated service center [13].

## Conclusion

DC Medicaid, administered by the DHCF, is a robust program that provides a vital safety net for the District's most vulnerable populations. From comprehensive medical services to the specialized, community-focused support of the EPD Waiver, the program is structured to deliver quality care and promote independent living. Residents are encouraged to utilize the resources provided by the DHCF and the ADRC to navigate the eligibility and enrollment process.
