# North Carolina Licensing Authority: Ensuring Quality and Compliance in Long-Term Care

The quality and safety of long-term care facilities in North Carolina are overseen by the **Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR)**, a division of the North Carolina Department of Health and Human Services (NCDHHS). The DHSR is the state's primary regulatory body, responsible for licensing, monitoring, and enforcing standards in a wide range of healthcare facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living facilities.

This comprehensive guide details the structure of the DHSR's licensing authority, the specific sections responsible for different types of long-term care, the inspection process, and the public tools available to consumers, such as the Adult Care Home Star Rating Program.

## I. The Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR)

The DHSR is tasked with protecting the health, safety, and well-being of North Carolina citizens through the regulation of medical, mental health, and adult care facilities. Its functions are divided into specialized sections, with two being most relevant to long-term care: the Nursing Home Licensure and Certification Section (NHLCS) and the Adult Care Licensure Section (ACLS).

## II. Nursing Home Licensure and Certification Section (NHLCS)

The NHLCS is responsible for the regulatory oversight of skilled nursing facilities (nursing homes) in North Carolina. This section ensures that facilities comply with both state licensing requirements and federal certification standards (Medicare and Medicaid).

### A. Licensing and Certification Process
1.  **Certificate of Need (CON):** Before a new nursing home can be built or an existing one expanded, the owner must obtain a Certificate of Need (CON) from the DHSR. This process is designed to ensure that new facilities are only established where there is a demonstrated need for the services.
2.  **Licensure:** The NHLCS licenses nursing homes to operate in the state. Licenses are typically in effect for one year and require an initial survey and annual renewal.
3.  **Federal Certification:** Most nursing homes in North Carolina participate in the Medicare/Medicaid program. To do so, they must be certified by the federal government, which requires compliance with strict federal rules. The NHLCS conducts the necessary inspections on behalf of the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS).

### B. Inspections and Enforcement
*   **Routine Inspections:** Routine inspections (surveys) of Medicare/Medicaid-certified nursing homes occur every 9 to 15 months. These comprehensive surveys are conducted by multi-disciplinary teams, including nurses, pharmacists, and social workers.
*   **Scope of Review:** Surveys review all aspects of resident care and facility operation, including resident rights, quality of care, infection control, staffing, and the physical environment [1].
*   **Enforcement:** If a facility is found to be non-compliant, the NHLCS issues a **Statement of Deficiency**. The facility must then submit a plan of correction. Depending on the severity of the deficiency, the facility may face penalties, including fines, denial of payment, or license revocation.

## III. Adult Care Licensure Section (ACLS)

The ACLS is the regulatory body for assisted living facilities, which are referred to in North Carolina as **Adult Care Homes** and **Family Care Homes**.

### A. Scope of Licensure
The ACLS is responsible for:
*   **Adult Care Homes:** Licensing facilities with seven or more beds.
*   **Family Care Homes:** Licensing smaller facilities with two to six beds.
*   **Multi-Unit Housing with Services:** Registering these assisted living facilities.
*   **Administrator Certification:** Issuing and renewing certificates for Adult Care Home administrators.

### B. Inspection and Monitoring
The ACLS performs initial and annual inspections of licensed adult care facilities. However, the DHSR delegates much of the routine monitoring and complaint investigation for Adult Care Homes to the **County Departments of Social Services (DSS)**. The ACLS survey teams focus on regulatory oversight, administrative actions, and providing technical assistance to both facilities and the county DSS offices [2].

### C. The Adult Care Home Star Rating Program

North Carolina has implemented a **Star Rating Program** for Adult Care Homes to provide consumers with an easy-to-understand measure of a facility's compliance history. This program is a key public tool for making informed care decisions.

#### 1. Rating Criteria
*   **Base Score:** Each facility begins with a base score of 100 points at the time of its annual inspection.
*   **Demerit Points:** Demerit points are deducted from the base score for any deficiencies or violations of state rules and statutes found during the inspection.
*   **Star Scale:** The rating is based on the final score, ranging from zero to four stars.
    *   **Four Stars (Highest Rating):** To achieve the highest rating of four stars, a facility must obtain a score of 100 points or greater on **two consecutive annual surveys** [3].
    *   **Zero Stars (Lowest Rating):** A facility with a score below a certain threshold, or with severe, uncorrected deficiencies, may receive a zero-star rating.

#### 2. Consumer Use
The Star Rating is intended to be a simple, accessible tool for consumers. By searching the DHSR website, families can quickly view a facility's rating, which reflects its recent history of compliance with state regulations. It is important to note that the Star Rating primarily measures regulatory compliance and should be considered alongside other factors, such as personal visits and the facility's specific services.

## IV. Public Access to Inspection Reports

Transparency is a key component of North Carolina's regulatory system. The DHSR makes facility inspection reports and enforcement actions publicly available.

*   **Nursing Homes:** Consumers can search for Medicare-certified nursing homes and view their inspection reports and ratings on the federal **Care Compare** website, which is linked through the DHSR.
*   **Adult Care Homes:** The DHSR website provides a search function to find facilities and view their Star Ratings, as well as information on penalties and violations.

By providing clear regulatory oversight, conducting rigorous inspections, and offering transparent public reporting tools, the North Carolina DHSR plays a vital role in protecting the vulnerable population in long-term care facilities across the state.
