# North Carolina Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Regulatory, Protective, and Advocacy Channels

A transparent and effective complaint process is essential for maintaining accountability and quality of care in North Carolina's long-term care facilities. For residents, family members, and concerned citizens, understanding the distinct roles of the state's three primary complaint-handling entities—the **Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR)**, **Adult Protective Services (APS)**, and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)**—is crucial for ensuring a concern is directed to the correct authority for the fastest and most appropriate response.

This comprehensive guide details the complaint process in North Carolina, outlining the jurisdiction, reporting methods, and investigation procedures for each key agency.

## I. Regulatory Complaints: The DHSR Complaint Intake Unit (CIU)

The **Division of Health Service Regulation (DHSR)**, a division of the North Carolina Department of Health and Human Services (NCDHHS), is the state's licensing and regulatory authority. The DHSR's **Complaint Intake Unit (CIU)** is the central hub for receiving and triaging complaints against licensed healthcare facilities.

### A. DHSR Jurisdiction and Scope
The DHSR investigates complaints that allege a facility is in violation of state licensing standards or federal certification requirements. These are regulatory complaints that focus on the facility's compliance with the law.

| Facilities Covered by DHSR CIU | Types of Complaints Investigated |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nursing Homes** (Skilled Nursing Facilities) | Quality of Care (e.g., poor wound care, medication errors) |
| **Adult Care Homes** (Assisted Living) | Environmental Issues (e.g., sanitation, fire safety) |
| **Hospitals** and other licensed healthcare facilities | Resident Rights Violations (e.g., improper transfer/discharge) |
| **Home Health Agencies** and **Hospices** | Regulatory Non-Compliance (e.g., inadequate staffing levels) |

### B. How to File a Complaint with DHSR
The DHSR strongly encourages the use of its toll-free hotline for immediate reporting of serious concerns.

| DHSR Complaint Intake Unit Contact Information |
| :--- |
| **Complaint Hotline (Toll-Free within NC):** **(800) 624-3004** |
| **Local Phone:** (919) 855-4500 |
| **Hotline Hours:** 9:00 a.m. – 12:00 p.m. and 1:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m. (Weekdays, except holidays) [1] |
| **Mailing Address:** 2711 Mail Service Center, Raleigh, NC 27699-2711 |
| **Website:** https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/ciu/filecomplaint.html |

### C. Complaint Triage and Investigation Priority
The DHSR prioritizes complaints based on the severity of the alleged violation to ensure the most serious issues are addressed first.
*   **Immediate Jeopardy (IJ):** Allegations that pose an immediate threat to a resident's health or safety. These complaints are investigated **immediately** (within 2 business days).
*   **High Priority:** Allegations that may cause physical or psychosocial harm, but do not meet the IJ threshold. These are typically investigated within **15 days** [2].
*   **Lower Priority:** Complaints that do not involve immediate harm are often investigated during the facility's next scheduled annual inspection.

If a complaint is substantiated, the DHSR issues a **Statement of Deficiency**, requiring the facility to submit a plan of correction and face potential penalties, including fines or license action.

## II. Protective Services: Adult Protective Services (APS)

**Adult Protective Services (APS)** is a program administered by the local **County Departments of Social Services (DSS)**. APS is the designated agency for investigating allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of disabled adults. This is a crucial distinction from the DHSR, which handles regulatory compliance.

### A. APS Jurisdiction and Scope
APS investigates situations where a disabled adult (age 18 or older) is alleged to be abused, neglected, or exploited, and is in need of protection. This includes disabled adults living in their own homes, in the community, or in long-term care facilities.

| APS Focus | Types of Allegations Investigated |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Abuse** | Willful infliction of physical pain, injury, or mental anguish. |
| **Neglect (Caregiver)** | Failure of a caregiver to provide necessary services (e.g., food, shelter, medical care). |
| **Neglect (Self-Neglect)** | The disabled adult's inability to provide for their own essential needs. |
| **Exploitation** | Misappropriation or misuse of the disabled adult's property or funds. |

### B. How to File a Complaint with APS
Reports of abuse, neglect, or exploitation must be made to the local DSS office in the county where the adult resides.

| Adult Protective Services Contact Information |
| :--- |
| **Contact:** Local County Department of Social Services (DSS) |
| **Process:** Call the local DSS office and ask for Adult Protective Services. |
| **Source:** NCDHHS Adult Protective Services website [3] |

**The APS Investigation:** Once a report is accepted, an APS social worker conducts a thorough investigation. The identity of the reporter is kept confidential. If the investigation confirms the need for protective services, APS works to develop an intervention plan, which may involve coordinating with law enforcement, the DHSR, or the Long-Term Care Ombudsman.

## III. Advocacy and Grievance Resolution: The Long-Term Care Ombudsman

The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** offers a non-regulatory, confidential avenue for resolving grievances and advocating for residents' rights. The Ombudsman is an independent voice for the resident.

### A. Ombudsman Jurisdiction and Scope
The Ombudsman is best suited for addressing quality of life issues, resident rights concerns, and non-regulatory grievances that may not rise to the level of a DHSR violation or APS case.

| Ombudsman Focus | Types of Grievances Handled |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Resident Rights** | Issues related to privacy, dignity, choice, and freedom of association. |
| **Quality of Life** | Concerns about food, activities, facility environment, or staff attitudes. |
| **Grievances** | Disputes over facility policies, communication issues, or minor administrative decisions. |
| **Consultation** | Providing guidance on long-term care options, Medicare/Medicaid, and residents' rights. |

### B. How to Contact the Ombudsman
The Ombudsman is often the best first contact for non-emergency grievances, as their process is confidential and focused on mediation.

| Long-Term Care Ombudsman Contact Information |
| :--- |
| **Toll-Free Phone:** **(800) 662-7030** |
| **State Office Phone:** (919) 855-3400 |
| **Website:** https://www.ncdhhs.gov/divisions/aging/long-term-care-ombudsman |

**The Ombudsman Process:** The Ombudsman will not take action without the resident's consent. They investigate the complaint and work with all parties to find a satisfactory resolution. If the complaint involves a regulatory violation or abuse, the Ombudsman will refer the complaint to the DHSR or APS while continuing to advocate for the resident.

## IV. Summary of Complaint Avenues and Best Practices

| Complaint Avenue | Primary Focus | Facilities Covered | Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **DHSR Complaint Intake Unit (CIU)** | Regulatory violations, health and safety standards, quality of care. | Licensed Facilities (Nursing Homes, Adult Care Homes, etc.) | **Hotline:** (800) 624-3004 |
| **Adult Protective Services (APS)** | Abuse, neglect (caregiver or self), and exploitation of disabled adults. | All settings, including long-term care facilities. | Local County Department of Social Services (DSS) |
| **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** | Resident rights, quality of life, and grievance resolution. | Nursing Homes, Adult Care Homes. | **Toll-Free:** (800) 662-7030 |

**Best Practices for Filing an Effective Complaint:**
1.  **Immediate Danger:** If a resident is in immediate danger, call 911 first.
2.  **Document Everything:** Keep a detailed, chronological log of incidents, including dates, times, and names of staff involved.
3.  **Choose the Right Channel:** Select the agency whose jurisdiction best matches the nature of your complaint (Regulatory, Protective, or Advocacy).
4.  **Be Specific:** Provide clear, factual details about what happened, who was involved, and the location.
5.  **Follow Up:** Note the date and time of your report and the name of the person you spoke with. Follow up periodically to check on the status of the investigation.

By understanding and utilizing these three distinct channels, North Carolina residents and their advocates can effectively ensure that long-term care facilities are held to the highest standards of care and compliance.
