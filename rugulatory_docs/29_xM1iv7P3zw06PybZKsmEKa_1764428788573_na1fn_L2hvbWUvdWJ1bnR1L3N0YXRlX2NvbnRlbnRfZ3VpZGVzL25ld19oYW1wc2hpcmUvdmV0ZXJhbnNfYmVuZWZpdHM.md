# New Hampshire Veterans Benefits: A Comprehensive Guide to State and Local Support

The State of New Hampshire, known as the Granite State, honors its military community with a robust and comprehensive package of state-specific benefits, programs, and services. These benefits are designed to support veterans, service members, and their families in areas ranging from financial relief and property tax exemptions to healthcare, employment, and long-term care. The primary coordinating body for these services is the **New Hampshire Office of Veterans Services (OVS)**, a division of the Department of Military Affairs and Veterans Services (DMAVS) [1].

This guide provides an in-depth look at the state-level benefits available to New Hampshire veterans, focusing on eligibility requirements, application processes, and the critical resources, including the **New Hampshire Veterans Home** and various financial assistance programs, that contribute to the well-being of those who have served.

## I. Administration and Advocacy for New Hampshire Veterans

The New Hampshire Office of Veterans Services (OVS) is the central point of contact for veterans seeking assistance. Its mission is to act as an advocate and resource, ensuring that every eligible veteran and their dependent receives the full range of federal and state benefits they have earned.

### A. Key Administrative Details

### B. The Role of the Veterans Service Officer (VSO)

The OVS employs and coordinates a network of **Veterans Service Officers (VSOs)** who are accredited by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA). These VSOs are highly trained professionals who provide free assistance with:

*   Filing claims for federal VA benefits, including disability compensation, pension, and education.
*   Appealing unfavorable VA decisions.
*   Connecting veterans with state and local resources.
*   Providing guidance on eligibility for state programs, such as tax exemptions and the State Veterans Home.

Veterans are strongly encouraged to work with a New Hampshire VSO to navigate the complex application processes for both state and federal benefits.

## II. State-Specific Financial and Tax Benefits

New Hampshire offers some of the most significant property tax relief programs for veterans in the nation, alongside other financial incentives designed to ease the financial burden on military families.

### A. Property Tax Credits and Exemptions

Because New Hampshire does not have a state income tax or general sales tax, property tax relief is a cornerstone of its veterans benefits package. These benefits are administered at the local level by the city or town, but the eligibility criteria are set by state law (RSA 72:28 and 72:28-a).

1.  **Standard Veterans Tax Credit:**
    *   **Benefit:** A mandatory tax credit of **\$50** is available to every veteran who served in the U.S. Armed Forces during a qualifying war period and was honorably discharged [2].
    *   **Eligibility:** The veteran must have been a New Hampshire resident for at least one year before April 1st of the tax year and own the property.
2.  **All Veterans Tax Credit:**
    *   **Benefit:** A tax credit equal to the amount of the standard veterans tax credit, or a greater amount as adopted by the municipality, is available to veterans who meet the service requirements but did not serve during a qualifying war period [2].
3.  **Service-Connected Disability Tax Credit (Total and Permanent Disability):**
    *   **Benefit:** A significant tax credit of **\$700** is available to veterans who are determined to be totally and permanently disabled from a service-connected disability by the VA [2].
    *   **Eligibility:** The veteran must be honorably discharged and own the property. Some municipalities may vote to increase this credit amount.
4.  **Surviving Spouse Tax Credit:**
    *   **Benefit:** The surviving spouse of a veteran who was killed in action or died from a service-connected disability is eligible for a tax credit of **\$2,000** on their property tax bill [2].

### B. War Bonuses

New Hampshire has historically provided bonus payments to veterans who served during specific conflicts. While the application periods for some of the older bonuses have closed, the state has periodically offered new bonuses, and the OVS is the best resource for current information.

### C. Financial Assistance Programs

Several state and non-profit programs provide direct financial assistance to New Hampshire veterans in need:

1.  **Veterans Relief and Support Fund:** This state-managed fund is designed to assist homeless veterans, provide mental health support, and offer traditional services to veterans and their families. It often works in partnership with local veterans organizations to distribute aid [3].
2.  **Veterans Count (Easterseals New Hampshire):** This is a critical non-profit program that provides a safety net of case management and emergency financial assistance to meet the unexpected needs of veterans and their families. This can include assistance with rent, utilities, food, and other essential living expenses [4].
3.  **Supportive Services for Veteran Families (SSVF):** Administered by organizations like Harbor Care, the SSVF program is federally funded but locally executed to help veterans at risk of homelessness secure and maintain stable housing. Services include housing counseling, benefits assistance, and temporary financial assistance [5].

## III. New Hampshire Veterans Home (NHVH)

The **New Hampshire Veterans Home (NHVH)**, located in Tilton, is the state's only long-term care facility dedicated exclusively to the care of New Hampshire veterans. It provides a high level of skilled nursing care, residential care, and specialized services in a setting that honors their service.

### A. Services Provided

The NHVH is a comprehensive facility offering:

*   **Skilled Nursing Care:** 24-hour medical care for veterans with complex health needs.
*   **Residential Care:** A less intensive level of care for veterans who require assistance with daily living activities.
*   **Rehabilitation Services:** Physical, occupational, and speech therapy.
*   **Specialized Dementia Care:** Dedicated units for veterans with Alzheimer's disease and other forms of dementia.
*   **Hospice and Palliative Care:** Comfort care for end-of-life needs.

### B. Eligibility and Admission

Admission to the NHVH is based on a specific set of criteria established by the state (RSA 119:1-a):

1.  **Veteran Status:** The applicant must have served on active duty, other than active duty for training, in the U.S. Armed Forces and have been honorably discharged [6].
2.  **Residency:** The applicant must have been a resident of New Hampshire for at least **one year** prior to the date of application, **OR** had New Hampshire listed as their home of record upon entry into the service [6].
3.  **Medical Need:** The applicant must be medically qualified to receive the level of care provided by the Home, as determined by the NHVH medical staff.
4.  **Financial:** While there is a cost of care, no veteran will be denied admission due to an inability to pay. The Home assists veterans in applying for federal VA per diem payments and other financial resources.

The application process is managed by the NHVH Admissions Office, and interested veterans or their families should contact the Home directly for the most current application packet and waitlist information.

## IV. Employment, Education, and Licensing Benefits

New Hampshire provides several benefits to help veterans transition to civilian life, advance their careers, and pursue educational goals.

### A. Employment Preference

Veterans are afforded preference in hiring for state employment positions.

*   **State Employment Preference:** Honorably discharged veterans, their spouses, and the surviving spouses of veterans killed in action are granted preference in hiring and promotion for classified state positions, provided they meet the minimum qualifications for the job [7].
*   **Licensing and Certification:** The state often works to expedite the process of converting military training and experience into civilian professional licenses and certifications, particularly for medical and technical fields.

### B. Educational Assistance

While the federal GI Bill is the primary source of educational funding, New Hampshire offers supplemental benefits:

*   **Tuition Waivers:** The state offers tuition waivers for certain dependents of veterans who are totally and permanently disabled or who died as a result of service-connected disabilities, allowing them to attend state-supported colleges and universities tuition-free [8].
*   **Veterans' Education Counselors:** The OVS and various state educational institutions employ counselors to help veterans navigate their educational benefits and transition to academic life.

### C. Recreational and Other Benefits

1.  **Free Hunting and Fishing Licenses:** Veterans with a service-connected disability rating of **100% permanent and total** are eligible for a free, perpetual New Hampshire Fish and Game License, covering all hunting and fishing privileges [9].
2.  **Motor Vehicle Registration and Plates:** The state offers special veteran license plates, including plates for disabled veterans, former prisoners of war, and recipients of high military honors. Disabled veterans may also be eligible for certain exemptions from motor vehicle registration fees.

## V. Healthcare and Burial Benefits

In addition to the federal VA healthcare system, New Hampshire provides support for veterans' health and final resting place.

### A. State-Level Healthcare Support

While the VA provides the bulk of healthcare, the state supports veterans through:

*   **Mental Health Services:** The state actively funds and supports programs, often through partnerships with organizations like Easterseals, to provide mental health and substance use disorder treatment specifically tailored for veterans and their unique challenges [3].
*   **Coordination of Care:** The OVS works closely with the Manchester VA Medical Center and the VA Regional Benefit Office to ensure seamless coordination between state and federal resources [10].

### B. Burial and Cemetery Benefits

New Hampshire does not operate a state veterans cemetery. However, the state assists veterans and their families in securing burial benefits at the **New Hampshire State Veterans Cemetery** in Boscawen, which is a federally-funded cemetery managed by the state.

*   **Eligibility:** Any veteran who was a resident of New Hampshire at the time of death, or who was a resident for a minimum of one year and was honorably discharged, is eligible for burial at the State Veterans Cemetery, along with their spouse and dependent children [11].
*   **Cost:** There is no charge for the grave site, opening and closing, or perpetual care for the veteran.

## VI. Conclusion

New Hampshire's commitment to its veterans is evident in the breadth and depth of its state-specific benefits. From the critical property tax relief that directly impacts a veteran's cost of living to the compassionate, high-quality care provided at the New Hampshire Veterans Home, the state provides a strong support system. Veterans and their families are strongly encouraged to contact the **New Hampshire Office of Veterans Services** at (603) 624-9230 to connect with an accredited Veterans Service Officer who can provide personalized guidance and ensure they access every benefit they have earned through their service to the nation.
