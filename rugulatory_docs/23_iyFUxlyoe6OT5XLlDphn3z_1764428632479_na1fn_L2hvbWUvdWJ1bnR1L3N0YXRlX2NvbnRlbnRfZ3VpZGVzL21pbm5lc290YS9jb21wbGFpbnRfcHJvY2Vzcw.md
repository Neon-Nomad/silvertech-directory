# Navigating the Complaint Process in Minnesota Long-Term Care: MAARC and OHFC

Minnesota has established a clear, two-pronged system for reporting concerns and complaints regarding the care and treatment of vulnerable adults in long-term care settings. This system is designed to ensure that reports of immediate harm, such as abuse and neglect, are handled by protective services, while complaints about regulatory violations and quality of care are managed by the state’s health facility regulators.

The two primary entities involved are the **Minnesota Adult Abuse Reporting Center (MAARC)** and the **Office of Health Facility Complaints (OHFC)**, a division of the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH). Understanding the distinct roles of these two offices is the most critical step for any resident, family member, or concerned citizen seeking to file a complaint in Minnesota. This comprehensive guide details the process, the types of complaints handled by each office, and the steps taken once a report is filed.

## I. The Critical Distinction: MAARC vs. OHFC

The nature of the concern dictates which agency should receive the initial report. Minnesota law clearly separates reports of suspected maltreatment from other types of complaints.

### A. Minnesota Adult Abuse Reporting Center (MAARC)

MAARC is the state’s central, toll-free telephone intake system for receiving all reports of suspected maltreatment of vulnerable adults. The center operates 24 hours a day, seven days a week, and serves as the gateway for the state’s Adult Protective Services (APS) system [1].

*   **What MAARC Handles (Maltreatment):**
    *   **Physical or Emotional Abuse:** Any non-accidental act or omission that causes or is likely to cause fear, injury, or pain.
    *   **Sexual Abuse:** Any sexual contact between a staff member and a resident.
    *   **Neglect:** The failure to provide necessary care, services, or supervision that is essential to maintain the vulnerable adult's physical or mental health.
    *   **Financial Exploitation:** The illegal or improper use of a vulnerable adult's funds, property, or assets.
    *   **Unexplained Injury:** Any injury of a resident/patient/client of a licensed facility that is not adequately explained.

*   **The MAARC Hotline:** **(844) 880-1574** (Toll-Free)

### B. Office of Health Facility Complaints (OHFC)

The OHFC is a division within the Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) responsible for investigating reports and complaints of health care facilities violating state or federal regulations that **do not** rise to the level of maltreatment [2].

*   **What OHFC Handles (Regulatory Violations):**
    *   **Inadequate Care:** Concerns about poor quality of care that do not constitute neglect (e.g., slow response to call lights, inadequate staffing levels).
    *   **Rights Violations:** Infringement of a resident’s rights (e.g., privacy, freedom of movement, right to refuse treatment).
    *   **Licensing Standards:** Violations of state licensing rules or federal certification requirements (e.g., sanitation issues, improper documentation).
    *   **Discharge/Transfer Issues:** Concerns related to involuntary discharge or termination of services.

*   **The OHFC Contact:** **(651) 201-4200** or `health.ohfc-complaints@state.mn.us`

## II. The Complaint Process for Suspected Maltreatment (MAARC)

When a report of suspected maltreatment is made to MAARC, a specific and time-sensitive process is initiated to protect the vulnerable adult.

### A. Reporting to MAARC

1.  **Immediate Action:** If a reporter suspects maltreatment, they should call the MAARC hotline immediately. Prompt action is vital, especially for mandated reporters who have a 24-hour window to report.
2.  **Information Gathering:** The MAARC intake specialist will ask for detailed information, including:
    *   The name and location of the facility.
    *   The name and age of the vulnerable adult.
    *   A detailed description of the suspected maltreatment, including dates, times, and names of staff involved.
    *   The name and contact information of the reporter (which can be kept confidential).
3.  **Triage and Referral:** MAARC triages the report to determine the appropriate response. Reports are then referred to the relevant investigative body, which could be:
    *   **Local County or Tribal Adult Protective Services (APS):** For cases of abuse or neglect occurring in a private residence or a non-licensed setting.
    *   **MDH Office of Health Facility Complaints (OHFC):** For cases of maltreatment occurring in a licensed facility (e.g., nursing home, assisted living).

### B. The Investigation by OHFC (Maltreatment Cases)

When the OHFC receives a maltreatment referral from MAARC, a specialized investigation is launched:

1.  **Unannounced Visit:** Investigators conduct an unannounced visit to the facility, often within 24 hours for high-priority cases.
2.  **Evidence Collection:** The investigation involves interviewing the alleged victim, witnesses, and facility staff, reviewing medical records, and inspecting the environment.
3.  **Determination:** The OHFC determines whether the report of maltreatment is **substantiated** (found to be true) or **unsubstantiated** (not enough evidence to prove the claim).
4.  **Enforcement:** If the maltreatment is substantiated, the OHFC will issue a **Correction Order** to the facility and may impose sanctions, including civil penalties, temporary suspension of admissions, or license revocation.

## III. The Complaint Process for Regulatory Violations (OHFC)

For complaints that do not involve suspected maltreatment—such as poor food quality, slow response times, or rights violations—the process is managed directly by the OHFC.

### A. Filing a Complaint with OHFC

1.  **Contact:** The complaint can be filed by calling the OHFC directly at (651) 201-4200 or by emailing `health.ohfc-complaints@state.mn.us`.
2.  **Initial Review:** The OHFC reviews the complaint to determine if it falls within their jurisdiction and if it alleges a violation of state or federal regulations.
3.  **Prioritization:** Complaints are prioritized based on the severity of the alleged violation and the potential for harm to residents. High-priority complaints are investigated immediately.
4.  **Investigation:** An OHFC investigator will conduct an unannounced visit to the facility. The investigation follows a similar process to maltreatment cases, focusing on gathering evidence to prove or disprove the alleged regulatory violation.

### B. Outcomes of an OHFC Investigation

The outcome of an OHFC investigation is documented in a public report:

*   **Substantiated:** The OHFC found sufficient evidence to prove that a regulatory violation occurred. A **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD)** and a **Correction Order** are issued to the facility.
*   **Unsubstantiated:** The OHFC did not find sufficient evidence to prove that a regulatory violation occurred.
*   **No Violation:** The OHFC found that the facility was in compliance with the relevant regulations.

The facility is required to submit a **Plan of Correction (POC)** for all substantiated violations, detailing the steps they will take to remedy the issue and prevent recurrence. The OHFC follows up to ensure the corrections are implemented.

## IV. The Role of the Ombudsman in the Complaint Process

While the Ombudsman does not conduct the regulatory or protective services investigations, the **Office of Ombudsman for Long-Term Care (OOLTC)** plays a vital, supportive role in the complaint process [3].

### A. Triage and Referral

The OOLTC is an excellent resource for initial guidance. If a person is unsure whether to call MAARC or OHFC, they can call the Ombudsman first. The Ombudsman will listen to the concern and ensure the complaint is directed to the correct agency for investigation.

### B. Advocacy and Support

The Ombudsman can provide crucial support to the complainant and the resident throughout the investigation process:

*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman can file a complaint on behalf of a resident or family member while maintaining the reporter’s confidentiality, which is a key protection against potential retaliation.
*   **Monitoring:** The Ombudsman can monitor the progress of an OHFC investigation and ensure that the resident’s concerns are being addressed.
*   **Mediation:** The Ombudsman can attempt to resolve the issue directly with the facility through mediation, which can often lead to a faster resolution for non-regulatory issues.

## V. Accessing Public Complaint and Investigation Results

Minnesota provides robust public access to the results of facility inspections and complaint investigations, promoting transparency and allowing consumers to make informed choices.

### A. MDH Health Care Provider Evaluation and Investigation Results

The MDH maintains an online database where the public can search for the results of all OHFC investigations and routine surveys for licensed facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living facilities [4]. This database includes:

*   **Statements of Deficiencies (SODs):** The official report detailing the violations found.
*   **Plans of Correction (POCs):** The facility’s response and plan to fix the problems.
*   **Maltreatment Determinations:** Summaries of substantiated maltreatment findings.

### B. Assisted Living Report Card

For licensed assisted living facilities, the **Assisted Living Report Card** provides an additional layer of transparency, offering quality metrics and compliance data in an easy-to-understand format [5].

## Conclusion

Minnesota’s complaint process is a structured and vital mechanism for protecting vulnerable adults in long-term care. By clearly delineating the roles of the Minnesota Adult Abuse Reporting Center (MAARC) for maltreatment and the Office of Health Facility Complaints (OHFC) for regulatory violations, the state ensures that every concern is directed to the appropriate investigative authority. The support of the Ombudsman for Long-Term Care further strengthens this system, providing residents and their families with an independent advocate to navigate the process and ensure their voices are heard. Utilizing these resources effectively is the best way to safeguard the quality of care and uphold the rights of long-term care residents across Minnesota.
