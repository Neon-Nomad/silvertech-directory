# South Dakota Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Step-by-Step Guide

## How to Report Concerns About Quality of Care, Abuse, or Neglect

For residents, family members, and concerned citizens in South Dakota, reporting a concern about the quality of care, abuse, or neglect in a long-term care facility is a critical step toward ensuring resident safety and accountability. The state has established clear, confidential channels for filing complaints, primarily through the **South Dakota Department of Health (DOH)** and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program**.

This comprehensive guide outlines the formal complaint process in South Dakota, detailing where to file a complaint, what information is needed, and what to expect during the investigation and resolution phases.

***

## The Primary Complaint Authority: SD Department of Health

The **South Dakota Department of Health (DOH)**, through its **Office of Health Care Facilities Licensure & Certification (OLC)**, is the official state agency responsible for investigating complaints against licensed healthcare facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living centers [1]. The OLC is tasked with enforcing state licensing standards and federal certification requirements (for Medicare/Medicaid facilities).

### Types of Complaints Handled by the DOH

The DOH OLC handles complaints related to violations of state and federal regulations that impact the health, safety, welfare, or rights of residents. This includes, but is not limited to:

*   **Abuse and Neglect:** Physical, sexual, verbal, or mental abuse, as well as neglect leading to injury or harm.
*   **Poor Quality of Care:** Inadequate medical care, improper medication administration, failure to provide necessary services, or poor hygiene.
*   **Facility Conditions:** Unsafe or unsanitary living conditions, fire safety violations, or inadequate staffing levels.
*   **Resident Rights Violations:** Violations of a resident's right to privacy, dignity, or the right to refuse treatment.

### How to File a Complaint with the DOH

The DOH encourages complainants to submit their concerns in writing, but they also accept complaints via phone, email, and fax.

### The Complaint Filing Process (DOH)

1.  **Contact the OLC:** Initiate the complaint via phone, mail, email, or fax.
2.  **Describe the Incident:** Provide a clear, concise description of the nature of the complaint. This should include:
    *   The name and address of the facility.
    *   The name of the resident involved (if applicable).
    *   The date(s) and time(s) of the occurrence.
    *   The name(s) of any staff members involved.
3.  **Confidentiality:** The DOH OLC ensures that the complainant's identity will **not** be disclosed to the healthcare facility or the public [1].
4.  **Investigation:** The OLC prioritizes complaints based on the seriousness of the allegations.
    *   **Immediate Jeopardy:** Complaints alleging immediate threat to a resident's health or safety are investigated within two working days.
    *   **High Priority:** Complaints alleging actual harm or potential for serious harm are investigated within a time frame consistent with the seriousness of the allegation.
5.  **Report of Findings:** Once the investigation is complete, the complainant will receive a brief report of the results, as will the health care provider. If deficiencies are found, the facility is required to submit a Plan of Correction (POC).

## The Ombudsman: An Advocate for Complaint Resolution

While the DOH is the regulatory and investigative authority, the **South Dakota Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** serves as an independent advocate for residents and is an excellent first resource for resolving concerns [2].

### The Ombudsman's Role in Complaints

The Ombudsman's primary role is to mediate and resolve complaints from the resident's perspective. They are not regulators and cannot issue citations or fines, but they are highly effective at facilitating communication and achieving satisfactory outcomes for residents.

*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman maintains strict confidentiality and will only proceed with a complaint with the resident's express permission.
*   **Scope:** The Ombudsman can address a broader range of issues than the DOH, including quality of life concerns, disputes over facility policies, and issues related to resident rights.
*   **Facilities:** The LTCOP covers nursing facilities, assisted living centers, licensed adult foster care homes, and community living homes.

### When to Contact the Ombudsman

It is often advisable to contact the Ombudsman first, as they can frequently resolve issues quickly and informally. They can also assist the complainant in filing a formal complaint with the DOH if necessary.

## Other Avenues for Complaint and Reporting

Depending on the nature of the complaint, other state agencies or boards may be the appropriate authority.

### Reporting Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation

For allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, the matter should be reported to the **South Dakota Department of Social Services (DSS)**, which oversees Adult Protective Services (APS).

*   **South Dakota Adult Protective Services (APS) Hotline:** **(800) 430-6240** [3]
*   APS investigates reports of vulnerable adults who are being abused, neglected, or exploited, regardless of whether they live in a facility or in the community.

### Complaints Against Licensed Professionals

Complaints concerning the conduct of a specific licensed professional (e.g., a nurse, nursing facility administrator, or social worker) should be directed to the relevant professional licensing board.

*   **South Dakota Board of Nursing:** Handles complaints against Registered Nurses (RNs), Licensed Practical Nurses (LPNs), and Certified Nursing Assistants (CNAs) [4].
*   **South Dakota Board of Nursing Facility Administrators:** Handles complaints against administrators of nursing homes [5].

### Reporting Medicaid Fraud

Concerns about fraudulent billing or misuse of Medicaid funds should be reported to the **South Dakota Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)**, which is part of the Office of the Attorney General.

*   **Medicaid Fraud Hotline:** **(800) 430-6240** (This is the same number as the APS hotline, which directs calls to the appropriate division) [6].

## What to Expect After Filing a Complaint

The process following a complaint is designed to be thorough and transparent, though it can take time.

### The Investigation Process

*   **On-Site Survey:** For serious complaints, the DOH OLC will conduct an unannounced, on-site investigation. Surveyors will interview the resident, staff, and other witnesses, and review relevant medical records and facility documents.
*   **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD):** If the investigation confirms a violation of regulations, the OLC issues an SOD to the facility.
*   **Plan of Correction (POC):** The facility must submit a POC detailing how they will correct the deficiencies and prevent them from recurring. The OLC must approve this plan.
*   **Follow-Up:** The OLC conducts a follow-up visit to ensure the corrections have been implemented and are effective.

### Enforcement Actions

If a facility fails to correct deficiencies or if the violations are severe, the DOH OLC can impose enforcement actions, including:

*   **Civil Money Penalties (CMPs):** Fines levied against the facility.
*   **Directed Plan of Correction:** A specific plan the facility must follow.
*   **Denial of Payment:** Withholding Medicare or Medicaid payments.
*   **License Revocation:** In the most severe cases, the facility's license can be revoked.

## Empowering Yourself: Documentation and Preparation

The success of a complaint investigation often hinges on the quality of the information provided. Complainants should be prepared with detailed documentation.

| Documentation Checklist | Importance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dates and Times** | Crucial for establishing a timeline and verifying staff on duty. |
| **Specific Details** | What exactly happened? Who was involved? What was the outcome? |
| **Witnesses** | Names and contact information for any other residents, staff, or visitors who witnessed the incident. |
| **Photos/Videos** | If safely and legally obtained, visual evidence of poor conditions or injuries can be powerful. |
| **Medical Records** | Copies of any relevant medical records, especially those documenting injuries or changes in condition. |
| **Prior Communication** | Records of any attempts to resolve the issue with facility management before filing the formal complaint. |

By utilizing the established complaint channels and providing thorough information, South Dakota residents and their advocates can play a vital role in maintaining high standards of care and protecting the rights of vulnerable adults.
