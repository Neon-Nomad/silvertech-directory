# Verified Authority Contacts for Long-Term Care in New Jersey

Navigating the complex landscape of long-term care in New Jersey requires direct access to the state and federal agencies responsible for funding, licensing, oversight, and advocacy. This comprehensive guide compiles the verified contact information for all critical authorities, providing a single, reliable resource for residents, family members, and caregivers seeking assistance, filing complaints, or applying for benefits. The information is organized by function to ensure quick access to the correct department.

## 1. Medicaid and Long-Term Services and Supports (MLTSS)

The state's Medicaid program, **NJ FamilyCare**, is the primary source of funding for long-term care services for eligible residents. The program is administered by the Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services (DMAHS), which oversees the **Managed Long Term Services and Supports (MLTSS)** program.

### A. Primary Medicaid Authority

The DMAHS is the central administrative body for Medicaid in New Jersey, setting policy and overseeing the managed care system.

| Agency | Program Name | Website | Phone Number |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **New Jersey Department of Human Services, Division of Medical Assistance and Health Services (DMAHS)** | NJ FamilyCare / MLTSS | [https://njfamilycare.dhs.state.nj.us/]() [1] | **1-800-701-0710** (Managed Care Hotline) [2] |
| **Assisted Living Waiver Program** | Managed Long Term Services and Supports (MLTSS) | [https://nj.gov/humanservices/dmahs/home/mltss.html]() [3] | **1-877-222-3737** (Aging & Disability Resource Connection) [4] |

### B. Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)

MLTSS services are delivered through a network of Managed Care Organizations (MCOs). Individuals enrolled in MLTSS must select one of these plans, which then coordinates all their medical and long-term care services.

## 2. Licensing, Regulation, and Quality Oversight

The **New Jersey Department of Health (NJDOH)** is the state's regulatory body, responsible for licensing all healthcare facilities, conducting inspections, and enforcing state and federal standards of care.

### A. Licensing Authority

The Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure is the specific division within the NJDOH that handles the licensing of long-term care facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living residences.

| Agency | Office | Website | Phone Number |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **New Jersey Department of Health (NJDOH)** | Office of Certificate of Need and Healthcare Facility Licensure | [https://www.nj.gov/health/healthfacilities/]() [6] | **609-292-6552** (Office Phone) [7] |
| **Mailing Address** | New Jersey Department of Health, P.O. Box 360, Trenton, NJ 08625-0360 [8] |

### B. Key Regulatory Documents

The standards enforced by the NJDOH are codified in the New Jersey Administrative Code (N.J.A.C.). These codes define the minimum requirements for staffing, resident rights, and quality of care.

| Facility Type | Governing Regulation (N.J.A.C.) | Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Long-Term Care Facilities (Nursing Homes)** | N.J.A.C. 8:39 [9] | Mandatory nurse staffing, quality of care, infection control. |
| **Assisted Living Residences & Comprehensive Personal Care Homes** | N.J.A.C. 8:36 [10] | Resident rights, individualized service plans, freedom from restraint. |

## 3. Ombudsman and Advocacy Services

The **Office of the Ombudsman for the Institutionalized Elderly (OOIE)** is a unique state agency that houses the federally mandated Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP). This office is the primary advocate for residents aged 60 and older in long-term care settings.

### A. Ombudsman and Advocacy Contacts

The Ombudsman's office provides confidential investigation and resolution of complaints related to the health, safety, welfare, and rights of long-term care residents.

| Agency | Program Name | Website | Phone Number |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Office of the Ombudsman for the Institutionalized Elderly (OOIE)** | New Jersey Office of the Long Term Care Ombudsman (NJLTCO) | [https://www.nj.gov/ooie/]() [11] | **1-877-582-6995** (Toll-Free Hotline) [12] |
| **Email** | | ombudsman@ltco.nj.gov [13] | |
| **Legal Aid for Seniors** | Legal Services of New Jersey (LSNJ) | [https://www.lsnj.org/]() [14] | **1-888-LSNJ-LAW** (1-888-576-5529) [15] |
| **Disability Rights** | Disability Rights New Jersey (DRNJ) | [https://disabilityrightsnj.org/]() [16] | **1-800-922-7233** (Toll-Free) [17] |

### B. Scope of Ombudsman Authority

The NJLTCO has jurisdiction over complaints in various facilities, including:

*   Nursing Homes
*   Assisted Living Residences
*   Comprehensive Personal Care Homes
*   Residential Health Care Facilities
*   State and County Psychiatric Hospitals (for residents aged 60 and older) [11]

## Conclusion

The State of New Jersey has established a comprehensive network of government agencies and advocacy organizations to support its elderly and disabled populations. From the financial assistance provided by NJ FamilyCare and MLTSS, to the regulatory oversight of the NJDOH, and the dedicated advocacy of the Long Term Care Ombudsman, these authorities are the cornerstones of the state's long-term care system. By utilizing the verified contact information provided in this guide, residents and their families can efficiently access the resources, benefits, and support necessary to ensure high-quality care and protection of resident rights. It is highly recommended that individuals seeking long-term care assistance first contact the ADRC for guidance, and in cases of suspected abuse or neglect, immediately use the dedicated hotlines to ensure prompt intervention.
