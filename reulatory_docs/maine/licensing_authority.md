# Maine Licensing Authority: Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC)

The oversight and regulation of long-term care facilities in Maine are managed by the **Division of Licensing and Certification (DLC)**, a key component of the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS). The DLC's mission is to ensure that all licensed medical and long-term care facilities, including nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and residential care facilities, comply with both state rules and federal regulations, thereby protecting the health, safety, and welfare of residents [1].

## Scope of Regulatory Oversight

The DLC’s authority extends across the spectrum of long-term care settings in Maine. The primary regulatory framework for assisted living and residential care is established in the **Code of Maine Rules (C.M.R.) 10-144 Chapter 113, Assisted Housing Programs Licensing Rule** [2] [3].

### 1. Nursing Facilities

The DLC’s Medical Facilities Unit oversees the licensing of all long-term care nursing facilities in the state. This includes ensuring compliance with state licensing rules and federal certification requirements for participation in Medicare and Medicaid (MaineCare) programs. Oversight involves regular surveys, complaint investigations, and enforcement actions to maintain a high standard of care.

### 2. Assisted Living Facilities (Chapter 113, Part A)

Assisted Living Facilities (ALFs) are defined as houses or other places that provide assisted living services to residents in **private apartments** within buildings that include a common dining area [2]. A private apartment must include an individual bathroom, bedroom, and a food preparation area [2].

#### Key Regulatory Areas for ALFs:

*   **Licensing and Application:** The rule details the application process, including requirements for a provisional license, conditional license, and annual renewal. Licenses are non-transferable and must be posted publicly. The application process requires submission of documentation, fees, and a plan of operation [2].
*   **Assisted Living Services:** These services, provided directly or indirectly, include housing, assistance with **Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)** and **Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs)**, personal supervision, protection from environmental hazards, meals, diet care, care management, and diversional activities. They may also include medication administration and nursing services if the facility is licensed to provide them [2].
*   **Administration and Staffing:** Requirements cover the qualifications and responsibilities of the Administrator, who is responsible for the management of the facility and compliance with the rule. Staff training and employee records are strictly mandated. The rule emphasizes that the licensee is responsible for the acts of its employees, and staff nonadherence can result in a citation against the licensee [2].
*   **Resident Rights:** A comprehensive section is dedicated to resident rights, including the right to privacy, freedom from abuse and neglect, the right to refuse treatment, the right to a service plan, and the right to appeal an involuntary discharge. Facilities must provide notification of these rights to residents [2].
*   **Medications and Treatments:** Detailed procedures are outlined for the safe and acceptable use and administration of medications, including storage, labeling, and disposal. The rule specifies requirements for the use of unlicensed assistive personnel in medication administration and the handling of controlled substances [2].
*   **Physical Plant Standards:** These standards ensure the safety and general condition of the facility, covering code compliance, heating, hot water, plumbing, electrical systems, and solid waste management. The rule also addresses the use of apartments and common areas [2].

### 3. Residential Care Facilities (Chapter 113, Part B)

Residential Care Facilities (RCFs) also provide assisted housing services but differ from ALFs in their physical plant and the level of care they historically provided. The DLC has discontinued the use of the Level I-IV terminology in the current rule, but clarifies that RCFs previously categorized by level are now licensed under this single rule [3].

| Former Residential Care Levels | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Level I** | A facility with a licensed capacity of one (1) or two (2) residents. |
| **Level II** | A facility with a licensed capacity of three (3) to six (6) residents. |
| **Level III** | A facility with a licensed capacity of three (3) to six (6) residents and which employs three (3) or more persons who are not owners and are not related to the owner. |
| **Level IV** | A facility with a licensed capacity of more than six (6) residents. |

#### Key Regulatory Areas for RCFs:

*   **Assisted Housing Services:** The definition of services is similar to ALFs, encompassing ADL/IADL assistance, personal supervision, meals, and care management [3].
*   **Standards for Resident Care:** This section mandates a general rule of care, resident assessment procedures, the development of a **Service Plan**, and standards for medical and health care, including managing residents with communicable diseases. The service plan must be developed with the resident and reviewed regularly [3].
*   **Staffing Requirements:** RCFs have specific minimum staff requirements and staff-to-occupied-bed ratios, which are crucial for ensuring adequate supervision and care. The rule also addresses the provision of registered nurse services and the use of shared staffing between facilities [3].
*   **Physical Plant Requirements:** Detailed standards are set for the facility design, heating, water, living and dining areas, and resident bedrooms, including requirements for fire safety and emergency planning. The rule also includes specific standards for resident bedrooms, such as minimum size and window requirements [3].
*   **Specialized Care Unit Standards:** The rule includes specific standards for facilities that operate **Memory Care Units**, covering admission, information disclosure, design, physical safety, therapeutic activities, and specialized staffing and service planning. These standards are designed to ensure a safe and therapeutic environment for residents with cognitive impairment [3].

## Enforcement and Compliance: The Survey Process

The DLC is responsible for monitoring compliance and taking action to assure that licensed facilities meet the required standards. The primary mechanism for this oversight is the **survey process**, which is a comprehensive, on-site inspection.

### Inspections and Surveys

*   **Frequency:** The DLC conducts unannounced inspections and surveys to assess compliance with state and federal regulations. These inspections are typically conducted at least once every 9 to 15 months for nursing facilities, as mandated by federal law. Assisted housing facilities are also subject to regular, unannounced inspections.
*   **Types of Surveys:**
    *   **Standard Survey:** The comprehensive, periodic inspection of a facility's compliance with all regulatory requirements.
    *   **Complaint Survey:** An unannounced inspection triggered by a complaint alleging a violation of state or federal regulations. These are prioritized based on the severity of the alleged harm.
    *   **Abbreviated Survey:** A focused inspection to investigate a specific, limited scope of concern, such as a change of ownership or a follow-up to a previous deficiency.
*   **Survey Team:** Surveys are conducted by a multidisciplinary team of surveyors, which may include registered nurses, social workers, dietitians, and fire safety experts. The team's goal is to observe care, interview residents and staff, and review documentation to determine if the facility is meeting all standards.

### Deficiencies and Sanctions

When a facility is found to be non-compliant, the DLC issues a **Statement of Deficiencies (SOD)** and requires a **Plan of Correction (POC)**. Failure to correct deficiencies can lead to enforcement actions, which are designed to compel compliance and protect residents.

*   **Provisional License:** Issued to new facilities or facilities that have not yet demonstrated full compliance but are making progress.
*   **Conditional License:** Issued when a facility has serious deficiencies that are not immediately life-threatening but require correction within a specified timeframe.
*   **Intermediate Sanctions:** Penalties such as:
    *   **Civil Monetary Penalties (CMPs):** Fines imposed on the facility for each day of non-compliance or for each instance of a deficiency.
    *   **Directed Plan of Correction:** A specific plan mandated by the DLC to correct a deficiency.
    *   **Denial of Payment for New Admissions:** A temporary halt on admitting new residents, which can significantly impact a facility's revenue.
    *   **Temporary Management:** The appointment of a temporary manager to oversee the facility's operations and correct deficiencies.
*   **License Revocation:** The most severe action, taken when a facility demonstrates a persistent inability or unwillingness to comply with rules, posing a serious threat to resident health and safety. This action results in the facility being shut down.

### Public Disclosure and Transparency

The DLC is committed to transparency, making facility compliance information readily available to the public. This is a critical component of consumer protection, allowing prospective residents and their families to make informed decisions.

*   **Public Records:** All inspection reports, statements of deficiencies, and enforcement actions are considered public records. For Medicare/Medicaid certified facilities (primarily nursing homes), this information is available through the federal **Care Compare** website.
*   **State-Level Disclosure:** The DLC maintains a public-facing system where consumers can search for licensed facilities and view their compliance history, including any citations for violations of state licensing rules.
*   **Resident Rights and Information:** The DLC ensures that facilities adhere to the resident rights outlined in the licensing rules. Furthermore, the DLC makes information about facility compliance publicly available.
*   **Waiver of a Licensing Rule:** Facilities may apply for a waiver of a specific licensing rule if they can demonstrate that the waiver will not adversely affect the health, safety, or welfare of the residents and that an alternative method of compliance is in place [2].

## The Role and Responsibilities of the Administrator

The Administrator is the key individual responsible for the day-to-day management and overall compliance of an Assisted Housing Facility. The DLC's rules place significant emphasis on the Administrator's qualifications and duties to ensure effective leadership and quality care.

#### Qualifications of the Administrator

The Administrator must meet specific qualifications, which typically include:

*   **Age and Character:** Must be at least 21 years of age and of good moral character.
*   **Education and Experience:** Must possess the necessary education, training, and experience to manage the facility effectively. This often involves a combination of formal education (e.g., a degree in healthcare administration or a related field) and practical experience in a supervisory role within a long-term care setting.
*   **Training:** Must complete specific training requirements, including initial orientation and ongoing continuing education in areas such as resident rights, fire safety, medication management, and dementia care.
*   **Licensure/Certification:** While not all RCFs require a licensed nursing home administrator, the individual must be approved by the DLC and meet the requirements set forth in the Assisted Housing Programs Licensing Rule.

#### Core Responsibilities

The Administrator's responsibilities are broad and encompass all aspects of facility operation:

*   **Compliance Oversight:** Ensuring the facility is in continuous compliance with all state and federal laws, rules, and regulations. This includes being the primary contact for the DLC during surveys and complaint investigations.
*   **Financial Management:** Overseeing the facility's budget, financial records, and ensuring the proper management of residents' personal funds.
*   **Personnel Management:** Hiring, training, supervising, and evaluating all staff. This includes ensuring adequate staffing levels are maintained at all times to meet resident needs.
*   **Resident Care Management:** Overseeing the resident assessment process, the development and implementation of individualized Service Plans, and ensuring the provision of high-quality care and services.
*   **Physical Plant Maintenance:** Ensuring the facility's physical structure and equipment are safe, functional, and well-maintained, including compliance with all fire and life safety codes.
*   **Resident Rights Protection:** Establishing and enforcing policies that protect the rights of all residents and ensuring that staff are trained on these rights.
*   **Quality Assurance:** Implementing a continuous quality improvement program to monitor and improve the quality of care and services provided.

## Conclusion

The DLC serves as the primary guardian of quality and safety in Maine's long-term care facilities. By establishing and enforcing comprehensive rules like the Assisted Housing Programs Licensing Rule (Chapter 113), the Division ensures that residents of Assisted Living Facilities and Residential Care Facilities receive the individualized care and protection they are entitled to. Prospective residents and their families should use the DLC's resources to verify a facility's license status, review inspection reports, and understand the rights and protections afforded to all residents. The DLC's commitment to rigorous oversight and enforcement is vital for maintaining high standards across the long-term care continuum in Maine.
