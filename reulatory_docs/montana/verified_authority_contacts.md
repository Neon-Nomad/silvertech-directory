# Verified Authority Contacts for Long-Term Care in Montana: A Comprehensive Directory

## Introduction to Montana's Long-Term Care Authority Network

Navigating the landscape of long-term care services, regulations, and benefits in Montana requires access to accurate and verified contact information for the responsible state agencies. The State of Montana, primarily through the **Department of Public Health and Human Services (DPHHS)**, maintains a robust network of divisions and programs dedicated to the health, safety, and well-being of its senior and disabled populations.

This comprehensive directory serves as a single, verified resource for all critical contacts related to Medicaid, facility licensing, resident advocacy, complaint reporting, and veterans' benefits. Each entry includes the agency's name, its specific role, and the verified contact details, ensuring that residents, family members, and caregivers can quickly connect with the appropriate governmental or advocacy authority.

## I. Montana Department of Public Health and Human Services (DPHHS)

The DPHHS is the overarching state agency responsible for administering most health and human services programs, including Medicaid, long-term care oversight, and public assistance. It is the central authority for many of the services detailed in this guide.

### Detailed Overview of DPHHS Divisions

The DPHHS is organized into several divisions, each with a specific mandate crucial to long-term care. Understanding these divisions is key to directing inquiries to the correct authority.

#### A. Senior and Long-Term Care Division (SLTC)
The SLTC Division is responsible for programs that support seniors and individuals with disabilities, including the **Big Sky Waiver** and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program**. Their focus is on promoting independence and providing community-based alternatives to institutional care.

#### B. Quality Assurance Division (QAD)
The QAD is the regulatory arm of the DPHHS. Its primary function is to license and certify healthcare facilities, conduct inspections, and investigate formal complaints against nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and other licensed providers.

## II. Medicaid Programs and Eligibility Contacts

The Montana Medicaid program, known as **Montana Healthcare Programs**, is administered by the DPHHS and provides essential funding for long-term care services, including nursing home care and the **Big Sky Waiver** for assisted living.

| Program/Agency | Specific Function | Verified Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Big Sky Waiver Program** | Provides Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS), including assisted living support, for those who meet the Nursing Facility Level of Care. | **Referral Contact (Mountain Pacific Quality Health):** (800) 219-7035 [4] |
| **Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)** | Handles the state's efforts to recover costs of long-term care services from the estates of deceased recipients. | **Contact DPHHS for specific MERP contact details.** |

### Navigating Medicaid Inquiries

When contacting the DPHHS regarding Medicaid, it is important to distinguish the nature of the inquiry:
*   **Eligibility and Application Status:** Contact the **Office of Public Assistance (OPA)** at (888) 706-1535 or use the online portal [2].
*   **Covered Services and Benefits:** Contact the **Montana Medicaid** line at (800) 362-8312 [3].
*   **Waiver Services and Assessments:** Contact the **Mountain Pacific Quality Health** referral line at (800) 219-7035 to initiate the functional assessment process for the Big Sky Waiver [4].

## III. Licensing and Regulatory Authority Contacts

The **Quality Assurance Division (QAD)** of the DPHHS is the state's licensing authority for long-term care facilities. These contacts are for facilities seeking licensure, as well as for the public to verify a facility's license status and inspection history.

| Agency | Specific Function | Verified Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **DPHHS Quality Assurance Division (QAD)** | Licensing and certification of all healthcare facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living facilities. | **Licensure Bureau Phone:** (406) 444-2037 [5] |
| | | **Website:** [https://dphhs.mt.gov/qad/licensure](https://dphhs.mt.gov/qad/licensure) [5] |
| **QAD Public Facility Search** | Provides public access to facility inspection reports, licensing status, and enforcement actions. | **Online Search Portal:** (Available via the QAD website) [5] |

### The Importance of the Licensing Authority

The QAD ensures that all licensed facilities, from Category A to the specialized Category D assisted living facilities, meet the minimum standards set forth in the **Administrative Rules of Montana (ARM)**. The licensing phone number is the direct line for facility administrators and prospective licensees, but it is also a resource for the public to confirm the regulatory standing of a long-term care provider.

## IV. Resident Advocacy and Complaint Contacts

Montana provides multiple, distinct channels for residents and their families to report concerns, ranging from confidential advocacy to formal regulatory investigation and reporting of suspected abuse.

### A. Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The LTCOP is the primary resource for confidential advocacy and mediation for residents of long-term care facilities.

| Program/Agency | Specific Function | Verified Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Montana Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** | Provides confidential advocacy, investigates and resolves complaints on behalf of residents, and protects resident rights. | **Toll-Free Phone:** (800) 332-2272 [6] |
| | | **Website:** [https://dphhs.mt.gov/sltc/aging/longtermcareombudsman](https://dphhs.mt.gov/sltc/aging/longtermcareombudsman) [6] |

The Ombudsman is a non-regulatory body, meaning they do not issue fines or revoke licenses. Their strength lies in their independence and their ability to resolve issues through mediation, with the resident's consent and best interest as the sole priority.

### B. Formal Complaint and Regulatory Reporting

For serious allegations of regulatory violations, the formal complaint process through the QAD is the appropriate channel.

### C. Adult Protective Services (APS)

Any suspicion of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult must be reported immediately to APS.

## V. Veterans Benefits and Services Contacts

The **Montana Veterans Affairs Division (MVAD)** is the state-level authority dedicated to assisting veterans and their families in accessing state and federal benefits, including long-term care options like the State Veterans Homes.

| Program/Agency | Specific Function | Verified Contact Information |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Montana Veterans Affairs Division (MVAD)** | State agency providing assistance with federal VA claims, state benefits, and overseeing the State Veterans Homes. | **General Phone:** (406) 324-3740 [10] |
| | | **Website:** [https://dma.mt.gov/mvad/](https://dma.mt.gov/mvad/) [10] |
| **Montana State Veterans Homes** | Provides skilled nursing and domiciliary care for eligible veterans and their spouses at three locations (Columbia Falls, Glendive, Butte). | **Contact MVAD for specific home admissions information.** |
| **Veterans Service Officers (VSOs)** | Accredited professionals who provide free assistance with filing VA claims and navigating the benefits system. | **Contact MVAD for local VSO directory.** |

### The Critical Role of the MVAD

The MVAD's general phone number is the gateway to connecting with a local **Veterans Service Officer (VSO)**. VSOs are the most important resource for veterans, as they provide expert, free assistance in applying for complex federal benefits such as the **Aid and Attendance Pension**, which can be used to pay for long-term care.
