# New Hampshire Medicaid Programs: A Comprehensive Guide to Eligibility, Benefits, and Long-Term Care

The **New Hampshire Medicaid** program, officially known as Medical Assistance, is a vital, federally and state-funded healthcare program administered by the **New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS)**. It is designed to ensure that eligible residents, including low-income families, children, pregnant women, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities, have access to necessary medical and long-term care services [1]. This comprehensive guide provides an in-depth look at the program's structure, eligibility pathways, the extensive range of covered benefits, and the critical role of the **Choices for Independence (CFI) Waiver** in supporting home and community-based long-term care, including assisted living services.

## I. Overview of the New Hampshire Medicaid Program

New Hampshire Medicaid is more than just a health insurance program; it is a foundational safety net that provides payment for a broad spectrum of healthcare services. The program's mission is to improve the health and well-being of New Hampshire residents by providing access to quality healthcare services. The DHHS is the single state agency responsible for the administration of the program, including determining financial and non-financial eligibility, enrolling providers, and managing the delivery of services [1].

### A. Program Administration and Contact Information

The program is centrally managed by the DHHS, which oversees all aspects of Medicaid operations, policy development, and compliance with federal regulations set by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS).

### B. The Importance of Medicaid in New Hampshire

Medicaid plays a crucial role in the state's healthcare landscape, covering services that range from routine preventive care for children to complex institutional care for the elderly and disabled. It also funds services for individuals with developmental disabilities and acquired brain disorders, ensuring a holistic approach to healthcare for the state's most vulnerable populations [1]. The program is a major source of funding for hospitals, nursing facilities, and community-based providers across New Hampshire.

## II. Medicaid Eligibility Pathways and Financial Criteria

Eligibility for New Hampshire Medicaid is determined based on a combination of financial and non-financial factors, including income, assets, age, disability status, and household composition. The state has adopted the Medicaid expansion under the Affordable Care Act (ACA), which has broadened coverage to a larger group of low-income adults.

### A. Major Eligibility Groups

New Hampshire Medicaid serves several distinct populations, each with its own set of eligibility rules:

1.  **Children and Families:** Includes coverage for children through the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) and Medicaid, as well as coverage for parents and caretaker relatives who meet income requirements.
2.  **Pregnant Women:** Provides comprehensive coverage for prenatal care, delivery, and postpartum care.
3.  **Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ABD):** This pathway is critical for individuals who require long-term care services. Eligibility for this group often involves more stringent income and asset tests, particularly for those seeking institutional or home and community-based services.
4.  **Medicaid Expansion Adults:** Non-elderly, non-disabled adults with incomes up to a certain percentage of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) are covered under the ACA expansion.

### B. Financial Eligibility Standards

Financial eligibility is complex and varies significantly depending on the applicant's category.

*   **Income Limits:** For most non-disabled, non-elderly adults, income is compared to the Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) limits. For the aged, blind, and disabled seeking long-term care, the income limit is often tied to the Special Income Limit (SIL), which is 300% of the Supplemental Security Income (SSI) Federal Benefit Rate (FBR) [3].
*   **Asset Limits:** For long-term care Medicaid (Nursing Home Medicaid and the CFI Waiver), there are strict asset limits. In 2025, a single applicant's countable assets must generally be under a specific limit (e.g., \$2,000), though certain assets like the primary residence (up to a certain equity value), one vehicle, and personal belongings are typically exempt [3].

The DHHS Customer Service Center and the NH EASY portal are the primary resources for obtaining the most current and accurate eligibility standards.

## III. Comprehensive Covered Services

New Hampshire Medicaid provides a comprehensive package of medical and long-term care services. The goal is to cover all medically necessary services to maintain health and functional capacity.

### A. Acute and Primary Care Services

The program covers essential acute care services, ensuring recipients can access necessary medical treatment:

*   **Physician and Clinic Services:** Routine check-ups, sick visits, and specialist consultations.
*   **Hospital Services:** Inpatient and outpatient hospital care.
*   **Prescription Drugs:** Coverage for a wide range of medications, subject to the state's formulary and prior authorization requirements.
*   **Laboratory and X-ray Services:** Diagnostic testing.
*   **Preventive Services:** Including Early and Periodic Screening, Diagnostic and and Treatment (EPSDT) for children under 21, which covers comprehensive and preventative health services.
*   **Dental Services:** Coverage is provided through the NH Smiles Program, ensuring access to necessary dental care [1].

### B. Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS)

For the elderly and individuals with disabilities, Medicaid is the primary payer for long-term services and supports, which include:

*   **Nursing Facility Services:** Coverage for skilled nursing care in a licensed facility.
*   **Home and Community-Based Care:** Services provided in the recipient's home or community setting, primarily through the CFI Waiver.
*   **Behavioral Health Services:** Including psychotherapy, substance use disorder treatment, and services at community mental health centers [1].

### C. Other Specialized Services

New Hampshire Medicaid also covers a variety of specialized services tailored to specific needs:

*   **Durable Medical Equipment (DME) and Supplies:** Items like wheelchairs, oxygen equipment, and medical supplies.
*   **Therapies:** Physical therapy (PT), occupational therapy (OT), and speech therapy.
*   **Medical Transportation:** Non-emergency medical transportation to and from appointments.
*   **Hospice Services:** Comprehensive care for individuals with a terminal illness.

## IV. The Choices for Independence (CFI) Waiver

The **Choices for Independence (CFI) Waiver** is New Hampshire's primary Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver, designed to allow individuals who require a nursing facility level of care to receive services in their own homes or other community settings, such as assisted living facilities [2]. This waiver is crucial for promoting independence and quality of life outside of institutional settings.

### A. CFI Waiver Purpose and Eligibility

The CFI Waiver is authorized under Section 1915(c) of the Social Security Act and is administered by the DHHS Bureau of Adult and Aging Services (BAAS).

*   **Purpose:** To provide a wide range of services that support eligible individuals in remaining in their homes and communities, preventing unnecessary institutionalization [2].
*   **Target Population:** Adults aged 18 or older who are financially eligible for Medicaid and meet the state's medical criteria for a nursing facility level of care [2]. This includes seniors (65+) and younger individuals (18-64) with disabilities.
*   **Assisted Living Support:** The CFI Waiver is the mechanism through which New Hampshire Medicaid provides financial assistance for services received in an assisted living setting, making it a critical resource for those who need residential support but do not require the 24-hour skilled care of a nursing home.

### B. Services Provided Under the CFI Waiver

The CFI Waiver offers a flexible package of services tailored to the individual's needs, as determined by a comprehensive assessment and outlined in a person-centered service plan. Services can include:

| CFI Waiver Service | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **In-Home Services** | Assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as eating, bathing, dressing, and personal care, as well as Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs) like meal preparation and household tasks [2]. |
| **Assisted Living Services** | Coverage for personal care and other supportive services provided within a licensed assisted living facility. |
| **Respite Care** | Temporary relief for the primary caregiver, allowing them to take a break from their caregiving duties. |
| **Environmental Modifications** | Home modifications and adaptations (e.g., ramps, grab bars) necessary to ensure the health, welfare, and safety of the individual in their home [2]. |
| **Specialized Medical Equipment** | Devices and supplies not covered under the standard Medicaid State Plan. |
| **Participant-Directed Services** | Allows eligible individuals to have greater control over their care by hiring, training, and managing their own personal care attendants. |
| **Supported Employment** | Services to help individuals find and maintain competitive employment in the community [2]. |

The CFI Waiver is subject to renewal and amendment processes every five years, ensuring that the program remains compliant with federal regulations and responsive to the evolving needs of the state's population [2].

## V. Application and Enrollment Process

Applying for New Hampshire Medicaid is a streamlined process primarily conducted through the state's online portal.

### A. The NH EASY Gateway to Services

The primary method for applying for all types of assistance, including Medicaid, is through the **NH EASY - Gateway to Services** online portal [4].

*   **Online Application:** NH EASY provides a fast, secure, and convenient way to apply for Medicaid, SNAP (Food Assistance), cash assistance, and other programs.
*   **Required Information:** Applicants will need to provide information about their household, income, assets, and any existing health insurance.
*   **Assistance:** If an applicant needs help with the application or choosing a plan, they can contact the DHHS Customer Service Center at 1-844-ASK-DHHS (1-844-275-3447) [4].

### B. Managed Care Enrollment

New Hampshire operates a Medicaid Care Management program, meaning most recipients are enrolled in a Managed Care Organization (MCO) to receive their services.

*   **MCOs:** These private health plans contract with the state to coordinate and provide a comprehensive set of covered services.
*   **Enrollment:** Once determined eligible for Medicaid, recipients choose an MCO. The MCO becomes the primary point of contact for accessing most healthcare services.
*   **Fee-For-Service (FFS):** A small number of recipients, typically those with complex needs or those receiving certain long-term care services, may remain in the Fee-For-Service model, where the state pays providers directly for each service [1].

## VI. Recipient Rights and Administrative Appeals

Medicaid recipients have the right to appeal any decision made by the DHHS regarding their eligibility or the services they receive.

*   **Right to Appeal:** If an individual disagrees with a decision—such as a denial of eligibility, a reduction in services, or a determination of financial liability—they have the right to request an **Administrative Appeals Hearing** [1].
*   **Process:** The request for an appeal must follow the instructions provided on the official Request Form and be submitted to the DHHS. The hearing provides an opportunity for the recipient or their authorized representative to present their case before an impartial hearing officer.

## VII. Conclusion

New Hampshire Medicaid, administered by the DHHS, is a comprehensive program that provides essential healthcare and long-term services to a diverse population. The program's commitment to community-based care is highlighted by the **Choices for Independence (CFI) Waiver**, which is instrumental in providing the necessary support for individuals to live independently, including those residing in assisted living facilities. Understanding the eligibility requirements, the scope of covered services, and the application process through the NH EASY portal is the first step for New Hampshire residents seeking to access this critical resource.
