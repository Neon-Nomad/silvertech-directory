# Comprehensive Guide to Minnesota's Medical Assistance (MA) and Elderly Waiver (EW) Programs

The state of Minnesota, through its Department of Human Services (DHS), administers a robust and comprehensive healthcare system for its low-income residents, primarily through the program known as **Medical Assistance (MA)**. MA is Minnesota’s version of the federal Medicaid program, and it stands as the largest healthcare program in the state, serving a diverse population that includes families, children, pregnant women, adults, seniors, and individuals who are blind or have a disability [1].

For the senior population and those requiring long-term care, the most critical component of this system is the **Elderly Waiver (EW)**. This Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver is designed to provide necessary care and support in a person’s home or a community setting, such as an assisted living facility, thereby offering a vital alternative to institutionalization in a nursing home [2]. Understanding the intricacies of both MA and the EW is essential for Minnesota residents seeking financial assistance for healthcare and long-term care services.

## I. Medical Assistance (MA): The Foundation of Minnesota’s Healthcare Safety Net

Medical Assistance (MA) is a joint federal and state program that provides comprehensive health coverage. Unlike some states that have multiple names for their Medicaid programs, Minnesota consistently refers to its program as Medical Assistance (MA).

### A. Who is Covered by Medical Assistance?

MA is structured to cover a wide array of populations, each with specific eligibility pathways:

*   **Families and Children:** Coverage for children up to age 21 and their parents or caretaker relatives, often based on Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) rules.
*   **Pregnant Women:** Comprehensive coverage to ensure healthy pregnancies and births.
*   **Adults without Children:** Coverage for low-income adults under age 65.
*   **Seniors (Age 65 and Older):** Coverage for medical and long-term care services, often subject to different financial rules than the MAGI group.
*   **People with Disabilities or Blindness:** Individuals who meet the Social Security Administration’s definition of disability or blindness.

### B. MA Service Delivery Models

Most MA members receive their healthcare through **Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)**, which are health plans contracted by the state. Members choose a health plan from those serving MA members in their county. However, some members, particularly those receiving certain long-term care services, may receive care on a **fee-for-service** basis, where providers bill the state directly for services rendered.

## II. Navigating Eligibility for Medical Assistance

Eligibility for MA is determined by a combination of factors, including residency, citizenship status, and financial criteria (income and assets). The rules can be complex, particularly for seniors and individuals seeking long-term care, as they fall under non-MAGI rules.

### A. General Eligibility Requirements

To qualify for MA, an applicant must generally meet the following non-financial criteria [1] [4]:

*   **Residency:** Must be a resident of Minnesota.
*   **Citizenship/Immigration Status:** Must be a U.S. citizen or a qualifying noncitizen.
*   **Social Security Number (SSN):** Must provide an SSN for each person requesting MA, unless an exception is met.
*   **Other Program Rules:** Must meet any other program rules, such as cooperating with child support enforcement if applicable.

### B. Financial Eligibility: Income and Asset Limits

The financial criteria for MA vary significantly based on the applicant’s age, household size, and whether they are applying for standard healthcare or long-term care services.

| Applicant Group | Income Limit (Approximate) | Asset Limit (Approximate) | Key Program |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Children & Families (MAGI)** | Up to 275% FPL (for children) | No Asset Test | Medical Assistance (MA) |
| **Seniors/Disabled (Non-MAGI)** | Varies, often 100% FPL | \$3,000 for an individual | Medical Assistance (MA) |

### C. The Spenddown Provision

For individuals whose income exceeds the MA income limit, Minnesota offers a **spenddown** program. This allows an applicant to qualify for MA by incurring medical expenses that reduce their countable income to the MA limit. The difference between the applicant’s income and the MA income limit is their "spenddown" amount, which functions similarly to an insurance deductible. Once the applicant has incurred medical expenses equal to their spenddown amount, they become eligible for MA coverage for the remainder of the eligibility period [1].

## III. Medical Assistance for Long-Term Care (MA-LTC)

MA-LTC is the specific pathway for individuals who require the high level of care provided in a nursing home, whether they receive that care in an institution or in the community through an HCBS waiver like the Elderly Waiver.

### A. Level of Care Requirement

To qualify for MA-LTC, an individual must meet the **institutional level of care** requirement. This typically involves a comprehensive assessment by a certified assessor to determine that the individual has a medical need for the level of care provided in a nursing facility. For MA-LTC, the individual must generally require this level of care for at least 30 consecutive days [5].

### B. Spousal Impoverishment Protections

When one spouse requires long-term care (the "institutionalized spouse") and the other remains in the community (the "community spouse"), federal and state laws protect the community spouse from becoming impoverished. Minnesota implements these protections through:

1.  **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The community spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple’s combined assets, up to a maximum limit (e.g., \$148,620 in 2024), without affecting the institutionalized spouse’s MA eligibility [4].
2.  **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The community spouse is allowed to keep a certain amount of the couple’s combined income to meet their monthly living expenses. If the community spouse’s own income is below this allowance, a portion of the institutionalized spouse’s income can be allocated to them [4].

### C. Medical Assistance Estate Recovery

Minnesota law requires the state to recover the costs of certain MA services paid on behalf of a member. This is known as the **Medical Assistance Estate Recovery** program [1].

*   **Services Subject to Recovery:** Generally, the state seeks recovery for the cost of long-term care services, including nursing facility services, and HCBS waiver services (like the EW) received by individuals aged 55 or older.
*   **Estate Definition:** The estate includes all real and personal property and other assets included in the MA member’s probate estate.
*   **Exemptions and Waivers:** Recovery is generally postponed or waived if there is a surviving spouse, a child under age 21, or a child of any age who is blind or permanently disabled. The state may also waive recovery if it would cause an undue hardship [1].

## IV. The Elderly Waiver (EW): Supporting Community Living

The **Elderly Waiver (EW)** is Minnesota’s primary HCBS program for seniors. It is designed to fund the necessary services and supports that allow individuals aged 65 and older who are eligible for MA and require a nursing home level of care to remain in their homes or community settings [2] [6].

### A. EW Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible for the Elderly Waiver, an individual must meet three main criteria:

1.  **Age:** Must be 65 years of age or older.
2.  **Financial:** Must be financially eligible for Medical Assistance (MA).
3.  **Functional:** Must be assessed as needing the level of care provided in a nursing facility.

### B. Comprehensive Services Covered by the EW

The EW is highly valued because it covers a broad range of services that are not typically covered by standard MA or Medicare, with the goal of supporting independence and quality of life in the community. Key services include [2] [6]:

*   **Assisted Living Services:** The EW can pay for the services component of assisted living, which includes help with activities of daily living (ADLs), medication management, and other personal care. *It is important to note that the EW does not pay for room and board costs in assisted living, only the services.*
*   **Home Health Services:** Skilled nursing visits, home health aide services, and therapy services (physical, occupational, speech).
*   **Personal Care Assistance (PCA):** Services provided by a trained personal care assistant to help with ADLs and instrumental activities of daily living (IADLs).
*   **Adult Day Services:** Structured programs in a community setting that provide health, social, and therapeutic activities.
*   **Case Management:** A professional case manager works with the individual to develop a service plan, coordinate services, and monitor the quality of care.
*   **Home Modifications and Technology:** Funding for modifications to the home (e.g., ramps, grab bars) and assistive technology to enhance safety and independence.
*   **Respite Care:** Temporary care provided to an EW participant to give the primary caregiver a break.

### C. EW and Assisted Living: A Critical Partnership

The ability of the Elderly Waiver to cover services in a licensed assisted living facility is a cornerstone of Minnesota’s long-term care system. This funding mechanism makes assisted living an affordable option for many low-income seniors who would otherwise be forced into a nursing home.

The services covered by the EW in an assisted living setting are determined by the individual’s assessed needs and outlined in their personalized service plan. This ensures that the care provided is person-centered and appropriate for their specific functional and medical requirements [3].

## V. Applying for Medical Assistance and the Elderly Waiver

The application process for MA and the EW is managed by the Minnesota Department of Human Services (DHS) and local county or tribal human services offices.

### A. Application Pathways

Applicants can apply for MA through several avenues [1]:

1.  **MNsure:** Minnesota’s health insurance marketplace, which uses the MAGI rules for most non-LTC applicants.
2.  **County or Tribal Human Services Offices:** These offices handle all non-MAGI applications, including those for seniors, people with disabilities, and long-term care services (MA-LTC and EW).
3.  **Online:** Applications can be submitted through the state’s online portal.

### B. The Assessment Process

For the Elderly Waiver, the application process includes a mandatory **Long-Term Care Consultation (LTCC)**. This consultation is performed by a certified assessor and is used to determine:

*   Whether the individual meets the nursing home level of care.
*   The individual’s preferences for care setting (home, assisted living, or nursing home).
*   The specific services and supports needed to meet their health and safety needs in the community.

The LTCC is a critical step, as the results directly inform the service plan and the allocation of EW funds.

## Conclusion

Minnesota’s Medical Assistance (MA) program, particularly when coupled with the **Elderly Waiver (EW)**, provides a crucial financial lifeline for thousands of seniors and individuals with disabilities. The EW’s focus on community-based care, including the coverage of services in assisted living, reflects a commitment to person-centered care and independence. Navigating the eligibility requirements, especially the complex financial rules for long-term care, requires careful attention, but the comprehensive benefits offered by these programs ensure that Minnesotans can access the necessary medical and supportive services to age with dignity and security.
