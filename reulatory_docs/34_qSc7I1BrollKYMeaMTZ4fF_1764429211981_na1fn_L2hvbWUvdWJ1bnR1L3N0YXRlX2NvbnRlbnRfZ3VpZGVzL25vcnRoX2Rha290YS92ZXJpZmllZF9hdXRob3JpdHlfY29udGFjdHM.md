# North Dakota Verified Authority Contacts for Long-Term Care: A Comprehensive Directory and Guide

Navigating the long-term care system in North Dakota requires access to accurate and reliable information from official state and federal agencies. This comprehensive guide provides a consolidated directory of verified authority contacts for key areas, including Medicaid, facility licensing, resident advocacy, and veterans' benefits. More than just a list, this guide explains the specific function of each office and provides guidance on when and how to engage with them effectively.

All contact information is sourced directly from the **North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (ND DHHS)** and the **North Dakota Department of Veterans Affairs (NDDVA)**, ensuring the highest level of accuracy and trustworthiness.

## I. North Dakota Medicaid Program (ND Medicaid)

The ND Medicaid program, administered by the Department of Health and Human Services, is the primary source of funding for long-term care services for eligible residents. This contact information is essential for inquiries regarding eligibility, application status, and covered services, including the Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) Waiver.

| **Program Name** | ND Medicaid |
| **Administering Agency** | North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (ND DHHS) |

| **General Information Phone** | (866) 614-6005 |
| **Medical Services Division Phone** | (701) 328-7068, 711 (TTY) |
| **Toll-Free Phone** | (800) 755-2604 |
| **E-mail** | `dhsmed@nd.gov` |
| **Mailing Address** | North Dakota Health and Human Services, Medical Services Division, 600 E. Boulevard Ave., Dept. 325, Bismarck, ND 58505-0250 |

### When to Contact ND Medicaid

You should contact the ND Medicaid office when you have questions about financial eligibility for long-term care, need to apply for the HCBS Waiver, or require clarification on what services are covered. When calling, be prepared to provide the applicant's full name, date of birth, and any existing case number. For complex financial questions, it is often best to schedule an in-person appointment at your local **Human Service Zone Office**, as these local offices handle the direct application intake and eligibility determination process. The central office can provide policy guidance, but the local offices are your primary point of contact for case-specific actions.

## II. Health Facility Licensing and Regulation

The **Health Facilities Unit (HFU)** within the ND DHHS is the state's regulatory body responsible for licensing and certifying long-term care facilities. This unit conducts surveys, ensures compliance with state and federal standards, and investigates complaints related to regulatory violations.

| Contact Detail | Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Licensing Agency** | North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Unit |
| **Website** | [https://www.hhs.nd.gov/health/regulation-licensure-and-certification/health-facilities-unit](https://www.hhs.nd.gov/health/regulation-licensure-and-certification/health-facilities-unit) [2] |
| **General Phone** | (701) 328-2352 |
| **Complaint Hotline** | (701) 328-2352 (for regulatory complaints) |
| **Online Complaint Form** | [Health Facility Program Complaint Intake Form](https://www.hhs.nd.gov/form/health-facility-program-complain) |
| **Mailing Address** | North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services, Health Facilities Unit, 600 E Boulevard Ave Dept 301, Bismarck, ND 58505-0200 |

### When to Contact the Health Facilities Unit

Contact the HFU when you suspect a facility is violating state or federal regulations, particularly concerning patient safety, quality of care, or environmental hazards. This is the agency that can impose fines, restrict admissions, or revoke a facility's license. Before filing a complaint, it is helpful to gather specific details, including the date, time, and location of the incident, the names of any staff involved, and the names of any witnesses. While you can remain anonymous, providing detailed, verifiable information significantly aids the investigation process. You can also contact the HFU to check the licensing status and view inspection reports for any long-term care facility in the state.

## III. Long-Term Care Ombudsman and Advocacy

The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** is an independent advocate for residents of nursing homes, basic care facilities, and assisted living homes. They work to resolve complaints, educate residents on their rights, and advocate for quality of care and life.

| Contact Detail | Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Program Name** | North Dakota Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program |
| **Administering Agency** | ND DHHS, Adult and Aging Services |
| **Website** | [https://www.hhs.nd.gov/adults-and-aging/human-services/adults-and-aging/long-term-care-ombudsman-program](https://www.hhs.nd.gov/adults-and-aging/human-services/adults-and-aging/long-term-care-ombudsman-program) [3] |
| **Toll-Free Phone** | (855) 462-5465, select option 3 |
| **Direct Phone** | (701) 328-4617 |
| **Email** | `dhsagingombud@nd.gov` |
| **Online Complaint Form** | [Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program Complaint Form (SFN 1829)](https://www.nd.gov/eforms/Doc/sfn01829.pdf) |

### When to Contact the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman is your primary resource for issues related to resident rights, quality of life, and mediation with facility staff. Unlike the HFU, the Ombudsman's goal is resolution and advocacy, not regulatory enforcement. You should contact them for concerns such as involuntary discharge notices, disputes over personal property, poor food quality, lack of activities, or any instance where a resident feels their rights are being violated. The Ombudsman's services are confidential and resident-directed, meaning they will not take action without the resident's consent. They are also an excellent resource for information on Resident and Family Councils.

### When to Contact the NDDVA

Contact the NDDVA or your local **Veterans Service Officer (VSO)** for assistance with filing claims for VA benefits, such as the Aid and Attendance pension, disability compensation, or the state's Hardship Assistance Grant. VSOs provide free, expert assistance in navigating the complex VA system. You should also contact them for information regarding admission to the North Dakota Veterans Home. When contacting a VSO, have your military discharge papers (DD-214) and any relevant medical records ready.

## V. Critical State Contacts for Protection and Assistance

For comprehensive long-term care planning and protection, the following contacts are also vital resources for North Dakota residents.

### A. Adult Protective Services (APS)

APS investigates reports of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults who are unable to protect themselves. This is the primary reporting channel for all forms of suspected maltreatment.

| Contact Detail | Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agency** | North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services |
| **Reporting** | Contact your local Human Service Zone Office |
| **Vulnerable Adult Abuse Reporting** | [Reporting Abuse and Neglect of a Vulnerable Adult](https://www.hhs.nd.gov/adults-and-aging/reporting) [6] |
| **Emergency** | Call 911 |

### When to Contact APS

You must contact APS immediately if you suspect a vulnerable adult is being abused, neglected, or financially exploited. This includes self-neglect where the individual is unable to care for themselves. Reports can be made anonymously, but providing your name allows the investigator to follow up for more details. For immediate danger, always call 911 first. APS works to intervene and provide protective services to the vulnerable adult.

### B. Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)

The MFCU investigates and prosecutes fraud committed by Medicaid providers and abuse or neglect of residents in Medicaid-funded facilities that rises to the level of a criminal offense.

| Contact Detail | Information |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Agency** | North Dakota Attorney General's Office |
| **Information** | [ND Medicaid Fraud Control Unit Information](https://attorneygeneral.nd.gov/attorney-generals-office/medicaid-fraud/) [7] |
| **Phone** | (701) 328-5500 (Attorney General's Office) |

### When to Contact the MFCU

Contact the MFCU if you suspect a healthcare provider or facility is committing Medicaid fraud (e.g., billing for services not rendered, falsifying records) or if you have evidence of criminal abuse or neglect of a resident in a Medicaid-funded facility. The MFCU's focus is on criminal prosecution and recovering fraudulently obtained funds.

Their counselors are trained to provide personalized, objective advice to help you make informed decisions about your health coverage.

## VI. Conclusion

This consolidated directory of verified authority contacts serves as a critical resource for anyone seeking information, assistance, or resolution for issues related to long-term care in North Dakota. By understanding the specific role of each agency—from the regulatory oversight of the HFU to the resident advocacy of the Ombudsman and the financial assistance provided by the NDDVA—residents and their families can confidently navigate the state's support systems. Utilizing these official channels ensures that you receive accurate information and that your concerns are directed to the authority best equipped to address them.
