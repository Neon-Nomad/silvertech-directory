# North Dakota Veterans Benefits: Long-Term Care and Financial Assistance

North Dakota is committed to honoring its veterans, service members, and their families by providing a comprehensive suite of benefits, with a particular focus on long-term care and financial assistance. The **North Dakota Department of Veterans Affairs (NDDVA)** serves as the central coordinating agency, working in partnership with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) and the state's Veterans Service Officers (VSOs) to ensure veterans receive the support they have earned [1].

This guide details the key long-term care and financial benefits available to North Dakota veterans, including the state's Veterans Home, federal programs like Aid and Attendance, and state-specific financial grants.

## I. The North Dakota Veterans Home (NDVH)

The most significant state-level long-term care benefit is the **North Dakota Veterans Home (NDVH)**, located in Lisbon. Established in 1893, the NDVH is a living tribute to North Dakota veterans and their spouses, providing a range of care services in a dedicated, compassionate environment [2].

### A. Levels of Care Provided

The NDVH offers two primary levels of care to meet the diverse needs of its residents:

1.  **Skilled Care (Skilled Nursing Facility - SNF):** This unit provides 24-hour skilled nursing supervision and assistance for residents with significant medical needs and those requiring assistance with all Activities of Daily Living (ADLs). The NDVH has a capacity of 52 skilled nursing beds.
2.  **Basic Care:** This unit is designed for residents who are largely independent but require personal care and services. Residents in basic care must have total independence with personal care needs such as bathing, dressing, and ambulating. The Basic Care Unit has a capacity of 98 beds [2].

### B. Eligibility for Admission

Admission to the North Dakota Veterans Home is governed by state law and is open to veterans, their spouses, and surviving spouses who meet specific criteria [3]:

*   **Veteran Status:** A veteran is defined as an individual who has served on continuous federalized active military duty for 180 days or the full period for which they were called to duty (other than training), and who was discharged or released under other than dishonorable conditions.
*   **Spouses/Surviving Spouses:** The spouse or surviving spouse of an eligible veteran or service member may also be admitted.
*   **Residency:** Applicants must be bona fide residents of North Dakota preceding the application for admission.
*   **Disqualifications:** No veteran whose last period of service was terminated under dishonorable conditions is eligible. Additionally, no veteran convicted of a felony or a crime involving moral turpitude may be admitted without sufficient evidence of subsequent good conduct and reformation of character.

### C. Cost of Care and Financial Calculation

The cost of care at the NDVH is determined on a sliding scale, ensuring that the home remains accessible to veterans regardless of their financial situation. The calculation is based on the resident's ability to pay, with a clear formula established for basic care.

*   **Basic Care Cost:** Room and board charges for basic care are calculated at **55% of the resident's monthly net income** [4]. The net income is determined by subtracting allowable medical expenses from the gross income. This structure ensures that residents retain a significant portion of their income for personal needs.
*   **Skilled Care Cost:** The cost for skilled nursing care is often covered by a combination of Medicare, Medicaid, VA benefits, and private insurance. The NDVH works with residents to maximize their benefits to cover the cost of care.
*   **Financial Planning:** Veterans are strongly encouraged to work with a Veterans Service Officer (VSO) to ensure all potential benefits, such as the VA Aid and Attendance benefit, are applied for to help offset the cost of care.

## II. State-Specific Financial Assistance Programs

The NDDVA administers several state-funded programs designed to provide direct financial relief to veterans and their families, which can be crucial for those facing long-term care expenses.

### A. Hardship Assistance Grant

The **Hardship Assistance Grant** program provides direct financial support to eligible North Dakota veterans, their spouses, and un-remarried surviving spouses who are experiencing a financial hardship [5].

*   **Purpose:** The grant is intended to assist with essential needs, which can include medical expenses, housing costs, utilities, and other critical financial burdens that may arise during a long-term care crisis.
*   **Eligibility:** Applicants must be North Dakota residents and meet the definition of a veteran or eligible dependent as defined in the North Dakota Century Code. The hardship must be verifiable and not caused by the applicant's own negligence.
*   **Application:** Applications are processed through the local County or Tribal Veterans Service Officer (VSO), who assists the veteran in compiling the necessary documentation and submitting the request to the NDDVA.

### B. Other State Benefits

The NDDVA also oversees other benefits that can indirectly support long-term care:

*   **Property Tax Exemptions:** Disabled veterans may be eligible for property tax exemptions, which can reduce the financial burden of maintaining a home while receiving long-term care services.
*   **State Employment Preference:** While not a direct care benefit, this helps ensure veterans and their spouses have employment opportunities, contributing to overall financial stability.

## III. Federal Financial Benefits for Long-Term Care

North Dakota veterans are also eligible for federal benefits administered by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), which can significantly offset the cost of long-term care, whether in a facility or at home.

### A. VA Aid and Attendance (A&A) Benefit

The Aid and Attendance (A&A) benefit is a special monthly payment added to the amount of a VA pension for qualified veterans and survivors. This benefit is specifically designed to help cover the costs of a caregiver or long-term care [6].

#### Eligibility for A&A:

To qualify for the A&A benefit, a veteran must be eligible for the basic VA pension and meet one of the following medical criteria:

*   Require the aid of another person to perform Activities of Daily Living (ADLs), such as bathing, feeding, dressing, or using the restroom.
*   Be confined to bed due to a disability.
*   Be a patient in a nursing home due to mental or physical incapacity.
*   Have severely limited eyesight (5/200 visual acuity or less).

The A&A benefit is a non-service-connected pension, meaning the disability does not have to be related to military service. However, it is an income-adjusted benefit, and applicants must meet certain financial thresholds.

### B. VA Health Care and Long-Term Services

The VA provides a range of long-term services and supports (LTSS) through its health care system, including:

*   **Home-Based Primary Care:** Health care services provided to veterans in their homes.
*   **Skilled Home Health Care:** Short-term services for veterans who are homebound.
*   **Medical Foster Home Program:** A program that offers an alternative to nursing home care in a private home setting, where a trained caregiver provides 24-hour supervision and care [7].

## IV. The Critical Role of Veterans Service Officers (VSOs)

The most valuable resource for any North Dakota veteran seeking benefits is the network of **VA Accredited Veterans Service Officers (VSOs)**. These officers are experts in VA benefits and provide free, professional assistance at the county and tribal level.

### A. VSO Services

VSOs provide comprehensive assistance, including:

*   **Claims Assistance:** Filing claims for disability compensation, pension, and A&A, ensuring all necessary documentation is included.
*   **Appeals:** Assisting veterans with the complex process of appealing denied claims.
*   **Benefit Counseling:** Advising veterans and their dependents of their rights and entitlements under various federal and state laws [8].
*   **Long-Term Care Planning:** Guiding veterans through the process of applying for the North Dakota Veterans Home and federal long-term care benefits.

### B. Finding a VSO

The NDDVA strongly encourages all veterans to work with a VSO. The NDDVA website provides a locator tool to find the nearest County or Tribal VSO, ensuring personalized, local support [9].

## Conclusion

North Dakota provides a strong support system for its veterans, with the North Dakota Veterans Home offering a dedicated facility for long-term care and the NDDVA actively connecting veterans to crucial federal and state financial benefits, including the Hardship Assistance Grant. By utilizing the expertise of a Veterans Service Officer, North Dakota veterans can successfully navigate the complex landscape of benefits to secure the long-term care and financial stability they deserve. The state's commitment to its veterans is evident in the comprehensive nature of these programs.
