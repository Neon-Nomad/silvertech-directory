# North Dakota Long-Term Care Licensing Authority: Regulations and Oversight

The quality and safety of long-term care facilities in North Dakota are rigorously overseen by the **North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (ND DHHS)**, primarily through its **Health Facilities Unit (HFU)** [1]. This unit is the state's central licensing authority, responsible for ensuring that all healthcare providers and facilities, including those offering long-term care, meet stringent state and federal standards for patient health, safety, and quality of service.

This comprehensive guide explores the critical role of the Health Facilities Unit, the different types of long-term care facilities it licenses, the detailed regulatory framework in place, and the enforcement actions taken to protect North Dakota's vulnerable residents.

## I. The Role and Mission of the Health Facilities Unit

The mission of the Health Facilities Unit is to promote the health, safety, quality of life, and independence of North Dakota residents by educating and monitoring the care and services provided by healthcare suppliers and providers [2]. This is achieved through a multi-faceted approach that includes:

*   **Licensure:** Granting initial licenses and renewing them annually for various healthcare facilities.
*   **Certification:** Ensuring facilities that wish to participate in the Medicare and Medicaid programs meet federal certification requirements.
*   **Surveys and Inspections:** Conducting unannounced, periodic inspections to verify compliance with state and federal regulations.
*   **Complaint Investigation:** Investigating complaints of abuse, neglect, or regulatory violations in licensed facilities.

The HFU's work is essential to maintaining a high standard of care and providing a safe environment for all residents receiving long-term care services in the state.

## II. Types of Licensed Long-Term Care Facilities and Regulations

The HFU licenses and certifies several categories of facilities that provide long-term care services. The regulatory requirements vary significantly based on the level of care provided, as outlined in the North Dakota Administrative Code and Century Code.

### A. Skilled Nursing Homes (SNF)

Skilled Nursing Homes provide the highest level of medical care outside of a hospital. They are licensed by the state and certified by the federal government to participate in Medicare and Medicaid programs.

*   **Regulatory Authority:** Licensing is governed by **Chapter 33-07-03.2 NURSING FACILITIES** of the North Dakota Administrative Code [3].
*   **Scope of Care:** Services include 24-hour skilled nursing care, rehabilitation services (physical, occupational, and speech therapy), and medical management for complex conditions.
*   **Staffing Requirements:** Regulations mandate specific staffing levels to ensure adequate care, including registered nurses (RNs), licensed practical nurses (LPNs), and certified nurse aides (CNAs). The facility must ensure sufficient staff to meet the needs of all residents.
*   **Resident Rights:** SNFs must adhere to extensive federal and state resident rights, including the right to be free from physical and chemical restraints, the right to privacy, and the right to participate in care planning [4].

### B. Basic Care Facilities (BCF)

Basic Care Facilities are licensed to provide personal care and services to residents who require assistance with activities of daily living but do not require the level of skilled nursing care found in a nursing home. This category often includes facilities that function as assisted living.

*   **Regulatory Authority:** Licensing is governed by **Chapter 33-03-24.1 BASIC CARE FACILITIES** of the North Dakota Administrative Code [5].
*   **Scope of Care:** Services typically include assistance with bathing, dressing, medication management, meals, and housekeeping.
*   **Staffing Requirements:** While specific staffing ratios may not be mandated, BCFs must provide **24-hour supervision** and sufficient staff to meet the needs of all residents, as determined by the residents' care plans [6]. The facility must also have policies to check state registries and licensure boards prior to employment [7].
*   **Medicaid Status:** It is important to note that BCFs are generally **not certified by CMS to participate in the Medicare/Medicaid programs** for the basic care services themselves [8]. However, the state's HCBS Waiver may cover the cost of "Adult Residential Care" in certain settings.

### C. Intermediate Care Facilities for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)

These facilities provide comprehensive and individualized health care and rehabilitation services to people with intellectual disabilities or related conditions. The HFU ensures these facilities comply with both state licensing standards and federal certification requirements for Medicaid participation.

## III. The Licensing, Survey, and Enforcement Process

The process for a facility to obtain and maintain a license is rigorous and continuous, with the HFU employing a robust system of oversight and enforcement.

### A. Initial Licensure and Ongoing Surveys

1.  **Initial Licensure:** A prospective facility must submit a detailed application, including architectural plans and operational policies. An unannounced survey is conducted before the facility can open to ensure compliance with all regulations.
2.  **Periodic Surveys:** Licensed facilities are subject to periodic, unannounced surveys. For federally certified facilities (like SNFs), these surveys are conducted on behalf of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) and typically occur at least once every 15 months. Surveyors review resident care, facility operations, and compliance with resident rights.
3.  **Complaint Investigations:** The HFU also conducts unannounced investigations in response to complaints of abuse, neglect, or regulatory violations.

### B. Enforcement Actions and Penalties

If a facility is found to be out of compliance with state or federal regulations, the HFU is authorized to impose a range of enforcement remedies, as detailed in **Chapter 75-02-05.2 NURSING FACILITY ENFORCEMENT ACTION** of the North Dakota Administrative Code [9].

| Enforcement Remedy | Description and Application |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Civil Money Penalties (CMPs)** | Fines imposed on the facility for each day of non-compliance or for each instance of a violation. These penalties are scaled based on the severity and scope of the deficiency. |
| **Directed Plan of Correction (DPOC)** | The HFU mandates specific actions the facility must take to correct the deficiency, rather than allowing the facility to propose its own plan. |
| **Directed In-Service Training** | The HFU requires the facility to provide specific training to staff to address the deficiencies found during the survey. |
| **Denial of Payment for New Admissions** | The facility is prohibited from admitting new residents who are covered by Medicare or Medicaid until the deficiencies are corrected. |
| **Temporary Management** | In severe cases, the HFU may appoint a temporary manager to operate the facility and correct the deficiencies. |
| **License Revocation/Termination** | For persistent or severe non-compliance, the HFU can revoke the facility's state license or terminate its participation in the Medicare/Medicaid program. |

Any funds received as penalties must be applied to protect residents of the nursing facility, to relocate residents, or to maintain the operation of the nursing facility pending correction of the deficiencies [10].

## IV. Resident Rights and Advocacy

A core function of the HFU's oversight is the protection of resident rights. The state provides detailed summaries of rights for residents in different settings:

*   **Nursing Facility Resident Rights:** These rights are based on both Federal and North Dakota State Law and cover areas like freedom from abuse, the right to make independent choices, and the right to voice grievances [4].
*   **Basic Care Residents' Rights:** These rights are summarized in a dedicated booklet and cover the right to treat one's living quarters as one's home, the right to privacy, and the right to manage one's own financial affairs [11].
*   **Assisted Living Resident/Tenant Rights:** These rights are also summarized in a guide and ensure that tenants keep the rights they had before moving into the assisted living apartment [12].

## Conclusion

The North Dakota Health Facilities Unit serves as the essential regulatory backbone for the state's long-term care system. By diligently licensing, surveying, and enforcing regulations through a clear and comprehensive legal framework, the unit ensures that facilities like Skilled Nursing Homes and Basic Care Facilities operate at a standard that protects the health, safety, and dignity of their residents. Understanding the scope of this authority, the specific regulations for each facility type, and the enforcement actions available is crucial for residents, families, and providers alike in navigating the long-term care landscape in North Dakota.
