# Navigating the Complaint Process for Long-Term Care in California

For residents and families of long-term care facilities in California, the ability to file a complaint is a fundamental right and a critical mechanism for ensuring quality of care and patient safety. The state has established a multi-layered system for reporting concerns, with the **California Department of Public Health (CDPH)** and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** serving as the primary authorities.

This comprehensive guide outlines the official channels, procedures, and timelines for filing a complaint against a long-term care facility in California, ensuring that residents and their advocates can effectively seek resolution and accountability.

## Primary Complaint Authority: CDPH Licensing and Certification Program

The **California Department of Public Health (CDPH)**, through its **Licensing and Certification Program (L&C)**, is the official state agency responsible for investigating complaints against licensed and certified health care facilities, most notably **Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs)**.

### Key Complaint Contact

| Authority | Program | Complaint Hotline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CDPH** | Licensing and Certification Program | (800) 554-0354 |

### Who Can File a Complaint?

Anyone can file a complaint against a health care facility, including:

*   A patient or facility resident
*   A relative or friend
*   A facility employee (whistleblower protections apply)
*   A general member of the public
*   The Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Complaints can be filed anonymously, but providing contact information is encouraged as it allows the investigator to follow up for additional details, which can strengthen the investigation.

### How to File a Complaint Against a Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF)

The CDPH offers several convenient and official methods for submitting a complaint:

#### 1. Online Filing (The Most Direct Route)

The most efficient way to file a complaint is through the CDPH's online portal, the **California Health Facilities Information Database (Cal Health Find)**.

*   **Procedure:** Users can search for the facility on Cal Health Find and select the 'File a Complaint' option.
*   **Benefit:** This method ensures the complaint is immediately routed to the correct CDPH district office that has oversight authority for the facility in question.

#### 2. Telephone Hotline

For urgent matters or for those who prefer to speak directly with an official, the CDPH maintains a toll-free complaint hotline.

*   **Hotline Number:** **(800) 554-0354**
*   **Procedure:** Callers can report their concerns directly to a representative. The hotline is especially critical for reporting situations that pose an immediate threat to a resident's health or safety.

#### 3. Mail or Fax

Complaints can also be submitted in writing via mail or fax to the appropriate CDPH district office.

*   **Procedure:** Complainants can draft their own letter detailing the concerns or use a standardized complaint form (if available).
*   **District Office:** It is essential to consult the **District Office directory** on the CDPH website to ensure the complaint is sent to the office responsible for the county where the facility is located.

### The Complaint Investigation Process and Timelines

Once a complaint is received, the CDPH L&C Program follows a structured process that prioritizes the most serious allegations.

#### 1. Triage and Prioritization

Complaints are classified based on the severity and immediacy of the alleged violation:

| Priority Level | Description | Investigation Timeline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate Jeopardy (IJ)** | Allegations that pose an imminent threat of death or serious bodily harm to a resident. | **Within 2 working days** |
| **High Priority** | Allegations that involve potential harm or injury to a resident, but not immediate jeopardy. | **Within 10 working days** |
| **Low Priority** | Allegations that do not involve direct patient harm (e.g., administrative issues, minor non-compliance). | **Within 45 working days** |

#### 2. Investigation

The investigation is conducted by a Health Facilities Evaluator from the CDPH district office. This typically involves an unannounced visit to the facility, where the investigator will:

*   Interview the complainant (if contact information was provided).
*   Interview residents, staff, and management.
*   Review medical records and facility policies.
*   Conduct an on-site inspection of the facility.

#### 3. Mandated Completion Time for Long-Term Care (LTC) Complaints

The state of California has established a strict timeline for the completion of long-term care complaint investigations:

> "Complaints against skilled nursing facilities and other long-term care (LTC) facilities that involve the threat of imminent danger, death, or serious bodily harm must be completed within **90 days**. Beginning July 1, 2018, the department must complete LTC complaint investigations within **60 days**."

This 60-day mandate for non-Immediate Jeopardy LTC complaints ensures a timely resolution process.

#### 4. Notification of Results

Once the investigation is complete, the CDPH district office is required to notify the complainant in writing of the results. If a deficiency is found, the facility is required to submit a Plan of Correction (POC), and the CDPH will follow up to ensure the corrective actions have been implemented.

## Complaints Against Assisted Living Facilities (RCFEs)

It is crucial to understand that complaints against **Residential Care Facilities for the Elderly (RCFEs)**, which include most assisted living facilities, are handled by a different state agency: the **California Department of Social Services (CDSS)**, Community Care Licensing Division (CCLD).

*   **CDSS CCLD Complaint Hotline:** **(844) 538-8766** (LET-US-NO)
*   **Online Filing:** Complaints can also be filed online through the CDSS website.

## Complaints Against Health Care Professionals

The CDPH also investigates complaints against certain licensed and certified professionals:

*   **Certified Nursing Assistants (CNAs), Home Health Aides (HHAs), and Hemodialysis Technicians (CHTs):** Complaints regarding misconduct or abuse by these professionals are investigated by the Investigation Section of CHCQ's Investigation Branch (IB).
    *   **Email:** `cnamisconduct@cdph.ca.gov`
    *   **Phone:** (916) 492-8232 (Message Center)
*   **Nursing Home Administrators (NHAs):** Complaints against the administrator's conduct are investigated by the Nursing Home Administrator Program.
    *   **Email:** `NHAP@cdph.ca.gov`

## The Role of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman

While the CDPH is the regulatory body, the **California Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** is an essential resource for residents and families. The Ombudsman acts as an advocate, mediating and investigating complaints on behalf of residents in SNFs and RCFEs.

*   **Ombudsman's Role:** The Ombudsman can help residents file a complaint with the appropriate agency (CDPH or CDSS), investigate the issue independently, and work with the facility to resolve the concern.
*   **Confidentiality:** All communication with the Ombudsman is confidential.

For any resident or family member facing an issue in a long-term care facility, contacting the local Long-Term Care Ombudsman is often the recommended first step.

## Conclusion

The California complaint process is designed to be accessible and responsive, with a clear mandate for timely investigation of serious allegations. By utilizing the **CDPH Complaint Hotline (800) 554-0354** for nursing homes and the **CDSS CCLD Hotline (844) 538-8766** for assisted living, and by leveraging the advocacy of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman, residents and their families can ensure that the highest standards of care are maintained across California's long-term care continuum.
