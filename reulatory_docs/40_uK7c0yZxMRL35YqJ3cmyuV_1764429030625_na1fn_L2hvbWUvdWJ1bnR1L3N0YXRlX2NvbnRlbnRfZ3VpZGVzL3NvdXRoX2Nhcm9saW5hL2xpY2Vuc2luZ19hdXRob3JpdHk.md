# South Carolina Healthcare Facility Licensing: A Comprehensive Guide to the Department of Public Health (DPH) Oversight

The regulation and oversight of healthcare facilities, particularly nursing homes and long-term care providers, in **South Carolina** is a critical function of the state government, ensuring patient safety and quality of care. The primary authority responsible for the licensing and certification of these facilities is the **South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH)**, specifically through its **Bureau of Healthcare Quality** [1]. This guide provides a detailed look at the DPH's role, the licensing process, the key regulations governing nursing homes, and the critical recent restructuring that established the DPH.

## I. The Licensing Authority: South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH)

The DPH, established following the restructuring of the former Department of Health and Environmental Control (DHEC), is the central agency for regulating healthcare facilities in the state. The Bureau of Healthcare Quality within the DPH is tasked with protecting the health and safety of South Carolina residents by ensuring that healthcare facilities meet both state licensing requirements and federal certification standards [2].

### A. The DHEC Restructuring and the Birth of DPH

A significant change in South Carolina's regulatory landscape occurred with the restructuring of the South Carolina Department of Health and Environmental Control (DHEC). Effective in 2024, DHEC was divided into two new agencies: the **South Carolina Department of Environmental Services (SCDES)** and the **South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH)** [4].

The DPH inherited all public health functions, including the critical responsibility of healthcare facility licensing and certification. This transition was designed to create a more focused and efficient agency dedicated solely to public health and quality of care oversight. All licenses and certifications previously issued by DHEC are now managed and enforced by the DPH [5].

## II. Facilities Under DPH Oversight

The DPH's Bureau of Healthcare Quality licenses and certifies a wide range of healthcare facilities, with a particular focus on those providing long-term care.

### A. Nursing Homes (Skilled Nursing Facilities)

Nursing homes, also known as Skilled Nursing Facilities (SNFs), provide 24-hour skilled nursing care, rehabilitation services, and other health-related services to residents who require continuous medical attention.

*   **Licensing Standard:** Nursing homes must comply with **South Carolina Code of Regulations, Regulation 61-17, Standards for Licensing Nursing Homes** [6].
*   **Federal Certification:** The DPH also acts as the state's survey agency for the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This means DPH surveyors inspect facilities to ensure they meet the federal requirements for participation in the Medicare and Medicaid programs [7].

### B. Community Residential Care Facilities (CRCFs)

CRCFs, often referred to as assisted living facilities, provide room, board, and personal care services to residents who are unable to live independently but do not require the level of care provided in a nursing home.

*   **Licensing Standard:** CRCFs are licensed under a separate set of regulations, ensuring they meet standards for resident safety, staffing, and service provision [8].
*   **Importance to Medicaid:** CRCFs are a key component of the state's long-term care system, as services in these facilities can be covered for eligible individuals through the **Community Choices (CC) Waiver** [9].

### C. Other Licensed Facilities

The DPH's oversight extends to numerous other healthcare providers, including:
*   Hospitals (Acute Care and Specialty)
*   Home Health Agencies
*   Hospices
*   Ambulatory Surgical Centers
*   End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD) Facilities [10]

## III. The Licensing and Renewal Process

The DPH licensing process is rigorous, designed to ensure that only qualified entities operate healthcare facilities in South Carolina.

### A. Initial Licensure

Any person or entity wishing to operate a new healthcare facility must first apply for an initial license. This process typically involves:
1.  **Application Submission:** Filing a comprehensive application with the Bureau of Healthcare Quality, including architectural plans, ownership information, and a plan of operation.
2.  **Plan Review:** Review of the facility's design and construction to ensure compliance with building codes and health standards.
3.  **Initial Survey:** A pre-licensure inspection by DPH surveyors to verify that the facility is constructed, equipped, and staffed in accordance with all applicable state regulations and federal standards [11].
4.  **Issuance:** Upon successful completion of the survey and review, a license is issued.

### B. License Renewal

Licenses are not permanent and must be renewed periodically. The renewal process requires the facility to demonstrate continued compliance with all state and federal regulations.

### C. Administrator Licensing

A crucial aspect of facility regulation is the licensing of the facility administrator. In South Carolina, nursing home administrators must be licensed by the **State Board of Examiners for Nursing Home Administrators** [12]. This ensures that the facility's leadership possesses the necessary qualifications and ethical standards to manage a complex healthcare environment.

## IV. Key Regulations and Standards for Nursing Homes

The **Regulation 61-17, Standards for Licensing Nursing Homes** is the foundational document governing the operation of nursing homes in South Carolina. Key areas covered by this regulation include:

### A. Resident Rights and Quality of Life

The regulations place a strong emphasis on protecting the rights and dignity of residents. This includes:
*   The right to be fully informed of their medical condition and to participate in their care planning.
*   The right to privacy and confidentiality.
*   The right to voice grievances without fear of reprisal.
*   The requirement for a written care and services agreement between the resident and the facility [13].

### B. Staffing Requirements

Regulation 61-17 mandates specific staffing levels and qualifications to ensure adequate care. This includes requirements for:
*   Licensed nursing staff (Registered Nurses and Licensed Practical Nurses) available 24 hours a day.
*   Certified Nursing Assistants (CNAs) to provide direct resident care.
*   A licensed administrator and a Director of Nursing (DON) [14].

### C. Quality of Care and Services

Facilities must provide services that meet the needs of each resident, as identified in their comprehensive care plan. This encompasses:
*   Dietary services and nutritional support.
*   Rehabilitative services (physical, occupational, and speech therapy).
*   Pharmaceutical services.
*   Infection control and prevention programs [15].

## V. Inspection, Survey, and Enforcement

The DPH's Bureau of Healthcare Quality conducts regular, unannounced inspections, known as surveys, to monitor compliance.

### A. The Survey Process

1.  **Annual Surveys:** Every licensed and certified nursing home is subject to an unannounced standard survey at least once every 15 months, with the average interval not exceeding 12 months [16].
2.  **Complaint Investigations:** DPH surveyors also conduct investigations in response to complaints filed by residents, family members, or staff. These investigations are typically unannounced and must be initiated promptly, especially for allegations of immediate jeopardy [17].
3.  **Federal vs. State Surveys:** While the DPH conducts the surveys, they are often performed on behalf of CMS to determine compliance with federal Medicare/Medicaid requirements. A facility must pass both state licensing and federal certification standards to operate and receive federal funding.

### B. Enforcement Actions

When a facility is found to be non-compliant with state regulations or federal standards, the DPH has a range of enforcement actions it can take, including:
*   **Deficiency Citations:** Issuing official statements of deficiency (Form 2567) detailing the violations.
*   **Civil Monetary Penalties (CMPs):** Fines levied against the facility for serious or repeated violations.
*   **Directed Plan of Correction:** Requiring the facility to implement specific measures to correct the deficiencies.
*   **License Suspension or Revocation:** For severe, persistent, or life-threatening non-compliance, the DPH can suspend or revoke the facility's state license [18].
*   **Termination of Participation:** For federal non-compliance, CMS can terminate the facility's participation in the Medicare and Medicaid programs, effectively shutting down its ability to serve most long-term care residents.

## VI. Transparency and Public Access to Information

The DPH is committed to transparency, making information about licensed facilities and their compliance history publicly accessible.

*   **Online Database:** The DPH maintains an online database where the public can search for licensed facilities and view the results of their most recent inspection surveys and any enforcement actions taken [19].
*   **Consumer Resources:** The DPH website provides resources for consumers, including guides on choosing a long-term care facility and instructions on how to file a complaint [20].

The robust licensing and oversight system managed by the South Carolina Department of Public Health is essential for maintaining a high standard of care in the state's long-term care facilities, providing a layer of protection and accountability for vulnerable residents.
