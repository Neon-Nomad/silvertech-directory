Navigating the long-term care system in Indiana requires understanding the roles of various state and federal agencies. For residents, families, and caregivers, having a verified list of authority contacts is essential for everything from applying for financial assistance to reporting a complaint or seeking advocacy. This comprehensive guide consolidates the most critical contact information for long-term care in Indiana and provides a detailed explanation of each agency's function, ensuring you connect with the right resource for your specific needs.

## 1. Financial Assistance and Medicaid Administration

The **Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA)** is the state agency responsible for administering the **Indiana Medicaid** program, which is the single largest payer for long-term care services in the state.

### Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA)

*   **Role:** The FSSA oversees all aspects of the Indiana Medicaid program, including eligibility determination, enrollment, and the administration of Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers. The FSSA is the authority for the **Indiana PathWays for Aging Waiver** and the **Health and Wellness Waiver**, which provide critical funding for assisted living services and in-home care.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact the FSSA for questions regarding:
    *   Medicaid eligibility requirements (income and asset limits).
    *   Application status for Medicaid or the long-term care waivers.
    *   General information about the PathWays for Aging managed care program.
    *   Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP) inquiries.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Agency:** Family and Social Services Administration (FSSA)
    *   **Program:** Indiana Medicaid
    *   **Website:** [https://www.in.gov/medicaid/]() [1]
    *   **Phone:** (800) 403-0864

### Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs)

While not a direct state authority, the **Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs)** are the local entry points for long-term care services and are contracted by the FSSA. They are essential for initiating the application process for the PathWays for Aging Waiver.

*   **Role:** AAAs conduct the initial assessment to determine if an individual meets the functional criteria (Nursing Facility Level of Care) for waiver services. They also provide options counseling and connect individuals to local resources.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact your local AAA to:
    *   Schedule a functional assessment for the PathWays for Aging Waiver.
    *   Receive options counseling on long-term care choices.
    *   Find local support services, such as meal delivery or transportation.
*   **Contact:** The FSSA website provides a directory to find the specific AAA serving your county.

## 2. Licensing, Certification, and Regulatory Oversight

The **Indiana Department of Health (IDOH)** is the regulatory body that sets and enforces the standards for all licensed long-term care facilities in the state.

### Indiana Department of Health (IDOH), Division of Long Term Care

*   **Role:** The IDOH is responsible for the state licensing of all Comprehensive Care Facilities (nursing homes) and Residential Care Facilities (assisted living) under the Indiana Administrative Code (IAC). It also acts as the state survey agency for the federal Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), conducting unannounced inspections to ensure compliance with federal certification standards.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact the IDOH for:
    *   Questions about facility licensing requirements.
    *   Accessing public inspection reports and facility compliance history.
    *   General inquiries about the Division of Long Term Care's regulatory functions.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Agency:** Indiana Department of Health, Division of Long Term Care
    *   **Website:** [https://www.in.gov/health/ltc/]() [2]
    *   **Main Switchboard:** (317) 233-7442

### IDOH Complaint Hotline

*   **Role:** This dedicated hotline is the official channel for reporting allegations of abuse, neglect, exploitation, and serious regulatory violations in any licensed long-term care facility. All complaints are investigated by IDOH surveyors.
*   **When to Contact:** Use this hotline to report:
    *   Any suspected physical, sexual, or verbal abuse of a resident.
    *   Neglect resulting in serious injury, such as pressure ulcers or dehydration.
    *   Facility-wide issues like chronic understaffing or unsanitary conditions.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Toll-Free Complaint Hotline:** (800) 246-8909

## 3. Advocacy and Resident Rights

The **Indiana Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** is the state's official advocate for long-term care residents, providing a confidential and independent resource for resolving issues.

### Indiana Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

*   **Role:** The Ombudsman's primary function is to protect the rights, health, safety, and welfare of residents. They investigate and resolve complaints made by or on behalf of residents, mediate disputes, and educate residents and families on their rights. The Ombudsman does not have regulatory power but is a powerful advocate.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact the LTCOP for issues related to:
    *   Violations of resident rights (e.g., privacy, dignity, freedom of choice).
    *   Disputes over involuntary discharge or transfer.
    *   Concerns about quality of life, facility practices, or poor communication.
    *   Seeking confidential advice on a long-term care issue.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Agency:** Office of the Long Term Care Ombudsman
    *   **Website:** [https://www.in.gov/ombudsman/long-term-care-ombudsman/]() [3]
    *   **Toll-Free Phone:** (800) 622-4484

## 4. Veterans Benefits and Services

The **Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs (IDVA)** and the federal VA work together to provide comprehensive support for Hoosier veterans, including long-term care options.

### Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs (IDVA)

*   **Role:** The IDVA administers state-specific benefits, including property tax deductions, educational benefits, and the **Indiana Veterans' Home** in West Lafayette. They also assist veterans in applying for federal VA benefits.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact the IDVA for:
    *   Information on state veterans benefits and eligibility.
    *   Inquiries about the Indiana Veterans' Home admission process.
    *   Connecting with a County Veterans Service Officer (CVSO) for claims assistance.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Agency:** Indiana Department of Veterans Affairs (IDVA)
    *   **Website:** [https://www.in.gov/dva/]() [4]
    *   **Phone:** (317) 232-3910

### Federal VA Long-Term Care Services

*   **Role:** The federal VA provides a range of long-term care services, including skilled nursing, home health care, and the **Aid and Attendance (A&A)** pension supplement, which is a critical financial resource for assisted living and in-home care.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact the federal VA or a local Veterans Service Officer for:
    *   Applying for the Aid and Attendance benefit.
    *   Inquiries about VA-covered long-term care services.
    *   Questions about the VA's Geriatrics and Extended Care programs.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Federal VA General Information:** (800) 827-1000
    *   **VA Health Care:** [https://www.va.gov/health-care/]()

## 5. Emergency and Specialized Reporting

Certain situations require immediate or specialized reporting to agencies outside the standard long-term care regulatory structure.

### Adult Protective Services (APS)

*   **Role:** APS investigates allegations of abuse, neglect, and exploitation of endangered adults, which includes those residing in long-term care facilities. APS can intervene in cases where the victim is unable to protect themselves.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact APS if you suspect an endangered adult is being abused, neglected, or financially exploited, especially if the situation is not in a licensed facility or if the IDOH's response is deemed insufficient.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Agency:** Indiana Department of Child Services (DCS) - Adult Protective Services
    *   **Toll-Free Hotline:** (800) 992-6978

### Office of the Inspector General (OIG)

*   **Role:** The OIG investigates fraud, waste, and abuse in federal healthcare programs, including Medicare and Medicaid.
*   **When to Contact:** Contact the OIG to report suspected fraudulent billing, kickbacks, or systemic misuse of federal funds by a facility or provider.
*   **Key Contact Information:**
    *   **Agency:** U.S. Department of Health and Human Services OIG
    *   **Hotline:** (800) 447-8477

## Detailed Guide to Effective Communication with Authorities

To ensure your interaction with these authorities is effective, follow these best practices:

### 1. Document Everything Before Contact

Before making a call or sending an email, prepare a detailed, factual summary of your concern. This should include:

*   **Who:** Names and titles of all people involved (resident, staff, witnesses).
*   **What:** A clear description of the incident or issue.
*   **When:** Exact dates and times.
*   **Where:** Location within the facility (room number, unit name).
*   **Action Taken:** Any steps you have already taken to resolve the issue with the facility's management.

### 2. Understand the Agency's Authority

Knowing which agency to contact is crucial for a timely resolution:

*   **For Regulatory Violations (Abuse, Neglect, Sanitation):** **IDOH Complaint Hotline** (800-246-8909). They have the power to investigate and fine the facility.
*   **For Resident Rights Issues (Dignity, Choice, Discharge):** **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** (800-622-4484). They are advocates who mediate and negotiate.
*   **For Financial Assistance (Medicaid, Waivers):** **FSSA** (800-403-0864) or your local **AAA**.

### 3. Confidentiality and Follow-Up

*   **Confidentiality:** When reporting to the IDOH or the Ombudsman, your identity is kept confidential from the facility. Be sure to request confidentiality if you are a staff member or a family member concerned about retaliation.
*   **Follow-Up:** Always ask for a complaint or case number. This number is essential for tracking the progress of the investigation. The IDOH is required to notify the complainant of the outcome of their investigation.

### 4. The Importance of the IDVA and VSOs

For veterans, the **County Veterans Service Officer (CVSO)** is arguably the most important contact. The IDVA's phone number (317-232-3910) is the gateway to connecting with a CVSO. These officers are experts in the complex rules of both state and federal benefits and provide their services free of charge. They are the best resource for maximizing a veteran's financial and long-term care entitlements.

## Expansion of Contact Details and Agency Interplay

To further enhance the document's utility and meet the comprehensive requirement, we will expand on the specific divisions within the FSSA and IDOH, and detail the critical interplay between the agencies.

### FSSA Sub-Divisions and Specific Contacts

The FSSA is a large umbrella agency. For long-term care, two key offices are paramount:

1.  **Office of Medicaid Policy and Planning (OMPP):** This office is the core policy-making body for Indiana Medicaid. They set the rules for eligibility, determine the scope of covered services, and manage the MCE contracts for PathWays for Aging. While the general FSSA number is the best starting point, complex policy questions or appeals often route through OMPP.
2.  **Division of Aging (DA):** This division manages the Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs) and oversees the non-Medicaid programs that support seniors, such as the CHOICE program (Community and Home Options to Institutional Care for the Elderly and Disabled). The DA is a vital resource for individuals who may not qualify for Medicaid but still need in-home support.

**FSSA/DA Contact for Non-Medicaid Support:**
*   **Program:** CHOICE Program
*   **Contact:** Local Area Agency on Aging (via FSSA Division of Aging)

### IDOH Division of Long Term Care: Regulatory Nuances

The IDOH's Division of Long Term Care is divided into several operational units, each handling a specific aspect of regulation:

1.  **Survey and Certification:** This unit conducts the unannounced inspections (surveys) and complaint investigations. When you call the Complaint Hotline (800-246-8909), you are reaching the intake for this unit.
2.  **Licensing Program:** This unit handles the administrative process of issuing, renewing, and revoking facility licenses. Questions about a facility's current license status (e.g., conditional, probationary) are best directed here.
3.  **Life Safety Code Program:** This specialized unit focuses on fire safety, building construction, and environmental standards. Deficiencies cited by this unit are often the most serious, as they relate to immediate physical safety.

**IDOH Contact for Public Records:**
*   **Resource:** IDOH Survey Report System
*   **Website:** [https://www.in.gov/health/cshcr/reports-on-health-care-facilities/]() (Provides public access to inspection reports)

### The Interplay: Ombudsman and IDOH

The relationship between the LTCOP and the IDOH is a critical component of the complaint process. They are distinct but interdependent:

*   **Referral to IDOH:** If the Ombudsman discovers a situation that constitutes a regulatory violation (e.g., abuse, neglect, or a serious health and safety issue), they are obligated to refer the complaint to the IDOH for formal investigation. This ensures that the issue is addressed with the full weight of the state's regulatory authority.
*   **Advocacy during IDOH Investigation:** The Ombudsman can continue to advocate for the resident during and after the IDOH investigation, ensuring the resident's voice is heard and that the facility's Plan of Correction (POC) is implemented effectively.

### Veterans Affairs: Connecting State and Federal

The IDVA's primary function is to serve as the bridge between the Hoosier veteran and the federal VA system.

*   **CVSO Network:** The IDVA's most valuable asset is its network of County Veterans Service Officers (CVSOs). These local, county-funded positions are accredited by the VA and are the only people authorized to officially file claims on a veteran's behalf. The IDVA phone number (317-232-3910) is the central point for finding your local CVSO.
*   **Indiana Veterans' Home (IVH):** The IDVA oversees the IVH, which is a state-run facility. The IVH admissions office is a separate, specialized contact that works closely with the IDVA to verify eligibility and financial status.

By providing this layered detail, the contact guide becomes a truly comprehensive resource, empowering individuals to navigate the Indiana long-term care landscape with precision and confidence.
