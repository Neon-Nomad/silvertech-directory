# Tennessee Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Reporting Concerns and Ensuring Accountability

Ensuring the safety and quality of care in Tennessee's long-term care facilities is a shared responsibility between state regulatory bodies, advocacy groups, and the public. When concerns arise regarding the care, treatment, or rights of a resident in a nursing home, assisted care living facility (ACLF), or other licensed healthcare setting, Tennessee provides multiple official channels for filing a complaint. This comprehensive guide outlines the primary avenues for reporting issues, detailing the roles of the **Tennessee Department of Health** and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman**, and explaining the process from initial report to investigation and resolution.

## The Primary Regulatory Authority: Tennessee Department of Health

The **Tennessee Department of Health (TDH)**, through its **Division of Licensure and Regulation**, is the state's primary regulatory agency responsible for investigating complaints against licensed healthcare facilities. These complaints typically involve violations of state licensing standards or federal certification requirements (for Medicare/Medicaid participating facilities) [1].

### The Health Care Facilities Complaint Hotline

The most direct and immediate way to report a serious concern to the TDH is through the dedicated complaint hotline. This line is staffed by professionals who triage the complaint and initiate the investigation process.

*   **Health Care Facilities Complaint Hotline:** **(877) 287-0010**
*   **Purpose:** To report concerns about the quality of care, resident safety, sanitation, staffing levels, or any violation of licensing standards in a licensed facility.
*   **Availability:** The hotline is typically available during standard business hours, with procedures in place for reporting urgent, life-threatening situations after hours.

### Filing a Complaint with the Division of Licensure and Regulation

For non-emergency complaints, or for those who prefer a written record, the TDH provides a formal process for submitting a complaint to the Division of Licensure and Regulation.

#### 1. Online Submission (Public Records and Complaints Portal)

The TDH encourages the use of its **Public Records and Complaints portal** for all complaints against a licensed health care facility. This online system ensures that all necessary information is collected and the complaint is routed correctly for investigation [2].

#### 2. Submission by Mail or Fax

Complaints can also be submitted in writing via mail or fax. The complaint should include:
*   The name and address of the facility.
*   The name of the resident (if applicable and with consent).
*   A detailed description of the incident(s), including dates, times, and names of staff involved.
*   The name and contact information of the complainant (which can be kept confidential upon request).

### The Investigation Process (TDH)

Once a complaint is received, the TDH follows a structured investigation process:

1.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are assessed for severity. Those alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are prioritized and investigated within two business days. Less severe complaints are investigated within a set timeframe.
2.  **Unannounced Survey:** The investigation is typically conducted through an **unannounced on-site survey** by state surveyors. This surprise visit is crucial for observing conditions as they normally exist.
3.  **Fact-Finding:** Surveyors interview the resident, family members, staff, and management; review medical records, staffing schedules, and facility policies; and observe the care provided and the physical environment.
4.  **Findings and Enforcement:** If the investigation substantiates the complaint, the facility is cited for deficiencies and must submit a **Plan of Correction (POC)**. Depending on the severity, the TDH may impose sanctions, including civil monetary penalties (CMPs) or, in extreme cases, license revocation [1].

## The Advocacy Channel: Long-Term Care Ombudsman

While the TDH focuses on regulatory compliance, the **Tennessee Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman** focuses on resident rights and quality of life issues. The Ombudsman is an advocate, not a regulator, and provides a confidential, non-regulatory path for resolving concerns [3].

### When to Contact the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman is the ideal contact for issues related to:
*   **Resident Rights:** Violations of the right to privacy, dignity, or self-determination.
*   **Quality of Life:** Concerns about food, activities, social isolation, or responsiveness of staff.
*   **Communication:** Disputes with facility management or staff regarding care planning or information access.
*   **Transfer/Discharge:** Concerns about involuntary discharge or transfer notices.

*   **Ombudsman Phone:** **(877) 236-0013**
*   **Agency:** Tennessee Commission on Aging and Disability (TCAD)

### The Ombudsman's Resolution Process

The Ombudsman's process is centered on mediation and negotiation. They work to resolve the issue to the satisfaction of the resident, often by:
*   **Direct Advocacy:** Speaking with facility staff and management on behalf of the resident.
*   **Mediation:** Facilitating a discussion between the resident and the facility to reach a mutually acceptable solution.
*   **Referral:** If the complaint involves suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation, the Ombudsman is legally required to refer the case immediately to **Adult Protective Services (APS)** and/or the TDH.

## Reporting Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation (Adult Protective Services)

For the most serious concerns—those involving suspected abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation of a vulnerable adult—the appropriate authority is **Adult Protective Services (APS)**, a program administered by the Tennessee Department of Human Services (DHS).

*   **APS Hotline:** The state maintains a dedicated, confidential hotline for reporting suspected abuse. *Note: While the TDH hotline is for facility complaints, the APS hotline is for the protection of the vulnerable adult.*
*   **Mandatory Reporting:** Tennessee law mandates that certain professionals, including healthcare workers and Ombudsman staff, must report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation.

## The Importance of Documentation and Follow-Up

Regardless of the channel used, effective complaint filing relies on clear, detailed documentation. Complainants should keep a record of:
*   **The Incident:** Date, time, location, and a detailed description of what happened.
*   **Witnesses:** Names and contact information of any staff or residents who witnessed the event.
*   **Previous Reports:** Any prior attempts to resolve the issue with facility staff or management.
*   **The Complaint Filing:** The date the complaint was filed, the agency it was filed with, and any reference or case number provided.

Following up with the investigating agency is crucial. The TDH and the Ombudsman are required to inform the complainant of the outcome of the investigation, though specific details may be limited by confidentiality laws.

## Conclusion: Accountability Through Action

The Tennessee long-term care complaint process is a multi-layered system designed to ensure accountability and protect residents. By utilizing the **Health Care Facilities Complaint Hotline (877) 287-0010** for regulatory concerns and the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman (877) 236-0013** for rights and quality of life issues, residents and their families can actively participate in maintaining high standards of care. The commitment of the Tennessee Department of Health to investigate all complaints, coupled with the tireless advocacy of the Ombudsman program, provides a vital mechanism for addressing concerns and fostering a safe, dignified environment for all long-term care residents.
