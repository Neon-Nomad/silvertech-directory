# North Dakota Medicaid Programs: A Comprehensive Guide to Long-Term Care and Financial Assistance

The **North Dakota Medicaid** program, known as **ND Medicaid**, is a crucial state and federal partnership administered by the **North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services (ND DHHS)** [1]. It provides essential health care coverage and financial assistance to eligible low-income adults, children, pregnant women, and individuals who are aged, blind, or disabled. For seniors and those with chronic illnesses or disabilities, ND Medicaid is the primary source of funding for long-term care services, both in institutional settings like nursing homes and through community-based alternatives.

This comprehensive guide delves into the various components of ND Medicaid, with a specific focus on the programs designed to cover long-term care, particularly the critical **Medicaid Waiver for Home and Community Based Services (HCBS Waiver)**, and outlines the financial and medical eligibility criteria.

## I. ND Medicaid and Long-Term Care Coverage

Long-term care encompasses a wide range of medical and non-medical services that help people with chronic illnesses or disabilities perform daily activities. In North Dakota, Medicaid covers this care through two primary pathways: institutional care and Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS).

### A. Institutional Long-Term Care

ND Medicaid covers the cost of care in a **Skilled Nursing Facility (SNF)** for individuals who meet the state's medical necessity criteria for this level of care. This is the traditional form of long-term care coverage.

*   **Medical Necessity:** To qualify, an applicant must undergo a comprehensive assessment to determine if they require the level of care provided in a nursing home. This is known as meeting the "institutional level of care."
*   **Patient Liability:** Even when eligible, recipients of institutional Medicaid are typically required to contribute most of their monthly income toward the cost of care, a payment known as the "patient liability" or "share of cost." Only a small personal needs allowance (PNA) is exempt from this contribution.
*   **Swing Bed Services:** Medicaid also covers "swing bed" services, which are provided in rural hospitals that have an agreement with Medicare and Medicaid to use their beds for either acute care or skilled nursing care, depending on the patient's needs [2].

### B. Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) Waiver

The **Medicaid Waiver for Home and Community Based Services (HCBS Waiver)** is the cornerstone of North Dakota's effort to allow individuals to receive long-term care in their homes or other community settings, such as assisted living facilities, rather than in a nursing home. Authorized under Section 1915(c) of the Social Security Act, this waiver allows the state to offer a broader array of services to a specific population [3].

The HCBS Waiver is particularly significant because it covers **Adult Residential Care Services**, which is the service that makes assisted living a financially viable option for many Medicaid recipients in North Dakota.

## II. Financial and Medical Eligibility Criteria

To qualify for ND Medicaid long-term care programs, applicants must meet stringent financial and medical criteria. These rules are complex and often require careful planning.

### A. Financial Eligibility: Income and Asset Limits

North Dakota applies specific financial limits for long-term care Medicaid, which are generally more restrictive than for other Medicaid programs.

| Financial Criteria | Individual Applicant | Married Couple (Both Applying) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Income Limit** | Generally, the income limit is 300% of the Federal Benefit Rate (FBR), which is subject to annual change. | Income is assessed separately, with each spouse limited to 300% of the FBR. |
| **Asset Limit** | \$3,000 in countable assets [4]. | \$6,000 in countable assets [4]. |

**Countable Assets** typically include cash, bank accounts, stocks, and bonds. **Exempt Assets** generally include the primary residence (up to a certain equity limit), one vehicle, household goods, and personal effects.

### B. Spousal Impoverishment Protections

For married couples where only one spouse is applying for long-term care Medicaid (the "institutionalized spouse"), federal and state rules protect the non-applicant spouse (the "community spouse") from financial ruin. These protections are known as the **Spousal Impoverishment Prevention Coverage** [4].

*   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The community spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined countable assets, up to a federally set maximum, which is adjusted annually. This allowance is intended to ensure the community spouse has sufficient resources to live on.
*   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The community spouse is also allowed to keep a portion of the institutionalized spouse's income, if needed, to meet a federally determined minimum monthly income level. This prevents the community spouse from becoming impoverished while their partner receives care.

### C. Medical Eligibility: Level of Care

All applicants for long-term care Medicaid, whether for institutional care or the HCBS Waiver, must meet the state's **Level of Care (LOC)** requirement. This means a medical assessment must confirm that the individual requires the level of care provided in a nursing home. The HCBS Waiver is specifically designed to provide services that meet this need in a community setting, thus preventing unnecessary institutionalization.

## III. Comprehensive Services Under the HCBS Waiver

The North Dakota HCBS Waiver offers a broad and individualized array of services and supports. An individual's specific care plan, developed through a comprehensive assessment, determines which of these benefits they will receive [5].

| Service Category | Description and Scope |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adult Residential Care Services** | This is the key service that covers the cost of care services (but not room and board) in licensed settings like assisted living residences and basic care homes. It is available for persons with memory loss and brain injuries, as well as the aged and disabled. |
| **Personal Care Services** | Includes **Waiver Personal Care** (hands-on assistance and cueing for daily living activities) and **Extended Personal Care** (hands-on medical care, which may include skilled or nursing care). |
| **Family Personal Care** | A unique benefit that allows live-in family members, including spouses and adult children, to be paid to provide care. The caregiver must enroll as a Qualified Service Provider with ND DHHS. |
| **Respite Care** | Provides temporary, planned, or emergency relief for the primary caregiver, available in-home or out-of-home. |
| **Case Management** | Essential service for coordinating, planning, accessing, and monitoring all services and supports in the individual's care plan. |
| **Home and Community Supports** | Includes **Homemaker Services** (light housecleaning, meal preparation, laundry), **Chore Services**, and **Companionship Service**. |
| **Adaptive and Assistive Technology** | Covers **Home Modifications** (e.g., wheelchair ramps, grab bars, widening of doorways) and **Specialized Equipment / Assistive Technology / Supplies** to enhance safety and independence. |
| **Community Transition Services** | Financial assistance to help persons relocate from a nursing home or other institution back into a community setting. This covers one-time expenses like security deposits and utility setup fees. |
| **Other Supports** | **Adult Day Care / Adult Day Health**, **Home Delivered Meals**, **Emergency Response Systems**, **Supported Employment Services**, and **Transitional Living Service** (training for independent living skills). |

## IV. The Application and Enrollment Process

Applying for ND Medicaid long-term care is a multi-step process that begins with a formal application to the ND DHHS.

1.  **Application Submission:** Applications can be submitted online, in person at a local human service zone office, by phone, or by mail. Applicants must provide extensive documentation, including proof of income, assets (for ABD applicants), and identity [4].
2.  **Level of Care Determination:** A medical team conducts a thorough assessment to determine if the applicant meets the nursing home Level of Care requirement.
3.  **Financial Determination:** A financial eligibility worker reviews the applicant's income and assets against the state's limits.
4.  **Care Plan Development:** Once approved, a case manager works with the individual and their family to develop an individualized care plan that outlines the specific HCBS Waiver services they will receive.

## V. Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)

Applicants for long-term care Medicaid who are 55 years of age or older should be aware of the **Medicaid Estate Recovery Program (MERP)**. This program allows the state of North Dakota to recover the costs of certain Medicaid benefits, primarily long-term care services, from the estate of a deceased recipient [6].

*   **Targeted Services:** Recovery is sought for payments made for nursing facility services, Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS), and related hospital and prescription drug services.
*   **Estate Definition:** For MERP purposes, the estate includes all real and personal property and other assets passing from the deceased by will or intestacy. In some cases, it may also include assets that passed outside of probate, such as property held in joint tenancy.
*   **Exemptions and Waivers:** North Dakota limits recovery in certain situations, such as when the deceased has a surviving spouse or a child who is under 21, blind, or permanently disabled. The state may also waive recovery if it would cause an undue hardship to the heirs.

## Conclusion

ND Medicaid is a complex but essential program that provides a financial safety net for long-term care in North Dakota. The **HCBS Waiver**, with its comprehensive list of services including Adult Residential Care, offers a vital alternative to institutionalization, allowing many seniors and disabled individuals to receive necessary support in their own communities. Understanding the strict financial eligibility rules, the protections for community spouses, and the implications of the Medicaid Estate Recovery Program is paramount for anyone planning for long-term care in the state. The **North Dakota Department of Health and Human Services** remains the authoritative source for all program details and application assistance.
