## 1. Arkansas Department of Human Services (DHS)

The DHS is the largest state agency in Arkansas and is the umbrella organization for most health and human services, including Medicaid, long-term care regulation, and adult protective services.

### A. Division of Medical Services (DMS) - Medicaid

The DMS is responsible for administering the **Arkansas Medicaid** program, including the unique **ARHOME (Arkansas Health and Opportunity for Me)** program and the **Living Choices Assisted Living Waiver**.

#### Key Contacts and Responsibilities

| **DMS General Information** | General inquiries about Medicaid, eligibility, and benefits. | **(800) 482-5431** | [https://humanservices.arkansas.gov/]() [1] |
| **Access Arkansas Portal** | Online application for Medicaid, ARKids First, and other assistance programs. | N/A | [https://access.arkansas.gov]() |
| **DHS County Offices** | In-person application assistance and case management. | *Varies by county* | *Search DHS website for local office* |
| **Living Choices Resource Center** | Information and application for the Living Choices Assisted Living Waiver. | 1-866-801-3435 | *See DHS website* |

#### Role and Structure

The DMS oversees the financing and delivery of healthcare for over a million Arkansans. Its primary functions include:

*   **Eligibility Determination:** Setting and enforcing the financial and non-financial criteria for all Medicaid categories (children, pregnant women, aged, blind, disabled, and ARHOME adults).
*   **Program Administration:** Managing the contracts with the private health plans (Blue Cross Blue Shield and Ambetter) that deliver the ARHOME benefits.
*   **Waiver Management:** Administering Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers, such as the Living Choices Waiver, which provides an alternative to institutional care.

### B. Office of Long Term Care (OLTC) - Licensing and Regulation

The OLTC is the state's regulatory body for long-term care facilities, ensuring compliance with state licensing standards and federal certification requirements.

#### Key Contacts and Responsibilities

| **OLTC Complaint Hotline** | Reporting regulatory violations, quality of care issues, and resident rights concerns. | **(800) 582-4887** | complaints.OLTC@arkansas.gov |
| **Nursing Home Administrator Licensing** | Inquiries regarding NHA licensure and continuing education. | (501) 682-8302 | *Contact OLTC directly* |

#### Role and Structure

The OLTC conducts unannounced, on-site inspections (surveys) of all licensed facilities, including nursing homes and assisted living facilities. Its core responsibilities include:

*   **Licensure:** Issuing and renewing licenses for all long-term care facilities.
*   **Investigation:** Investigating all complaints received through the hotline related to facility compliance.
*   **Enforcement:** Imposing sanctions, such as civil money penalties (fines) and license actions, against facilities found to be in violation of state or federal rules.
*   **Certified Nurse Aide (CNA) Registry:** Maintaining the registry of CNAs and tracking findings of abuse or neglect against them.

### C. Adult Protective Services (APS) - Maltreatment Reporting

APS is the state agency responsible for investigating allegations of abuse, neglect, and exploitation (maltreatment) of vulnerable adults, including those in long-term care facilities and private homes.

#### Key Contacts and Responsibilities

| Contact Point | Purpose | Phone Number | Online Reporting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Adult Maltreatment Hotline (24/7)** | **Mandatory reporting** of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation. | **(800) 482-8049** | *Online portal available for non-emergencies* [3] |
| **Emergency Situations** | Immediate, life-threatening danger. | **911** | N/A |

#### Role and Structure

APS investigators determine if maltreatment has occurred, assess the risk to the adult, and provide protective services. The hotline is staffed 24 hours a day, seven days a week, to ensure immediate response to urgent situations.

*   **Abuse:** Physical injury, pain, or mental anguish inflicted intentionally.
*   **Neglect:** Failure to provide necessary care, food, shelter, or medical services.
*   **Exploitation:** Illegal or improper use of a vulnerable adult or their resources for another person's profit.

## 2. Arkansas Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The LTCOP is an independent advocacy program mandated by the federal Older Americans Act. It is a non-regulatory body focused on protecting the rights and quality of life for long-term care residents.

#### Key Contacts and Responsibilities

| Contact Point | Purpose | Phone Number | Website |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LTCOP State Office** | General inquiries and to connect with a Regional Ombudsman. | **(501) 508-8857** | [https://www.arombudsman.com/]() [4] |
| **LTCOP Complaint Line** | Confidential reporting of resident concerns and rights violations. | (800) 582-4887 | *Same number as OLTC hotline, but for Ombudsman services* |

#### Role and Structure

The Ombudsman's primary function is to investigate and resolve complaints made by or on behalf of residents.

*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman is legally required to maintain the confidentiality of the resident and the complainant's identity unless explicit consent is given.
*   **Advocacy:** They mediate disputes, educate residents and staff on resident rights, and advocate for systemic changes in the long-term care system.
*   **Facilities Covered:** Nursing homes, assisted living facilities, and residential care facilities.

The ADVA is the state agency dedicated to serving Arkansas veterans and their eligible dependents by providing access to state and federal benefits, high-quality long-term nursing care, and burial honors.

The ADVA is the gateway to a wide range of benefits, including:

*   **Education:** Information and assistance with the **Military Dependents Scholarship (MDS)** program.
*   **Employment:** Advocacy for veterans preference in state employment.
*   **Long-Term Care:** Oversight of the two State Veterans Homes.

## In-Depth: The Operational Structure of Key Agencies

To fully appreciate the importance of these contacts, it is helpful to understand the operational structure and specific divisions within the larger agencies.

### The Role of the DHS Division of County Operations (DCO)

While the Division of Medical Services (DMS) sets the policy for Medicaid, the **Division of County Operations (DCO)** is the face of the DHS for most Arkansans.

*   **Local Access and Intake:** The DCO manages the network of local DHS County Offices across the state. These offices are the primary point of contact for in-person applications, eligibility interviews, and case management for all public assistance programs, including Medicaid and SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program).
*   **Case Renewal:** DCO staff are responsible for processing the annual renewal (redetermination) forms for Medicaid beneficiaries, a critical function for maintaining continuous coverage.
*   **Contact:** While the general DHS number is the best starting point, finding the direct phone number for a local DCO office is essential for applicants who require in-person assistance or need to submit documentation. This information is available through the main DHS website.

### The Regional Structure of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The LTCOP's effectiveness relies on its regional structure, which ensures that Ombudsmen are physically present and accessible to residents in facilities across all 75 counties.

*   **Regional Ombudsmen:** The State Ombudsman oversees the program, but the day-to-day advocacy is carried out by a team of Regional Ombudsmen. Each Regional Ombudsman is assigned a specific geographic area (a cluster of counties) and is responsible for visiting the nursing homes and assisted living facilities within that region.
*   **Importance of Local Contact:** When a family member has a concern, contacting the State Office is a good first step, but they will be directed to the specific Regional Ombudsman assigned to the facility's location. This local contact is crucial for timely and effective intervention.
*   **Volunteer Network:** The Regional Ombudsmen also recruit and train certified volunteer Ombudsmen, who provide additional coverage and support by making regular visits to facilities.

### The ADVA's VSO Network: A Decentralized Approach

Similarly, the Arkansas Department of Veterans Affairs (ADVA) utilizes a decentralized network of **Veteran Service Officers (VSOs)** to deliver its core mission.

*   **County VSOs:** Every county in Arkansas has a County VSO, who is accredited to assist veterans with filing claims for federal VA benefits. These VSOs are the most important resource for veterans seeking to access disability compensation, pensions, and educational benefits.
*   **State VSOs:** The ADVA also employs State VSOs who provide a higher level of expertise and can represent veterans in appeals before the VA's Board of Veterans' Appeals.
*   **Contact Strategy:** The most efficient way for a veteran to get assistance is to first contact the ADVA Headquarters, which can then direct them to the nearest and most appropriate County or State VSO.

## The Importance of Cross-Agency Coordination

While each agency has a distinct mandate, effective protection and service delivery often require coordination between them.

*   **OLTC and APS:** When the OLTC investigates a regulatory complaint and finds evidence of abuse or neglect, they are mandated to refer the case to APS for a protective services investigation. Conversely, if APS finds a facility violation during a maltreatment investigation, they refer the regulatory matter to the OLTC.
*   **LTCOP and Regulatory Agencies:** The Ombudsman frequently refers serious complaints to both the OLTC and APS. This ensures that the resident receives both confidential advocacy (from the LTCOP) and formal regulatory or protective action (from the OLTC/APS).
*   **DMS and ADVA:** The DMS and ADVA coordinate on long-term care financing. For example, a veteran admitted to a State Veterans Home may have their care costs covered by a combination of VA benefits and Arkansas Medicaid, requiring coordination between the two departments.

This complex web of coordination underscores the necessity of having all verified contacts readily available.
