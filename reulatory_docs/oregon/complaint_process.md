# Filing a Complaint Against a Long-Term Care Facility in Oregon: A Step-by-Step Guide

**Author:** Manus AI
**Date:** November 29, 2025

Ensuring the safety and quality of care for residents in long-term care facilities is a shared responsibility, and Oregon has established clear pathways for reporting concerns and filing formal complaints. The process involves multiple state agencies, each with a distinct role in investigation, resolution, and enforcement. Understanding which agency to contact for a specific issue is the first critical step in advocating for a resident's well-being.

This comprehensive guide details the various complaint processes in Oregon, focusing on the roles of the **Oregon Department of Human Services (ODHS)**, the **Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman (OLTCO)**, and **Adult Protective Services (APS)**.

## I. Overview of Complaint Channels

In Oregon, complaints against long-term care facilities—including nursing homes, assisted living facilities, residential care facilities, and adult foster homes—can be directed to several key authorities, depending on the nature of the concern.

| Complaint Type | Primary Agency to Contact | Hotline/Contact Information |

## II. The Regulatory Complaint Process (ODHS)

The **Oregon Department of Human Services (ODHS)**, specifically the Aging and People with Disabilities (APD) program, is the state's licensing authority and is responsible for investigating complaints related to violations of state licensing rules and federal certification requirements.

### A. How to File a Regulatory Complaint

For concerns about facility operations, staffing, sanitation, or general quality of care that violate state regulations, a complaint should be filed with the ODHS.

1.  **Contact the Appropriate Unit:**
    *   For **Nursing Facilities (NF)**, use the dedicated hotline: **877-280-4555** or email: nf.complaints@odhsoha.oregon.gov [4].
    *   For **Assisted Living, Residential Care, and Adult Foster Homes**, use the Community-Based Care hotline: **844-503-4773** or email: licensing.complaint@odhsoha.oregon.gov [1].
2.  **Provide Detailed Information:** The complainant should provide as much detail as possible, including the facility name and address, the name of the resident affected, the date and time of the incident(s), and a clear description of the concern.
3.  **Online Form:** ODHS also provides an online form for reporting concerns about community-based care facilities [1].

### B. The Investigation and Enforcement Process

Once a regulatory complaint is received, the ODHS follows a structured process:

*   **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are assessed for severity and urgency. Those alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are prioritized for immediate, on-site investigation.
*   **Unannounced Investigation:** An ODHS investigator will conduct an unannounced visit to the facility. The investigation involves interviewing the complainant, the resident, facility staff, and reviewing relevant records.
*   **Findings and Enforcement:** If the investigation substantiates the complaint, the facility is cited for a deficiency. The ODHS has the authority to impose sanctions, which can include civil penalties (fines), directed plans of correction, denial of payment for new admissions, or, in severe cases, license revocation [5].
*   **Confidentiality:** The identity of the complainant is kept confidential unless the complainant gives explicit permission for disclosure or a court order requires it.

## III. Reporting Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation (APS)

Any suspicion of abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation of a vulnerable adult in a long-term care setting must be reported immediately to **Adult Protective Services (APS)**.

### A. The APS Hotline

Oregon maintains a toll-free, 24/7 hotline for reporting suspected abuse: **1-855-503-SAFE (7233)** [2].

*   **Vulnerable Adult Definition:** A vulnerable adult includes any person 65 years of age or older, or any person 18 years of age or older who has a physical or mental disability that impairs their ability to care for themselves.
*   **Mandatory Reporting:** Many professionals, including facility staff, health care providers, and law enforcement, are **mandatory reporters** and are legally required to report suspected abuse.

### B. The APS Investigation

APS is responsible for conducting a thorough investigation to determine if abuse, neglect, or exploitation has occurred.

*   **Safety First:** The immediate priority of APS is to ensure the safety of the vulnerable adult. This may involve coordinating with law enforcement or emergency medical services.
*   **Intervention and Services:** If the allegation is substantiated, APS will work to connect the adult with necessary protective services, which may include relocation, legal assistance, or medical care.
*   **Coordination with Law Enforcement:** If the suspected abuse involves criminal activity, APS will coordinate its investigation with local law enforcement agencies.

## IV. The Advocacy Role of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman

The **Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman (OLTCO)** provides a non-regulatory, advocacy-focused channel for resolving complaints, particularly those related to resident rights and quality of life.

### A. When to Contact the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman is the ideal contact for issues that require an advocate to mediate or resolve conflicts, such as:

*   Violations of the Resident's Bill of Rights (e.g., lack of privacy, refusal of choice) [6].
*   Concerns about quality of life, food, or social activities.
*   Disagreements with facility staff or administration.
*   Concerns about involuntary discharge or transfer.

### B. Ombudsman Complaint Process

1.  **Contact the OLTCO:** Call the toll-free number: **800-522-2602** [3].
2.  **Confidential Investigation:** The Ombudsman will investigate the complaint, but only with the resident's consent. The investigation is confidential and focuses on achieving a resolution satisfactory to the resident.
3.  **Mediation and Negotiation:** The Ombudsman works with the resident and the facility to find a solution, often acting as a mediator.
4.  **Referral:** If the issue is a serious regulatory violation or a case of abuse, the Ombudsman will refer the case to the ODHS or APS, while continuing to advocate for the resident's rights.

## V. Other Specialized Complaint Avenues

Depending on the specific nature of the complaint, other state agencies may be involved.

### A. Oregon Medical Board (OMB)

If the complaint involves the conduct of a licensed physician, physician assistant, or other medical professional, the **Oregon Medical Board (OMB)** is the appropriate authority.

*   **Scope:** The OMB investigates complaints related to medical competency, unprofessional conduct, or illegal activities by licensed medical practitioners [7].
*   **Contact:** Complaints can be filed online or by calling the Investigations Unit at (971) 673-2702.

### B. Oregon State Board of Nursing (OSBN)

Complaints concerning the professional conduct of a Registered Nurse (RN), Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN), or Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA) should be directed to the **Oregon State Board of Nursing (OSBN)**.

*   **Scope:** The OSBN investigates allegations of practice violations, incompetence, or ethical misconduct by nursing professionals [8].

## VI. Conclusion: A Multi-Layered Safety Net

Oregon's complaint process for long-term care facilities is designed as a multi-layered safety net, ensuring that every concern, from a minor rights violation to a serious case of abuse, has a dedicated channel for investigation and resolution. For residents and their families, the most effective approach is to first determine the nature of the complaint and then contact the most appropriate agency—be it the ODHS for regulatory issues, APS for safety concerns, or the OLTCO for advocacy and rights protection. This proactive engagement is essential to maintaining high standards of care and protecting the dignity of all long-term care residents in Oregon.
