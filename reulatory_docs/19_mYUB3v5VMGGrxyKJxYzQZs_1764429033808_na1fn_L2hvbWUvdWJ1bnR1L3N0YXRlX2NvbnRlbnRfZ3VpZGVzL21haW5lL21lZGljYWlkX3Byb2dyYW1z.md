# MaineCare: Comprehensive Guide to Medicaid Programs in Maine

The state of Maine provides essential healthcare and long-term services and supports through its Medicaid program, known as **MaineCare**. Administered by the **Office of MaineCare Services (OMS)** within the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS), MaineCare is a critical resource for eligible residents, including older adults, individuals with disabilities, children, and low-income families. The program is designed to ensure access to a broad range of medical and community-based services, helping beneficiaries maintain their health, independence, and quality of life.

## Eligibility and Application for MaineCare

MaineCare eligibility is determined by a combination of factors, including income, assets, age, and medical need. For older adults and individuals seeking long-term care services, the financial and medical eligibility criteria are particularly important. The rules for long-term care are complex and differ significantly from standard MaineCare eligibility for acute medical services.

### Financial Eligibility Criteria for Long-Term Care

MaineCare follows federal and state guidelines for determining financial eligibility for long-term services and supports (LTSS), which include nursing facility care and Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers like Section 19. These limits are subject to change annually, and applicants should always consult the most current guidelines from the DHHS.

#### Income Limits

For individuals applying for institutional or HCBS waiver services, Maine utilizes a special income limit, often referred to as the **Medicaid Institutional Income Cap**.

*   **Applicant Income:** In 2025, a single applicant for Nursing Home Medicaid or the Section 19 waiver must have gross monthly income below a specific threshold, which is typically 300% of the Federal Benefit Rate (FBR). This limit is designed to ensure that only those with significant financial need qualify for costly long-term care.
*   **Post-Eligibility Treatment of Income:** Once financially eligible, the applicant's income is used to determine their **Patient Share** or **Share of Cost**. Most of the applicant's income, minus a small personal needs allowance, is expected to be contributed toward the cost of care.
*   **Medically Needy Pathway:** Maine also offers a Medically Needy pathway for individuals whose income exceeds the cap but whose medical expenses are so high that they "spend down" their excess income to the eligibility limit.

#### Asset Limits and Exemptions

The asset limit for a single applicant for long-term care is typically set at **\$2,000** (excluding certain exempt assets).

*   **Exempt Assets:** Certain assets are not counted toward the limit, including:
    *   The **primary residence**, provided the applicant intends to return home or the equity value is below a state-specific limit (e.g., \$713,000 in 2024).
    *   One **vehicle**, regardless of value.
    *   **Personal belongings** and household goods.
    *   Certain **life insurance policies** and burial funds.
*   **Look-Back Period:** Maine enforces a **60-month (five-year) look-back period** for all asset transfers. Any uncompensated transfer of assets during this period may result in a penalty period of ineligibility for long-term care services.

#### Spousal Impoverishment Protections

Federal **Spousal Impoverishment Protection** rules are designed to prevent the non-applicant spouse (Community Spouse) from becoming impoverished when the other spouse requires long-term care.

*   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The Community Spouse is permitted to keep a portion of the couple's combined countable assets, up to a maximum limit (e.g., approximately \$154,140 in 2024).
*   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The Community Spouse is also entitled to a minimum monthly income. If the Community Spouse's income falls below this minimum, a portion of the applicant's income can be allocated to the Community Spouse to meet the MMMNA.

### Medical Eligibility and Assessment

To qualify for long-term services and supports, an applicant must meet a specific **level of care** requirement, which is typically the level of care provided in a nursing facility. This medical eligibility is established through a comprehensive assessment process.

*   **Medical Eligibility Determination (MED) Assessment:** This face-to-face assessment is conducted by the Department or its **Assessing Services Agency (ASA)**. The assessment uses the Department’s Medical Eligibility Determination (MED) form to evaluate the applicant's functional status, including their ability to perform **Activities of Daily Living (ADLs)** and **Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs)** [1].
*   **Activities of Daily Living (ADLs):** These are basic self-care tasks necessary for independent living, such as bed mobility, transferring, locomotion, eating, toileting, bathing, and dressing [1]. The assessment determines the level of assistance required (e.g., cueing, limited assistance, extensive assistance, or total dependence).
*   **Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs):** These include tasks related to independent living in the community, such as preparing meals, managing medications, using the telephone, handling finances, shopping, and routine housework [1].

Meeting the medical eligibility criteria confirms the necessity of long-term care services, which can then be provided either in a nursing facility or through a Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waiver program.

## Home and Community Benefits for the Elderly and Adults with Disabilities (Section 19)

MaineCare’s primary HCBS waiver for older adults and individuals with physical disabilities is the **Home and Community Benefits for the Elderly and Adults with Disabilities**, codified as **Section 19** of the MaineCare Benefits Manual [1]. This waiver is crucial as it allows eligible members to receive the necessary care and supports in their own homes or other residential community settings, thereby avoiding or delaying institutional nursing facility care.

The services provided under Section 19 are designed as a package and require prior approval by the DHHS or the ASA, delivered according to the member’s **Authorized Plan of Care** using a **Person-Centered Planning** process [1].

### The Person-Centered Planning Process

The **Person-Centered Planning (PCP)** process is a core component of Section 19, ensuring that the services authorized are tailored to the individual's unique needs, preferences, and goals.

*   **Development of the Plan:** The ASA works with the member, their family, and other involved parties to develop the **Authorized Plan of Care**. This plan outlines the specific services, frequency, and providers.
*   **Member Choice:** The PCP process emphasizes the member's right to choose their services, providers, and living arrangements, promoting self-determination and independence.
*   **Risk Assessment:** A **Risk Assessment** is conducted to identify potential risks to the member's health and welfare and to develop a **Back Up Plan** to mitigate those risks, especially for those utilizing the Participant-Directed Option [1].
*   **Annual Review:** The Authorized Plan of Care is reviewed and updated at least annually, or whenever there is a significant change in the member's condition or circumstances.

### Covered Services under Section 19

The Section 19 waiver covers a comprehensive array of services and supports aimed at promoting independence and safety in the community. The services are authorized based on the individual's assessed needs and documented in the Plan of Care.

| Service Category | Description and Scope |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Care Coordination Services** | Professional services that assist the member in gaining access to needed waiver and other State Plan services, as well as medical, social, educational, and other services. This includes developing, monitoring, and revising the Authorized Plan of Care [1]. |
| **Personal Care Services** | Hands-on assistance with ADLs and IADLs provided by a **Personal Support Specialist (PSS)** or other qualified staff. Services include bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting, transferring, and light housekeeping tasks essential to the member's health and safety [1]. |
| **Attendant Services** | Services provided by an Attendant to a member utilizing the **Participant-Directed Option**. This allows the member to manage their own care staff [1]. |
| **Home Health Services** | Skilled nursing and home health aide services provided by a licensed home health agency, when medically necessary and prescribed by a physician [1]. |
| **Assistive Technology Devices and Services** | Devices, controls, or appliances that enable a member to increase, maintain, or improve their functional capabilities. This includes **Remote Monitoring** and **Transmission** services [1]. |
| **Environmental Modifications** | Physical adaptations to the member's home that are necessary to ensure the health, welfare, and safety of the member, or that enable the member to function with greater independence. Examples include ramps, grab bars, and specialized bathroom modifications [1]. |
| **Financial Management Services (FMS)** | Provided by a Fiscal Intermediary to assist members utilizing the Participant-Directed Option with payroll, tax withholding, and compliance with labor laws [1]. |
| **Home Delivered Meals** | Meals delivered to the member's home when the member is unable to prepare their own meals and there is no one else available to do so [1]. |
| **Personal Emergency Response System (PERS) Services** | An electronic device that enables a member to secure help in an emergency [1]. |
| **Respite Services** | Short-term, temporary relief for the primary caregiver, provided in the member's home or a community setting [1]. |
| **Transportation Services** | Non-emergency medical transportation to access waiver and other necessary medical services [1]. |
| **Skills Training** | Training provided to the member or their caregiver to acquire, maintain, or improve skills necessary to live in the community [1]. |
| **Wellness Programs** | Evidence-based programs like **Living Well for Better Health** and **Matter of Balance**, aimed at improving self-management of chronic conditions and reducing the risk of falls [1]. |

### The Participant-Directed Option (PDO)

The PDO is a key feature of Section 19, offering members maximum control over their long-term care services.

*   **Member as Employer:** Under the PDO, the member (or their Representative) acts as the employer, responsible for recruiting, hiring, training, supervising, and dismissing their Attendants. This level of control allows for a highly personalized care arrangement.
*   **Fiscal Intermediary Support:** The member is required to use a Fiscal Intermediary to handle the administrative and financial burdens of being an employer, such as processing payroll, managing tax withholdings, and ensuring compliance with state and federal labor laws [1].
*   **Eligibility for PDO:** To access the PDO, the member must meet additional requirements, including demonstrating the cognitive capacity to manage the responsibilities of an employer or appointing a capable Representative [1].

## Other MaineCare Programs and Services

Beyond the Section 19 waiver, MaineCare offers a variety of other programs and services that may be relevant to older adults and individuals with disabilities:

*   **Nursing Facility Services:** MaineCare covers the cost of skilled nursing care for eligible individuals who require an institutional level of care.
*   **Home Health Services (State Plan):** Medically necessary home health services, including nursing and home health aide services, are covered under the standard MaineCare State Plan.
*   **Prescription Medications:** MaineCare provides coverage for a wide range of prescription drugs.
*   **Acute Care Services:** Coverage includes inpatient and outpatient hospital services, as well as services provided by physicians and other licensed practitioners.

## Conclusion

MaineCare, through the Office of MaineCare Services, is the cornerstone of long-term care and health coverage for many residents of Maine. The **Home and Community Benefits for the Elderly and Adults with Disabilities (Section 19)** waiver is a robust program that offers a person-centered approach, providing a wide range of services to support community living. Understanding the complex financial and medical eligibility requirements, the comprehensive list of covered services, and the empowering option for participant-directed care is essential for navigating the long-term care landscape in Maine. The program's commitment to person-centered planning ensures that services are not just provided, but are tailored to the unique life and goals of each MaineCare member.
