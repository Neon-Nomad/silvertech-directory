# North Dakota Long-Term Care Complaint Process: Reporting Concerns and Ensuring Quality Care

Ensuring the safety and quality of care for residents in North Dakota's long-term care facilities is a shared responsibility involving state agencies, residents, families, and the public. North Dakota has established clear and accessible channels for reporting concerns, abuse, or neglect in facilities such as nursing homes, basic care facilities, and assisted living homes.

This comprehensive guide outlines the primary avenues for filing a complaint in North Dakota, detailing the roles of the key state agencies involved in the investigation and resolution process, and providing detailed steps for reporting.

## I. Primary Complaint Authorities in North Dakota

In North Dakota, complaints against long-term care facilities are primarily handled by three distinct, yet complementary, state entities, all working under the umbrella of the state government: the **Health Facilities Unit (HFU)**, the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program**, and **Adult Protective Services (APS)**.

### A. Health Facilities Unit (HFU)

The HFU is the state's regulatory authority responsible for licensing and certifying health care facilities. The HFU investigates complaints related to violations of state licensing regulations and federal certification requirements (for Medicare/Medicaid facilities) [1].

#### What to Report to the HFU:

The HFU is the appropriate contact for complaints concerning:

*   **Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation:** Any instance of physical, sexual, verbal, or mental abuse, or financial exploitation of a resident.
*   **Quality of Care:** Concerns about inadequate medical care, medication errors, poor wound care, or insufficient staffing levels.
*   **Environmental Safety:** Issues related to the physical environment, such as fire safety, sanitation, or structural hazards.
*   **Regulatory Violations:** Any suspected violation of state licensing laws or federal Medicare/Medicaid conditions of participation.

#### How to File a Complaint with the HFU:

1.  **Online Complaint Form:** The most direct method is to use the **Health Facility Program Complaint Intake Form** available on the ND DHHS website [2].
2.  **Phone:** Concerns can be reported directly to the Health Facilities Unit by phone at **(701) 328-2352** [3].
3.  **Mail:** Written complaints can be sent to the Health Facilities Unit's mailing address.

**Confidentiality:** Complainants can choose to remain anonymous. The HFU is required to keep the identity of the complainant confidential unless the complainant gives permission to release their name or a court order requires disclosure.

#### The HFU Investigation Process:

Upon receiving a complaint, the HFU will:

1.  **Triage and Prioritize:** Complaints alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are investigated within two working days.
2.  **Unannounced Investigation:** An unannounced on-site investigation is conducted by state surveyors. This involves interviewing residents, staff, and family members, observing care, and reviewing medical records and facility policies.
3.  **Enforcement:** If the investigation substantiates the complaint, the facility is cited for deficiencies, and enforcement actions, such as civil monetary penalties or license revocation, may be imposed.

### B. Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program

The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program** is an independent advocate that works to resolve complaints on behalf of residents. The Ombudsman acts as a mediator and advocate, focusing on the resident's satisfaction [4].

#### What to Report to the Ombudsman:

The Ombudsman is the ideal resource for complaints concerning:

*   **Resident Rights:** Violations of a resident's rights, such as the right to privacy, the right to manage one's own finances, or the right to refuse treatment.
*   **Quality of Life:** Issues related to food quality, social activities, or a facility's failure to accommodate a resident's personal preferences.
*   **Communication Issues:** Problems with communication between the resident, family, and facility staff.

#### How to File a Complaint with the Ombudsman:

1.  **Online Complaint Form:** The **Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program Complaint Form (SFN 1829)** is available for online submission [5].
2.  **Phone:** Call the toll-free number **(855) 462-5465, select option 3**, or the direct line **(701) 328-4617** [4].

**Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman maintains strict confidentiality and will not proceed with an investigation or disclose the resident's or complainant's identity without their explicit consent.

## II. Reporting Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation

For the most serious concerns involving the mistreatment of a vulnerable adult, two specialized agencies must be contacted: Adult Protective Services and the Medicaid Fraud Control Unit.

### A. Adult Protective Services (APS)

**Adult Protective Services (APS)** is the state program responsible for receiving and investigating reports of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults who are unable to protect themselves [6].

#### Definition of a Vulnerable Adult:

In North Dakota, a **vulnerable adult** is defined as an individual eighteen years of age or older who is unable to protect themselves from abuse, neglect, or exploitation due to a physical or mental impairment or a dependency on others [7].

#### Types of Maltreatment Investigated by APS:

*   **Abuse:** The willful infliction of physical pain, injury, or mental anguish, or the deprivation of necessary services by a caretaker.
*   **Neglect:** The failure of a caretaker to provide necessary services, or the failure of a vulnerable adult to provide for their own needs (self-neglect).
*   **Exploitation:** The illegal or improper use of a vulnerable adult or their resources for another's profit or advantage.

#### How to Report to APS:

North Dakota has mandatory reporting requirements, meaning certain professionals (e.g., medical, mental health, law enforcement) are required by law to report suspected maltreatment. However, any person can and should report suspected abuse.

1.  **Contact Local Human Service Zone Office:** The most direct way to report is to contact the local Human Service Zone office in the county where the vulnerable adult resides.
2.  **State Reporting Form:** A dedicated form for reporting abuse and neglect of a vulnerable adult is available on the ND DHHS website [8].
3.  **Emergency:** For immediate danger, always call **911**.

### B. Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)

The **North Dakota Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU)**, part of the North Dakota Attorney General's Office, has a dual mission [9]:

1.  **Medicaid Provider Fraud:** Investigating and prosecuting health care providers who defraud the ND Medicaid Program through false claims, billing for services not rendered, or other fraudulent activities.
2.  **Abuse or Neglect:** Investigating and prosecuting abuse or neglect of residents in health care facilities that receive Medicaid funding.

#### What to Report to the MFCU:

*   **Fraud:** Suspected fraudulent billing or financial schemes by a facility or provider.
*   **Criminal Abuse/Neglect:** Cases of abuse or neglect that rise to the level of a criminal offense.

The MFCU works closely with the HFU and local law enforcement to address these serious matters.

## IV. Resident Rights and the Complaint Process

North Dakota law mandates that all long-term care facilities must have a process for handling complaints made by residents or on behalf of residents [10]. This internal process is the first step in resolving many issues.

1.  **Internal Grievance:** Residents are encouraged to first discuss their concerns with the facility's staff, the Director of Nursing, or the Administrator.
2.  **External Reporting:** If the internal process fails, or if the concern is serious (e.g., abuse, neglect), the resident or family should immediately contact one of the external authorities listed above.
3.  **Protection Against Retaliation:** North Dakota law strictly prohibits retaliation against a resident who files a complaint or participates in an investigation. The Ombudsman and HFU are committed to protecting residents from any adverse action taken by a facility in response to a complaint.

## Conclusion

The North Dakota long-term care complaint process is structured to provide multiple layers of protection for residents. Whether the concern is a regulatory violation requiring an official investigation by the HFU, a quality-of-life issue best resolved through advocacy by the Ombudsman, or a serious case of abuse or fraud requiring the intervention of APS or the MFCU, residents and their families have clear, confidential, and free resources available. By understanding and utilizing these official channels, the public helps ensure that North Dakota's long-term care facilities are held to the highest standards of safety, quality, and compassion.
