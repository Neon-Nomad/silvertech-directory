Navigating the complex landscape of long-term care, healthcare benefits, and regulatory oversight in Georgia requires access to accurate and authoritative contact information. This comprehensive guide provides verified contact details for the key state agencies and programs responsible for licensing facilities, administering Medicaid, protecting vulnerable adults, and supporting veterans. These agencies serve as the primary resources for residents, family members, and caregivers seeking information, filing complaints, or applying for essential services.

## 1. Georgia Department of Community Health (DCH)

The **Georgia Department of Community Health (DCH)** is the state agency that administers the Georgia Medicaid program, known as **Georgia Medicaid** [1]. DCH is responsible for determining eligibility, managing the state's healthcare programs, and overseeing the Care Management Organizations (CMOs) that deliver services to most Medicaid members.

### Role and Scope of Contact

Contacting the DCH is essential for issues related to Medicaid enrollment, benefits, and policy.

*   **Eligibility and Enrollment:** Inquiries about the application process, eligibility criteria for various programs (like the Elderly and Disabled Waiver Program - EDWP), and status updates on applications.
*   **Benefit Coverage:** Questions regarding what services are covered by Georgia Medicaid, including long-term services and supports (LTSS).
*   **Managed Care:** Information about the Georgia Families program and the CMOs (Amerigroup, CareSource, Peach State Health Plan) that manage care for most beneficiaries.
*   **Policy and Administration:** General questions about the administration of the state's healthcare programs.

## 2. Healthcare Facility Regulation Division (HFRD)

The **Healthcare Facility Regulation Division (HFRD)** is a division within the DCH and serves as the state's primary licensing and regulatory body for healthcare facilities, including nursing homes, assisted living communities, and personal care homes [2].

### Role and Scope of Contact

HFRD is the authority for all matters concerning facility compliance, licensing, and the investigation of health and safety complaints.

*   **Licensing Verification:** Contact HFRD to verify the current license status of any long-term care facility in Georgia.
*   **Regulatory Complaints:** HFRD is the correct agency to report violations of state and federal regulations, such as poor sanitation, inadequate staffing, medication errors, and unsafe environments.
*   **Inspection Reports:** While HFRD provides public access to inspection reports through its **GaMap2Care®-Find a Facility** tool, direct contact can be made for specific inquiries or to file an Open Records Request for detailed documents.

**When to use the Complaint Hotline (800-878-6442):** This dedicated line should be used to report serious concerns that may constitute a violation of licensing standards, particularly those that pose a risk to the health and safety of residents. The identity of the complainant is kept confidential.

## 3. Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

The **Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman (OSLTCO)** is an independent advocate for residents of long-term care facilities, including nursing homes and personal care homes [3]. The program is administered by the Georgia Department of Human Services, Division of Aging Services (DAS).

### Role and Scope of Contact

The Ombudsman's role is to resolve problems and advocate for the rights of residents. They are the best contact for non-regulatory issues and disputes.

*   **Resident Rights Violations:** Concerns about a resident's right to privacy, dignity, or freedom from abuse (which they will refer to the appropriate investigative body).
*   **Quality of Life Issues:** Complaints about food, laundry, activities, or facility environment.
*   **Disputes:** Issues with facility staff, administration, or conflicts regarding care planning.
*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman operates with strict confidentiality and will only proceed with a complaint with the resident's consent.

## 4. Adult Protective Services (APS)

**Adult Protective Services (APS)** is the state entity responsible for investigating reports of abuse, neglect, and exploitation of older persons (age 65 and older) and adults with disabilities (age 18 and older) who are not residing in a licensed long-term care facility [4].

### Role and Scope of Contact

APS is the critical contact for suspected criminal or severe non-facility-based mistreatment.

*   **Abuse:** Physical, emotional, or sexual mistreatment.
*   **Neglect:** Failure of a caregiver to provide necessary care, or self-neglect where the adult is unable to provide for their own needs.
*   **Exploitation:** Illegal or improper use of an adult's funds, property, or resources.

**When to Call APS:** If you suspect a vulnerable adult living in the community (e.g., in their own home, a family member's home, or an unlicensed setting) is being abused, neglected, or financially exploited, you should immediately contact the APS hotline.

## 5. Georgia Department of Veterans Service (GDVS)

The **Georgia Department of Veterans Service (GDVS)** is the state agency dedicated to assisting veterans and their families in obtaining all federal and state benefits they are entitled to, including access to the state's two State War Veterans Homes [5].

### Role and Scope of Contact

The GDVS is the authoritative source for all state-level veterans' benefits.

*   **Benefit Claims:** Assistance with filing claims for federal VA benefits, including disability compensation, pension, and education benefits.
*   **State Benefits:** Information on Georgia-specific benefits, such as the Disabled Veteran Homestead Tax Exemption, military retirement income tax exemptions, and vehicle tax exemptions.
*   **State Veterans Homes:** Inquiries regarding eligibility and admission to the Georgia War Veterans Nursing Home (Augusta) and the Georgia War Veterans Home (Milledgeville).
*   **Veteran Service Officers (VSOs):** Connecting veterans with accredited VSOs for free, professional claims assistance.

## Conclusion: Utilizing the Right Authority

The key to successfully navigating Georgia's support systems is to match the nature of your need to the correct authority.

*   **For questions about Medicaid eligibility or benefits:** Contact the **DCH** at (877) 423-4746.
*   **For health and safety violations in a licensed facility:** Use the **HFRD Complaint Hotline** at (800) 878-6442.
*   **For resident rights issues or disputes in a facility:** Contact the **Ombudsman** at (866) 552-4464.
*   **For suspected abuse or neglect in the community:** Call **Adult Protective Services (APS)** at (866) 552-4464 (Option 3).
*   **For veterans' benefits and services:** Contact the **GDVS** at (404) 656-2300.

By leveraging these verified authority contacts, Georgia residents can ensure they have the necessary resources to secure quality care, protect their rights, and access the benefits they are entitled to.
