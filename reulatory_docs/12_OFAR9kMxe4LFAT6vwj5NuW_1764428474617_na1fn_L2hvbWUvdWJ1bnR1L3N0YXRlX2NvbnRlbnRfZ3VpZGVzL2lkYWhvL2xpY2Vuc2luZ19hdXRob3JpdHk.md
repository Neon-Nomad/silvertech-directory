## Introduction to the Licensing Authority

In Idaho, the primary agency responsible for the licensing, certification, and oversight of long-term care facilities is the **Bureau of Facility Standards (BFS)**, a division within the **Idaho Department of Health and Welfare (DHW)** [1]. The Bureau's mission is to serve and protect Idahoans who require health-related services, supports, and supervision in care facilities. This is achieved through a rigorous process of setting standards, conducting inspections, and enforcing compliance with both state rules and federal regulations.

The Bureau of Facility Standards plays a critical role in ensuring that facilities meet minimum health, safety, and quality-of-care standards. For consumers, understanding the role of the BFS is the first step in making informed decisions about long-term care options in the state.

## Facilities Under the Bureau's Jurisdiction

The Bureau of Facility Standards licenses and certifies a wide range of healthcare providers. For long-term care consumers, the most relevant facilities under their oversight include:

### 1. Nursing Facilities (NFs)

Nursing Facilities, often referred to as nursing homes, provide the highest level of long-term care outside of a hospital. They offer 24-hour skilled nursing care, rehabilitation services, and assistance with all activities of daily living (ADLs).

*   **Governing Rules:** The operation and licensing of Nursing Facilities are primarily governed by **IDAPA 16.03.10, "Medicaid Enhanced Plan Benefits"** and related sections of the Idaho Administrative Code [2]. These rules incorporate federal requirements for facilities that participate in Medicare and Medicaid programs.
*   **Oversight:** The BFS conducts regular surveys to determine compliance with federal and state regulations. These surveys are typically unannounced and occur at least once every 15 months.

### 2. Residential Assisted Living Facilities (RALFs)

Residential Assisted Living Facilities (RALFs) are non-medical residential settings that provide personal care services, supervision, and assistance with ADLs for residents who do not require the 24-hour skilled nursing care of a nursing home. Idaho's RALFs are designed to promote choice, dignity, and independence for residents [3].

*   **Governing Rules:** The licensing and operation of RALFs are governed by **IDAPA 16.03.22, "Rules for Licensed Residential or Assisted Living Facilities in Idaho"** [3]. These rules set standards for the physical environment, staffing, resident rights, and the scope of services that can be provided.
*   **Levels of Care:** RALFs are licensed to provide different levels of care, and the rules dictate the specific services, such as medication administration, that can be provided by facility staff, often requiring a licensed professional nurse to oversee medication regimens [4].

### 3. Certified Family Homes (CFHs)

Certified Family Homes are smaller, residential settings that provide care for one or two adults who are unable to reside on their own and require help with ADLs [5]. These homes are regulated separately but fall under the general oversight of the DHW.

## The Licensing and Certification Process

The process by which the Bureau of Facility Standards ensures quality care is multi-faceted, involving initial licensing, regular inspections, and complaint investigations.

### Initial Licensure

Before a new facility can open its doors, it must undergo a rigorous initial licensing process. This typically involves:

1.  **Application Submission:** The provider must submit a comprehensive application to the BFS, including detailed plans of operation, staffing plans, and financial information.
2.  **Plan Review:** For new construction or major renovations, the facility's blueprints and fire safety plans must be reviewed and approved by the BFS and the State Fire Marshal's office [1].
3.  **Initial Survey:** An on-site inspection is conducted to verify that the physical plant meets all construction, life safety, and health standards, and that the facility is prepared to operate in compliance with all rules.

### Inspections and Surveys

The BFS conducts two main types of inspections, known as surveys, to monitor compliance:

1.  **Standard Surveys:** These are routine, unannounced inspections conducted to assess a facility's compliance with all state and federal regulations. For Medicare/Medicaid certified facilities, these surveys occur on a cycle that averages 12 months, but no longer than 15 months [6].
2.  **Complaint Investigations:** The BFS is responsible for investigating all complaints received regarding licensed facilities. These investigations are also unannounced and are prioritized based on the severity of the alleged violation.

During a survey, inspectors review resident care plans, medical records, observe staff-resident interactions, interview residents and staff, and inspect the physical environment.

### Enforcement and Penalties

When a facility is found to be non-compliant with regulations, the BFS has a range of enforcement actions it can take, depending on the severity and scope of the deficiencies. These actions can include:

*   **Written Notices of Deficiency:** Requiring the facility to submit a plan of correction (POC) within a specified timeframe.
*   **Civil Monetary Penalties (CMPs):** Fines imposed for serious or repeated violations.
*   **Directed Plan of Correction:** Requiring the facility to take specific actions to correct deficiencies.
*   **Temporary Management:** Appointing a temporary manager to oversee the facility's operations.
*   **License Suspension or Revocation:** The most severe penalty, resulting in the facility being unable to operate.

## Accessing Inspection Reports and Consumer Information

Transparency is a key component of the licensing authority's function. Consumers have the right to access information about a facility's compliance history.

*   **Online Search:** The DHW often provides an online portal or link where consumers can search for licensed facilities and view their recent survey results and complaint investigation findings [7]. This is a crucial resource for comparing the quality of care between different providers.
*   **Facility Disclosure:** Licensed facilities are typically required to make their most recent inspection reports available to the public upon request.

Consumers are strongly encouraged to review a facility's inspection history before making a placement decision. A history of repeated, serious deficiencies can be a significant indicator of poor quality of care.

## Key Idaho Administrative Rules (IDAPA)

The Idaho Administrative Code (IDAPA) contains the specific rules that govern the operation of licensed facilities. Understanding these rules provides insight into the standards facilities must meet.

| Facility Type | Key IDAPA Rule | Focus of the Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nursing Facilities** | IDAPA 16.03.10 | Standards for Medicaid-certified nursing facility services, including resident rights, quality of care, and physical environment. |
| **Residential Assisted Living Facilities (RALF)** | IDAPA 16.03.22 | Licensing requirements, resident admission and retention criteria, staffing, medication management, and physical plant standards for assisted living. |
| **Certified Family Homes** | IDAPA 16.03.19 | Rules governing the licensing and operation of small, non-medical residential care homes. |
