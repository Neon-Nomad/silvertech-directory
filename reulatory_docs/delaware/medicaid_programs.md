# Delaware Medicaid Programs: Comprehensive Guide to the Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP) and Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus)

The **Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP)**, administered by the Delaware Department of Health and Social Services, Division of Medicaid and Medical Assistance (DMMA), serves as the state’s primary vehicle for providing essential healthcare services to its most vulnerable residents [1]. This comprehensive program is a joint federal and state initiative designed to ensure that low-income families, children, pregnant women, the elderly, and individuals with disabilities have access to necessary medical care. A critical component of Delaware’s Medicaid system, particularly for long-term care, is the **Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus)**, a mandatory managed care program that integrates both acute and long-term services and supports (LTSS) [2].

This guide provides an in-depth look at the structure, eligibility requirements, covered services, and application process for Medicaid in Delaware, with a specific focus on the DSHP-Plus program for long-term care.

## The Foundation: Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP)

DMAP is more than just a health insurance program; it is a vital safety net that covers a broad spectrum of medical services. The program is governed by federal guidelines but is tailored and administered at the state level by the DMMA.

### Covered Services Under DMAP

The standard DMAP benefit package is extensive, covering most medically necessary services. These typically include:

*   **Inpatient and Outpatient Hospital Services:** Coverage for hospital stays, surgeries, and emergency room visits.
*   **Physician and Clinic Services:** Routine doctor visits, specialist consultations, and primary care.
*   **Prescription Drugs:** Coverage for necessary medications, often managed through a preferred drug list.
*   **Laboratory and X-ray Services:** Diagnostic testing and imaging.
*   **Transportation:** Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) to and from medical appointments.
*   **Mental Health and Substance Abuse Services:** Comprehensive behavioral health treatment.
*   **Preventive Care:** Routine shots for children, immunizations, and wellness screenings.
*   **Adult Dental Services:** Delaware has expanded dental coverage for adults, a significant benefit often excluded in other state Medicaid programs [1].

### General Eligibility Criteria

Delaware’s Medicaid eligibility is determined by a combination of factors, including income, household size, and specific categories of need (e.g., age, disability, pregnancy). A notable feature of the Delaware program is its more generous resource policy for many categories.

> "You can have a car, bank account, and a home and still qualify for Medicaid. DMMA does not look at any of your resources when determining your eligibility for Medicaid." [3]

While this statement is generally true for the Affordable Care Act (ACA) expansion group and other non-long-term care categories, it is crucial to understand that **resource limits still apply for individuals seeking long-term care services**, such as nursing home care or Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) through DSHP-Plus.

## Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus): Integrated Long-Term Care

The **Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus)** is Delaware’s mandatory managed care program for individuals who require long-term services and supports (LTSS). It represents a significant shift towards integrating acute medical care with long-term care services, aiming to provide a more coordinated and person-centered system of care [2].

### The Purpose and Structure of DSHP-Plus

DSHP-Plus was developed in partnership between the DMMA and the Division of Services for Aging and Adults with Physical Disabilities (DSAAPD). Its primary goals are to:

1.  **Integrate Care:** Combine physical health, behavioral health, and long-term care services under a single managed care organization (MCO).
2.  **Expand Access to HCBS:** Increase the availability and flexibility of Home and Community-Based Services to allow more individuals to receive care in their homes or communities rather than in institutions.
3.  **Improve Coordination:** Ensure better communication and coordination among providers, resulting in higher quality care and better health outcomes.
4.  **Control Costs:** More effectively manage the rising costs associated with long-term care [2].

Individuals enrolled in DSHP-Plus receive their services through one of the state’s contracted MCOs, such as AmeriHealth Caritas Delaware or Highmark Health Options. These MCOs are responsible for arranging and managing all covered services, including LTSS.

### DSHP-Plus Eligibility for Long-Term Care

Eligibility for DSHP-Plus is a two-part process: an individual must meet both the **medical (functional)** and **financial** criteria for long-term care Medicaid.

#### 1. Medical (Functional) Eligibility: Level of Care

To qualify for LTSS through DSHP-Plus, an applicant must demonstrate a medical need for a **Skilled or Intermediate Level of Care** [4]. This determination is made through a comprehensive assessment process, often referred to as a Pre-Admission Screening (PAS) or Level of Care (LOC) assessment, conducted by a qualified healthcare professional.

The criteria for this level of care are defined in the Delaware Administrative Code and generally indicate that the individual requires the type of care and supervision typically provided in a nursing facility due to chronic illness, disability, or cognitive impairment.

#### 2. Financial Eligibility (2025 Estimates)

Unlike the broader DMAP categories, long-term care Medicaid has strict financial limits that applicants must meet. These limits are subject to change annually, but the following figures provide a close estimate for 2025 [5] [6]:

| Category | Income Limit (Monthly) | Asset Limit (Liquid Resources) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single Applicant** | $2,417.50 (250% of SSI) | $2,000.00 |
| **Married Applicant (Both Spouses Applying)** | $4,835.00 (Combined) | $3,000.00 (Combined) |
| **Married Applicant (One Spouse Applying)** | $2,417.50 (Applicant) | $2,000.00 (Applicant) |

**Key Financial Considerations:**

*   **Income:** The income limit for long-term care is tied to 250% of the Federal Poverty Level (FPL) or the Supplemental Security Income (SSI) standard. Applicants whose income exceeds this limit may still qualify by establishing a **Qualified Income Trust (QIT)**, also known as a Miller Trust, to deposit their excess income [5].
*   **Assets (Resources):** The asset limit is strictly enforced for long-term care. Countable assets include bank accounts, stocks, bonds, and other liquid resources. Exempt assets typically include the primary residence (up to a certain equity limit), one vehicle, household goods, and personal belongings.
*   **Spousal Impoverishment Rules:** For married couples where only one spouse is applying for long-term care Medicaid, federal rules protect the non-applicant spouse (Community Spouse) from becoming impoverished.
    *   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The Community Spouse is permitted to keep a portion of the couple's combined countable assets, up to a maximum limit (e.g., $157,920 in 2025) [5].
    *   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The Community Spouse is entitled to a minimum monthly income (e.g., $2,643.75/month in 2025). If the Community Spouse's own income falls below this amount, a portion of the applicant's income can be allocated to the Community Spouse to meet this minimum [6].

### Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS) Covered by DSHP-Plus

DSHP-Plus covers a comprehensive range of LTSS, providing flexibility for individuals to choose the setting that best meets their needs.

#### 1. Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS)

HCBS are designed to support individuals who meet the nursing facility level of care but prefer to remain in their homes or communities. Services provided under the DSHP-Plus HCBS component may include:

*   **Personal Care Services:** Assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, grooming, and toileting.
*   **Home Health Services:** Skilled nursing care, physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy provided in the home.
*   **Adult Day Services:** Structured programs in a community setting that provide health, social, and therapeutic activities.
*   **Respite Care:** Temporary relief for primary caregivers.
*   **Environmental Modifications:** Changes to the home to improve accessibility, such as ramps and grab bars.
*   **Personal Emergency Response Systems (PERS):** Devices that allow an individual to call for help in an emergency.
*   **Care Management/Coordination:** A dedicated care manager works with the individual, family, and providers to develop and monitor a person-centered service plan [2].

#### 2. Nursing Facility Care

For individuals whose medical needs cannot be safely met in a home or community setting, DSHP-Plus covers the cost of care in a Medicaid-certified nursing facility. This includes room and board, skilled nursing care, rehabilitation services, and medications.

Once a Medicaid recipient is admitted to a nursing facility, their income (minus a small personal needs allowance, typically $75.00 per month) must be contributed toward the cost of their care. Medicaid then pays the remaining balance [4].

## The Application Process for DMAP and DSHP-Plus

Delaware utilizes a streamlined process for applying for health and social services benefits.

### How to Apply

Applicants can apply for DMAP and DSHP-Plus through several methods:

1.  **Online (Preferred):** The most common and efficient method is through the **Delaware ASSIST** online portal, which allows applicants to apply for various health and social service programs simultaneously [7].
2.  **By Phone:** Applicants can call the Medicaid Customer Relations unit at **(800) 372-2022** or **(302) 255-9500** to request an application be mailed to them.
3.  **In-Person:** Applications can be submitted at local Division of Social Services (DSS) offices.

### The Role of the Health Benefits Manager (HBM)

Once an individual is determined eligible for Medicaid, they must choose a Managed Care Organization (MCO) under the Diamond State Health Plan or DSHP-Plus. The **Health Benefits Manager (HBM)** assists new enrollees in selecting an MCO and understanding their benefits and prescriptions. The HBM can be reached at **(800) 996-9969** [1].

## Conclusion

The Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP) and its integrated long-term care component, the Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus), provide a robust and essential system of healthcare and long-term support for Delaware residents. By integrating acute and long-term care services under a managed care model, Delaware aims to deliver high-quality, coordinated, and cost-effective care. Understanding the specific eligibility pathways, particularly the financial and medical requirements for DSHP-Plus, is the first critical step for individuals and families seeking comprehensive long-term care coverage in the state.

## The Evolution of Integrated Care: The Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus) in Context

The transition to the DSHP-Plus model was a significant undertaking for Delaware, reflecting a national trend toward managed long-term services and supports (MLTSS). Understanding the history and the current operational structure of this program is essential for grasping its impact on beneficiaries.

### Historical Context: The 1115 Demonstration Waiver

The DSHP-Plus program operates under a Section 1115 Demonstration Waiver granted by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) [8]. These waivers allow states to test new approaches in their Medicaid programs that are not otherwise permitted under federal rules, provided they are budget-neutral and advance the objectives of the Medicaid program.

Delaware's initial Diamond State Health Plan (DSHP) 1115 Demonstration Waiver was approved in 1995, primarily to mandate enrollment into managed care organizations (MCOs) for acute care services and to achieve cost efficiencies. The DSHP-Plus component, which specifically addresses long-term care, was a later amendment and expansion of this waiver, designed to address the growing need for coordinated LTSS. The move was driven by the recognition that institutional care was costly and that many individuals preferred to receive care in their homes and communities. The DSHP-Plus initiative was a direct response to this, aiming to rebalance the state's long-term care system [2].

### Managed Care Organizations (MCOs) in DSHP-Plus

The DSHP-Plus program is delivered through a network of contracted Managed Care Organizations (MCOs). These private health plans are responsible for managing and delivering the full spectrum of covered services—from primary care and hospital visits to home health and nursing facility care—for their enrolled members. The MCOs currently contracted with the DMMA to provide DSHP-Plus services include:

The MCOs are tasked with several critical functions under DSHP-Plus:

1.  **Care Coordination:** Each member is assigned a care coordinator who helps navigate the healthcare system, develops a person-centered service plan, and ensures all medical and long-term care needs are met.
2.  **Provider Network Management:** MCOs contract with doctors, hospitals, specialists, and LTSS providers (e.g., home health agencies, adult day centers) to ensure an adequate network of care is available to members.
3.  **Utilization Management:** They review and authorize the use of LTSS to ensure services are medically necessary and appropriate for the member's needs.
4.  **Grievance and Appeals:** MCOs must maintain a formal process for members to file grievances or appeal decisions regarding their care or services.

### The Importance of the Person-Centered Service Plan (PCSP)

A cornerstone of the DSHP-Plus program is the **Person-Centered Service Plan (PCSP)**. This plan is developed collaboratively by the member, their family or representative, the MCO's care coordinator, and other relevant healthcare professionals. The PCSP is not a static document; it is a dynamic blueprint that:

*   Identifies the member's strengths, preferences, goals, and needs.
*   Details the specific mix of medical and long-term services and supports required.
*   Specifies the frequency, scope, and duration of each service.
*   Outlines the natural supports (family, friends, community resources) that will be utilized.

The PCSP ensures that the care provided is not only clinically sound but also aligns with the member's personal choices and quality of life goals, a fundamental principle of modern long-term care delivery [2].
