# Vermont Veterans Benefits: A Comprehensive Guide to State and Federal Support for Long-Term Care and Well-being

The state of Vermont honors the service and sacrifice of its military personnel and their families by providing a range of benefits and support programs. These benefits are designed to assist veterans in areas critical to their well-being, including healthcare, long-term care, financial assistance, and burial services. The central coordinating body for these resources is the **Vermont Office of Veterans Affairs (VOVA)** [1].

This guide provides an in-depth look at the key benefits available to Vermont veterans, with a particular focus on long-term care options, including the **Vermont Veterans' Home (VVH)**, and the essential role of the VOVA in navigating the complex landscape of state and federal entitlements.

## The Vermont Office of Veterans Affairs (VOVA)

The VOVA serves as the primary advocate and resource center for Vermont's veteran community. Its mission is to ensure that every veteran and their dependents are aware of and receive all federal and state benefits to which they are entitled.

### Key Services Provided by VOVA

The VOVA does not administer all benefits directly but acts as a crucial liaison and information hub. Their services include:

1.  **Benefit Counseling and Claims Assistance:** VOVA staff are trained to assist veterans and their families in filing claims for federal benefits with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), including disability compensation, pension, education, and healthcare [2].
2.  **Information and Referral:** They provide comprehensive information on state-specific benefits and refer veterans to the appropriate state agencies, non-profit organizations, and community resources.
3.  **Advocacy:** The office advocates for legislative and policy changes at the state level to improve the quality of life and access to services for veterans.

## Long-Term Care: The Vermont Veterans' Home (VVH)

The most significant state-operated long-term care resource for Vermont veterans is the **Vermont Veterans' Home (VVH)**, located in Bennington. The VVH is a state-owned and state-operated facility that provides high-quality, compassionate care in a setting that respects the dignity and service of its residents [3].

### Services Offered at the VVH

The VVH is a multi-level care facility, offering a continuum of services to meet the varying needs of its residents:

*   **Skilled Nursing Care:** Comprehensive 24-hour medical care for veterans with complex health conditions, requiring the services of licensed nurses and therapists.
*   **Residential Care:** A less intensive level of care for veterans who need assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs) but do not require the constant medical supervision of a skilled nursing unit.
*   **Rehabilitation Services:** Physical, occupational, and speech therapy services are available to help residents recover from illness, injury, or surgery.
*   **Specialized Dementia Care:** Dedicated units and programs are available for residents with Alzheimer's disease or other forms of dementia, providing a secure and therapeutic environment.

### Eligibility and Admission to the VVH

Admission to the Vermont Veterans' Home is based on specific criteria established by the state and federal VA.

| Eligibility Requirement | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Veteran Status** | Must be an honorably discharged Veteran who served at least **90 consecutive days on active duty** in the Armed Forces of the United States [4]. |
| **Residency** | Vermont residents have priority for admission. The VVH may also accept non-Vermont residents on a space-available basis, often with specific financial requirements related to their state's Medicaid program [5]. |
| **Medical Need** | The applicant must have a medical need for the level of care provided by the VVH (skilled nursing or residential care). |
| **Financial Requirements** | The VVH accepts various payment sources, including Medicare, Medicaid (Vermont and New York), VA per diem payments, and private pay. The VA may cover a significant portion of the cost for eligible veterans [6]. |

The VVH is certified by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs and the state's Division of Licensing and Protection, ensuring adherence to high standards of care and safety.

## State-Specific Financial and Tax Benefits

Vermont offers several financial and tax-related benefits that can significantly impact a veteran's financial stability, particularly when planning for long-term care costs.

### 1. Property Tax Exemption

Vermont provides a property tax exemption for certain disabled veterans and their surviving spouses.

*   **Eligibility:** The exemption is available to veterans who are determined by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs to be **totally disabled** as a result of service-connected disability [7].
*   **Benefit:** The exemption reduces the equalized education property value of the veteran's primary residence, thereby lowering their property tax liability. The amount of the exemption is subject to annual legislative review.

### 2. Income Tax Exemption

Vermont offers a full exemption from state income tax for military retirement pay.

*   **Eligibility:** All military retirement pay, including Survivor Benefit Plan (SBP) payments, is exempt from Vermont state income tax [8]. This is a substantial benefit that can increase the disposable income available for long-term care planning.

### 3. Motor Vehicle Benefits

*   **License Plates:** Veterans are eligible for special veteran license plates, including plates for disabled veterans, former prisoners of war, and recipients of certain military decorations.
*   **Registration Fee Exemption:** Certain disabled veterans may be eligible for an exemption from motor vehicle registration fees.

## Federal Benefits for Long-Term Care

While the VOVA assists with all federal claims, two federal VA programs are particularly relevant for long-term care planning: **Aid and Attendance** and **VA Health Care**.

### 1. VA Pension with Aid and Attendance (A&A)

The Aid and Attendance benefit is an increased monthly payment added to the VA's non-service-connected pension. It is designed to help veterans and surviving spouses pay for long-term care services.

*   **Eligibility:** The veteran must meet the basic eligibility requirements for the VA Pension (Wartime service, limited income/assets) and meet one of the following clinical criteria:
    *   Require the aid of another person to perform ADLs (e.g., bathing, feeding).
    *   Be confined to bed.
    *   Be a patient in a nursing home due to mental or physical incapacity.
    *   Have severely limited eyesight [9].
*   **Benefit:** The A&A benefit provides a tax-free monthly payment that can be used to pay for in-home care, assisted living, or nursing home care. The maximum benefit amount is adjusted annually.

### 2. VA Health Care System

Veterans enrolled in the VA health care system may be eligible for a range of long-term care services through the **Geriatrics and Extended Care (GEC)** program.

*   **Services:** GEC services can include:
    *   **Home-Based Primary Care (HBPC):** Care provided to veterans in their homes.
    *   **Skilled Home Health Care:** Short-term, intermittent skilled services.
    *   **Community Living Centers (VA Nursing Homes):** Long-term residential care provided directly by the VA.
    *   **Community Nursing Home Program:** Contracted care in private nursing homes paid for by the VA for eligible veterans [10].

The level of coverage and co-payments depend on the veteran's priority group, which is determined by factors such as service-connected disability status and income. Veterans with a service-connected disability rating of **70% or higher** are often eligible for nursing home care at no cost.

## Survivor and Burial Benefits

Vermont also ensures that the final honors for its veterans are handled with respect and that surviving family members receive necessary support.

### 1. State Veterans Cemetery

Vermont does not operate a state veterans cemetery, but the VOVA assists families in coordinating burial at the **Vermont Veterans Memorial Cemetery** in Randolph, which is a state-owned cemetery that provides burial plots for eligible veterans and their spouses [11].

### 2. Burial Benefits

*   **Headstones and Markers:** The VA provides a headstone, marker, or medallion for the grave of any deceased eligible veteran in any cemetery worldwide.
*   **Burial Flag:** A U.S. flag is provided to drape the casket or accompany the urn of a deceased veteran.
*   **Presidential Memorial Certificate:** A certificate expressing the nation's recognition of the veteran's service is available to the veteran's next of kin.

### 3. Survivor Benefits

The VOVA assists surviving spouses and dependent children in applying for federal VA benefits, including:

*   **Dependency and Indemnity Compensation (DIC):** A tax-free monetary benefit paid to eligible survivors of military service members who died on active duty or veterans whose death resulted from a service-related injury or disease.
*   **Survivors Pension:** A needs-based benefit for low-income surviving spouses and children of deceased wartime veterans.

## Conclusion: Maximizing Benefits Through VOVA

For Vermont veterans and their families, the **Vermont Office of Veterans Affairs (VOVA)** is the indispensable first point of contact for navigating the complex array of state and federal benefits. From securing a place in the **Vermont Veterans' Home** for long-term care to accessing financial relief through property and income tax exemptions, the VOVA provides the expertise and advocacy needed to maximize entitlements. By proactively engaging with the VOVA at **(888) 666-9844**, veterans can ensure they receive the full recognition and support they have earned through their service.
