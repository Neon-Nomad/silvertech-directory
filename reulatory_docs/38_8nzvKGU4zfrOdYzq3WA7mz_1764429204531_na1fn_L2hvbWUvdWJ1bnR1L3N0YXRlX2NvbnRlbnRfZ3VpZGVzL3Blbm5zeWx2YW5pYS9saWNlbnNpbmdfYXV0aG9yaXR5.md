# Pennsylvania Licensing Authority: A Comprehensive Guide to Long-Term Care Facility Regulation

In the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, the licensing and regulation of long-term care facilities are managed by two distinct state agencies: the **Department of Health (DOH)** and the **Department of Human Services (DHS)**. This dual-agency approach is critical for residents, families, and providers to understand, as the regulatory standards, inspection processes, and complaint procedures vary significantly depending on the type of facility.

This guide provides a detailed overview of the licensing authorities for Pennsylvania's long-term care facilities, including nursing homes, assisted living residences (ALRs), and personal care homes (PCHs), outlining the regulatory framework, inspection cycles, and resources for consumers.

## The Dual Regulatory System in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's system for regulating long-term care is structured around the level of medical care provided by the facility.

| Facility Type | Licensing Authority | Governing Regulations | Primary Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nursing Homes** | Department of Health (DOH) | 28 Pa. Code, Chapters 201-211 | Skilled nursing care, medical treatment, and rehabilitation. |
| **Assisted Living Residences (ALR)** | Department of Human Services (DHS) | 55 Pa. Code, Chapter 2800 | Personal care, supportive services, and "aging in place" with higher acuity. |
| **Personal Care Homes (PCH)** | Department of Human Services (DHS) | 55 Pa. Code, Chapter 2600 | Housing, meals, and personal assistance for residents who need minimal medical care. |

## 1. Licensing and Regulation of Nursing Homes

Nursing homes, also known as skilled nursing facilities, provide the highest level of medical care outside of a hospital. They are licensed and rigorously regulated by the Pennsylvania Department of Health (DOH).

### The Department of Health (DOH)

The **Department of Health's Division of Nursing Care Facilities** is the primary authority responsible for ensuring that all licensed nursing homes comply with state and federal regulations.

| DOH Nursing Home Licensing Key Contacts | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Licensing Agency** | Department of Health, Division of Nursing Care Facilities |
| **Official Website** | [https://www.pa.gov/agencies/health/facilities/in-patient-healthcare-facilities/nursing-homes/patients-and-families] [1] |
| **Complaint Hotline** | 1-800-254-5164 |

### Regulatory Framework: 28 Pa. Code

Nursing homes in Pennsylvania must adhere to the regulations outlined in **Title 28 of the Pennsylvania Code**, specifically Chapters 201 through 211, which cover areas such as:

*   **Administration and Staffing:** Requirements for administrators, nursing staff ratios, and qualifications.
*   **Patient Care:** Standards for medical, dental, and pharmaceutical services, as well as resident assessment and care planning.
*   **Physical Plant:** Rules governing the facility's structure, safety, and environmental conditions.
*   **Resident Rights:** Protections ensuring residents' dignity, privacy, and right to participate in their care decisions.

### The Inspection and Survey Process

The DOH conducts annual, unannounced inspections, often referred to as **surveys**, to monitor compliance.

*   **Frequency:** Surveys are typically conducted at least once every 15 months, with the average interval not exceeding 12 months.
*   **Process:** Survey teams, which include registered nurses and other healthcare professionals, spend several days on-site. They review medical records, interview residents and staff, and observe care delivery.
*   **Deficiencies and Sanctions:** If a facility is found to be non-compliant, the DOH issues a Statement of Deficiencies. The facility must submit a Plan of Correction (POC). Depending on the severity and scope of the deficiency, the DOH may impose sanctions, including civil monetary penalties, denial of payment, or, in extreme cases, license revocation.

### Accessing Inspection Reports

Transparency is a key component of the DOH's oversight. Families and prospective residents are strongly encouraged to review a facility's history of compliance.

> "You can ask to see a copy of a survey report at any nursing home or you can view the survey reports on our website. You can also review sanctions given to facilities on our website." [1]

Consumers can typically find this information through the DOH's online portal or the federal **Medicare's Care Compare** website, which aggregates state and federal inspection data.

## 2. Licensing and Regulation of Assisted Living and Personal Care Homes

Assisted Living Residences (ALRs) and Personal Care Homes (PCHs) are non-medical residential settings that provide housing, meals, and assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs). These facilities are licensed and regulated by the **Department of Human Services (DHS)**.

### The Department of Human Services (DHS)

The **DHS Office of Long-Term Living (OLTL), Bureau of Human Services Licensing (BHSL)**, is the authority responsible for the licensure and regulatory compliance of PCHs and ALRs.

| DHS PCH/ALR Licensing Key Contacts | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Licensing Agency** | Department of Human Services (DHS), Bureau of Human Services Licensing (BHSL) |
| **Official Website** | [https://www.pa.gov/agencies/dhs/resources/licensing/pch-alr-licensing] [2] |
| **Complaint Hotline** | 1-877-401-8835 |

### Personal Care Homes (PCH): 55 Pa. Code Chapter 2600

Personal Care Homes are designed for individuals who need assistance with daily living but do not require the continuous medical care of a nursing home.

*   **Regulatory Focus:** PCH regulations, found in **55 Pa. Code Chapter 2600** [3], emphasize the provision of a safe, humane, comfortable, and supportive residential setting.
*   **Services:** PCHs provide housing, food, supervision, and assistance with ADLs (e.g., bathing, dressing, mobility). They are generally not permitted to provide skilled nursing services.
*   **Staffing:** Regulations cover staff qualifications, training, and the requirement for a PCH administrator to meet specific educational and experience criteria.

### Assisted Living Residences (ALR): 55 Pa. Code Chapter 2800

Assisted Living Residences represent a newer, distinct category of long-term care in Pennsylvania, established to offer a higher level of care and greater privacy than PCHs.

*   **Regulatory Focus:** ALR regulations, found in **55 Pa. Code Chapter 2800** [4], are structured to support the concept of **"aging in place."** This means residents can remain in the ALR even as their care needs increase, potentially requiring more complex services than a PCH can offer.
*   **Key Distinctions:**
    *   **Private Living Units:** ALRs are required to provide private living units that include a lockable door, a private bathroom, and a kitchen capacity (e.g., a sink, refrigerator, and microwave).
    *   **Higher Acuity:** ALRs are permitted to care for residents with more significant health needs, including those who may require some intermittent skilled nursing services, provided the facility can safely meet those needs.
    *   **Service Plan:** The regulations mandate a comprehensive, individualized service plan that is regularly reviewed and updated to reflect the resident's changing condition.

### The Inspection and Monitoring Process for PCH/ALR

The BHSL is responsible for the inspection and monitoring of PCHs and ALRs.

*   **Licensing Inspections:** BHSL conducts initial inspections for new facilities and renewal inspections for existing ones.
*   **Complaint Investigations:** A significant part of BHSL's role is investigating complaints filed by residents, family members, or the public. These investigations are unannounced and can occur at any time.
*   **Public Access to Reports:** Inspection and investigation reports for PCHs and ALRs are made publicly available through the DHS Human Services Provider Directory Search Form [5]. Consumers can search by facility name, location, or license number to view compliance history.

## Consumer Resources for Choosing a Facility

Choosing the right long-term care facility is one of the most important decisions a family can make. Pennsylvania's licensing authorities provide several resources to aid in this process.

### DOH Resources for Nursing Homes

The DOH provides guidance specifically for consumers selecting a nursing home:

*   **"Your Guide to Choosing a Nursing Home or Other Long-Term Services and Supports"** [6]: This comprehensive guide offers a checklist, questions to ask during a visit, and an explanation of the different types of long-term care options available in the state.
*   **Nursing Home Quality Improvement Task Force:** The DOH has been actively implementing recommendations from this task force to improve the quality of life and care in nursing facilities, focusing on person-centered care models.

### DHS Resources for PCH and ALR

The DHS BHSL provides resources focused on the residential care sector:

*   **Regulatory Compliance Guides (RCGs):** The BHSL publishes RCGs for both Chapter 2600 (PCH) and Chapter 2800 (ALR) [7]. While primarily for providers, these guides offer families a clear, plain-language explanation of the regulations a facility must follow.
*   **ListServs:** The BHSL maintains listservs to communicate updates, regulatory changes, and training opportunities to providers, which can be a source of current information for advocates and families.

### The Importance of Licensing Status

A facility's licensing status is the most fundamental indicator of its compliance with state law.

*   **Licensed:** The facility meets all minimum state requirements.
*   **Provisional License:** Often issued to new facilities or those with a history of non-compliance, indicating a temporary status while the facility works to meet full requirements.
*   **Revocation/Suspension:** Indicates serious, systemic, or uncorrected violations that pose a threat to resident health and safety.

Families should always verify the current licensing status of any facility they are considering using the DHS Provider Directory or the DOH's public search tools.

### Summary of Key Findings

*   **Nursing Homes** are licensed by the **Department of Health (DOH)** and are subject to the most stringent medical regulations.
*   **Assisted Living Residences (ALR)** and **Personal Care Homes (PCH)** are licensed by the **Department of Human Services (DHS)**, Bureau of Human Services Licensing (BHSL).
*   The **ALR** license (Chapter 2800) is a newer standard designed to allow residents to **"age in place"** with higher care needs than a PCH (Chapter 2600).
*   The DOH complaint hotline for nursing homes is **1-800-254-5164**.
*   The DHS complaint hotline for PCH/ALR is **1-877-401-8835**.
