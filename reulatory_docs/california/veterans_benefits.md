# Veterans Benefits: Long-Term Care and Support for California's Heroes

California is home to one of the largest populations of veterans in the United States, and the state is committed to honoring their service by providing comprehensive support, particularly in the realm of long-term care. The **California Department of Veterans Affairs (CalVet)** is the primary state agency responsible for administering these benefits, with the **Veterans Homes of California (CalVet Homes)** standing out as a cornerstone of their long-term care offerings.

This guide provides a detailed overview of the long-term care benefits available to California veterans, focusing on the state-operated Veterans Homes, eligibility requirements, and the range of services provided.

## The California Department of Veterans Affairs (CalVet)

CalVet serves as the state's advocate and service provider for veterans, their dependents, and survivors. The department works to ensure that California veterans receive the federal and state benefits they have earned.

## The Veterans Homes of California (CalVet Homes)

California operates **eight** Veterans Homes across the state, offering affordable, high-quality long-term care to aged and disabled veterans, as well as their eligible spouses and domestic partners. These homes are designed to provide a continuum of care, allowing residents to age in place as their needs change.

### Locations of CalVet Homes

California's eight Veterans Homes are strategically located to serve veterans across the state:

1.  **Barstow**
2.  **Chula Vista**
3.  **Fresno**
4.  **Lancaster**
5.  **Redding**
6.  **Ventura**
7.  **West Los Angeles**
8.  **Yountville** (the largest and oldest)

### Levels of Care Provided

The CalVet Homes offer a range of care levels, depending on the specific facility, to meet the diverse needs of the veteran population:

| Level of Care | Description | Target Population |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domiciliary (Independent Living)** | Residential care for veterans who are largely self-sufficient but may need some supportive services. | Independent veterans who require minimal assistance. |
| **Assisted Living (Residential Care for the Elderly)** | Care for veterans who need assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs) but do not require 24-hour skilled nursing. | Veterans needing help with bathing, dressing, and medication management. |
| **Intermediate Care** | Care for veterans who require a higher level of medical supervision and assistance than assisted living, but not the intensive care of a skilled nursing facility. | Veterans with chronic conditions requiring regular monitoring. |
| **Skilled Nursing Care** | The highest level of care, providing 24-hour licensed nursing care for veterans with complex medical needs. | Veterans requiring ventilator care, post-operative recovery, or intensive medical management. |
| **Memory Care** | Specialized units within the homes dedicated to veterans with Alzheimer's disease or other forms of dementia. | Veterans requiring a secure, structured environment with specialized programming. |

### Eligibility for CalVet Homes

To be eligible for admission to a Veterans Home of California, an applicant must meet specific criteria:

1.  **Veteran Status:** Must be a veteran who served on active duty and was discharged under honorable conditions.
2.  **Age/Disability:** Must be 55 years of age or older, or be disabled.
3.  **Residency:** Must be a resident of California.
4.  **Medical Need:** Must require the level of care provided by the home (Domiciliary, Assisted Living, Intermediate, or Skilled Nursing).

**Spouses and Domestic Partners:** Eligible spouses and domestic partners of veterans may also be admitted to the homes, provided they meet certain age and residency requirements and the veteran is also a resident or would be eligible for residency.

### Cost of Care

The CalVet Homes are designed to be affordable. The cost of care is based on the veteran's income and the level of care they receive. The payment structure is a percentage of the resident's income, with rates varying by the level of care:

*   **Residential Care:** Approximately 47.5% of the resident's income.
*   **Assisted Living/Residential Care for the Elderly:** Approximately 55% of the resident's income.
*   **Intermediate Care:** Approximately 65% of the resident's income.
*   **Skilled Nursing Care:** The highest percentage, as this is the most intensive level of care.

The goal is to ensure that the veteran retains a sufficient amount of their income to meet personal and incidental needs.

## Federal VA Long-Term Care Benefits

In addition to the state-run CalVet Homes, California veterans are also eligible for a variety of federal long-term care benefits administered by the **U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)**.

### 1. VA Health Care System

The VA provides long-term care services through its own medical centers and community-based programs, including:

*   **Community Living Centers (VA Nursing Homes):** VA-operated facilities that provide skilled nursing care, rehabilitation, and specialized services.
*   **Home-Based Primary Care:** Health care services provided to veterans in their homes.
*   **Aid and Attendance and Housebound Benefits:** These are monetary benefits added to the VA Pension for veterans who require the aid of another person to perform daily activities or are largely confined to their homes. This benefit can be used to pay for in-home care, assisted living, or nursing home care.

### 2. Aid and Attendance Pension

The **Aid and Attendance** benefit is a non-service-connected disability pension that can significantly offset the cost of long-term care.

*   **Eligibility:** The veteran must meet service requirements (at least 90 days of active duty, with one day during a wartime period) and financial requirements (a net worth limit). They must also require the aid of another person for ADLs.
*   **Benefit Amount:** The benefit provides a monthly, tax-free payment that can be substantial, helping to make assisted living or in-home care financially feasible.

## County Veterans Service Officers (CVSOs)

A critical resource for California veterans is the network of **County Veterans Service Officers (CVSOs)**. These officers are trained and accredited to assist veterans and their families in applying for and obtaining federal and state benefits, including:

*   Filing claims for VA disability compensation and pension.
*   Assisting with applications for the CalVet Homes.
*   Connecting veterans with local resources for housing, employment, and healthcare.

Every county in California has a CVSO, and their services are provided free of charge.

## Conclusion

California's commitment to its veterans is evident in the comprehensive long-term care options provided through the **eight Veterans Homes of California** and the supportive services of the **California Department of Veterans Affairs (CalVet)**. By leveraging both state and federal benefits, including the VA's Aid and Attendance pension and the expertise of the **County Veterans Service Officers**, California veterans and their families can access the high-quality, affordable care they deserve.
