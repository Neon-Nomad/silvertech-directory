# Illinois Veterans Benefits for Long-Term Care: State Homes and Financial Aid

Illinois has a strong commitment to its veteran population, offering a comprehensive suite of benefits, particularly in the realm of long-term care. The state is unique in its dedication, operating **five State Veterans Homes** across Illinois, a high number compared to many other states [1]. These homes, along with various financial and supportive programs administered by the **Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs (IDVA)**, provide essential resources for veterans and their eligible spouses seeking skilled nursing, domiciliary, or assisted living care.

Understanding the specific benefits available through the IDVA and the federal Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is a crucial step for veterans and their families planning for long-term care needs.

---

## The Illinois State Veterans Homes

The IDVA operates five residential veterans homes, which offer high-quality, affordable long-term care services to eligible Illinois veterans and their spouses. These facilities provide a range of care levels, from skilled nursing to domiciliary care.

### Locations of Illinois Veterans Homes

The five State Veterans Homes are strategically located across the state to serve veterans in different regions:

1.  **Illinois Veterans Home at Anna**
2.  **Illinois Veterans Home at Chicago**
3.  **Illinois Veterans Home at LaSalle**
4.  **Illinois Veterans Home at Manteno**
5.  **Illinois Veterans Home at Quincy**

### Eligibility for State Veterans Homes

Admission to an Illinois Veterans Home is based on a specific set of criteria established by the IDVA:

*   **Veteran Status:** The applicant must be a veteran who served in the U.S. Armed Forces and received an honorable discharge.
*   **Residency:** The veteran must have entered the service from the state of Illinois or have been a resident of Illinois for one year immediately preceding the date of application [2].
*   **Medical Need:** The applicant must have a medical need for the level of care provided by the home (e.g., skilled nursing, intermediate care, or domiciliary care).
*   **Spouses:** The spouse of an eligible veteran may also be admitted, provided space is available and they meet the medical need criteria.

### Services Provided

The services offered at the Illinois Veterans Homes are comprehensive and designed to meet the holistic needs of the residents:

| Level of Care | Description of Services |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Skilled Nursing Care** | 24-hour nursing and medical care for residents with complex medical needs, including rehabilitation services. |
| **Intermediate Care** | Care for residents who require professional nursing supervision but not the intensity of skilled nursing. |
| **Domiciliary Care** | A form of assisted living for veterans who are ambulatory and able to care for most of their personal needs but require some supervision and assistance. |
| **Support Services** | Social services, recreational activities, dietary services, and spiritual support. |

The cost of care in a State Veterans Home is significantly lower than in a private facility, as the state subsidizes a large portion of the expense. The resident's contribution is typically based on their income and assets, ensuring the homes remain accessible to veterans of all financial backgrounds.

## Federal VA Benefits for Long-Term Care

In addition to the state-run homes, Illinois veterans can access several federal VA benefits that help cover the cost of long-term care, both in VA facilities and in the community.

### 1. VA Health Care System

The VA provides a range of long-term care services through its health care system, which may include:

*   **VA Community Living Centers (VA Nursing Homes):** Providing skilled nursing and rehabilitation care.
*   **Home-Based Primary Care:** Healthcare services delivered to the veteran's home.
*   **Aid and Attendance (A&A) Benefit:** A pension benefit for veterans and surviving spouses who require the aid of another person to perform daily activities.

### 2. Aid and Attendance (A&A) and Housebound Benefits

The A&A benefit is a non-service-connected pension that provides a monthly monetary supplement to eligible veterans and surviving spouses to help pay for the cost of long-term care, such as in-home care, assisted living, or nursing home care.

*   **Eligibility:** The veteran must meet the basic VA pension requirements (wartime service, income, and asset limits) and require assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) or be housebound.
*   **Financial Criteria:** The VA has a net worth limit (income plus assets) that is adjusted annually. For 2025, this limit is approximately **$159,240** [3].
*   **Purpose:** The benefit is designed to offset the costs of a caregiver or facility, making long-term care more affordable.

### 3. State Veterans Assistance Commission (VAC)

Illinois is home to a network of **Veterans Assistance Commissions (VACs)**, which are county-level organizations funded by local property taxes.

*   **Role:** VACs provide emergency financial assistance, temporary housing, and help with filing claims for state and federal benefits.
*   **Accessibility:** Every county in Illinois is required to have a VAC or be part of a multi-county VAC, making this a highly localized and accessible resource for veterans in need of immediate support.

## Financial Protections and Medicaid Interaction

For veterans who also qualify for Medicaid, Illinois has specific rules regarding the interaction of VA benefits and Medicaid eligibility.

### Veterans Benefit Disregard

Illinois Medicaid rules include a **Veterans Benefit Disregard** for nursing home residents. For a veteran without a spouse or child, or a surviving spouse without a child, the monthly VA benefits are limited to a $90 personal needs allowance (PNA) that is disregarded for Medicaid purposes [4]. This ensures that the veteran retains a small amount of their VA pension for personal use, while the rest is applied to the cost of their care, with Medicaid covering the remaining balance.

### Planning for Dual Eligibility

Many veterans are **dually eligible** for both VA health care and Medicaid. Strategic planning is often required to maximize the benefits from both programs:

*   **VA Health Care:** Provides comprehensive medical services.
*   **Medicaid:** Covers the cost of long-term care, particularly in a nursing home, once the veteran has spent down their income and assets to the Medicaid limit.
*   **Aid and Attendance:** Can be used to pay for assisted living or in-home care, which Medicaid may not fully cover, or to pay the room and board portion of the Supportive Living Program (SLP) Waiver.

## The Illinois Department of Veterans' Affairs (IDVA)

The IDVA is the state agency dedicated to assisting veterans and their families in securing the benefits they have earned. The IDVA provides:

*   **Service Officers:** Accredited Service Officers who can assist veterans in preparing and filing claims for federal and state benefits, including disability compensation, pension, and education benefits.
*   **Outreach:** Programs to connect with veterans, especially those in rural or underserved areas.
*   **Cemetery Services:** Operating state veterans' cemeteries.

The IDVA serves as the central hub for all state-level veteran support and is the first point of contact for veterans seeking admission to the State Veterans Homes.
