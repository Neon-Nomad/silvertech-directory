# Hawaii Medicaid Programs: A Comprehensive Guide to QUEST Integration and Long-Term Care

The State of Hawaii administers its Medicaid program, known as **QUEST Integration (QI)**, through the Department of Human Services (DHS) Med-QUEST Division (MQD). Unique among state Medicaid programs, QUEST Integration is a comprehensive managed care system authorized under a Section 1115 Demonstration Waiver from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS). This innovative approach integrates both acute medical care and Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS) into a single, seamless system, ensuring that eligible residents of all ages—including the aged, blind, and disabled—receive coordinated care [1].

This guide provides a detailed overview of the QUEST Integration program, focusing specifically on the eligibility requirements, the extensive range of LTSS benefits, and how the program supports Hawaii residents who require long-term care, including assisted living and in-home support.

## Understanding QUEST Integration: Hawaii's 1115 Demonstration Waiver

QUEST Integration is the latest evolution of Hawaii’s pioneering Medicaid managed care system, which began in 1994. The program operates under a **Section 1115 Demonstration Waiver**, which grants the state flexibility to test new approaches that improve health care access, quality, and efficiency. The core principle of QI is to deliver all Medicaid benefits—from doctor visits and hospital stays to long-term care—through contracted Managed Care Organizations (MCOs) [2].

The integration of LTSS into the managed care model is a defining feature of QUEST Integration. This structure is designed to promote person-centered care, allowing beneficiaries to receive services in the least restrictive setting possible, which often means remaining in their own homes or communities.

## Eligibility Requirements for Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS)

To qualify for LTSS through QUEST Integration, applicants must meet both **financial eligibility** and **functional eligibility** criteria.

### Financial Eligibility (Income and Asset Limits)

Financial eligibility for Medicaid in Hawaii is complex and depends on the applicant's specific category (e.g., Aged, Blind, or Disabled - ABD) and the pathway they use to qualify. The following limits are based on current 2025 estimates for individuals seeking long-term care [3]:

#### Income Limits

Hawaii is considered a "no hard income limit" state for institutional (nursing home) Medicaid, but it does have strict limits for community-based programs.

*   **Medically Needy Pathway:** For those whose income exceeds the standard limit, Hawaii offers a Medically Needy program, which allows applicants to "spend down" their excess income on medical expenses to qualify. The Medically Needy Income Limit (MNIL) is significantly lower than the Federal Poverty Level (FPL):
    *   **Individual MNIL:** \$469 per month
    *   **Couple MNIL:** \$632 per month
*   **Aged, Blind, and Disabled (ABD) Income Limit:** For those applying directly for community-based Medicaid, the income limit is approximately **\$1,500 per month** for a single applicant (effective February 1, 2025) [4].
*   **Nursing Home Medicaid:** There is no hard income cap. However, almost all of the applicant's income, minus a small Personal Needs Allowance (PNA) and funds for spousal support (if applicable), must be contributed toward the cost of care.

#### Asset Limits

The asset limit for an individual applicant for long-term care Medicaid in Hawaii is **\$2,000** [3]. This includes countable resources such as bank accounts, stocks, and bonds. Certain assets are considered exempt, including:
*   The applicant's primary residence (up to a certain equity limit, provided the applicant or their spouse lives there).
*   One vehicle.
*   Household goods and personal effects.
*   Life insurance policies with a low cash surrender value.

#### Spousal Impoverishment Rules

For married couples where only one spouse is applying for long-term care Medicaid (the "applicant spouse"), the non-applicant spouse (the "community spouse") is protected from impoverishment by federal rules. The community spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined assets and income:

*   **Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA):** The community spouse can retain a portion of the couple's joint assets, up to a maximum limit (which changes annually).
*   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** The community spouse is entitled to a minimum monthly income. If their own income falls below this threshold, they can receive a portion of the applicant spouse's income to meet the minimum [3].

### Functional Eligibility (Level of Care)

To be eligible for LTSS, whether in a nursing facility or through Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS), an applicant must meet the state's criteria for a **Nursing Facility Level of Care (NF-LOC)**.

*   **Assessment Tool:** The determination of NF-LOC is made through a clinical assessment process using the **DHS Form 1147 (Level of Care and At Risk Evaluation)** [5].
*   **Criteria:** The assessment evaluates the applicant's need for assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, and eating, as well as their need for skilled nursing services and supervision due to medical or cognitive conditions. Meeting the NF-LOC is the clinical gateway to accessing all long-term care benefits under QUEST Integration.

## Long-Term Services and Supports (LTSS)

The QUEST Integration program provides a comprehensive package of LTSS, which are delivered through the contracted MCOs. These services are designed to help members maintain their health, independence, and quality of life in the setting of their choice.

### Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS)

The HCBS component of QUEST Integration is crucial for supporting individuals who wish to remain in their homes or other community settings. The program covers a wide array of services, including the following key benefits [6]:

#### 1. Assisted Living Services
QUEST Integration covers personal care and supportive services for members residing in an assisted living facility (ALF). This is a significant benefit, as many state Medicaid programs only cover services in a nursing home setting. Covered services in an ALF include:
*   **Personal Care and Supportive Services:** Assistance with ADLs and Instrumental Activities of Daily Living (IADLs) such as homemaker services, chore services, personal care, and meal preparation.
*   **Community Care Management Agency (CCMA) Services:** Members must be enrolled with a CCMA, which provides continuous case management, ongoing assessments, service plan development, and 24/7 accessibility to the caregiver.

#### 2. Residential Care
The program also covers services in other community residential settings, which are often a more homelike alternative to a traditional nursing facility:
*   **Expanded Adult Residential Care Homes (E-ARCHs):** Licensed facilities that provide 24-hour care, supervision, and personal care services.
*   **Community Care Foster Family Homes (CCFFHs):** Certified private homes where a care provider lives and offers personal care, nursing, and medication oversight to a small number of residents.

#### 3. Personal Assistance Services (PAS)
PAS provides in-home support to help members with daily tasks. The services are tiered based on the level of need:
*   **Level I (Limited Assistance):** Provided to individuals needing help primarily with IADLs (e.g., light housekeeping, shopping, errands) to prevent a decline in health. This level is limited to approximately 10 hours per week and may be self-directed.
*   **Level II (Substantial Assistance):** Provided to individuals requiring moderate to total assistance with ADLs and health maintenance activities. Services are delivered by qualified personnel such as Home Health Aides (HHAs) or Certified Nurse Aides (CNAs) and may also be self-directed.

#### 4. Respite Care
Respite care is a vital service that provides temporary relief for the primary, unpaid caregiver. It can be provided on an hourly, daily, or overnight basis in various settings, including the member's home, a CCFFH, an E-ARCH, or a Medicaid-certified nursing facility [6].

#### 5. Other Critical HCBS Supports
QUEST Integration also covers a variety of other services essential for community living:
*   **Skilled Nursing (Private Duty):** Ongoing nursing care for members with complex medical needs that exceed the scope of intermittent home health services.
*   **Environmental Accessibility Adaptations (Home Modifications):** Physical adaptations to the home, such as ramps, grab bars, and modifications to bathrooms, to ensure the member's health, safety, and independence.
*   **Home Delivered Meals:** Nutritionally sound meals for members who cannot prepare their own and would otherwise require institutionalization.
*   **Non-Medical Transportation:** Transportation to community services, activities, and resources specified in the service plan.
*   **Counseling and Training:** Education for members, family, and caregivers on the nature of the disease and the disease process.
*   **Specialized Medical Equipment and Supplies:** Durable and non-durable medical equipment not covered under the standard State Plan.

### Institutional Care (Nursing Facilities)

For individuals whose medical needs are too complex to be managed in a community setting, QUEST Integration covers the full cost of care in a Medicaid-certified nursing facility (NF), provided they meet the NF-LOC and financial eligibility requirements.

## The Role of Managed Care Organizations (MCOs)

In Hawaii, all QUEST Integration benefits, including LTSS, are administered by contracted MCOs. As of the latest information, these MCOs include:
*   **AlohaCare**
*   **HMSA (Hawaii Medical Service Association)**
*   **Kaiser Permanente Hawaii**
*   **Ohana Health Plan (a WellCare Company)**

Once a member is determined eligible for QUEST Integration, they choose an MCO. The MCO is responsible for managing all aspects of the member's care, from coordinating primary and specialty care to authorizing and arranging LTSS through their network of providers and Community Care Management Agencies (CCMAs).

## How to Apply for QUEST Integration

The application process for QUEST Integration is managed by the Med-QUEST Division.

1.  **Determine Eligibility:** Review the financial and functional requirements to ensure you meet the criteria for the Aged, Blind, or Disabled (ABD) category and the NF-LOC.
2.  **Gather Documentation:** Collect necessary documents, including proof of income, assets, residency, and citizenship/immigration status.
3.  **Submit Application:** Applications can be submitted through several methods [7]:
    *   **Online:** Through the state's online eligibility application portal, KOLEA.
    *   **By Phone:** Call the Med-QUEST Division at (800) 316-8005.
    *   **In-Person:** Visit a local Med-QUEST Division office.
    *   **By Mail or Fax:** Download the application form from the Med-QUEST website and submit it to the appropriate office.
4.  **Clinical Assessment:** If the application is for LTSS, a clinical assessment (DHS Form 1147) will be conducted by a designated entity to determine if the applicant meets the Nursing Facility Level of Care.
5.  **MCO Enrollment:** Once approved, the applicant will be enrolled in QUEST Integration and will select a Managed Care Organization to coordinate their care.

## Conclusion

Hawaii's QUEST Integration program stands as a model for comprehensive, integrated long-term care. By combining acute and long-term services under a managed care umbrella, and by utilizing the flexibility of the 1115 Demonstration Waiver, the state has created a robust system that provides extensive Home and Community-Based Services, including coverage for assisted living. For Hawaii residents requiring long-term care, understanding the unique structure and benefits of QUEST Integration is the first step toward accessing the support needed to live independently in the community.
