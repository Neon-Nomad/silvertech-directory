# New Mexico Health Facility Complaint Process: Reporting and Investigation for Quality of Care

In New Mexico, the process for reporting complaints against licensed health facilities is a critical component of the state's regulatory oversight, designed to protect the health, safety, and welfare of residents and patients. This system is primarily managed by the **New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA)**, specifically through its **Division of Health Improvement (DHI)** [1]. The DHI is the state's designated agency for licensing health facilities and investigating allegations of poor care, regulatory violations, and, most critically, abuse, neglect, or exploitation.

This guide provides a detailed breakdown of the complaint process, identifying the correct agencies to contact for different types of complaints, the information required to file a report, and the steps taken during the investigation, ensuring that consumers are fully informed on how to seek redress and ensure accountability.

## The Central Role of the HCA Division of Health Improvement (DHI)

The HCA's Division of Health Improvement (DHI) is the state's regulatory arm for all licensed health facilities. Its authority stems from state statutes and the responsibility to enforce the New Mexico Administrative Code (NMAC) regulations that govern facility operations [2].

### Jurisdiction of the DHI

The DHI is the correct agency to contact for complaints related to the facility's compliance with licensing standards and the quality of medical or personal care provided. This includes complaints against:

*   **Nursing Homes (Skilled Nursing Facilities)**
*   **Assisted Living Facilities (ALFs)**
*   **Hospitals**
*   **Home Health Agencies**
*   **Hospices**
*   **Intermediate Care Facilities for Individuals with Intellectual Disabilities (ICF/IID)**

### How to File a Complaint with the DHI

The HCA maintains a dedicated, toll-free hotline for the public to report concerns. This is the most direct and effective way to initiate a regulatory investigation.

| New Mexico Health Facility Complaint Contact |
| :--- |
| **Agency:** New Mexico Health Care Authority (HCA), Division of Health Improvement (DHI) |
| **Complaint Hotline:** **1-800-752-8649** [3] |
| **Purpose:** To report complaints regarding quality of care, regulatory violations, and allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation in licensed health facilities. |

Complaints can also be submitted in writing or through the HCA's website, but the hotline is generally the fastest method for urgent concerns.

### Essential Information for a Comprehensive Complaint

A successful investigation relies heavily on the quality and detail of the information provided by the complainant. While anonymity is protected, providing detailed information ensures the DHI can act swiftly and effectively.

1.  **Facility Identification:** The full, correct name and address of the facility.
2.  **Resident/Patient Information:** The name of the person affected (if the complaint is on their behalf) and their location within the facility (e.g., room number, unit).
3.  **Incident Details:** A clear, factual, and chronological account of the incident(s). This must include:
    *   The specific date(s) and time(s) the incident occurred.
    *   The names or descriptions of any staff members, residents, or witnesses involved.
    *   The specific harm or potential harm that resulted (e.g., "Resident fell and broke hip due to lack of supervision," "Medication was administered late/incorrectly").
4.  **Regulatory Concern:** Clearly state the nature of the complaint (e.g., inadequate staffing, poor sanitation, dietary issues, physical abuse).
5.  **Complainant Contact:** Your name and phone number. The DHI will protect your identity from the facility, but having a contact allows the investigator to follow up for clarification or to provide updates on the investigation's status.

## The DHI Investigation and Enforcement Process

Once a complaint is received, it enters a rigorous process of triage, investigation, and, if necessary, enforcement.

### Triage and Prioritization of Complaints

The DHI prioritizes complaints based on the potential risk to residents, following federal guidelines for Medicare/Medicaid certified facilities:

*   **Immediate Jeopardy (IJ):** Allegations that place residents in a situation where serious injury, harm, impairment, or death is likely. These complaints trigger an investigation that must begin within **two working days** [4].
*   **High Priority:** Allegations of actual harm or a potential for more than minimal harm. These are investigated promptly, typically within **ten working days**.
*   **Low Priority:** Complaints alleging non-harmful violations, such as minor administrative issues or maintenance concerns. These are often investigated during the facility's next scheduled annual survey to conserve resources for high-risk cases.

### The Unannounced On-Site Survey

The hallmark of the DHI's investigation is the unannounced, on-site survey. Investigators arrive without warning to observe the facility's operations in their normal state. The investigation team will:

1.  **Interview:** Conduct confidential interviews with the resident, family members, facility staff (including nurses, aides, and administrators), and other residents or visitors.
2.  **Observation:** Directly observe the care provided, the interaction between staff and residents, the cleanliness of the facility, and the overall environment.
3.  **Record Review:** Scrutinize the resident's medical records, care plans, medication administration records (MARs), staffing schedules, and facility policies and procedures.
4.  **Exit Conference:** At the conclusion of the on-site visit, the investigators hold an exit conference with facility management to discuss preliminary findings.

### Enforcement Actions and Corrective Measures

If the investigation substantiates the complaint and finds the facility in violation of state or federal regulations, the DHI issues a **Statement of Deficiencies (Form CMS-2567)**. The facility is then required to submit a **Plan of Correction (POC)** detailing the steps it will take to correct the deficiencies and prevent their recurrence.

For serious or repeated violations, the HCA has the authority to impose a range of sanctions, including:

*   **Civil Money Penalties (CMPs):** Fines levied against the facility, which can be substantial.
*   **Directed Plan of Correction:** Requiring the facility to implement specific, mandated corrective actions.
*   **Denial of Payment for New Admissions (DPNA):** A severe sanction that halts Medicare/Medicaid payments for new residents until the facility is back in compliance.
*   **State Monitoring:** Placing a state-appointed monitor in the facility to oversee operations.
*   **License Revocation or Non-Renewal:** The ultimate sanction, resulting in the facility being shut down [2].

## Specialized Complaint Agencies: Ombudsman and APS

While the DHI handles regulatory and quality-of-care complaints, two other agencies are essential for specific types of complaints and advocacy.

### 1. Long-Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The Ombudsman is the resident's advocate, focusing on quality of life and resident rights, rather than regulatory compliance. They are the best resource for issues that may not violate a specific regulation but still negatively impact a resident's well-being.

| Long-Term Care Ombudsman Contact |
| :--- |
| **Agency:** New Mexico Aging and Long-Term Services Department (ALTSD) |
| **Ombudsman Hotline:** **1-866-451-2901** [5] |
| **Focus:** Resident rights, quality of life, conflicts with staff/administration, and general advocacy. |

The Ombudsman's services are confidential and impartial. They often resolve issues through mediation and negotiation, which can be a faster and less adversarial process than a formal DHI investigation.

### 2. Adult Protective Services (APS)

For allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult, the New Mexico Aging and Long-Term Services Department (ALTSD) operates Adult Protective Services (APS).

| Adult Protective Services (APS) Contact |
| :--- |
| **Agency:** New Mexico Aging and Long-Term Services Department (ALTSD) |
| **APS Hotline:** **1-800-432-2080** [6] |
| **Focus:** Investigating allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults, whether in a facility or in the community. |

The DHI and APS have a mandatory cross-reporting agreement. If a complaint to the DHI involves suspected abuse, it is immediately reported to APS for a parallel investigation.

## Public Access to Complaint and Inspection Records

Transparency is a key feature of New Mexico's oversight system, allowing the public to review the performance of licensed facilities.

*   **Health Facility Provider Search:** The HCA maintains an online database where consumers can search for a facility and view its official survey reports, including those resulting from complaint investigations [7]. This allows the public to verify the outcome of a complaint and see a facility's history of compliance.
*   **Medicare Nursing Home Compare:** For nursing homes, the federal **Nursing Home Compare** website provides a star-rating system and detailed information on health inspections, staffing, and quality measures, all based on data collected by the DHI [8].

## Conclusion: Empowering Consumers Through Accountability

New Mexico's complaint process is a robust, multi-agency system designed to hold health facilities accountable. The **HCA's DHI** is the regulatory enforcer, the **Long-Term Care Ombudsman** is the resident's personal advocate, and **Adult Protective Services** is the investigative body for maltreatment. By understanding the roles of these agencies and utilizing the provided hotlines and public search tools, New Mexico consumers are empowered to ensure that their loved ones receive the high-quality, dignified care they deserve.
