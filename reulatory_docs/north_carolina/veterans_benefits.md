# North Carolina Veterans Benefits: Long-Term Care, State Homes, and Financial Assistance

North Carolina is deeply committed to supporting its veteran population, offering a comprehensive suite of benefits and services designed to ensure access to high-quality long-term care. The **North Carolina Department of Military and Veterans Affairs (DMVA)** is the state's central coordinating agency, working in close partnership with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) to deliver these essential services [1].

This guide provides an in-depth look at the long-term care landscape for North Carolina veterans, focusing on the state-operated Veterans Homes, the critical financial assistance available through the VA, and the indispensable role of local support services.

## I. North Carolina State Veterans Homes

The most significant state-level long-term care benefit is the provision of **four State Veterans Homes**. These facilities are dedicated to providing 24/7 skilled nursing care to aged and infirm veterans in a setting that honors their service. While the DMVA manages the program, the homes are operated by private contractors to maintain a high standard of professional care.

### A. Locations and Services
North Carolina's four State Veterans Homes are strategically located across the state to ensure accessibility for veterans and their families:
*   **Black Mountain State Veterans Home**
*   **Fayetteville State Veterans Home**
*   **Kinston State Veterans Home**
*   **Salisbury State Veterans Home** (Located on the W.G. Hefner Medical Center Campus)

These homes provide a full spectrum of skilled nursing services, including:
*   24-hour medical and nursing care
*   Physical, occupational, and speech therapy
*   Recreational and social activities
*   Dietary and nutritional services
*   Hospice and palliative care

### B. Eligibility and Admission
Admission to a State Veterans Home requires meeting both state and federal criteria.
1.  **Veteran Status:** The applicant must be a veteran of the U.S. Armed Forces who was discharged under **honorable conditions** [2].
2.  **Residency:** The veteran must have been a resident of North Carolina for **two years immediately prior to the date of application** [3].
3.  **Medical Need:** A licensed physician must certify that the veteran requires the level of care provided in a skilled nursing facility.
4.  **Financial Consideration:** The VA pays a significant per diem rate to the state for each eligible veteran resident. The veteran is responsible for a co-payment, which is determined based on their income and assets. The cost of care is generally much lower than in a private nursing home, making the State Veterans Homes a highly valuable benefit.

The application process is rigorous and requires a completed packet, including a medical examination and proof of residency and service.

## II. Federal VA Financial Assistance for Long-Term Care

Beyond the State Veterans Homes, North Carolina veterans can access federal benefits that provide financial relief for long-term care in various settings, including assisted living and in-home care.

### A. VA Aid and Attendance (A&A) Pension Benefit
The **Aid and Attendance (A&A)** benefit is a non-service-connected pension that provides a substantial monthly supplement to qualified veterans and surviving spouses who require the assistance of another person for daily living. This benefit is crucial for veterans who need financial help to pay for assisted living (Adult Care Homes) or in-home care.

**A&A Eligibility Requirements:**
*   **Basic Pension Eligibility:** The veteran must meet the service requirements (e.g., served at least 90 days of active duty, with at least one day during a wartime period) and the financial requirements (income and net worth below a limit set by the VA).
*   **Medical Need:** The applicant must meet one of the medical criteria for A&A, such as requiring assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) or being confined to a bed.

### B. VA Health Care Long-Term Services
Veterans enrolled in the VA health care system may be eligible for a variety of long-term services, including:
*   **Community Nursing Home Program:** The VA contracts with private nursing homes in North Carolina to provide care when a VA facility is not available.
*   **Community Residential Care (CRC):** A program that places veterans who need a supervised living environment but not skilled nursing care into state-licensed assisted living facilities.
*   **Home-Based Primary Care (HBPC):** Provides comprehensive care to veterans with complex medical needs in their own homes.
*   **Homemaker and Home Health Aide Care:** Offers personal care and support services to help veterans remain independent at home.

### County Veterans Service Officers (CVSOs)
The most direct and valuable resource for veterans seeking long-term care benefits is the **County Veterans Service Officer (CVSO)**. The DMVA maintains a network of accredited CVSOs in most North Carolina counties.
*   **Expert Assistance:** CVSOs are trained and accredited to provide free, professional assistance in preparing, filing, and prosecuting claims for VA benefits, including the complex A&A pension and State Veterans Home applications.
*   **Local Access:** They serve as the local point of contact, providing personalized guidance to veterans and their families.

## IV. Other State Benefits Supporting Veterans' Financial Well-being

North Carolina offers several other state-level benefits that can indirectly support a veteran's ability to afford long-term care by reducing other financial burdens.
*   **Tax Exemptions:** North Carolina provides a significant property tax exclusion for the primary residence of a disabled veteran or the surviving spouse of a disabled veteran. Additionally, military retirement and Survivor Benefit Plan payments are **tax-free** in North Carolina [4].
*   **Employment Preference:** Veterans receive preference in state employment, and the state offers various programs to support veteran entrepreneurship.
*   **Education:** The state offers tuition assistance and scholarships for veterans and their dependents through programs like the North Carolina National Guard Tuition Assistance Program.

By leveraging the state's four Veterans Homes, the financial power of the VA's A&A benefit, and the expert guidance of the DMVA and local CVSOs, North Carolina provides a robust and comprehensive support system for its aging veteran population.
