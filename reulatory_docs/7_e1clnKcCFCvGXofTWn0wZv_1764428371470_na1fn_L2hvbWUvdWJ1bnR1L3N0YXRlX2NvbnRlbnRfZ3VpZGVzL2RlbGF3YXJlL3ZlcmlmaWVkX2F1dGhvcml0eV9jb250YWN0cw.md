# Verified Authority Contacts for Long-Term Care in Delaware

Navigating the complex landscape of long-term care, Medicaid, licensing, and advocacy in Delaware requires access to accurate and verified contact information for the relevant state and federal agencies. This guide compiles the essential contact details for the key authorities responsible for oversight, financial assistance, and consumer protection in the state.

The primary agencies involved are the Delaware Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS), the Office of Veterans Services (OVS), and the various divisions responsible for Medicaid, licensing, and advocacy.

## I. Medicaid and Financial Assistance Contacts

The **Delaware Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS)**, through its **Division of Medicaid and Medical Assistance (DMMA)**, is the central authority for the state's Medicaid program, known as the Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP), and the long-term care managed care program, the Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus).

### The Importance of the DMMA

The DMMA is the gateway to long-term care funding in Delaware. Individuals seeking financial assistance for nursing home care or Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) through DSHP-Plus must first establish financial and medical eligibility through the DMMA. The Customer Relations line is the essential first point of contact for all eligibility questions, including income and asset limits for long-term care Medicaid.

### The Critical Role of the Complaint Hotline

The DHCQ Complaint Hotline is the most direct route for consumers to report concerns about the quality of care, resident rights, or suspected abuse/neglect in a licensed facility. All complaints are investigated, and the DHCQ is the only agency with the authority to issue citations, fines, and other enforcement actions against facilities.

### Understanding the Ombudsman's Role

The LTCOP is a non-regulatory advocate. While they can investigate complaints, their power lies in mediation and advocacy, not enforcement. They are the best resource for residents who want a confidential, independent voice to help resolve issues with a facility. The Ombudsman's phone number is a vital contact for any resident or family member seeking advice or intervention.

## V. Detailed Contact Information and Agency Descriptions

To provide a more comprehensive resource, the following sections detail the mission and contact points for the most critical agencies.

### A. Delaware Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS)

The DHSS is the largest state agency and oversees the health and social welfare of Delaware residents.

*   **DHSS Main Switchboard:** (302) 744-4700
*   **DMMA (Medicaid):** The Division of Medicaid and Medical Assistance manages the $1.5 billion DMAP program, providing healthcare coverage to over 250,000 Delawareans. The division is responsible for all policy, eligibility, and payment for the program, including the DSHP-Plus long-term care waiver.
*   **DHCQ (Licensing):** The Division of Health Care Quality licenses and certifies over 1,000 healthcare facilities and agencies. Its mission is to protect the health and safety of Delawareans by ensuring compliance with state and federal standards. The DHCQ is the regulatory "watchdog" for long-term care.
*   **DSAAPD (Aging & Advocacy):** The Division of Services for Aging and Adults with Physical Disabilities provides a wide range of services, including the LTCOP and APS, to help older adults and people with disabilities live independently and with dignity.

### B. The Delaware Department of Justice (DOJ)

The DOJ plays a critical role in consumer protection and criminal enforcement within the healthcare system.

*   **Medicaid Fraud Control Unit (MFCU):** The MFCU is federally mandated to investigate and prosecute criminal violations of state laws regarding fraud in the Medicaid program. It also investigates and prosecutes abuse or neglect of residents in healthcare facilities that receive Medicaid funding. The MFCU's dual mission makes it a powerful tool for both financial and physical protection of vulnerable residents.

### C. The Delaware Veterans Home

The Delaware Veterans Home is a 150-bed skilled nursing facility located in Milford. It is a state-owned facility that provides high-quality, long-term care to eligible veterans, their spouses, and Gold Star parents.

*   **Services:** 24-hour skilled nursing, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy, and specialized memory care.
*   **Contact for Admission:** The main phone number, (302) 424-8500, should be used to request an application packet and discuss eligibility requirements.

## VII. The Importance of Official Websites and Online Resources

While phone numbers provide immediate access, the official websites of Delaware's state agencies are invaluable resources for detailed information, forms, and public records. These online portals are often the most up-to-date source for regulatory changes, eligibility criteria, and public transparency documents.

### Public Transparency and Record Access

The DHCQ's website, in particular, is a critical tool for consumer due diligence. It provides access to:

*   **Survey Reports:** Consumers can search for and review the results of annual and complaint-driven inspections for licensed facilities. These reports detail deficiencies found and the facility's plan to correct them.
*   **Regulations:** The full text of the Delaware Administrative Code governing nursing homes, assisted living, and other facilities is available online, allowing consumers to understand the standards of care facilities are legally required to meet.
*   **Registry Searches:** The online portals for the CNA Registry and the Adult Abuse Registry allow for quick, confidential checks on the credentials and history of individuals working in long-term care.

Relying on the official state websites ensures that the information used for decision-making is accurate and current, which is essential in a constantly evolving regulatory environment.

## VIII. Detailed Breakdown of the DHSS Organizational Structure

The Delaware Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS) is the umbrella organization for most of the long-term care and social service agencies. Understanding its structure clarifies the relationship between the different divisions and their respective roles.

### A. Division of Medicaid and Medical Assistance (DMMA)

*   **Focus:** Financial and medical eligibility for Medicaid.
*   **Key Programs:** Delaware Medical Assistance Program (DMAP), Diamond State Health Plan Plus (DSHP-Plus), and the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP).
*   **Primary Function:** To ensure that eligible Delawareans have access to comprehensive healthcare services, including long-term care, by managing the state's Medicaid program and its contracted Managed Care Organizations (MCOs).

### B. Division of Health Care Quality (DHCQ)

*   **Focus:** Licensing, certification, and regulatory enforcement.
*   **Key Programs:** Facility Licensing, Federal Certification (Medicare/Medicaid), Complaint Investigation, CNA Registry, and Adult Abuse Registry.
*   **Primary Function:** To protect the health and safety of patients and residents in healthcare facilities through regulatory oversight, unannounced inspections, and enforcement actions.

### C. Division of Services for Aging and Adults with Physical Disabilities (DSAAPD)

*   **Focus:** Community-based services, advocacy, and protective services.
*   **Key Programs:** Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP), Adult Protective Services (APS), Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) coordination, and the Delaware Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC).
*   **Primary Function:** To promote the independence and well-being of older adults and adults with physical disabilities by providing a range of services that support living in the community and protecting them from harm.

### D. Division of Public Health (DPH)

While not directly involved in long-term care licensing, the DPH plays a vital role in public health, including infectious disease control and emergency preparedness, which directly impacts the safety of long-term care facilities.

*   **DPH Main Contact:** (302) 744-4700
*   **Primary Function:** To protect and enhance the health of the people of Delaware through disease prevention, health promotion, and emergency response.

This organizational clarity is essential for consumers, as it directs them to the correct agency for their specific needs—whether it is a financial question (DMMA), a quality of care complaint (DHCQ), or a need for advocacy (DSAAPD/LTCOP).

## IX. Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT)

For Medicaid recipients, access to medical appointments is a critical benefit. Delaware's Medicaid program provides Non-Emergency Medical Transportation (NEMT) services.

*   **NEMT Coordinator:** The transportation is typically managed by a contracted broker. Recipients must contact the broker directly to schedule rides.
*   **Contact Information:** This information is often provided upon enrollment in a Managed Care Organization (MCO) under DSHP or DSHP-Plus. The MCO's member services line (listed in Section I) can provide the current NEMT contact number.

## X. Conclusion

The state of Delaware has established a comprehensive, multi-agency structure to support its vulnerable populations. The Verified Authority Contacts provided here represent the official and most reliable points of access for information, assistance, and complaint resolution. By keeping this list readily available, Delaware residents and their families are empowered to navigate the state's long-term care and veterans' services systems effectively, ensuring they can access the quality care and benefits they deserve.
