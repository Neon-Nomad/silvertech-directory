# Comprehensive Guide to Ohio Medicaid Long-Term Care Programs and Waivers

The Ohio Medicaid program, administered by the **Ohio Department of Medicaid (ODM)**, is a vital lifeline for thousands of Ohioans who require long-term services and supports. These services, which include nursing home care, assisted living, and home- and community-based care, are essential for older adults and individuals with disabilities who need assistance with daily living activities. Navigating the complexities of Medicaid eligibility, especially for long-term care, can be challenging. This comprehensive guide provides a detailed overview of the financial, medical, and programmatic requirements for accessing long-term care through Ohio Medicaid, with a specific focus on the state’s Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers.

## Understanding Ohio Medicaid Long-Term Care

Ohio Medicaid provides coverage for three main categories of long-term care for eligible residents [1]:

1.  **Institutional / Nursing Home Medicaid:** This is an entitlement program, meaning anyone who meets the eligibility criteria will receive assistance. Benefits are provided only in nursing homes.
2.  **Medicaid Waivers / Home and Community Based Services (HCBS):** These are not an entitlement; they have limited enrollment capacity and may have wait lists. These programs are designed to delay or prevent nursing home admissions by providing services in the home, adult day care, or assisted living settings.
3.  **Regular Medicaid / Aged, Blind, or Disabled (ABD):** This category ensures assistance for individuals meeting the eligibility requirements, covering various long-term care benefits such as personal care assistance or adult day care, which may be available.

The Ohio Department of Medicaid (ODM) is the state agency responsible for administering the program, ensuring it aligns with both state and federal requirements [1].

## Financial Eligibility: Income and Asset Limits (2025)

To qualify for long-term care Medicaid in Ohio, applicants must meet strict financial criteria, which are subject to annual adjustments. The rules differ significantly for single applicants and married couples.

### Single Applicant Financial Criteria

For a single applicant seeking Nursing Home Medicaid or HCBS waiver services in 2025, the following financial limits apply [2]:

| Financial Category | 2025 Limit (Single Applicant) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Income Limit** | **\$2,901 per month** | This is 300% of the Federal Benefit Rate (FBR). All income is counted, including Social Security, pensions, and IRA distributions. |
| **Asset Limit** | **\$2,000** | This includes countable assets like bank accounts, stocks, and bonds. Certain assets are exempt, such as the primary residence (up to a value of \$713,000), one vehicle, and personal belongings. |

An applicant whose income exceeds the limit may still qualify through an **Income Cap Trust** (also known as a Miller Trust or Qualified Income Trust). This trust allows the applicant to deposit their excess income into the trust, which then pays for their care costs, effectively reducing their countable income to the limit [2].

### Spousal Impoverishment Protections for Married Couples

When one spouse (the "applicant spouse") applies for Medicaid long-term care while the other spouse (the "community spouse") remains in the community, federal and state laws provide protections to prevent the community spouse from becoming impoverished. These protections are governed by the **Spousal Impoverishment Rule**.

#### Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA)

The CSRA is the amount of the couple's combined countable assets that the community spouse is permitted to keep. In 2025, Ohio follows the federal guidelines for the CSRA [3]:

| CSRA Category | 2025 Limit (Ohio) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Minimum CSRA** | **\$31,584** |
| **Maximum CSRA** | **\$157,920** |

The community spouse is allowed to keep a portion of the couple's combined assets, up to the maximum CSRA. The applicant spouse must reduce their countable assets to the \$2,000 limit.

#### Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA)

The MMMNA is the minimum monthly income the community spouse is allowed to keep to cover their living expenses. If the community spouse's own income falls below this amount, a portion of the applicant spouse's income can be transferred to them as a **Spousal Income Allowance**.

In Ohio, the MMMNA is subject to annual adjustments. For the period of July 1, 2025, to June 30, 2026, the following figures apply [3]:

*   **Minimum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance (MMMNA):** \$2,643.75
*   **Maximum Monthly Maintenance Needs Allowance:** \$3,948.00
*   **Shelter Standard:** \$793.13 per month

If the community spouse's income is below the MMMNA, they can receive a transfer of income from the applicant spouse to bring their total income up to the MMMNA. They can also receive an additional allowance if their housing and utility costs exceed the shelter standard, up to the maximum monthly allowance [2].

## The Nursing Facility Level of Care (LOC) Requirement

Financial eligibility is only one part of the equation. To qualify for any of the HCBS nursing facility waivers or institutional care, an individual must also meet a **Nursing Facility Level of Care (LOC)**.

The LOC is an evaluation of an individual's physical, mental, social, and emotional status. To meet a nursing facility LOC, the following conditions must be met [4]:

*   **Intermediate or Skilled LOC:** The person needs assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs) and/or taking medications. They may also require skilled nursing or rehabilitation services.
*   **Alternative to Institutional Care:** The services provided must be an alternative to institutional care.

The determination of LOC is typically conducted by a registered nurse or a social worker from a designated agency, such as a local Area Agency on Aging (AAA) or a specialized assessment unit, using a standardized assessment tool.

## Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) Waivers

Ohio is a leader in providing HCBS waivers, which allow individuals to receive care in their homes or other community settings, such as assisted living facilities, instead of a nursing home. The ODM and the Ohio Department of Aging (ODA) jointly administer four key HCBS nursing facility waiver programs [4]:

### 1. Assisted Living Waiver (ALW)

The Assisted Living Waiver is designed for individuals who require a nursing facility level of care but can safely receive services in a community-based residential facility, such as a licensed residential care facility (assisted living).

*   **Eligibility:**
    *   Aged 21 or older.
    *   Medicaid eligible and able to pay for their room and board expenses.
    *   Require a nursing facility LOC.
    *   Must live in a residential care facility licensed by the Ohio Department of Health (ODH) [4].
*   **Services Covered:** The waiver covers the cost of care services, but the individual is responsible for the "room and board" portion. Covered services include [5]:
    *   Personal care (assistance with bathing, dressing, grooming).
    *   Nursing services (medication administration, health monitoring).
    *   Homemaker services (housekeeping, laundry).
    *   Meals and nutritional support.
    *   Social and recreational activities.
    *   Community transition services (for those moving from a nursing home).
*   **Administration:** The program is operated by the ODA, with case management provided by PASSPORT Administrative Agencies (PAAs) [4].

### 2. PASSPORT Waiver

The PASSPORT (Pre-Admission Screening System Providing Options and Resources Today) Waiver is Ohio’s largest and oldest HCBS program, serving older adults in their homes.

*   **Eligibility:**
    *   Aged 65 or older, or physically disabled between the ages of 60 and 64.
    *   Require a nursing facility LOC.
    *   Medicaid eligible [4].
*   **Services Covered:** PASSPORT provides a wide array of in-home services to support independent living, including [6]:
    *   **Personal Care:** Assistance with ADLs.
    *   **Homemaker Services:** Light housekeeping, laundry, meal preparation.
    *   **Adult Day Health Services:** Structured programs in a community setting.
    *   **Home-Delivered Meals:** Nutritional support.
    *   **Waiver Nursing:** Skilled nursing services in the home.
    *   **Emergency Response System:** For immediate help in an emergency.
    *   **Minor Home Modifications:** To ensure safety and accessibility.
    *   **Respite Care:** To provide temporary relief for unpaid caregivers.
*   **Goal:** The primary goal of PASSPORT is to delay or prevent institutionalization by providing a cost-effective alternative to nursing home care [7].

### 3. MyCare Ohio

MyCare Ohio is a managed care program that integrates Medicaid and Medicare benefits for individuals who are eligible for both. It is only available in certain counties.

*   **Eligibility:**
    *   Aged 18 or older.
    *   Meet a hospital or nursing facility level of care.
    *   Must reside in a MyCare Ohio county [4].
*   **Structure:** Managed care plans coordinate all medical, behavioral health, and long-term care services, including HCBS waivers, through a single point of contact.

### 4. Ohio Home Care Waiver

This waiver is specifically designed for younger individuals with physical disabilities and unstable medical conditions.

*   **Eligibility:**
    *   Aged 0 to 59.
    *   Require a nursing facility LOC.
    *   Medicaid eligible [4].
*   **Services:** The waiver provides services to allow these individuals to receive care in their homes and communities, focusing on medical and physical support.

## Application Process and Resources

The application process for long-term care Medicaid and HCBS waivers in Ohio involves several steps:

1.  **Financial Application:** Individuals must first apply for Medicaid through their local County Department of Job and Family Services (CDJFS) or online through the Ohio Benefits portal. This determines financial eligibility.
2.  **Level of Care Assessment:** Once financially eligible, a functional assessment is conducted by the Area Agency on Aging (for PASSPORT and Assisted Living) or a specialized unit to determine if the applicant meets the Nursing Facility Level of Care.
3.  **Waiver Enrollment:** If the LOC is met, the individual can be enrolled in an available waiver program. Due to limited capacity, wait lists may exist for some waivers.

The Ohio Medicaid system is complex but offers robust support for long-term care. Understanding the eligibility rules, the financial protections for spouses, and the specific services offered by each HCBS waiver is crucial for securing the necessary care in the most appropriate setting.

## Advanced Financial Planning and Look-Back Period

A critical component of Ohio Medicaid long-term care eligibility is the **Medicaid Look-Back Period** and the associated penalty for uncompensated transfers.

### The 60-Month Look-Back Period

Ohio Medicaid, like all state Medicaid programs, enforces a 60-month (five-year) look-back period immediately preceding the date an individual applies for long-term care Medicaid. During this period, the state reviews all financial transactions to determine if any assets were sold, gifted, or transferred for less than fair market value.

*   **Purpose:** The look-back period is designed to prevent applicants from giving away assets to meet the \$2,000 asset limit and immediately qualifying for Medicaid.
*   **Penalty:** If an uncompensated transfer is discovered, the applicant is subject to a **penalty period** of Medicaid ineligibility. The length of the penalty period is calculated by dividing the total value of the uncompensated transfer by the average monthly cost of private-pay nursing home care in Ohio (the "divisor").

**Example of Penalty Calculation:**
If an applicant gifted \$100,000 to a family member and the state's average monthly nursing home cost (divisor) is \$10,000, the penalty period would be 10 months (\$100,000 / \$10,000 = 10 months). Medicaid would not pay for the applicant's care for those 10 months, starting from the date they would have otherwise been eligible.

### Exempt Transfers and Planning Strategies

While the look-back period is strict, certain transfers are exempt from penalty, offering legitimate planning opportunities:

1.  **Transfer to a Spouse:** Assets can be transferred to the community spouse without penalty. This is a common strategy to maximize the Community Spouse Resource Allowance (CSRA).
2.  **Transfer to a Disabled Child:** Assets can be transferred to a child who is blind or permanently and totally disabled.
3.  **Transfer to a Caretaker Child:** Assets can be transferred to a child who has lived in the applicant's home for at least two years immediately before the applicant moved to a nursing home and who provided care that delayed the need for institutionalization.
4.  **Special Needs Trusts:** Assets can be transferred into certain types of trusts, such as a First-Party Special Needs Trust (SNT) for a disabled individual under age 65, or a Pooled Special Needs Trust (PSNT) for a disabled individual of any age.

Consulting with an elder law attorney specializing in Ohio Medicaid is highly recommended to navigate these complex rules and ensure compliance with the look-back period [8].

## Detailed Examination of HCBS Waiver Services

To fully appreciate the value of Ohio's HCBS waivers, a deeper look into the specific services they fund is necessary. These services are crucial for maintaining quality of life and independence in a community setting.

### Assisted Living Waiver (ALW) Services in Detail

The ALW focuses on providing a supportive environment within a licensed residential care facility. The services are designed to be comprehensive and person-centered [5]:

*   **Personal Care Services:** This is the core service, providing hands-on assistance with Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) such as bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, and eating.
*   **Homemaker Services:** This includes general household activities like laundry, light cleaning, and meal preparation, ensuring a safe and sanitary living environment.
*   **Nursing Services:** Routine health monitoring, medication management, and other skilled nursing tasks that can be delegated to a residential care facility nurse.
*   **Social and Recreational Programming:** Facilities are required to provide a variety of activities to promote social engagement and mental well-being.
*   **Emergency Response:** 24-hour staff availability and an emergency call system are mandatory to ensure immediate assistance when needed.
*   **Case Management:** A dedicated case manager coordinates all services, monitors the individual's health status, and ensures the care plan is followed and adjusted as needs change.

### PASSPORT Waiver Services in Detail

The PASSPORT waiver is the primary program for in-home care, offering flexibility to tailor services to the individual's unique needs [6]:

*   **Adult Day Health Services:** Provides health, therapeutic, and social services in a community-based setting for a portion of the day. This is a critical service for providing respite to family caregivers.
*   **Home-Delivered Meals:** Ensures the individual receives at least one nutritious meal per day, addressing a common challenge for homebound seniors.
*   **Waiver Nursing:** Allows for intermittent skilled nursing care, such as wound care, injections, and health assessments, to be delivered in the home.
*   **Chore Services:** Assistance with heavy household tasks that the individual cannot perform, such as deep cleaning or yard work.
*   **Transportation:** Non-medical transportation to essential appointments, such as grocery shopping or social activities.
*   **Personal Emergency Response System (PERS):** A device that allows the individual to call for help 24/7, providing peace of mind for both the recipient and their family.

## The Role of the Area Agency on Aging (AAA)

The Area Agencies on Aging (AAAs) play a pivotal role in the administration of the PASSPORT and Assisted Living Waivers in Ohio. The state is divided into 12 planning and service areas, each served by an AAA.

The AAAs are responsible for [9]:

1.  **Pre-Admission Screening (PAS):** Conducting the initial assessment to determine if an individual meets the Nursing Facility Level of Care.
2.  **Case Management:** Assigning a case manager to each waiver participant to develop, implement, and monitor the person-centered care plan.
3.  **Provider Network Management:** Contracting with and overseeing the network of providers who deliver the HCBS services.
4.  **Information and Referral:** Serving as a central resource for information on all aging and long-term care services in their region.

The decentralized nature of the AAA system ensures that services are delivered with a local focus, adapting to the specific needs and resources of each community within Ohio.

## Conclusion: Securing Long-Term Care in Ohio

Ohio Medicaid is a comprehensive program that offers a range of long-term care options, from institutional nursing home care to flexible, community-based services through its HCBS waivers. The state’s commitment to providing alternatives to institutional care is evident in the robust nature of the Assisted Living and PASSPORT Waivers.

However, access to these vital services requires careful planning and a thorough understanding of the complex eligibility rules, particularly the income and asset limits and the spousal impoverishment protections. For Ohio residents and their families, the key to successfully navigating this system lies in proactive engagement with the Ohio Department of Medicaid, the Ohio Department of Aging, and the local Area Agency on Aging. By utilizing the resources and programs detailed in this guide, Ohioans can secure the necessary support to maintain their health, dignity, and independence in the setting that best meets their needs.
