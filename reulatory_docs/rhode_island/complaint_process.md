# Rhode Island Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Reporting Concerns and Ensuring Quality Care

For residents, family members, and concerned citizens in Rhode Island, understanding the proper channels for reporting concerns about long-term care facilities is essential for ensuring accountability and maintaining high standards of care. The complaint process in Rhode Island is multi-layered, involving state regulatory bodies, advocacy groups, and law enforcement agencies, each with a distinct role in protecting vulnerable adults.

The primary regulatory authority for investigating complaints related to the quality of care and licensing standards in nursing homes and assisted living residences is the **Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH)**, specifically the **Center for Health Facilities Regulation**.

## 1. Reporting to the Rhode Island Department of Health (RIDOH)

The RIDOH is the state's official agency responsible for licensing and regulating healthcare facilities. Complaints filed with the Department are investigated to determine if a facility is in violation of state licensing regulations or federal certification requirements (for Medicare/Medicaid facilities).

### What to Report to RIDOH

The Department investigates complaints related to the **quality of care** and **licensing standards**, including:
*   **Neglect:** Failure to provide necessary care, supervision, or services to maintain a resident's physical and mental health.
*   **Abuse:** Physical, sexual, verbal, or mental abuse.
*   **Misappropriation of Resident Property:** Theft or misuse of a resident's personal belongings or funds.
*   **Inadequate Staffing:** Insufficient number of qualified staff to meet resident needs.
*   **Environmental Safety:** Unsafe or unsanitary conditions in the facility.
*   **Medication Errors:** Improper administration or dispensing of medications.
*   **Violations of Resident Rights:** Denial of privacy, choice, or the right to voice grievances.

### How to File a Complaint with RIDOH

Complaints can be filed anonymously and are kept strictly confidential. The Department encourages prompt reporting of all concerns.

| Method | Contact Information | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Telephone (Complaint Hotline)** | (401) 222-5960 | This is the local number for the Center for Health Facilities Regulation. |
| **Email** | doh.ecomplaint@health.ri.gov | For general licensee complaints. |
| **Online** | [RIDOH Licensee Complaints Portal]() | The Department's website directs users to click on the specific licensee and then use the "file a complaint" button. |
| **Mail/Fax** | Contact the Department for the appropriate form and address. | |

**Important Considerations for Filing with RIDOH [1]:**
*   **Confidentiality:** The investigation is kept strictly confidential. The named licensee will be notified of the allegations and provided a copy of the complaint, but the complainant can request to remain anonymous.
*   **Scope:** RIDOH **does not** investigate fee or billing disputes, personality conflicts, or poor customer service that does not impact the quality of care.
*   **Timeline:** Investigations may take several months, especially if medical records or other information need to be subpoenaed.

## 2. Reporting Elder Abuse and Neglect

For cases involving suspected abuse, neglect, or financial exploitation of an elder (age 60 or older) or an adult with a disability (age 18-59), the primary point of contact is **Adult Protective Services (APS)**, which is housed within the **Rhode Island Office of Healthy Aging (OHA)**.

### The Role of Adult Protective Services (APS)

APS is mandated by state law to investigate reports of abuse, neglect, self-neglect, and exploitation of vulnerable adults. **All Rhode Islanders are mandatory reporters** of suspected elder abuse [2].

### How to Report to APS

Reports can be filed 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and can be made anonymously.

| Method | Contact Information | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Telephone (24/7 Hotline)** | (401) 462-0555 | The dedicated hotline for reporting elder abuse and self-neglect. |
| **Online** | [OHA Web Intake Form]() | The OHA provides a secure online form for reporting, which allows for the upload of supporting documents. |

**Types of Abuse Investigated by APS [3]:**
*   **Physical Abuse:** Inflicting physical pain or injury.
*   **Sexual Abuse:** Non-consensual sexual contact.
*   **Emotional/Psychological Abuse:** Inflicting mental pain, anguish, or distress.
*   **Neglect:** The failure of a caregiver to provide goods or services necessary to avoid physical harm, mental anguish, or mental illness.
*   **Self-Neglect:** The inability of a vulnerable adult to perform essential self-care tasks.
*   **Financial Exploitation:** Illegal or improper use of an elder's funds, property, or assets.

## 3. Advocacy and Mediation through the Ombudsman

The **RI State Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (RISLTCOP)**, housed within the **Alliance for Better Long Term Care**, provides an independent and confidential channel for resolving concerns. The Ombudsman's role is not regulatory, but rather one of advocacy and mediation.

### When to Contact the Ombudsman

The Ombudsman is an excellent resource for:
*   **Violations of Resident Rights:** Issues related to dignity, privacy, choice, and quality of life.
*   **Grievances:** Concerns that may not rise to the level of a regulatory violation but still negatively impact a resident's well-being.
*   **Information:** Seeking information about a resident's rights, facility quality, or long-term care options.

The Ombudsman can often resolve issues quickly through direct negotiation with the facility staff and administration, without the need for a formal regulatory investigation.

| Method | Contact Information | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Telephone** | (401) 785-3340 or (888) 351-0808 (Toll-Free) | Direct line to the Ombudsman's office. |
| **Website** | [Alliance for Better Long Term Care]() | Provides detailed information on the program and how to file a concern. |

## 4. Reporting Criminal Misconduct and Fraud

For serious matters involving criminal activity, such as severe physical abuse, sexual assault, or large-scale financial fraud, reports should be made to law enforcement.

### Rhode Island Attorney General's Office

The **Rhode Island Attorney General's Office** has a dedicated **Medicaid Fraud and Patient Abuse Unit** that investigates and prosecutes criminal offenses involving the Medicaid program and the abuse or neglect of residents in healthcare facilities.

| Method | Contact Information | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Telephone** | (401) 274-4400, ext. 1896 | Contact for the Health Care Unit. |
| **Online** | [AG Health Care Unit Complaint Form]() | For reporting health care complaints, including misconduct in nursing homes. |

The Attorney General's office works closely with the RIDOH and local police to ensure that criminal misconduct is addressed through the justice system [4].

## Summary of Complaint Channels

The following table provides a quick reference for the appropriate agency based on the nature of the complaint:

| Nature of Complaint | Primary Agency to Contact | Focus of Investigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Quality of Care / Licensing Violations** | RI Department of Health (RIDOH) | Regulatory compliance with state and federal standards. |
| **Abuse, Neglect, or Exploitation (Elder/Disabled)** | Adult Protective Services (APS) / OHA | Investigation and intervention to protect the vulnerable adult. |
| **Resident Rights / Quality of Life Issues** | RI State Long Term Care Ombudsman Program | Mediation and advocacy to resolve grievances. |
| **Criminal Misconduct / Medicaid Fraud** | RI Attorney General's Office | Criminal investigation and prosecution. |

## Conclusion

The health and safety of residents in Rhode Island's long-term care facilities are protected by a network of state agencies and advocacy organizations. Whether the concern is a regulatory violation, a case of suspected abuse, or a simple grievance about quality of life, a clear pathway exists for reporting. By utilizing the **RIDOH's Complaint Hotline (401) 222-5960** for licensing issues, the **APS Hotline (401) 462-0555** for abuse, and the **Ombudsman (401) 785-3340** for advocacy, citizens can play an active role in ensuring that all long-term care residents in Rhode Island receive the compassionate and high-quality care they deserve.
