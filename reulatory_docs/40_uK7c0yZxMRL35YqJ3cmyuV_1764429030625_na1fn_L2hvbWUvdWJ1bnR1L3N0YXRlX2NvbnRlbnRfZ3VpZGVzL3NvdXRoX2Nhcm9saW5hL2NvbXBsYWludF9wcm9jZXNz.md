# South Carolina Long-Term Care Complaint Process: A Guide to Reporting and Resolution

Ensuring the safety and quality of care for residents in long-term care facilities is a paramount concern for the state of **South Carolina**. The state has established a multi-layered system for receiving, investigating, and resolving complaints against nursing homes, assisted living facilities (Community Residential Care Facilities or CRCFs), and other healthcare providers. This guide details the official complaint process, identifying the key agencies involved, the types of complaints they handle, and the steps residents and their families can take to report concerns.

The primary regulatory body for facility-related complaints is the **South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH)**, while allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults are handled by the **Department of Social Services (DSS) Adult Protective Services (APS)**. Additionally, the **Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** provides confidential advocacy and mediation services.

## I. The Regulatory Complaint Authority: South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH)

The **South Carolina Department of Public Health (DPH)**, through its **Bureau of Healthcare Quality**, is the state agency responsible for licensing, certifying, and inspecting healthcare facilities. This bureau is the official channel for reporting violations of state licensing regulations and federal certification standards (Medicare/Medicaid) [1].

### A. What to Report to the DPH

Complaints filed with the DPH should focus on issues related to the facility's compliance with health, safety, and quality of care standards. These typically include:

*   **Quality of Care:** Inadequate medical care, medication errors, failure to follow physician's orders, or poor wound care.
*   **Facility Environment:** Unsanitary conditions, safety hazards, poor maintenance, or temperature control issues.
*   **Staffing Issues:** Insufficient staffing levels, unqualified staff, or failure to provide necessary services due to staff shortages.
*   **Dietary Concerns:** Inadequate nutrition, unappetizing food, or failure to accommodate special dietary needs.
*   **Resident Rights Violations:** Violations of a resident's rights that do not rise to the level of abuse (e.g., lack of privacy, failure to allow visitors) [2].

### B. Methods for Filing a Complaint with DPH

The DPH encourages complainants to use the most direct method possible, especially for urgent concerns.

#### 1. The Toll-Free Complaint Hotline

The most immediate way to report a concern is by calling the DPH's dedicated, toll-free hotline. This number is often referred to as the **CNA Abuse, Neglect, or Misappropriation Hotline**, but it serves as the central intake for all health facility complaints.

> **DPH Complaint Hotline:** **1-800-922-6735** [3]
> **DPH Local Phone:** (803) 545-4370

The hotline is staffed during business hours, and an on-call system is typically in place for urgent reports outside of those hours.

#### 2. Online Complaint Submission

The DPH website provides an online form for submitting complaints against licensed health facilities. This method is preferred for non-emergency situations as it allows the complainant to provide detailed information and attach supporting documents [4].

#### 3. Mail or Email

Complaints can also be submitted in writing via mail or email to the Bureau of Healthcare Quality. Written complaints should be as detailed as possible, including:
*   The name and address of the facility.
*   The name of the resident (if applicable).
*   A detailed description of the incident(s), including dates, times, and names of staff involved.
*   The names and contact information of any witnesses.

### C. The DPH Investigation Process

Once a complaint is received, the DPH Bureau of Healthcare Quality initiates an investigation process designed to be thorough and timely.

1.  **Triage and Prioritization:** Complaints are triaged based on the severity of the alleged violation. Complaints alleging immediate jeopardy to a resident's health or safety are investigated within two working days [5]. Less severe complaints are investigated during the next scheduled on-site visit or within a specific timeframe.
2.  **Unannounced Survey:** The investigation is conducted by DPH surveyors who arrive at the facility unannounced. They interview residents, family members, and staff, observe care practices, and review medical records and facility policies.
3.  **Findings and Enforcement:** If the investigation confirms a violation of state or federal standards, the DPH issues a **Statement of Deficiencies (Form 2567)**. The facility is then required to submit a **Plan of Correction (POC)** detailing how they will remedy the violations.
4.  **Enforcement Actions:** Depending on the severity and scope of the deficiencies, the DPH, in coordination with the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), can impose various enforcement actions, including:
    *   Civil Monetary Penalties (fines).
    *   Denial of payment for new admissions.
    *   Appointment of temporary management.
    *   Termination of the facility's participation in Medicare and Medicaid [6].

## II. Reporting Abuse, Neglect, and Exploitation: Adult Protective Services (APS)

While the DPH focuses on facility compliance, the **South Carolina Department of Social Services (DSS) Adult Protective Services (APS)** is the agency responsible for investigating allegations of abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults (age 18 and older) [7].

### A. What Constitutes a Reportable Offense to APS

APS investigates cases where a vulnerable adult is unable to protect themselves from harm. A vulnerable adult is defined as a person 18 years of age or older who has a physical or mental impairment that substantially impairs the person's ability to provide for his or her own care or protection.

Reportable offenses include:

*   **Physical Abuse:** Infliction of physical pain or injury.
*   **Sexual Abuse:** Non-consensual sexual contact.
*   **Emotional Abuse:** Infliction of mental anguish or distress.
*   **Neglect:** The failure of a caregiver to provide necessary care, or the failure of the vulnerable adult to provide for their own essential needs (**Self-Neglect**).
*   **Exploitation:** Illegal or improper use of a vulnerable adult's funds, property, or resources [8].

### B. The APS Reporting Hotline

All reports of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult should be made to the statewide APS hotline.

> **Adult Protective Services (APS) Hotline:** **1-888-CARE4US (1-888-227-3487)** [9]

This hotline is available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. South Carolina law mandates that certain professionals (e.g., healthcare providers, law enforcement, social workers) are **mandatory reporters** and must report suspected abuse immediately.

### C. The APS Investigation and Intervention

1.  **Investigation:** Once a report is received, APS initiates an investigation to determine if the vulnerable adult is at risk and if the allegations are substantiated.
2.  **Intervention:** If the allegations are substantiated, APS works to minimize the risk and provide protective services, which may include arranging for in-home services, seeking court-ordered protection, or facilitating a safe placement [10].
3.  **Coordination with Law Enforcement:** If the abuse or exploitation is criminal in nature, APS coordinates with local law enforcement or the **South Carolina Law Enforcement Division (SLED)**, which has a dedicated unit for investigating abuse in long-term care facilities [11].

## III. The Advocacy Channel: Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)

The **South Carolina Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** offers a confidential, non-regulatory channel for resolving complaints, focusing on resident rights and quality of life issues. The Ombudsman acts as an advocate for the resident, mediating disputes and educating all parties involved [12].

### A. What to Report to the LTCOP

The LTCOP is the ideal resource for complaints related to:

*   **Resident Rights:** Issues concerning privacy, dignity, freedom of choice, and access to personal belongings.
*   **Quality of Life:** Concerns about food, activities, social isolation, or the general atmosphere of the facility.
*   **Grievances:** Any complaint a resident has that they feel the facility has not adequately addressed.
*   **Transfer and Discharge:** Concerns about involuntary moves or evictions from the facility [13].

### B. Contacting the LTCOP

The LTCOP is administered by the South Carolina Department on Aging (SCDOA) and can be reached via their toll-free number.

> **LTCOP Toll-Free Phone:** **1-800-868-9095** [14]

### C. Confidentiality and Mediation

The LTCOP's strength lies in its independence and commitment to confidentiality.

*   **Confidentiality:** The Ombudsman will not reveal the identity of the complainant or the resident without their express permission, ensuring that residents can report concerns without fear of retaliation [15].
*   **Mediation:** The Ombudsman's approach is primarily one of mediation and negotiation, working with the resident, family, and facility staff to find a mutually acceptable solution. They are often successful in resolving issues quickly and informally.
*   **Referral of Serious Cases:** If the Ombudsman uncovers evidence of abuse, neglect, or serious regulatory violations, they are mandated to refer the case immediately to the DPH or APS for formal investigation [16].

## IV. Resident Protections Against Retaliation

South Carolina law and federal regulations provide strong protections for residents who file complaints or participate in an investigation.

*   **Right to Complain:** Residents have the right to voice grievances and recommend changes in services or staff without discrimination or reprisal [17].
*   **Whistleblower Protection:** Facility staff who report violations in good faith are also protected from retaliation, ensuring that those closest to the residents can speak up without fear of losing their jobs.
*   **Facility Policy:** Every licensed facility is required to have a written policy for handling resident grievances and must inform residents of the contact information for the DPH and the LTCOP [18].

## V. Summary of Key Complaint Contacts

To navigate the South Carolina complaint system effectively, it is essential to contact the correct agency based on the nature of the concern.

| Agency | Type of Complaint | Hotline/Contact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SC Department of Public Health (DPH)** | Violations of licensing/certification standards, quality of care, facility conditions, staffing. | **1-800-922-6735** (Toll-Free Hotline) |
| **DSS Adult Protective Services (APS)** | Suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a vulnerable adult (including self-neglect). | **1-888-CARE4US (1-888-227-3487)** (24/7 Hotline) |
| **Long Term Care Ombudsman Program (LTCOP)** | Resident rights issues, quality of life concerns, mediation, and advocacy. | **1-800-868-9095** (Toll-Free) |
| **Local Law Enforcement/SLED** | Immediate danger, criminal activity, or serious physical harm. | **911** (Emergency) |

By utilizing these resources, residents and their advocates can ensure that long-term care facilities in South Carolina are held accountable to the highest standards of care and that the rights and dignity of every resident are upheld.
